How doses are found

Every dose here is collected and compared across sources, not made up. Each compound is checked against as many of these five kinds of source as can be reached, and its card says which ones were:

  1. Labels, trials and patents. Approved drug labels (US, EU, Russia, Japan), published human trials, and the original patents. When one exists, it is the Label dose.
  2. Dosing guides. Peptide dosing wikis and guides, read side by side to see where they agree.
  3. Clinics. Telehealth, anti-aging and compounding-pharmacy protocols.
  4. Practitioners. Doctors, coaches and researchers who publish protocols on Substack, blogs and podcasts.
  5. Forums. What people say they run on Reddit and Longecity.

Check a dose yourself

  • mcg or mg. 1 mg is 1000 mcg. A slip between the two is the most common error online.
  • Vial size is not a dose. A 10 mg vial is often 20 to 40 doses.
  • Per kg or flat. Animal studies dose per kilogram. 10 mcg/kg is about 0.7 mg for a 70 kg person, not 10 mg.
  • Route. Oral, nasal, injected and IV doses are not interchangeable.
  • Count the sources. A number that appears on one site only, with no study or user reports, is not a dose. Look for two guides plus a trial or real user reports.
  • Higher is not always better. For some compounds the higher trial dose worked worse.

Not medical advice. Doses differ by person, and many of these compounds are not approved for human use. Talk to a clinician.
