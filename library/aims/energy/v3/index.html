Energy

Everyone files these under one word. They are not doing one thing. What actually makes energy in a cell, which compounds touch it, and which of them have anything real behind them.

Goal · Cellular energyResearch page · v3Compiled 6 Nov 2026

What they are. One enzyme blocker, two signal peptides, a membrane patch, a coenzyme, and a shelf of supplements. They plug into different points of the same assembly line, so none of them is a swap for another.

What studies show. The strongest human evidence here is not a peptide. It is exercise, then CoQ10 and creatine. SS-31 is the only approved drug and only for a rare disease. The rest is mice, cells, or still running.

What people report. A clean daytime lift for 5-Amino-1MQ and MOTS-c. CoQ10 and creatine are everyday supplements. For injected NAD+, the most common report is feeling nothing.

Watch out. A lab number is not a feeling, a short time in the blood is not a short effect, and fatigue with normal bloods is often iron, B12, thyroid or sleep, not a peptide.

1Which kind of energy are we talking about?

“Energy” is one word for at least six different things, and these compounds touch different ones. This is the whole reason they are not interchangeable.

ATP production
The actual fuel a cell spends, made in the mitochondria.
NAD+ carries the electrons in; SS-31 steadies the machine that runs it.
Mitochondrial function
How well the power plants are built and sealed.
SS-31 on the membrane; MOTS-c signalling the cell to adapt.
Metabolic signalling
Messages to burn fat, take up sugar, build mitochondria.
MOTS-c, 5-Amino-1MQ, PQQ, and exercise live here.
Exercise capacity
How far or long you can work before you stop.
Shown for MOTS-c and SS-31 in mice; a single SS-31 dose lifted muscle ATP capacity in older adults.
Fatigue
Whether you tire out, in a disease or with age.
SS-31 was tested head-on and did not beat placebo; CoQ10 helps mainly in heart failure or statin users.
The feeling of energy
The subjective lift people describe.
Almost all of the community reports, almost none of the trial data.

A compound can move one of these and leave the rest flat. SS-31 raised a mitochondrial number in older adults in two hours14 yet changed nothing about how tired people with a mitochondrial disease felt over 24 weeks.11 That gap is the honest centre of this page.

2How energy actually works, from scratch

If none of the words below mean anything yet, good, start here. The whole thing is four parts.

1

ATP is the cash. Your body cannot spend food directly. Every cell runs on one molecule, ATP, the only currency it takes, and you make and burn roughly your own body weight in it every day.

2

Mitochondria are the mint. Tiny power plants inside almost every cell. They take the raw material from food and stamp out ATP, on a folded inner wall called the membrane.

3

NAD+ is the delivery van. Food is pulled apart into electrons, and NAD+ is the van that carries those electrons to the plant. The plant uses their energy to mint ATP. That is why NAD+ comes up again and again.

4

NNMT is a slow leak. An enzyme called NNMT quietly drains the pool of NAD+ vans. Block it, which is what 5-Amino-1MQ tries to do, and more vans stay on the road. That one idea sits behind half the compounds here.

Now watch it run. Food goes in, NAD+ carries the electrons, they move down a chain of stations (CoQ10 is one of the ferries), the flow spins a turbine, and out comes ATP. Tap a compound to see exactly where it plugs in.

The whole chain, running. Follow one blue electron from NADH on the left all the way to oxygen on the right, then watch the turbine spin.

Most compounds touch this line at a single point. Only exercise builds more of the line itself. The picture shows where each one is thought to act, not how well it works.

3The ways people try to boost it, and which hold up

There is not one theory of energy, there are six, each tugging a different part of that line. This is the honest scorecard of the theory itself, before any single product.

Build more plantsProven, but only exercise

Make the cell grow new mitochondria (the PGC-1a switch). Exercise, MOTS-c, PQQ, SLU-PP-332

Endurance training reliably builds new mitochondria in people26. The compounds that claim to copy it are mice and markers, not proof.

Store the cashWorks under load

Keep a quick-release copy of ATP on the bench. Creatine

Solid for strength, and real for the brain when you are short on sleep or older32; close to nothing in a rested young adult.

Ferry the electronsWorks if you are low

Top up CoQ10, the shuttle in the middle of the chain. CoQ10

A real mortality win in heart failure27 and help for statin users; little evidence in the already well-stocked.

Protect the plantReal effect, did not translate

Stop the membrane leaking so the chain runs clean. SS-31

A single dose lifted a mitochondrial number in older adults14, yet it did not change fatigue in disease trials11. The machine ran better; people did not feel it.

Feed the carrierUnproven in people

Raise NAD+, the delivery van, or stop it leaking. 5-Amino-1MQ, injected NAD+

The mouse and mechanism story is good; in humans injected NAD+ is broken down before it lands17, and even the oral precursors give mixed functional results18.

Recycle old plantsHousekeeping, not a jolt

Clear worn mitochondria so the good ones work. Urolithin A

Improved endurance and inflammation over months, but missed its energy endpoints35. Quality, not a lift.

Fix a shortageReliable, if you are short

Replace a cofactor the line genuinely lacks. B12, magnesium, iron

The most dependable of all, and the most ignored: correcting a real iron or B12 shortfall beats every compound here49, and does nothing if you are not short.

The pattern: the theories that work best are the least exciting (move, sleep, fix a deficiency, train), and the most exciting ones (raise NAD+, build mitochondria with a shot) are exactly where the human proof runs thin.

5The peptides and research compounds

These are injected or experimental. None is a proven energy treatment in a healthy person. They run from “real drug, wrong job” to “mice only.”

5-Amino-1MQ

Not a peptide, a small molecule.

Your cells keep a pool of NAD+, the thing that carries energy, and an enzyme called NNMT quietly drains it. 5-Amino-1MQ is the plug for that drain: slow NNMT down, and more NAD+ and methyl groups stay in the cell to be spent. In obese mice it took off about 5% of body weight in eleven days while untreated mice gained, with roughly a third smaller fat cells1, and in old mice it beat exercise for grip strength.2 It is not a peptide, and no person has been in a trial yet.

The deeper read
Human: none. No registered trial, no human pharmacokinetics. Animal/cell: fat loss in obese mice without eating less1; ~40% more grip strength in aged mice, more than exercise alone and additive with it2; bigger regrown fibres and ~70% more force in injured old-mouse muscle.3 Much of the work comes from the group commercialising NNMT inhibitors. In the body: rat only, oral bioavailability ~38%, half-life ~6.9 h; human numbers not established.4 Status: unapproved research chemical; dosed in milligrams because it acts on an enzyme, so microgram injectable doses may do nothing.

Full card: 5-Amino-1MQ in the Library

MOTS-c

A peptide your own mitochondria write when you exercise.

When you work out, your mitochondria write a tiny note called MOTS-c that tells the body to burn fat and take up sugar like a trained athlete. Injecting it is an attempt to send that note without the workout. Given to old mice it let them run about twice as far as untreated ones5, and in people MOTS-c climbs roughly twelve-fold in muscle during hard exercise.5 The catch: the note your cells write and a shot in the fat are not proven to be the same thing, and no finished human trial of injected MOTS-c exists yet.

The deeper read
Human: measured, not given. Exercise raises it ~12-fold in muscle, ~1.5x in blood.7 No completed trial of injected MOTS-c; the nearest finished human data is for a modified analog, CB4211, safe over four weeks in fatty liver but both arms improved and the programme was dropped8, and a Phase 2a of MOTS-c itself began recruiting in 2026.9 Animal: old mice ran ~2x farther; blocked diet-induced weight gain, mainly through muscle.6 In the body: cleared in minutes; human half-life not established. Status: unapproved, banned in sport at all times by WADA as an AMPK activator23, and recommended 7-5 for pharmacy compounding by an FDA panel in July 2026 (advisory only).24

Full card: MOTS-c in the Library

SS-31 (elamipretide)

The only approved drug here, for one rare disease.

Every cell makes energy on a folded membrane, and when that fold frays the energy leaks out. SS-31 binds the fold and holds it steady, like patching a leaking power line so the current flows again. It is the only one of these that is an approved drug, cleared in 2025 for the rare disease Barth syndrome10, and a single dose lifted muscle energy capacity in healthy older adults within two hours.14 The honest part: in its bigger trials, for mitochondrial myopathy and for heart failure, it did not beat placebo.11,12

The deeper read
Human: the most data, and mixed. Approved for Barth syndrome on a muscle-strength endpoint; the randomized phase did not beat placebo.10 Negative in primary mitochondrial myopathy (218 people, no change in walking or fatigue at 24 weeks)11 and in heart failure (safe, no change in pumping).12,13 A single 2-hour infusion raised older adults’ muscle ATP capacity, gone by day 7.14 Animal: a single dose restored ATP toward young levels within an hour; weeks improved exercise tolerance.15,16 In the body: peak ~0.5 to 1 h under the skin, cleared to urine within 48 h, dose halved in severe kidney impairment.10 Status: approved only for Barth; grey-market SS-31 is not the approved product.

Full card: SS-31 (elamipretide) in the Library

NAD+ (injected or infused)

The energy coenzyme itself. Not NADH, NR or NMN.

NAD+ is the molecule that actually carries energy through every cell, and it is thought to run lower with age, so the pitch is to top it straight up by drip or shot. The problem sits at the front door: whole NAD+ put into the blood is taken apart within minutes, so how much ever reaches a cell as NAD+ is unknown.17 The trials that clearly raise NAD+ used the precursors NR and NMN, by mouth, which are different molecules on a different path.20,22 Keep them apart: a precursor study is not evidence for an NAD+ drip.

The deeper read
Human (injected NAD+ itself): a 6-hour IV infusion showed no plasma rise for 2 hours as it was broken apart17; a 2026 systematic review found no outcome trials of IV or IM NAD+ for anti-aging and judged it unproven18; a 2026 pilot compared IV NAD+ with IV NR for tolerability only.19 Kept separate (precursors, oral): NR raises NAD+ and lowers blood-pressure markers20, NR in aged muscle21, NMN and muscle insulin sensitivity in prediabetic women.22 None used injected NAD+. In the body: cleared in minutes to hours; subcutaneous route has no published human trial. Status: not approved, not on the FDA 503A list, one injection recalled for endotoxins, a death reported in 2026 after an unlicensed IV infusion.25

Full card: NAD+ (injected or infused) in the Library

Humanin

MOTS-c’s sibling, a distress flare, not a fuel.

Humanin is another peptide the mitochondria write, but it works like a distress flare: it summons protection for a stressed cell rather than making energy. The interesting human fact is who has a lot of it. Children of people who reached 100 carry much higher levels, and it falls with age and in Alzheimer’s.40 That is a correlation, not a treatment. There are no trials of giving humanin to people, and the age link does not prove that raising it helps.

SLU-PP-332, BAM15, methylene blue

The frontier: exciting in mice, thin or risky in people.

SLU-PP-332 forges the muscle’s “I just ran” memo without the run. Sedentary mice on it ran about 70% longer, with more mitochondria, and in obese mice it raised energy burn and improved metabolism42, but there is not a single human trial.41 BAM15 is an uncoupler: it makes mitochondria burn fuel less efficiently, like idling an engine with the heater on, so mice lost about 15% of body weight as fat at the same calories.43 It has no human data, and its whole class carries the lethal history of DNP, which is why uncouplers are dangerous. Methylene blue is the one approved drug of the three, a backup electron carrier that at low dose nudged memory in a small human trial (about 7% better retrieval).44 But it is a strong MAO inhibitor that can cause serotonin toxicity with antidepressants45, must be avoided in G6PD deficiency46, and flips to harmful at higher doses. None of these is an energy supplement.

6The supplements with human data

Less glamorous than peptides, better evidence. These are the ones a doctor would reach for first, and two of them have stronger human trials than anything above.

CoQ10 (ubiquinol)

The electron ferry you can top up by mouth.

Your energy chain has a little ferry in the middle that carries electrons between the big stations, and that ferry is CoQ10. Your body makes it, statins lower it, and it drops with age, which is why topping it up sometimes helps. It is the one on this page with the strongest human evidence: in a 420-person heart-failure trial, 300 mg a day for two years cut deaths from 18% to 10%.27 It will not do much for an already well-stocked young person, and the statin-muscle-pain trials disagree with each other.29,30

The deeper read
Human: Q-SYMBIO (420 with heart failure), 300 mg/day for 2 years, major cardiac events 15% vs 26%, all-cause death 10% vs 18%; no change in the short 16-week symptoms.27 Statins lower circulating CoQ10, and two meta-analyses on statin muscle pain flatly disagree, one null29, one positive30. In the body: absorbed slowly (fat-soluble), peak ~6 h, half-life ~33 h; ubiquinol and solubilised forms absorb better.28 Status: dietary supplement, good safety record; may blunt warfarin. Plain truth: a real win in heart failure and statin users, little evidence it lifts energy in the already-healthy.

Creatine monohydrate

The rechargeable battery for instant ATP.

ATP gets spent in a flash, and creatine is the rechargeable battery pack that snaps a fresh copy back onto it, in muscle and in brain. It is one of the most studied and cheapest supplements there is. Beyond strength, it shows up for the brain when you are short on sleep or older: a memory review found a clear gain in people aged 66 to 76 and almost none in the young and rested.32 It is not in the energy chain itself, and the mental effect is modest and mostly shows when you are depleted.33

The deeper read
Human: with resistance training, meaningful strength gains (meta-analysis of 23 studies)31; memory meta-analysis overall small, but a real effect in older adults (66 to 76) and essentially none in rested young people32; a single dose blunted cognitive decline during sleep deprivation by up to 12%.33 In the body: oral, raises muscle creatine over days of loading; the acute brain effect appears within hours under stress. Status: supplement; safe up to 30 g/day for 5 years in trials; causes a little water-weight gain.34 Plain truth: rock-solid for strength, real for the brain mainly under load, close to nothing in a rested young adult.

Urolithin A (Mitopure)

The signal to take out the mitochondrial trash.

Old, worn-out mitochondria clog a cell like dead batteries in a drawer, and urolithin A is the signal to take out the trash, a process called mitophagy. It is a gut metabolite of pomegranate and berries that many people cannot make on their own. In a four-month trial in older adults it did not move the headline numbers, walk distance or peak ATP, but it did improve muscle endurance and lower inflammation.35 Read it as mitochondrial house-cleaning, not an energy jolt, and note most of its trials come from the company that sells it.

The deeper read
Human: first-in-human showed it is safe and shifts mitochondrial gene markers36; the older-adult RCT missed both primary endpoints (6-minute walk, max ATP) but improved muscle endurance and lowered CRP35; a maker-authored trial improved strength but missed its primary power endpoint.37 The pattern repeats across trials: secondary wins, primary misses, mostly maker-affiliated. In the body: oral; circulates as conjugates; clean human half-life not established. Status: supplement (FDA GRAS). Plain truth: best read as mitochondrial quality and endurance over months, not acute energy.

PQQ

Sold as the one that builds new mitochondria. The proof is thin.

Most things on this list help the mitochondria you already have; PQQ is sold as the one that makes new ones. The honest version is thinner than the pitch: the “build new mitochondria” story rests on a single 2009 study in mouse liver cells38, and no human trial has shown more mitochondria. The small human studies that exist are mostly funded by the makers, with modest signals for inflammation and cognition.39 Promising mechanism, early evidence, not a proven cornerstone.

The deeper read
Cell/animal: in mouse liver cells PQQ switched on the CREB to PGC-1a biogenesis program.38 Human: a 10-person crossover lowered inflammation markers with biogenesis only inferred from urine39; other small trials (cognition, cholesterol) are mostly industry-funded and not about ATP. No human trial shows more mitochondria. In the body: oral ~20 mg/day studied; human half-life not established. Status: supplement (FDA GRAS).

The cofactors: carnitine, ALA, magnesium, B2, B12

The parts the assembly line genuinely needs. They help most when you are short on them.

Acetyl-L-carnitine. Shuttles fat into the mitochondria to be burned. Fatigue trials exist in frail elderly, but the standard view is no clear benefit in healthy people who make their own.50
Alpha-lipoic acid. A cofactor and antioxidant for the enzymes that feed the chain. Its real evidence is in diabetic nerve pain (symptoms down about half at 600 mg), not energy in healthy people.47
Magnesium. ATP only works as Mg-ATP, so you cannot run the line without it, and low magnesium is common. But topping up a replete person has no clean fatigue trial behind it.51
Riboflavin (B2). The raw material for two electron carriers in the chain. Its best trial is migraine prevention (responders 59% vs 15%), not everyday tiredness.48
Vitamin B12. A cofactor whose deficiency genuinely causes fatigue and anemia, and quiet shortfalls are common with age. Correcting a real deficiency helps; dosing a replete person does not.52

The thread through all five: they fix a shortage. If your level is already fine, another dose does little, which is the opposite of how they are usually sold.

7Do they add up?

People stack these and call it a mitochondrial or energy stack. On paper the pieces fit: 5-Amino-1MQ frees the raw material for NAD+, NAD+ is that material arriving ready-made, CoQ10 ferries the electrons, SS-31 protects the machine, creatine stores the output, and MOTS-c, PQQ and exercise tell the cell to build more. You can see why the story sells.

This is a hypothesis, not a finding. There is no human study of any two of these given together, let alone the whole stack. The pieces looking complementary is a reasonable guess from the mechanisms, nothing more, and mechanisms that look like they fit often do nothing extra, or get in each other’s way, once they meet in a real body. Nothing here is a recommended stack.

8What people report

First-hand reports from forums, kept with the no-change and worse ones. Not a trial, not an effectiveness rate: people choose what to post, many stack several things, and nobody checks the vial.

5-Amino-1MQ53 logged

A fast clean lift is the most consistent theme (15+), often within an hour, plus some fat loss (6). Split overall. Doses ran from under 1 mg to 50 mg, so “worked” and “nothing” are often different doses. Injection lumps and jitters at the top end.

MOTS-c56 logged

Clean daytime energy and pre-workout without jitters lead (15+), with more endurance (9). About two-thirds of the clear reports said it worked. The standout downside is an injection-site histamine reaction in about 11 people.

SS-31small pool

Mental clarity and energy top a smaller set; some describe a few hours of a lift. Too thin for a rate.

NAD+ (injected)67 logged

Felt nothing is the single most common outcome (15, some after months). Most positive reports sit inside stacks. IV drips bring nausea and chest tightness run fast; shots sting.

CoQ10 & creatinemainstream

Not grey-market. Decades of everyday use and real trials; creatine for training and the aged brain, CoQ10 for statin users and heart failure. Both low-drama, both better evidenced than the peptides.

9Check these before a peptide

The most common fixable cause of low energy is not a missing peptide. It is a missing nutrient or a bad night. Fatigue with “normal” bloods is often low iron, low B12, an underactive thyroid, poor sleep, or low mood, and correcting a real shortfall beats any compound on this page.

Iron (ferritin)
A normal blood count does not rule it out. In tired women who were not anemic but had low ferritin, iron cut fatigue about 29% vs 13% on placebo.49
Vitamin B12
Quiet shortfalls are common with age and on plant-based diets, and deficiency genuinely causes fatigue. Worth testing before supplementing blindly.52
Thyroid
An underactive thyroid is a classic, treatable cause of persistent tiredness. A simple test rules it in or out.
Sleep and mood
The least glamorous and most common. No mitochondrial supplement competes with fixing short or broken sleep, or treating low mood.

10Safety and uncertainty

Read before you trust any of this

  • A lab number is not a feeling. SS-31 can lift a mitochondrial measurement in hours and still not change how tired someone feels over months.
  • Short in the blood is not short in effect, and detection is not benefit. NAD+ clears in minutes; that tells you nothing either way.
  • Mice are not people. The strongest results for the peptides, SLU-PP-332 and BAM15 are in rodents; human doses are estimated.
  • Precursors are not injected NAD+. The good NAD+ trials used oral NR or NMN. They do not show a drip works.
  • Uncouplers are dangerous. BAM15’s class includes DNP, which has killed people. No human has safely used BAM15 for energy.
  • Methylene blue is a drug, not a supplement. It can cause serotonin toxicity with antidepressants and must be avoided in G6PD deficiency.45,46
  • Supplements help a shortage, not a surplus. CoQ10, magnesium, B12 and the rest do most when you are low. Topping up a replete, rested young person does little.
  • Legality and purity. Only SS-31 and methylene blue are approved drugs, each for something else. MOTS-c is banned in sport; grey-market material has unknown purity; a death followed an unlicensed IV NAD+ infusion.

This page reports what has and has not been shown. It is not medical advice and not a dosing, cycling or combination protocol. Study doses are given only to explain an experiment.

11Common questions

Are all these doing the same thing?

No. They hit different points of the ATP line, an enzyme, a signal, a membrane, a coenzyme, a buffer, plus supplements that fix shortages. Grouped under one word, not interchangeable.

Which has the best human evidence?

Exercise, then CoQ10 and creatine. SS-31 is the only approved drug but only for Barth syndrome, and its bigger trials were negative. The injected peptides have no completed human energy trial.

What should I try first for low energy?

Rule out the boring causes: iron, B12, thyroid, sleep, mood. Fixing a real shortfall beats every compound here. Exercise is the only thing proven to build new mitochondria.

Does injecting NAD+ raise my NAD+?

Unclear. Infused NAD+ is broken apart before it can be measured rising. The trials that clearly raise NAD+ used oral precursors (NR, NMN), different molecules.

Does PQQ really build new mitochondria?

In a dish, in mouse cells. No human trial has shown more mitochondria. Treat the biogenesis claim as promising, not proven.

Is CoQ10 worth it if I am healthy?

Mostly if you are on a statin or over 50, or have heart failure. In an already well-stocked young person the evidence for an energy lift is weak.

Do they work better together?

Unknown. No human combination study exists. The complementary-stack idea is a hypothesis, and this page is deliberately not a stack recommendation.
