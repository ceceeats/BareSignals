Brain

Focus, memory, mood and drive. Around thirty compounds get stacked for this, from Russian nasal peptides to mushroom extracts, and the gap between what they promise and what has been measured is wider here than anywhere else on this site. So this page starts with the thing all of them have to beat.

Goal · Brain & cognitionResearch page · v2Compiled 10 Nov 2026

What they are. Four separate families that get lumped together: short Russian peptides sprayed up the nose, bigger molecules built to copy growth factors, stimulants and wakefulness drugs, and ordinary supplements. They work by completely different means and only the last group has a solid trial base.

What studies found. Searched on 10 November 2026, ClinicalTrials.gov holds no registered trial for any of the Russian peptides, and none for Dihexa, P21, PE-22-28 or Adamax either. The compounds with real trials, modafinil and the supplements, mostly produce effects smaller than taking the same test twice.

What people report. Semax and Cerebrolysin carry genuine first-hand data, around 25 usable reports each, and people describe clarity and drive rather than memory. Several of the most heavily marketed compounds have zero reports from anyone, anywhere.

Watch out. Two foundational papers behind compounds on this page have been retracted. One compound has no literature at all, and the name may not even match the molecule. And the search results for all of this are now dominated by invented "user reports".

If you take one thing from this page: simply sitting the same cognitive test a second time improves your score by about as much as the best supplement here, and by more than modafinil does in a rested person. Almost nothing on this page has been shown to beat the act of practising.

1How the brain actually works, from scratch

Every compound on this page claims a piece of one process: one brain cell getting a message to the next, and that connection getting stronger. Before any of them make sense, it helps to see the thing they are claiming to improve, because it is stranger and more specific than the marketing suggests.

Start with the scale, because the numbers are the part people get wrong.

86 billionneurons, counted rather than estimated. And only 19% of them are in the cortex, the folded outer part everyone means by "the brain". Most of them are in the cerebellum, which is a tenth of the weight.74
4 laps of the Earthof insulated wiring. Between 150,000 and 180,000 kilometres of myelinated fibre in one head, and the total shrinks with age.75
2% and 20%of your body weight, and of your energy. And the thing nobody expects: hard thinking barely moves the total. Task-evoked changes are often under 5%. Regions shift and reallocate; the bill does not go up.76
4 secondsfrom the blood supply stopping to the lights going out, measured directly in people in the 1940s. Vision narrows to a tunnel at one second, the eyes fix at three.77
15%the chance that a signal arriving at a synapse releases anything at all. Five out of six do nothing. The brain is built out of unreliable parts and gets reliability from numbers.78
98 of 100small-molecule drugs do not cross into the brain. The barrier is 600 kilometres of sealed plumbing, and it is the central obstacle for everything on this page.79

Now follow one signal all the way in. Six steps, from a stubbed toe to a connection that is physically different afterwards. Tap through it.

Two things in that sequence are worth carrying into the rest of the page. Release is unreliable by design, which is why a compound that nudges the odds slightly could in principle matter. And the five places compounds act are genuinely different places, which is why treating them as one category is the first mistake.

2Which kind of better brain do you mean?

"Brain fog", "focus" and "memory" get used as one thing, and they are not. The compounds on this page move different ones, and most of the disappointment in this field comes from someone taking something for the wrong one. Before anything else, work out which of these you are actually after.

Alertness

Being awake rather than foggy. Whether you can stay switched on through an afternoon.

What actually moves it: sleep, caffeine, modafinil. This is the one compounds move most reliably, and it gets sold as the others.

Drive

Wanting to start. The difference between knowing what to do and being able to begin.

What moves it: the dopamine side, bromantane and phenylpiracetam, and modafinil. Not a thinking improvement, and worth separating from one.

Focus, or sustained attention

Staying on one thing while other things pull at you.

What moves it: modest gains from caffeine with L-theanine, and from the stimulants. The most claimed and least measured.

Working memory

Holding several things in mind at once while you use them. Mental juggling.

The one domain where modafinil showed anything at all, at 0.28, and the one most cognitive tests actually measure.

Learning and recall

Laying down something new and getting it back later. What people usually mean by memory.

Almost nothing here moves it in a healthy person. Creatine does in people over 66. Bacopa moves recall by milliseconds.

Processing speed

How fast you get to the answer, not whether you get there.

Declines with age, with poor sleep and with alcohol. Responds to fixing those, not reliably to anything else here.

Mental stamina

Whether hour five is as good as hour one.

What people actually buy modafinil for, and the thing effect sizes from single-session tests completely fail to capture.

Mood and calm

Anxiety quiet enough to think. Often the real complaint underneath a cognitive one.

What Selank, L-theanine and ashwagandha are really for. Treating low mood is the highest-yield single move for a cognitive complaint.

The honest pattern across the whole page: the compounds that work move the top three, and the claims are mostly about the middle three. Almost nothing on this page has been shown to improve learning and recall in a healthy adult.

3The baseline, which almost nothing here beats

Every other page on this site states the natural history before the reports: how many people get better anyway. For a brain page that question takes a strange and much more awkward form, because the thing you are trying to improve is measured by a test, and people get better at tests by taking them. That single fact explains most of what people believe about this entire category, so it belongs before any compound, not in a footnote.

Across 68 studies and 3,281 healthy people, simply sitting a working-memory test a second time raised scores by an effect size of 0.28. By the fifth sitting it reached 0.97. Using a different version of the test does not help: identical forms scored 0.32, alternate forms 0.30, a difference that was not statistically meaningful.1 Gains only wash out after something like a year.

The cleanest demonstration of why that matters came from a trial that was not studying supplements at all. Over sixteen weeks and three testing sessions, the healthy comparison group improved by an effect size of 0.33 from practice alone. The group actually taking a drug improved by 0.36.2 The drug's apparent benefit was indistinguishable from the benefit of having sat the test before.

Now put the two interventions nobody is selling next to that. Exercise, pooled across 36 studies in adults over 50, at 45 to 60 minutes of at least moderate intensity: 0.29.3 And sleep, which is the uncomfortable one. In a laboratory trial, 48 healthy adults held to six-hour nights for two weeks accumulated cognitive deficits equivalent to up to two nights of no sleep at all. Their own ratings of how sleepy they felt plateaued after a few days and could not tell six-hour nights from four-hour nights.4 People lose this much without noticing, then go looking for a peptide.

The honest centre of this page. Of thirteen supplements with real trials, exactly two produce an effect larger than an hour of moderate exercise. Both work only in people who are already impaired or deficient. One of those two rests on a single trial of 44 people run by the company selling it. And the research peptides, the expensive half of this page, mostly have no human trial at all to compare.

One more reason to be careful with your own impressions. Two trials on this page could not report their own results honestly because of this problem. In the nicotinamide riboside trial, walking speed improved significantly in the placebo group and not in the treated group, which the authors put down to practice.5 In the magnesium L-threonate trial, the sleep and anxiety outcomes could not be assessed at all, in the authors' own words, "because of strong placebo effects".6

4Check these first

The most common cause of poor focus and foggy thinking is not a missing peptide. It is a bad night, a nutrient shortfall, a mood problem or a drug you are already taking. Every one of these is cheaper to test than a vial, and the first two are more common than everything on this page put together.

This section is also where the honesty gets uncomfortable, because several of these are clearly causes and much less clearly fixable. Both halves are given.

Sleep apnoea. Between 425 million and 936 million adults have it depending on severity, and most do not know.47 It is the single most common hidden cause of waking up unrefreshed.

And the honest half: treating it reliably fixes sleepiness and only weakly moves cognition. The big trial, 1,098 people with a sham-CPAP control over six months, was null on two of its three cognitive domains at both visits. One meta-analysis found attention improving by 0.19 and nothing else. Another found no effect on any scale once treatment ran past twelve weeks. Fix it for your heart and your sleepiness; do not expect it to make you cleverer.

Depression and anxiety. The most common reason a cognitive complaint is not a cognitive disease. Current depression costs 0.39 on cognitive testing across 33 studies, and the deficits do not fully lift when the mood does: across 252 studies and over 20,000 people in remission, 55 of 75 cognitive measures were still impaired, and the number of past episodes explained most of the difference.48

Treating it is the highest-yield single move for a cognitive complaint, and it does not fully normalise things. Both are true.

Medications you are already taking. The most immediately fixable item on this list and the one almost nobody checks. Drugs with anticholinergic action, which includes common antihistamines, some bladder and sleep medicines and a few antidepressants, carry 1.49 times the odds of dementia at the highest cumulative exposure in a study of 284,343 people, with about 10% of cases attributable at population level.49

Worth noting for fairness: antidepressant classes without anticholinergic action show no such signal. It is the drug property, not the category.

Iron, if you menstruate. Low ferritin without anaemia is common and a normal blood count does not rule it out. Across four trials in 714 people, fatigue fell by 0.38; in the best single trial, 29% of women improved against 13% on placebo, and it only worked in those whose ferritin was genuinely low.50

The null worth knowing: in blood donors whose ferritin was successfully raised, fatigue did not move at all. Correcting a real shortfall helps. Topping up a normal level does not.

Vitamin B12. A genuine, reversible cause, and the clearest success story here. Of 121 followed neurological episodes every single one responded to treatment, 47% recovered completely, and how well someone did tracked how long they had been symptomatic before treatment.51 More than a quarter had a normal blood count, so the usual test misses them.

And the other half, which the supplement aisle depends on you not knowing: in people who are not deficient, B vitamins do nothing for cognition. Across 14 trials and 27,882 people the effect was between −0.03 and 0.06.

Alcohol, at ordinary levels. The "moderate drinking protects the brain" story does not survive imaging. At 7 to 14 units a week the odds of hippocampal shrinkage were 3.4 times higher than in abstainers, rising to 5.8 above 30 units, and light drinking conferred no protection at all.52

What nobody can tell you yet is how much comes back when you stop. We looked; no usable recovery figure has been published.

Thyroid, but only if it is properly underactive. Overt hypothyroidism matters. Subclinical almost certainly does not. In a trial of 737 people whose thyroid numbers were corrected from 6.40 to 3.63, the difference in symptoms was zero, against a threshold of nine points for a change anyone would notice, with no benefit on cognition either.53

Hearing. Strongly linked to cognitive decline, and the honest result is awkward. Unaided hearing loss carries a higher risk than aided. But the one large trial of hearing aids, 977 people over three years, was flatly null on its primary analysis, with a difference of 0.002.54 The much-quoted 48% slowdown is a 238-person subgroup inside it.

Vitamin D. Weak, and expected to be weak. Pooled across 24 trials and 7,557 people the effect on global cognition was 0.128, with nothing on any specific domain, and a trial of 4,143 people over nearly eight years found nothing.55 Worth correcting a real deficiency, not worth expecting a brain effect.

Long COVID fog. Real and common: brain fog in about 32% and measurable deficits in about 20% across 1.29 million people.56 The uncomfortable part is that almost nothing is established for it. Online cognitive behavioural therapy has moderate-certainty evidence for concentration. Vortioxetine, CoQ10, hyperbaric oxygen, probiotics and the rest do not.

The pattern worth taking from this section. Several of these are genuine causes whose treatment does not restore cognition, which is a different and more honest claim than "fix this and you will think better". The two that reliably repay the effort are correcting a real B12 or iron shortfall and treating low mood. Everything else on this list is worth doing for its own sake, and should not be sold to you as a cognitive fix.

5The ten theories, and which ones hold up

Every compound on this page rests on a theory of why thinking goes well or badly. The theories are rarely stated out loud, which is convenient, because several of them have been tested and did not survive. Here they are with a verdict each, the way the Energy page does it, and the pattern comes out the same way: the theories that explain the most are the ones nobody can sell you.

It is mostly expectancy and practiceExplains the most

That the felt effect is belief, practice and the fact that you chose to change something.

This is not a dismissal, it is the highest-scoring theory on the list. In one experiment, handing people a suggestive flyer and one hour produced a gain equivalent to 5 to 10 IQ points.58 Nothing else here comes close to that. Add the practice effect of 0.28 rising to 0.97, and expectancy plus retesting can manufacture the entire experience people describe. Every unblinded personal account on this page, including a convincing one, belongs here first until something rules it out.

It is mostly sleepHolds up

That cognition is a sleep-quality problem wearing a disguise.

Sleep loss drives attention lapses at 0.78, an effect no compound on this page reverses or comes near.59 Note the shape of it, which is the useful part: the damage lands hard on attention and barely touches reasoning, at 0.13 and not significant. Sleep loss does not make you stupid, it makes you unable to stay on task, which is exactly what people describe as brain fog. The most common real cause, and the least monetisable.

Dopamine, restated honestly as driveHolds, but not as cognition

That raising dopamine improves thinking. It improves the willingness to think, which is different.

A direct demonstration settled this: the lever dopamine moves is how much effort feels worth spending, not capability.60 The stimulant numbers agree. Methylphenidate manages 0.21 overall, modafinil 0.12, and d-amphetamine produces no cognitive effect at all. Keep the theory, change its name. It explains what stimulant users report and it is not a theory of cognition.

Nutrient shortfallTrue where there is a shortfall

That most cognitive complaints are a deficiency rather than a missing drug.

Large and reliable when a deficiency genuinely exists, and nil otherwise. The cleanest evidence anywhere on this page: vitamins and minerals across 83,000 participants, and nothing.61 B vitamins in healthy people, between −0.03 and 0.06. The category's most useful honest claim and its least profitable one.

NeuroinflammationHalf a theory

That brain fog is inflammation.

The causal half is the best-demonstrated thing in this whole section: give healthy people a dose of bacterial endotoxin and memory drops by 0.55 to 0.64, on demand.62 The treating half is empty. Minocycline over two years moved cognition by 0.1 points. Naproxen made fast decliners worse. The most abused frame in consumer copy, because as used there it names no measurable quantity, so no result can disconfirm it.

Mitochondrial energyOnly under load

That thinking is limited by energy supply.

The entire theory sits in two numbers from one meta-analysis of the same compound: creatine at 0.88 in adults aged 66 to 76 and 0.03 in people aged 11 to 31.33 It works when the system is strained, by age, sleep loss or illness, and does nothing when it is not. A theory about stress on the system, not about raising a ceiling.

Vascular, blood flowReal, over decades

That better perfusion means better thinking.

Genuine but on a timescale nobody is shopping for: intensive blood-pressure control cut mild cognitive impairment with a hazard ratio of 0.81 over years, while the dementia endpoint missed significance.63 And the supplement version failed: ginkgo at full dose for 6.1 years came in at 1.12, pointing the wrong way. Real medicine, slow, and not a supplement.

The cholinergic hypothesisRight biology, dead as a reason to buy choline

More acetylcholine means better memory. The oldest theory here and the one that built the choline aisle.

Tested harder than any other, by the drugs built on it. Donepezil genuinely works on the scale: 2.67 points on ADAS-Cog at 26 weeks. Then the outcome that matters: at three years, 42% versus 44% went into institutional care, a difference of nothing.64 And in healthy young adults, 2 to 2.5 grams of choline produced no effect, with the statistics actively supporting the null. Intact as a theory of memory. Finished as a reason to take choline.

The plasticity window, and trainingSustained by being untestable

That the gains come from practice and the compound only opens the window.

The training half is settled and the answer is narrow: you get better at the trained task and it does not transfer. Three large negative syntheses say so, two scientific consensus statements say so, and a regulator fined the industry's biggest brain-training company two million dollars over its claims.65 The "a compound opens the window" extension has no human demonstration at all. It survives because it cannot fail: every null result becomes "you did not train hard enough during the window".

The neurotrophic, or BDNF, hypothesisThe most oversold here

Grow and protect synapses, and cognition follows. Half this market is sold on this sentence.

It breaks into two questions that get collapsed into one. Do these raise BDNF in a person? The entire human evidence for the whole Russian family is one open-label study in 110 stroke patients that published no BDNF values, and Selank's only brain-tissue result goes the other way, lowering it. And if you raised it, would you notice? Nobody has tested that chain in a healthy person. The biomarker correlates with the state it is meant to index at about 0.1841, and a review of the field states flatly that blood BDNF cannot be recommended as a clinical biomarker.42 Its commercial function is to give a mechanism diagram to products with no outcome data.

The ranking, plainly. Expectancy explains the most. Sleep is the most common real cause. Dopamine explains what stimulant users actually feel, once you stop calling it cognition. Nutrient shortfall is true exactly where a shortfall exists. And the two theories doing the most commercial work, BDNF and the plasticity window, finish last. The best-supported explanations for feeling sharper are a good night, a corrected deficiency, and the belief that you did something about it. None of those has a vial.

One independent test of the BDNF chain is worth more than the rest of the argument: a group outside Russia gave Noopept to rats for 14 days and measured hippocampal BDNF, NGF and learning directly. No change in any of the three.15

6Where these actually came from

Almost nothing on this page was invented to make a healthy person think better. They came from looking for something else: a motion-sickness drug, a cotton dye, a blood-pressure fragment, a Parkinson's poison, a piece of a protein that was being thrown away. The accidents are more interesting than the mechanisms, and they explain the field better than any theory does.

The word itself, and the bar nobody clears

Corneliu Giurgea, a Romanian working at a Belgian pharmaceutical company, was not looking for a memory drug. He was working on motion sickness. He was studying GABA, GABA does not get into the brain when swallowed, so he made versions that would. One of them was piracetam, and it did not help motion sickness. It was eventually sold for myoclonus, a movement disorder. The cognitive use was off-label from the beginning.

In 1972 he coined a word for what he thought he had found, from the Greek noos, mind, and tropein, to turn. Mind-turning. There is a detail in that paper's own indexing that tells the whole story: it is filed under the vestibular system, the balance organ.66 The motion-sickness work and the naming of the entire field are the same document.

He also set five criteria for what deserved the name, as they are usually summarised: it should improve learning and memory; protect what is learned against disruption; protect the brain against injury; sharpen the brain's control mechanisms; and have none of the usual effects of a psychoactive drug, no sedation, no stimulation, very low toxicity.

Hold the page against that bar and almost everything falls off. The fifth criterion alone removes modafinil, phenylpiracetam, bromantane and 9-Me-BC, all of which are stimulating by design. The first removes everything whose human record is repair in the impaired rather than improvement in the healthy, which is Cerebrolysin, citicoline, alpha-GPC, cGP, P21, Dihexa, PE-22-28 and arguably Semax. The honest conclusion is a circular one: the only compound that clearly meets the criteria is piracetam, because the criteria were written to describe it. Fifty-four years on, almost nothing has met the bar the man who named the field set for it.

We have not read the 1972 French original, which we could not obtain, so the five criteria above are given as they are universally reproduced rather than as verified wording.

Ten origins worth knowing

1

Methylene blue: the dye cotton rejected. Synthesised in 1876 at BASF as a textile dye, and a commercial failure at it. It became the first synthetic compound used as a clinical antiseptic, carried Ehrlich's malaria work in 1891, and was used to calm psychotic agitation in 1899. A hundred and fifty years later it is still a medicine. It is also the only compound on this page whose founding idea, that you could build a chemical to seek out one target, turned out to be right.

2

Semax: a stress hormone with the hormone switched off. The surprise is in one line of a 1986 abstract. The ACTH fragment acted as a learning stimulant and "did not reveal steroidogenic and melanocyte-stimulating activity".67 They had taken a piece of a stress hormone and kept the behaviour while losing the hormone. The Dutch scientist who started that whole line of work titled his own account of it David and Goliath.

3

Noopept and cGP are the same ring, found from opposite ends of the earth. This is the best story here and nobody tells it. A Moscow institute reverse-engineered piracetam into a two-amino-acid version and discovered that the body already makes its prototype, a ring called cyclo-prolylglycine.68 Separately, a laboratory in Auckland found the same ring as a natural breakdown product of IGF-1.69 A Belgian motion-sickness drug and a growth hormone messenger, converging on one tiny ring, on two continents, without citing each other. Both are on this page as separate compounds.

4

PE-22-28 was found in the offcut. A receptor protein gets cut by an enzyme, and the 44-amino-acid scrap released in the process was assumed to be waste. Someone looked at the waste. It blocks a potassium channel and produced antidepressant effects in mice in four days, where the standard drugs need weeks. It was described as the first natural antidepressant peptide ever identified.70

5

9-Me-BC: they were hunting a poison and found the opposite. A Dresden laboratory was looking for the natural neurotoxin behind Parkinson's, inside a family of chemicals that turns up in cooked meat and tobacco smoke. They reported, for the first time, a member of that family that grows dopamine neurons instead of killing them.71

6

Dihexa came from a blood-pressure fragment, and its mechanism paper was retracted eleven years later. It was built from angiotensin IV, a piece of the system that controls blood pressure. The paper establishing how it works was published in 2014 and retracted in April 2025.23 Most of what is written about Dihexa online predates the retraction. The authors' own 2014 review had already described the receptor identity as contested.

7

Lion's mane: the strong compounds are in the part nobody eats. Two different families were found, chemically unrelated to each other: hericenones in the fruiting body, the mushroom you buy, in 1991, and erinacines in the mycelium, the root-like network, in 1994. The mycelial ones were the ones titled "strong stimulators". Practically, that is the most useful fact about this product on the whole page.

8

Magnesium L-threonate is the only purpose-built compound here, and it works backwards. It was made at MIT specifically to get magnesium into the brain. The mechanism is counterintuitive enough to remember: it improves memory by reducing the chance of any single synaptic release while increasing the number of release sites, which selectively favours bursts of signal over background noise.72

9

Creatine took a hundred and sixty-one years to get from jar to tub. Isolated from meat broth in 1832 and named after it, kreas, flesh. Shown in 1912 that eating it loads the muscle. The mechanism was not understood until the 1960s. It went on sale as a supplement in 1993.

10

Adamax has no discovery at all. No inventor, no institute, no year, no patent, no paper. We looked three different ways. That absence is the entry, and it is the most honest thing on this list.

Myths this page does not repeat

Stories everyone tells about these compounds that do not survive being checked.

Nine stories that do not survive checking

"Bromantane was a masking agent and the Russians were caught with it in 1996." Several Russian athletes did test positive at those Games. But the cases went to arbitration and the athletes won: the appeals were allowed and the disqualifications set aside, on the ground that the IOC had not proved to a sufficient degree of certainty that bromantane was comparable to explicitly banned substances.30 The ban came in 1997, after the case. What should be dropped outright is the idea that it was designed as a masking agent. It was built as an actoprotector, for heat, low oxygen and exertion.

"Bromantane was given to Soviet cosmonauts." That story belongs to a different drug, bemitil, whose own review states plainly that its first recipients were Soviet cosmonauts and that it was used to prepare athletes for the 1980 Moscow Olympics. For bromantane the same review mentions no space use at all.73 The page can still tell a cosmonaut story, just not that one: the claim legitimately attaches to phenylpiracetam, through the Soviet space medicine institute, though that trace runs through the manufacturer.

"Methylene blue was the first synthetic drug ever given to a person." Too strong, and we had it too strong ourselves at first. The supportable claim is narrower: the first synthetic compound used as an antiseptic in clinical therapy. And the review that states even that carries no citation of its own for the sentence.

"Lion's mane contains nerve growth factor." It does not. Its compounds were isolated as stimulators of NGF synthesis, in cells in a dish. The mushroom contains no NGF, and NGF itself does not cross into the brain.

"Bacoside A is the active compound in bacopa." False as stated. The institute's own work in the 1970s showed bacoside A is a mixture of four different compounds.

"Piracetam was invented to improve memory." No. Motion sickness programme, approved for a movement disorder, cognitive use off-label from the start.

"Epitalon lengthens telomeres in people." That claim traces to a slide presentation from 2015, not to a peer-reviewed paper.

"Cerebrolysin was developed in Austria in 1949." Every source we found asserting that date was a seller, with no citation. So the date is not printed here.

And one pattern rather than one myth. For Semax, Selank, Cortagen, Pinealon and Epitalon, the overwhelming majority of the published literature comes from the institute that originated them, and in the Khavinson family essentially from one group. That is not a reason to dismiss the compounds. It is a reason to say it once, plainly, instead of citing twelve papers that all came out of the same building.

7Four families, not one shelf

"Nootropics" is a shopping category, not a mechanism. What actually sits on this page is four groups that have almost nothing to do with each other, and keeping them apart is most of the work of understanding the field. The evidence quality runs in almost the opposite direction to the price.

Family one · short peptides, usually nasal

The Russian family

Tiny fragments of hormones the body already makes, developed in Soviet and Russian institutes and approved there, for narrow uses, on evidence that would not pass elsewhere. They are cheap, well tolerated, and genuinely interesting. They also have no registered trial anywhere in the international system.

Who lives here: Semax, Selank, both N-acetyl amidate versions, Noopept, Cortagen, Pinealon.

Family two · built to copy growth factors

The plasticity peptides

Bigger, newer molecules designed in Western labs to do what BDNF, NGF and HGF do: build and keep synapses. The most ambitious group on the page, and the one where the gap between the marketing and the record is widest. Four of six have never been given to a person in a trial.

Who lives here: Cerebrolysin, Dihexa, P21, PE-22-28, Adamax, cGP.

Family three · stimulants and wakefulness

Drive compounds

Not peptides, and not really about memory. These change how awake and how motivated you are, and the honest reading of the evidence is that this is the whole of what they do. The one licensed drug in the group has the most human data on the page, and the smallest measured cognitive effect.

Who lives here: bromantane, phenylpiracetam, 9-Me-BC, modafinil, methylene blue.

Family four · the ordinary half

Supplements

The cheapest things here and the only ones with a real trial base, which is exactly why they are the most useful section to read carefully. The lesson that runs through all of them is that almost every positive result comes from people who were already impaired or deficient, not from healthy adults.

Who lives here: Lion's Mane, bacopa, alpha-GPC, phosphatidylserine, magnesium L-threonate, L-theanine, ashwagandha, taurine, creatine, omega-3, citicoline, B12, NAD+ precursors.

One compound people expect here is deliberately absent. ARA-290 is a small-fibre nerve compound, acting on corneal nerve density and peripheral pain signalling, not a brain compound, so it stays in the Library rather than on this page.

8Where each one acts, and how well that is known

Tap a compound. The brain lights where there is evidence it acts, and the way it lights tells you how good that evidence is: a solid block means it was measured in living humans, a softer block means it was measured in animals, and a dashed outline means nobody has measured it at all and the region is reasoned from how the molecule works.

Six compounds light nothing, and a panel says so. That is not a gap in this page. It is the finding. The richest map belongs to modafinil, because one experiment ran it against amphetamine and recorded where it did not act as carefully as where it did.43

10The Russian family

These are the compounds that make this category interesting and also the hardest to judge fairly. They are real molecules with real pharmacology, registered as medicines in Russia, used by hundreds of thousands of people, and almost entirely absent from the international evidence system. Dismissing them as fake is wrong. Treating a Russian registration as equivalent to a Western approval is also wrong. The useful move is to look at exactly what each registration covers and what the pivotal trial actually did.

One number frames the whole family. Searched on 10 November 2026, ClinicalTrials.gov holds zero registered trials for Semax, Selank, Noopept, Epitalon, Cortagen and Pinealon combined. Not failed trials. No trials.7

Semax

ACTH fragment 4-7 + Pro-Gly-Pro7 amino acidsNasal or injectedRegistered in Russia

The flagship, and the only one here with both an approval and a real body of user reports.

Semax is a four-amino-acid piece of ACTH, a hormone you already make, with a three-amino-acid tail added to stop it being broken down immediately. The ACTH piece was chosen because it has the behavioural effects of the hormone without the hormonal ones: it does not raise cortisol.

The approval, stated precisely, because this is where most confusion starts. Semax is registered in Russia as 1% nasal drops under registration Р N000812/01, and the indication is only the acute period of moderate to severe ischaemic stroke, as part of combination therapy.8 The memory, attention and optic nerve indications that get quoted online belong to a separate 0.1% product. Two products, two different indication lists. Merging them is how "approved in Russia for cognitive enhancement" gets into circulation, and it is not what the stroke registration says.

The pivotal stroke study is the part that has to be read honestly: 30 treated patients against 80 patients on conventional therapy. Not randomised, not blinded, no placebo, no effect size published, available in Russian only.9 That is the foundation of the registration.

What does exist in healthy people is two small imaging studies and neither measured whether anyone thought better. One gave a single 1% nasal dose to 24 people, 14 active and 10 placebo, and found a larger frontal part of the brain's resting network afterwards, in a twenty-minute window, with no cognitive outcome measured at all.10 The other, 52 people comparing Semax, Selank and placebo, found changes in how the right amygdala connected to nearby temporal regions, again with no cognitive or anxiety outcome.11 These are genuine placebo-controlled human studies. They just do not answer the question anyone is asking.

What people feel. 26 usable first-hand reports, the largest real set on this page: 17 positive, 4 mixed, 4 worse, 1 no change. The recurring description is clarity and drive arriving within minutes, not better memory, lasting four to nine hours. Trouble falling asleep is the commonest complaint. Several people report no withdrawal after stopping.

The clearest practical finding in the entire community sweep is about dose, not route. One careful logger found roughly 200 micrograms per dose did nothing much, and about 600 micrograms produced clear-headedness. Nobody anywhere compares nasal against injected on its own terms; the two people who inject said they did it for dose accuracy, not for strength.

The deeper read
In rats, the best-characterised thing about it, and it is not a simple boost. A single nasal dose matched to human use raised BDNF protein 1.4 times and its receptor's activation 1.6 times in hippocampus, with BDNF gene activity up threefold. But the timing is the interesting part: at 20 minutes expression fell in hippocampus while rising in frontal cortex; at 40 minutes both were back to normal; at 90 minutes both rose; by 8 hours both were at baseline again. A biphasic, region-opposite response that nobody selling it describes. Other targets: it is the strongest inhibitor of enkephalin-degrading enzymes in human serum of the three tested, at 10 micromolar against Selank's 15 to 20 and Epitalon's 500. In rat cell culture it raised survival of cholinergic basal forebrain neurons 1.5 to 1.7 times, without affecting other neuron types. Side effects on record: a Russian review reports nasal discoloration in about 10% and raised blood glucose in about 7.4% of diabetic patients; long-term effects are described as unknown. Human pharmacokinetics: searched and not found. The label states 60 to 70% nasal absorption, which is a label figure, not an independently published study.

N-Acetyl Semax Amidate, and NA Selank Amidate

Modified formsNasal, injectedZero PubMed recordsUnapproved

The versions most people actually buy, and the ones with no literature whatsoever.

Both modifications do the same thing in principle: cap the front end with an acetyl group and the back end with an amide, so the enzymes that chew peptides from either end have nothing to grab. The chemistry is sound and the degradation they are built to prevent is real and documented.

What does not exist is any test of it. Searched three different ways, PubMed holds zero records for N-Acetyl Semax Amidate and zero for N-Acetyl Selank Amidate.12 No human study, no animal study, no pharmacokinetics, no safety data, no regional work. The potency multipliers quoted for them, commonly two to three times the parent compound, have no traceable source. The most careful user log on the forums independently judged the amidate "at least twice as potent" as plain Semax, which is a real observation from one person and the entire basis of that figure as far as anything published goes.

What people feel. Reports exist and are generally positive, folded into the Semax count above, describing the same clarity with a longer window. One practical note recurs: nasal sprayers clogging after a few days.

Selank

Tuftsin analogue + Pro-Gly-Pro7 amino acidsNasalRegistered in Russia

Sold as a non-sedating anti-anxiety peptide. The evidence for the anxiety part is the weakest thing in this family.

Selank is built on tuftsin, a fragment of an antibody with immune effects, with the same stabilising tail as Semax. Its label figures are striking if accurate: 92.8% absorption from the nose, in the bloodstream within 30 seconds, gone over about five minutes, with no metabolites detected and nothing in 24-hour urine.

The pivotal trial compared 30 people on Selank against 32 on medazepam, a benzodiazepine, and reported the anxiety effects as "similar". No effect size, no confidence interval, no p-value published.13 And the thing that matters most: there is no placebo arm anywhere in the Selank literature. A compound whose entire claim is a subjective feeling of calm has never been tested against a dummy.

There is also a specific claim worth correcting. Selank is routinely described as working on the amygdala like a benzodiazepine does. Targeted searches pairing Selank with the amygdala, and with GABA or benzodiazepine receptors, return zero results. That is why the brain map above leaves the amygdala dark for Selank. What does exist is rat work: one nasal dose changed the expression of 36 genes more than twofold in hippocampus, mostly membrane proteins, and in mice the route changed the pharmacology, with injection raising frontal-cortex GABA binding sites 38% while nasal dosing raised hippocampal NMDA binding sites 23%, and both effects appearing only in the anxious mouse strain and not the calm one.

What people feel. Only 5 usable reports, the thinnest set of any compound people actually buy, and they lean towards mild. The one report that addressed the question directly described feeling "mildly calmer, not sedated" with no real anxiety relief. The one strong anxiolytic claim came from someone taking four other substances who had already retracted an earlier attribution to Selank.

Noopept

Dipeptide, piracetam-derivedOralHalf-life ~23 minRegistered in Russia

The one you swallow, the one that splits people most sharply, and the one where mechanism and user reports agree about something uncomfortable.

Noopept was built to be a peptide version of piracetam, and by effective dose it is about a thousand times more potent in rodents. The twist is that Noopept itself is undetectable in the brain. What appears there is a breakdown product, cyclo-prolylglycine, which rises 2.5-fold an hour after dosing. So the active substance is the metabolite, whose human pharmacokinetics are unpublished. The label half-life of 0.38 hours, about 23 minutes, cannot by itself support twice-daily dosing; the metabolite has to be doing the work.

Its pivotal Russian trial is, in a sense, worse documented than the others: PubMed carries no abstract for it and the full text could not be reached, so its sample size and design are unknown to us. We are not going to describe a trial we could not read.

The most useful finding is the one that corroborates the complaints. In rats, Noopept raised fast EEG activity across the brain, and the effect depended on NMDA receptors. But with repeated dosing the effect was replaced by a different pattern by the eighth injection and was absent by the ninth.14 The mechanism predicts the tolerance people describe. And the only independent, non-Russian test of the whole story gave Noopept to rats for 14 days and found no change in hippocampal BDNF, no change in NGF and no change in learning.15

What people feel. The most divided compound here, and there is a number for it. A volunteer blinded trial, 16 active and 4 placebo, found 68.75% on Noopept reporting effects against 25% on placebo, though its own author disowns its rigour. A later community survey rated it at about the level the survey itself treated as the placebo threshold. Of 21 reports: 12 positive, 5 no change, 4 worse, with headache the usual complaint and two serious reactions involving alcohol.

Cortagen and Pinealon

Khavinson peptide bioregulatorsInjected or oralNo human brain dataUnapproved outside Russia

Marketed as gene-level regulators of specific organs. The record behind them is thinner than any other group on this page.

Cortagen has no human study. Its single best result is in rats: a cut and sutured sciatic nerve regrew 27% faster with 40% better conduction velocity. That is peripheral nerve repair, not cognition, and it has no human replication. Figures of 10 to 20 milligrams circulating online for it trace to one unsourced template copied between sites, which is why no dose tier is shown for it in the Library.

Pinealon is sold on pineal targeting and brain ageing. Two things should be said plainly. Its one human study, 32 people, open, no placebo, co-administered with another peptide, reported prooxidant activity and significant suppression of blood-cell production through reduced CD34+ cells.16 That is an adverse signal, and it is the entire human record. Separately, the pineal-targeting claim asks less than it implies: the pineal has no blood-brain barrier, so a compound in the bloodstream reaches it without crossing anything.

What people feel. Pinealon: 2 reports, one describing something very subtle, one nothing. Cortagen: 1 report, uninterpretable. These are the thinnest community datasets on the page.

A related correction, because it affects how Epitalon is sold. The famous longevity figures, 28% fewer deaths at twelve years and a melatonin rhythm restored to the young-adult pattern, belong to Epithalamin, a bovine pineal extract, not to the AEDG tetrapeptide people buy as Epitalon. There is no human cognitive or sleep outcome for the tetrapeptide. The extract studies were also unblinded, developer-run, with no placebo and 79 people in total for a mortality endpoint.17

11The plasticity peptides

This family is where the most exciting claims on the page live, and where checking them changes the picture most. These are molecules designed to copy the growth factors from step four of the synapse above. The ambition is real and the laboratory work behind several of them is genuinely good. What is missing is people.

Cerebrolysin

Porcine brain peptide fractionIM or IVApproved in ~50 countriesNot in the US

The most tested compound in this family by a wide margin, and the test results are not what its reputation suggests.

Cerebrolysin is not a molecule. It is a filtered extract of pig brain: 215.2 milligrams per millilitre of low-molecular-weight material that is 15% peptides and 85% free amino acids by nitrogen content. That composition matters for every claim made about it, because there is no single active substance to study.

Its own regulatory document contains an unusual admission: direct measurement of its pharmacokinetics was unsuccessful. There is no half-life. Whether its active fraction crosses the blood-brain barrier is argued indirectly from the fact that effects are observed, not demonstrated. A search for a region-specific mechanism study returns nothing.

What the big trials found. Its flagship stroke trial enrolled 1,070 people and missed its primary endpoint, finding no significant difference.18 The widely quoted mortality figure from it, 10.5% against 20.2%, is a post hoc subgroup of the more severe patients, which is a hypothesis, not a result. A rehabilitation trial did find a clear benefit on arm function at 90 days,19 and a 2025 aphasia trial found a 14.8-point advantage on a language battery in 123 people,20 so the picture is not uniformly negative.

Then the independent reviews. Cochrane, across three successive versions, concludes there is no demonstrated benefit on death or dependence in acute stroke, and reports a harm signal: non-fatal serious adverse events at a risk ratio of 2.15, rising to 2.86 in the subgroup receiving the same total dose the big trial used.21 In vascular dementia, Cochrane found a cognitive benefit of 0.36 but rated the evidence very low quality, and recorded why: five of six trials came from three countries, and all disclosed industry funding.22 An independent meta-analysis of 14 trials in 2,884 people found a small advantage on stroke severity scores but no significant difference on the outcome that matters, independent function.

There is also a pattern in what happened next: a large registry study terminated at 1,823 people, one trial terminated at 46 with its primary endpoint missed and significance only in the per-protocol analysis, another terminated at 4, and a head-to-head against donepezil withdrawn before enrolling anyone.

What people feel. 24 reports, the second-largest set here, and the most detailed protocol data of anything on this page. The dominant pattern is 20 ampoules of 5 or 10 millilitres intramuscularly, five days a week for about four weeks, repeated roughly every three months. Five or more separate people each report: effects on day one rather than a loading period, disrupted sleep, a delayed headache, benefits fading off cycle, and, specifically, that memory does not improve. 18 positive, 3 no change, 3 worse.

On the pig-brain question, honestly
Neither the official product document nor the regulator record mentions prion or TSE clearance at all. Pigs are not a known natural host for these diseases and no transmission has ever been reported from this product. So the honest answer is neither reassurance nor alarm: there is no documented risk, and there is also no published clearance validation. The more practical concern with a biological extract of undefined composition is batch-to-batch variability, which the 15% peptide / 85% amino acid specification does not resolve.

Dihexa

Angiotensin IV analogueOral or transdermalResearch chemicalBanned in sport

The compound with the biggest number attached to it anywhere in this field, and the number does not survive being traced.

Dihexa is sold on one claim: that it builds synapses something like ten million times more powerfully than BDNF itself. We went looking for the source of that figure.

It is a university press release, dated 11 October 2012. The figure comes from an assay counting dendritic spines in cultured rat neurons, and the release itself states that the number is not from the animal studies. There is also something structurally odd about the comparison: it measures a molecule that amplifies a growth-factor signal against the growth factor it amplifies, which is why the ratio comes out so enormous. It is not a claim that the compound is ten million times more effective at anything a person would notice.

The mechanism story, that it binds HGF and activates its receptor to grow synapses in the hippocampus, rests on a single foundational paper. That paper is listed on PubMed as retracted.23 It is also the source of the oral activity and barrier-crossing claims. With it withdrawn, Dihexa has no standing mechanism base.

And there is a clean test of the underlying idea, because the same laboratory lineage produced a clinical drug. Fosgonimeton, built on the same HGF logic, missed its primary endpoint in a Phase 2/3 Alzheimer's trial with 554 people enrolled, announced 3 September 2024. An earlier Phase 2 of 77 people failed in June 2022. A third trial was terminated at 28. It was given by injection, as a prodrug, which also undercuts the claim that this chemistry works taken orally.24

What people feel. One report, four days long, confounded by other substances. No half-life figure exists in any species, and no toxicology study could be found, which is the genuinely serious gap given that its claimed mechanism runs through a pathway involved in cancer biology.

P21, PE-22-28 and Adamax

No registered trialsNo human dataResearch chemicalsBanned in sport

Three compounds, three different kinds of nothing.

P21 is the respectable one. A six-amino-acid piece derived from a growth factor called CNTF, with genuinely good mouse work behind it: new neuron growth in a specific part of the hippocampus, reduced tau and amyloid in an Alzheimer's mouse model given orally for twelve months, and effects in aged rats. It is stated to cross the blood-brain barrier, and an independent group replicated findings in a different disorder. What it has never had is a single registered human trial, and no half-life figure exists.

PE-22-28 is the most interesting case on the page. It has exactly one primary paper in existence, and that paper contains the hardest single number in this family: it blocks the TREK-1 potassium channel at 0.12 nanomolar, against 40 to 60 for the parent molecule it was cut down from.44 Its regional map is the most precise here, hippocampus, prefrontal cortex and the dorsal raphe. Its dose-response is biphasic, with a low dose activating the channel and a higher dose blocking it, which means more is a different drug rather than a stronger one. In mice its antidepressant-like effect appears in four days against three to four weeks for fluoxetine. All of that is rodent. It has zero human data of any kind, its plasma half-life has never been measured, its barrier penetration has never been measured, and it has zero first-hand reports from anyone, anywhere.

Adamax is a different situation. Three separate searches found no primary paper, no patent and no trial. Worse, the identity is unstable: the sequence circulating for it is Semax's own sequence with an adamantane cage attached, labelled as something it is not, and the descriptions in circulation contradict each other. The real published work pairing an adamantane cage with a neurotrophic peptide produced P21, not this. There are also zero first-hand reports. We are not able to tell you what Adamax is, and nobody selling it has published anything that would.

The inversion worth noticing. PE-22-28 has the most precise brain map on this page and not one person's experience behind it. Semax has 26 real reports and a rat gene-expression pattern so complicated that nobody quotes it. Cerebrolysin has 24 reports and no regional mechanism at all. Mechanism detail and real-world evidence are not the same currency, and in this family they run in opposite directions.

cGP, and the one approved drug in this class

IGF-1 regulatorOralNo pure-compound trial

The honest surprise, because its best feature points the wrong way.

cGP is a small molecule that regulates how available IGF-1 is, and it has the best-measured brain entry of anything in this family: an oral dose demonstrably raises its own level in human spinal fluid. But read its own origin work carefully and the direction is not what a healthy person would want. Where IGF-1 is already sufficient, cGP lowers its activity, and it inhibited tumour growth in mice by that route. It is a normaliser, so its predicted effect in someone whose IGF-1 is fine is downward. Its analogue is in a recruiting Phase 3 trial of 160 participants reading out in October 2027, on the back of open-label uncontrolled Phase 2 work in 17 to 28 children.

For scale, the realistic ceiling for this whole class is worth knowing. Trofinetide is the one approved drug built on this idea, for Rett syndrome. Its approved benefit is a 3.2-point difference on a roughly 45-point scale and 0.3 on a 7-point scale, with diarrhoea in 82% of patients and 40.4% stopping treatment. It also failed twice in traumatic brain injury across about 520 patients, and failed in Fragile X.25 That is what success looks like in this family when it is properly measured: a narrow, real, modest effect in a serious condition, bought with significant side effects.

12Drive and wakefulness

These are not peptides and, on the evidence, they are not really memory compounds either. They change how awake and how willing you are, and a great deal of what people call cognitive enhancement is that and nothing more. This family also contains the single best-documented compound on the page and the single worst-flagged interaction risk, so it is worth reading closely.

Modafinil

Wakefulness drugOralPrescriptionBanned in competition

The most studied compound here, and the one whose reputation is furthest from its measured effect.

Modafinil is a licensed drug for excessive sleepiness, and at that it works. The interesting question is the other one, the reason most people on this page are interested: does it make a rested, healthy person think better?

Two independent meta-analyses say barely. One pooled 14 studies and 64 effect sizes in healthy non-sleep-deprived adults and found an overall effect of 0.12, with exactly one domain moving: memory updating, at 0.28. In the same paper methylphenidate managed 0.43 on recall, and amphetamine showed nothing at all.26 The second pooled 19 placebo-controlled trials and 67 effect sizes and found 0.10, with no domain standing out, no difference between 100 and 200 milligrams, and no difference between psychiatric and non-psychiatric groups.27

Put those next to the baseline at the top of this page. Taking the same test twice is worth 0.28. Modafinil's whole-battery effect in a rested person is 0.12, which sits below the conventional threshold for calling an effect "small". The first team's own conclusion was that there is a user perception these drugs are effective cognitive enhancers, and that it is not supported by the evidence.

The primary trials show why the pooled number is so low. One trial of 30 students with a full battery found no significant difference on any cognitive test, while measurably raising somatic anxiety, shaking, palpitations and irritability. A trial of 60 found scattered gains on a handful of measures and nothing on long-term memory, executive function or fluency. The best pro-modafinil argument is about task difficulty: a trial of 64 found gains on working memory and planning at the hardest levels only. That is a real position, and it is also exactly the sort of subgroup analysis that inflates effect sizes, and the larger meta-analysis found no such moderation.

Two findings matter more than the effect size. The first is that what reliably improves is how the work feels. The same trial that found gains only at the hardest levels also found that enjoyment of the task rose significantly while overall mood did not. The second is harder. During prolonged sleep deprivation, modafinil disrupted people's ability to judge their own performance and made them reliably overconfident, most strongly two to four hours after dosing. Placebo subjects judged themselves accurately. Amphetamine subjects judged themselves accurately. Modafinil was the only one of the three that broke self-assessment.28

Where it does more is where there is more room: in adult ADHD and in schizophrenia, single doses improved things they did not improve in healthy volunteers. In Huntington's disease it raised alertness, produced no cognitive benefit, and made visual recognition and working memory worse. The pattern is deficit-dependent, not uniformly pro-cognitive.

What people feel. By far the largest dataset on the page, 547 reviews on a patient platform splitting roughly 63% positive and 18% negative, plus a 59-person survey. The consistent theme is hours of wakefulness and willingness, not sharper thinking. It is also the only compound here where the reports come from people with a prescriber involved.

Safety, at label level
Serious rash. The label carries rare cases of serious or life-threatening rash including Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis and DRESS, with the instruction to discontinue at the first sign of rash. This is the one genuinely dangerous thing about an otherwise well-tolerated drug and it is frequently omitted from enthusiast write-ups. Regulatory narrowing: European regulators removed three of its four indications in July 2010, leaving only narcolepsy, on a benefit-risk assessment. Sport: prohibited in competition.

Methylene blue

Mitochondrial electron carrierOral, IV as a drugApproved for one useSerious interactions

Two completely different things sold under one name, and they need separating before anything else.

Half one, the medicine. Pharmaceutical-grade methylene blue is an approved intravenous drug at 1 milligram per kilogram for methemoglobinemia, a blood emergency. That is its approved use, and it is not related to anything on this page.

Half two, the low-dose cognitive use. Here it has something nothing else here has: randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled functional brain imaging in living humans. 26 healthy people aged 22 to 62 were scanned before and an hour after a single low oral dose. During sustained attention, the response rose in the insula on both sides. During a short-term memory task, it rose in prefrontal, parietal and occipital cortex. Behaviourally, there were 7% more correct responses on memory retrieval.29 A companion study found stronger resting-state connectivity between perception and memory regions. The mechanism is believable too: it carries electrons in the mitochondria, the same machinery as the Energy page.

The honest limits. That is one study of 26 people at a single timepoint, and it is the whole human cognitive base. Two psychotherapy-augmentation trials used much larger doses for a different purpose. And the compound's own derivative, developed as an Alzheimer's drug, failed all three of its Phase 3 trials across 891, 220 and 598 patients, with the sponsor's own 2026 pharmacokinetic paper conceding that the low-dose "inactive colourant" control arm was itself active.

Methylene blue: two interactions to take seriously

  • Serotonin drugs. There is a boxed warning. It requires 72 hours of separation from serotonergic medication, and in one published review 24 of 25 affected patients were on a serotonin drug. Regulators have logged cases at intravenous doses, and the oral risk is described as unclear rather than absent. If you take an SSRI, SNRI, or any serotonergic antidepressant, this one is not a grey area.
  • G6PD deficiency. This is a contraindication on the label, not a caution. Roughly 400 million people worldwide carry it, it is inherited on the X chromosome, and most carriers have no symptoms and do not know. It is common across Southeast Asia, which makes it directly relevant to readers here. Methylene blue can trigger red-cell breakdown in people who have it.

What people feel. 13 reports, genuinely split: 5 positive, 4 no change, 3 worse, 1 mixed. A recurring and useful detail: two people reported they could not turn their urine blue, which is the simplest signal that what they had was not what it claimed to be.

Bromantane

Atypical psychostimulantOralRegistered in RussiaBanned in competition

The compound with the best story in this field, and the story is told backwards nearly everywhere.

The usual version: Russian athletes were caught at the 1996 Olympics using bromantane as a masking agent, which proves it is a serious performance drug. What the arbitration record actually says is close to the opposite. The Court of Arbitration for Sport, in its award of 4 August 1996, set aside the disqualifications, because bromantan had not been proven to be a stimulant. The ban came in 1997, after the case was decided. The masking-agent framing does not appear in the record at all.30

A second correction, because it recurs in every write-up of this compound: the cosmonaut story belongs to a different drug. The Soviet space programme, the 1980 Moscow Olympics and Chernobyl history attaches to bemitil, a related but separate compound. Bromantane's own date and institute of origin do not appear in any primary source we could find.

What it does, in rats, is specific and includes something unusual. Chronic dosing raised serotonin and its breakdown product in frontal cortex with a delayed rise in deeper regions, and produced a stable decrease in the cerebellum, which makes it the only compound on this page with a documented fall in a named region. Mechanistically it is dopamine-positive by blocking reuptake, with a much weaker noradrenaline effect and no effect on nerve-to-muscle transmission. Russian trials used it for asthenia, meaning the fatigue and exhaustion state, not for cognition.

What people feel. This is the surprise of the community sweep. Bromantane has a strong reputation for tolerance and a crash, and outside Reddit there are only three first-hand reports, all of them null or close to it. The reputation appears to exist almost entirely in one place. That does not make it wrong, but it means the evidence for it is thinner than its confidence.

Phenylpiracetam and 9-Me-BC

OralPhenylpiracetam: banned in competition9-Me-BC: no human data

One works and wears off. The other has never been given to a person in any recorded study.

Phenylpiracetam is piracetam with a phenyl group added, and it behaves like a mild stimulant rather than a classical nootropic. Its cosmonaut origin story traces to the manufacturer's own website, which states 1983 and an institute name, with no citation on the space claim; encyclopedias repeat it from a Russian promotional article. We report it as a claim, not a fact. Its best-looking number, a 16.3-point improvement on a fatigue scale, comes from pooling uncontrolled before-and-after changes in Russian studies, which is not the same as a controlled effect. In rats it was the most protective of four agents tested on cortical mitochondria, alongside better learning.

The useful finding is that tolerance is documented in print, not just in reports: in the only 12-week controlled comparison, its anxiolytic effect declined at four to eight weeks.31 That matches the community data, where 6 separate people describe tolerance. Interestingly, the crash people warn about is not corroborated: one mild comedown, and one person explicitly denying a crash. Two people reported the loss of effect persisting after months off, which is worth knowing.

9-Me-BC has 14 PubMed records in total, several of them unrelated chemistry, and no trial, no case series, no pharmacokinetics and no safety data in humans. Targeted searches pairing it with substantia nigra, hippocampus, dopaminergic and nigrostriatal all return zero. It has nonetheless turned up in a European and Australian market sweep of unapproved substances.

And there is one fact about it that nobody selling it warns about: it inhibits MAO-A at around 1 micromolar, with roughly 15-fold selectivity over MAO-B. That is the same class of pharmacology that earns methylene blue its boxed warning, and it has never been checked in a person. If you take any serotonergic medication, that is a real and unstudied hazard, not a theoretical one.

The yardstick: caffeine with L-theanine

Oral, or teaNot bannedMost replicated acute effect

Here only as the thing everything above has to beat, which it mostly does not.

A systematic review of 21 studies concluded that the cognitive benefits of green tea appear under the combined influence of caffeine and L-theanine, and that either alone does less. Controlled trials find specific, modest gains: task-switching accuracy, sustained-attention accuracy, reaction time, subjective alertness and less subjective tiredness. Repeatedly null: visual search, choice reaction time, mental rotation. In one trial theanine alone made arithmetic worse and raised headache ratings.

Two honest caveats make this a better benchmark rather than a worse one. The studies do not converge on a dose, spanning 97 to 250 milligrams of theanine and 40 to 160 of caffeine, with one trial's dose described as the equivalent of eight cups of black tea. And caffeine's measured benefit is partly its own withdrawal being paid off: in a trial comparing habitual non-consumers against overnight-withdrawn consumers, the performance and mood effects appeared only in the consumers, and in some non-consumers performance got worse.32 So the yardstick's own effect is partly an artefact, which is exactly why clearing it should be the minimum bar.

13The supplements

The cheapest half of the page and the only part with a proper trial base, which makes it the most useful section to read slowly. One distinction decides almost every entry, and marketing hides it systematically: was the trial in healthy people, or in people who were already impaired or deficient? Grades below are Proven, Likely, Plausible, Unproven or Myth, and they refer to healthy adults unless stated.

CreatineProven over 66, nothing under 31

The cleanest demonstration of the population point on this entire page, and it comes from one meta-analysis that split by age. In people aged 11 to 31, creatine's effect on memory was 0.03, with a confidence interval crossing zero and no heterogeneity at all. In adults aged 66 to 76: 0.88.33 Same compound, same analysis, same outcome. Pooled across all ages it comes to 0.29, identical to exercise and to the practice effect, which is how it ends up described as a brain supplement at all.

Mechanistically it is consistent: brain creatine is directly measurable by scan in living people, supplementing raises it, but far less than it raises muscle creatine because the transporter is the bottleneck.

Magnesium L-threonatePlausible in impairment, unproven in health

The biggest effect size on this page, 0.91 in cognitively impaired adults aged 50 to 70, and the reason to be most careful. It is one trial of 44 people run by the company commercialising it, with no independent replication. The authors also could not assess their own sleep and anxiety outcomes, in their words, because of strong placebo effects.

The mechanism work is genuinely good and unusual. It is the threonate, not the magnesium, that matters: threonate occurs naturally in spinal fluid, an oral dose measurably raises it there, and other magnesium salts do not do what this one does. In cultured hippocampal neurons it raised internal magnesium, upgraded NMDA receptors and increased the number of functioning synapses, replicated in human-derived neurons.

One disclosure. The companion Alzheimer's-model paper, the source of the widely repeated claims that raising brain magnesium cleared plaque and reversed memory decline, is listed on PubMed as retracted.34 Those specific claims should not be used. The 2016 mechanism paper above is not retracted and stands on its own, though both came from the same group with a commercial affiliate, so the evidence is narrower than the volume of citation suggests.

Bacopa monnieriLikely, and tiny

One of only two supplements here with a genuinely replicated effect, which earns it the grade. Then look at the units. Its pooled effect across 437 people is on delayed word recall and reaction time, and the magnitude is 11 to 18 milliseconds.35 That is a real signal and a trivial quantity. A reviewer also noted that no two studies found significance on the same two tests, which is the pattern you get when many measures are taken and a few come out significant by chance. Gastrointestinal upset is the common complaint, and it also partly unblinds the trials.

Lion's ManeUnproven in healthy people

The most popular compound in this section rests on one trial, and the trial deserves describing in full because the category is built on it. 30 people, who had mild cognitive impairment rather than healthy brains, over 16 weeks, with the first author's listed affiliation being the mushroom laboratory of the company involved, and scores falling significantly four weeks after stopping.36 The two trials in healthy adults show either declining raw scores or acute-only effects.

The mechanism claim is that its compounds induce NGF. That is an in-a-dish result, and the honest difficulty is twofold: NGF itself does not cross into the brain, and whether the mushroom's active compounds do so in meaningful amounts in a person has not been established.

What people feel. Zero usable first-hand reports were found outside Reddit, which is remarkable for something this widely sold. On a consumer review platform the distribution is sharply split, with 44% rating it top marks and 25% rating it the lowest, which is the shape you see when a product does a lot for some people and nothing for most.

Alpha-GPCUnproven, and carries a reported stroke signal

There is no randomised trial of alpha-GPC for cognition in healthy people at all. What there is, instead, is a safety question that deserves careful handling in both directions.

A 2021 study of 12,008,977 Koreans found that matched alpha-GPC users had a 43% higher ten-year risk of stroke, and the association rose with dose.37 That is a large dataset and a dose-response relationship, which is what makes it worth taking seriously. It is also observational, not randomised, and users were older at baseline, which is exactly the confounding that inflates this kind of finding. And there is direct counter-evidence: a 2025 study of 508,107 people with mild cognitive impairment found reduced stroke risk among those who did not progress.45 Neither study was randomised and no trial exists that would settle it.

AshwagandhaPlausible for stress, unproven for cognition

It works on the stress axis and lowers cortisol, which is a real and reasonably supported effect, and is probably why people like it. For cognition the evidence is one 50-person pilot in people with mild impairment, with twelve uncorrected endpoints and no effect sizes published. Nothing in healthy people.

The safety item belongs on the page. A causality-assessed case series documented five cases of liver injury, three in Iceland and two in a United States network, all with jaundice, appearing between two and twelve weeks after starting. All resolved on stopping and none were fatal.38 Five cases against very wide use is a low rate, and it is also the kind of thing you want to know before you start. On a consumer platform its reviews run roughly 40% positive and 45% negative, and one of only two clinician-involved reports in the whole community sweep is a doctor advising someone to stop it.

·

Omega-3.Unproven for cognition Two large, clean nulls: a Cochrane review of 3,221 cognitively healthy older adults found a point estimate of −0.07 on cognitive scores,39 and a 3,501-person trial over five years returned a p-value of 0.63.40 Worth taking for other reasons; not a brain supplement on this evidence.

·

Phosphatidylserine.Plausible in impairment The regulator that permitted a qualified claim for it described the evidence in its own words as "very limited and preliminary", and the trials that produced the effect used a bovine-derived form that is no longer what is sold. The soy-derived version has weaker support.

·

L-theanine alone.Likely, with caffeine, acutely Covered above as the yardstick. Alone it is less impressive than paired, and on a consumer platform it is the one item here where negative reviews outnumber positive, 46% to 42%, with three independent reports of palpitations or blood-pressure effects. The other clinician-involved report in the sweep is a doctor finding very low blood pressure.

·

Citicoline.Plausible Its main healthy-person trial is manufacturer-staffed and published p-values without effect sizes, so it cannot honestly be compared against the baseline. That absence is itself informative.

·

Vitamin B12.Proven in deficiency, myth otherwise The only compound here that reverses a genuine cognitive deficit, in people who are actually deficient. In unselected healthy older adults, Cochrane found no adequate evidence of benefit. Even in those with raised homocysteine, three years of folic acid gave effects of 0.05 to 0.14, far below the practice effect.

·

NAD+ precursors.Unproven for cognition Nicotinamide riboside raised blood NAD+ by 2.6 times, which proves it is doing something, and left cognition unchanged. The target was engaged; the outcome was not. See the Energy page for the rest.

Which of these beat the baseline. Of thirteen, two: creatine in older adults and magnesium L-threonate in impaired adults. Both only in people who already had something to recover. One of the two is a single company-run trial. Everything else sits at or below the effect of taking the test a second time, and none of the thirteen has a replicated, independent, adequately powered trial showing benefit in healthy adults above the practice effect. That last sentence is our judgement against the evidence above, not a published finding.

14Pairings, and what the combination has actually been tested as

People rarely take one of these alone. The useful question about a stack is not whether the parts work, it is whether anyone has ever tested the combination itself, and here the answer is almost always no. Of ten stacks people genuinely run, two have a human trial of the combination, and one of those is in patients rather than healthy people.

Caffeine + L-theanineBest studied, and not what you think

The most popular stack in the field, the only one with a proper meta-analysis, and the results point sideways from the marketing.

The reason people take it is to keep caffeine's alertness and lose the jitter. A 2024 meta-analysis pulled in 50 randomised trials, 15 of them poolable, and found the combination did produce real gains in accuracy: digit vigilance up 1.59 percentage points, attention switching up 1.10, visual information processing up 1.20, with reaction time about 10 milliseconds faster at two hours.57

Now the awkward part. The two things people actually take it for, mood and alertness, did not reach significance: mood +3.46 with a confidence interval crossing zero, alertness +2.94, likewise. The measured benefit is a percentage point or so of accuracy, and the felt benefit people describe is the part the trials could not confirm.

What people report. Only four usable first-hand accounts outside Reddit, which is below our threshold for calling anything consistent. For the most popular stack in the field, the community evidence is thinner than the trial evidence, which is the reverse of everything else on this page.

Noopept or a racetam + a choline sourceNineteen years of advice, zero trials

The oldest piece of received wisdom in nootropics: take choline with it or you will get a headache.

It has been the default beginner advice for about nineteen years and no trial of the pairing exists. The first-hand record is now eight named reports and it does not support the advice: one person got partial headache relief, two had no trouble either way, three noticed nothing at all, and two were made worse by the choline source itself. Alpha-GPC also carries its own unresolved stroke question, covered above.

Cerebrolysin + a therapy or a prescription drugSix trials, all in patients

The one stack here with a real trial base: six randomised trials of Cerebrolysin combined with rehabilitation or an approved drug. Every one of them is in patients with a diagnosed condition, combined with a therapy, and not one is a peptide-plus-peptide stack in a healthy person. If you are healthy and stacking it, you are outside everything that has been studied.

Semax + SelankNo basis of any kind

Sold as a pre-mixed blend, and the most popular peptide stack on this page.

There is no study of the combination, and the foundation underneath each part is thinner than people assume: PubMed returns six human cognition records in total across both compounds, and the only one in healthy volunteers measured resting-state brain network volume in 24 people, not cognition. Three unattributable anecdotes exist for the pair, one of them negative. The BDNF rationale traces to rat basal forebrain tissue.

The four-way blend of Semax, Selank, PE-22-28 and Pinealon is worse again: zero reports, zero trials, and two of its four components have no human data at all.

Stimulant on stimulantThe common one worth not doing

Phenylpiracetam or modafinil added to caffeine, or to each other, is the most frequently described stack in the drive family and the one where the reports turn negative most often. Tolerance arrives faster, and the thing people are chasing, mental stamina, is the thing that degrades.

Combinations with a real mechanism for harm

  • Methylene blue with any serotonin drug. This is the one with a regulator behind it. The 2011 safety communication is explicit: methylene blue inhibits MAO-A, and the required separation is two weeks after stopping most serotonergic drugs and five weeks after fluoxetine. In one review of affected patients, 24 of 25 were on a serotonin medication. There is a counter-argument from a clinician that the risk is dose and route dependent, based on 51 cases; it has three weaknesses worth naming, in that the cases are uncited, the author has a commercial interest, and the arithmetic is his own.
  • And here is the part that matters. In the forum threads we read, four people combined methylene blue with caffeine and one correctly identified the MAO problem, back in 2011. Nobody mentioned SSRIs at all. The single most dangerous interaction on this page is the one the community is not discussing.
  • 9-Me-BC with any serotonin drug. Same class of risk, inferred from its MAO-A activity, with no human data either way. No trial, and no first-hand report anywhere outside Reddit. Nine of our ten search results for it were machine-generated pages.
  • Two MAO inhibitors together. Methylene blue and 9-Me-BC are both on this page and both have MAO-A activity. Nobody appears to have written about running them together, which is not the same as it being safe.
  • 5-HTP or tryptophan with an antidepressant. The same serotonin problem, from the supplement side, and more common because both are sold freely.

These are mechanisms, not predictions. The reason they are here is that the community discussion around the first two is close to absent, and a page that lists these compounds without listing this would be incomplete.

Timing, tolerance, and what happens when you stop

Does it stop working? For the drive family, yes, and it is documented in print as well as in reports. Phenylpiracetam's controlled comparison showed the effect declining at four to eight weeks, and six separate people describe the same. Noopept's rat work found the effect gone by the ninth injection. The Russian peptides are conventionally run in short courses for exactly this reason.

How long until anything happens? The split is sharp. The nasal peptides and the stimulants are described as minutes to an hour. Cerebrolysin users consistently report day one rather than a loading period. The supplements that do anything take weeks, and bacopa's trials ran twelve.

Stopping. Most of this is described as uneventful, and several Semax users specifically report no withdrawal. Two exceptions are worth stating plainly. Phenylpiracetam: two people report the loss of effect persisting months after stopping, which nothing explains. And Noopept at high doses: two dated reports describe a significant drop in mood on stopping abruptly, one of them serious. These are self-reported, at doses well above usual, with no clinical assessment and no published case report backing them, so treat them as a signal rather than a finding. If you are stopping something you have taken daily at a high dose, taper it, and talk to a doctor if your mood drops.

16Two retractions, and a warning about what you will find online

Two foundational papers behind compounds on this page have been withdrawn from the literature, and both are load-bearing. Neither retraction has reached the marketing.

1

Dihexa's mechanism paper is retracted. It is the single source of the HGF and brain-penetration story and, by extension, of everything the compound is sold on. With it withdrawn, there is no second paper holding the claim up.

2

Magnesium L-threonate's Alzheimer's paper is retracted. It is the source of the widely repeated claims that raising brain magnesium cleared amyloid plaque, prevented synapse loss and reversed memory decline. Those specific claims should not be used. The separate 2016 mechanism paper is not retracted and stands.

What searching for this yourself will turn up

  • A majority of search results for these compounds are now machine-generated pages that invent community reports. They are formatted to look like forum data, with plausible usernames, doses and timelines.
  • In this sweep, fabricated-looking "user experience" pages appeared for compounds that have no genuine first-hand data anywhere: PE-22-28, Adamax and Pinealon all have invented testimonials circulating, and between them they have two real reports in total.
  • Every report counted on this page was traced to a named human post on a real forum, a published survey, a patient review platform or a blinded self-experiment. None came from an aggregator page.
  • The practical test: if a page gives you glowing user accounts of PE-22-28 or Adamax, it is not reporting anything. Those accounts do not exist.

17Safety and uncertainty

Read before you trust any of this

  • Practice beats most of this. If you test yourself before and after, you will improve by about as much as the best compound here from the testing alone. Any personal before-and-after without a blind and a control is not evidence, including ours.
  • Methylene blue and serotonin drugs. Boxed warning, 72 hours of separation required, and 24 of 25 affected patients in one review were on a serotonin medication. Not a grey area.
  • Methylene blue and G6PD deficiency. A contraindication, not a caution. About 400 million carriers worldwide, common across Southeast Asia, and most do not know they have it.
  • 9-Me-BC inhibits MAO-A with roughly 15-fold selectivity, and nobody has ever checked what that does in a person. The same pharmacology carries a boxed warning elsewhere on this page.
  • Modafinil's rash warning is real. Rare cases of Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis and DRESS. The label says stop at the first sign of a rash. It is routinely left out of enthusiast write-ups.
  • Cerebrolysin has a harm signal, not just an absent benefit. Cochrane found non-fatal serious adverse events at more than twice the rate, worst at the same total dose its largest trial used.
  • Pinealon's only human study reported harm. Prooxidant activity and suppression of blood-cell production. That is the whole human record, and it is not a benefit record.
  • Ashwagandha has documented liver injury. Five causality-assessed cases, all with jaundice, two to twelve weeks in, all resolving on stopping.
  • Alpha-GPC has an unresolved stroke question. A 43% higher ten-year risk in 12 million people, dose-responsive, against a 2025 study pointing the other way. Neither is randomised and no trial will settle it soon.
  • Four compounds here have never been given to a person in a recorded study: Dihexa, P21, PE-22-28, Adamax, with 9-Me-BC alongside them. No dose, no safety profile, no half-life, and for Dihexa no toxicology study at all despite a mechanism that runs through a cancer-related pathway.
  • The amidate forms you probably own have zero published data. Not thin data. Zero records, searched three ways.
  • Sport.46 Modafinil, bromantane and phenylpiracetam are prohibited in competition. Dihexa, P21, PE-22-28 and Adamax are caught by the catch-all clause banning unapproved substances at all times, and Dihexa is additionally caught as an IGF-1-pathway agent.
  • Grey-market purity is the confound users raise most. A compound that does nothing and a vial that contains nothing look identical from the inside.

This page explains what has and has not been shown. It is not medical advice and not a dosing, cycling or combination protocol. Study doses appear only to describe what an experiment did. If you take any serotonergic medication, the methylene blue and 9-Me-BC entries above are the ones to read twice.

18Questions people actually ask

From the basic to the awkward, grouped so you can skip to what you want.

The basics

What is the best one to start with?

On the evidence rather than the reputation, the honest ranking puts sleep first, exercise second, and then, if you want a compound, the ordinary ones with trials: creatine if you are older, caffeine with L-theanine for an acute effect you will actually notice. Among the peptides, Semax has the most real user data and the clearest dose threshold, and it still has no placebo-controlled trial testing whether it improves cognition.

Do any of these actually make you smarter?

No, on current evidence, if smarter means a lasting rise in capability. Several reliably change how awake, motivated and clear you feel, and that is worth something real. The measured effects on test performance in healthy people are smaller than the effect of having sat the test before.

Nasal or injected?

For Semax and Selank, nobody has compared the two routes against each other, in a study or in the community. The two people in the sweep who injected said they did it for dose accuracy, not strength. What does change the effect, clearly, is the dose. One thing worth knowing: in mice, route changed which receptors Selank affected, so it is not a neutral choice mechanistically, just an untested one.

Why is everything here Russian?

Because the Soviet and Russian pharmaceutical system pursued peptide drugs for brain conditions in a way the West largely did not, and registered several of them on evidence standards that were different. That produced real molecules with real pharmacology and a documentation trail that does not translate: no placebo arms, no international registration, and most of the literature published only in Russian.

Are peptides safer than stimulants?

Not as a rule, and the framing hides the real difference. The Russian peptides are generally well tolerated in use and almost entirely unstudied for long-term effects, which is a different thing from being safe. Modafinil is extensively studied and carries one rare but serious risk you can act on. Known risks are easier to manage than unknown ones.

Is Semax approved for cognitive enhancement?

No. The Russian registration quoted most often is for 1% nasal drops in the acute phase of moderate to severe ischaemic stroke, as combination therapy. The cognitive indications belong to a different, lower-strength product. They get merged online into an approval that does not exist in that form.

Can I just take BDNF?

No, and this is the whole reason this market exists. BDNF is a large protein that does not cross from the blood into the brain, so it cannot be taken. Every compound in the plasticity family is an attempt to get around that, which is also why their barrier-crossing claims deserve the most scrutiny.

On the evidence

If there are no trials, how do people know anything?

They mostly do not. What exists is animal mechanism work, which is often genuinely good, plus first-hand reports, which are unblinded and uncontrolled, plus Russian trials without placebo arms. Those three together can support "this molecule does something in a brain". They cannot support "this will improve your memory", and the gap between those two sentences is this page.

Why does the practice effect matter so much?

Because almost everyone judges these compounds by testing themselves, and the test itself is the intervention. Sitting the same cognitive test a second time is worth 0.28, and a fifth time 0.97. Using a different version of the test does not fix it. Most published supplement effects in healthy people are at or below that line, so the thing people take as proof is the strongest competing explanation.

What does an effect size actually mean?

It is how big a difference is, in units of how much people naturally vary. Roughly: 0.2 is small, 0.5 moderate, 0.8 large. The useful part is comparison. Modafinil's 0.12 in a rested person, exercise's 0.29 and practice's 0.28 are on the same scale, which is why putting them on one chart is more informative than any one of them alone.

Why trust Cochrane over the trials showing benefit?

Not blind trust. Cochrane reviews pool everything including the unflattering results and grade the quality of each study, which is why their conclusions are usually less exciting than individual trials. For Cerebrolysin they also recorded something specific and checkable: five of six dementia trials came from three countries and all disclosed industry funding. That is a reason to weigh the pool over any single trial, not a reason to dismiss the positive ones.

Does a retracted paper mean the compound does not work?

No. It means the evidence that it works is no longer part of the record. For Dihexa the retracted paper was the only mechanism evidence, so the claim has nothing left standing behind it. For magnesium L-threonate the retraction took out the Alzheimer's claims while a separate mechanism paper survived. The difference matters.

Why does the brain map show nothing for some compounds?

Because no study places them anywhere in the brain. Six of the compounds light nothing: Dihexa, P21, Adamax, Cortagen, 9-Me-BC and Cerebrolysin for region. Showing that honestly seemed more useful than inventing a glow.

The uncomfortable ones

I felt a huge difference. Does the evidence say I am wrong?

It says your impression is not usable as proof, which is not the same thing. You may be responding genuinely; individual responses vary and group averages hide that. But unblinded self-observation picks up practice, expectancy, placebo and the fact that you changed something deliberately. And for modafinil specifically there is a measured case where the feeling of doing well rose while performance did not, so for that one the impression has been shown to be unreliable.

Is the N-acetyl amidate version worth the extra?

There is no published basis to answer that. Zero records exist for either amidate form, so the potency multipliers quoted for them come from nowhere traceable. The chemistry rationale is sound and the one careful self-logger judged it about twice as potent. That is the entire evidence base.

What about Adamax? I see it sold everywhere.

We could not establish what it is. No paper, no patent, no trial, and the sequence circulating for it is Semax's own sequence with a cage molecule attached, labelled as something else, with descriptions that contradict each other. It also has zero first-hand reports. That combination is unusual even for this field.

Is Dihexa really ten million times stronger than BDNF?

That figure comes from a university press release in 2012, from an assay counting spines in cultured rat neurons, and the release itself says it is not from the animal studies. It also compares a molecule that amplifies a growth-factor signal to the growth factor itself, which is why the ratio is so vast. The paper behind the mechanism has since been retracted, and the clinical drug built on the same chemistry failed in 554 people.

Why is PE-22-28 on the page at all if nobody has taken it?

Because it is the clearest lesson here. It has the most precise brain map of any peptide on the page, a mechanism with a hard number behind it, and not one person's experience anywhere. Precise mechanism detail feels like strong evidence and is not the same currency as having been tried.

So is this whole category a waste of money?

Not quite, and it depends what you are buying. If you want a lasting improvement in how well you think, the evidence does not support anything here over sleep and exercise. If you want to be more awake and more willing to work for a few hours, several of these do that, with modafinil the best documented and caffeine with L-theanine the cheapest. The expensive end of the page, the research peptides, is where the money and the evidence are furthest apart.

What would change your mind?

For the Russian family, one properly placebo-controlled trial in healthy people with a cognitive outcome, which has never been run for any of them. For the plasticity family, any registered human trial at all. For the supplements, independent replication of the two effects that beat the baseline, in people who are not already impaired. None of that is unreachable. It just has not been done.
