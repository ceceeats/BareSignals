# Semax
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/semax
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Семакс, (Pro8,Gly9,Pro10)ACTH(4-10), H-Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro-OH
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: MEHFPGP (7 amino acids)
4. Shown with an FDA approved badge
5. Legal and approval status: Registered and used in Russia; research chemical elsewhere. The July 2026 FDA compounding advisory panel recommended it; non-binding, no rule yet.

## Summary
6. Semax is a seven-amino-acid lab-made peptide based on a piece of ACTH, with a stabilizing Pro-Gly-Pro tail and without the hormone's stress effects. It is used in Russia after stroke, and people elsewhere take it nasally for focus, motivation and mental clarity, with BDNF and monoamine changes proposed as the mechanism. Evidence is overwhelmingly Russian and not confirmed to Western standards.
7. Punch line: Russian stroke medicine built on a piece of ACTH, minus the stress. A nasal spray that raises BDNF.
8. Used for: Focus, memory, drive

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
9. Cognition: Focus and attention
10. Neural plasticity: Raises BDNF, a nerve growth signal
11. Neural protection: Used after stroke in Russia
12. Drive & mental stamina: More motivation reported
13. Pain & sensory (backed only by animal or user reports): Pain relief studied
14. Mood (backed only by animal or user reports): Brighter mood reported

## How it works
15. A piece of ACTH without the hormone action · ACTH(4-7) fragment · role Cognition
   Semax is ACTH(4-7), a small piece of the stress hormone ACTH, with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail for stability. It keeps the brain activity of this fragment but does not trigger cortisol release. Its precise mechanism is described as unknown even in the Russian literature.
16. Raises brain growth factors · BDNF, TrkB, reported · role Neural plasticity
   Semax is reported to raise BDNF and its receptor TrkB in the hippocampus and cortex. BDNF supports nerve connections, learning and neuron survival. This comes mainly from Russian rodent work, with minimal Western replication.
17. Claimed melanocortin signalling · MC receptors, claimed · role Cognition
   Because its sequence matches part of alpha-MSH, Semax is claimed to act on melanocortin receptors in the brain. Direct binding has not been established, so this remains a claim rather than a known mechanism.
18. Used for stroke in Russia; protects neurons in rats · Ischaemia gene expression · role Neural protection
   In rat stroke models, Semax shifts gene activity in damaged brain tissue toward protection and repair. Russia registers a stronger form for acute ischaemic stroke, and Russian trials report better recovery, but these have not been confirmed to Western standards.
19. Raises dopamine and serotonin turnover, in rodents · Monoamine release · role Drive & mental stamina
   In rodents, Semax increases the turnover of dopamine and serotonin, messengers tied to motivation, attention and mood. This fits the drive and focus users report, but it has not been measured in people.
20. Mechanism overview: Semax is ACTH 4-7 (Met-Glu-His-Phe) extended with Pro-Gly-Pro for stability. It keeps the neuroactive properties of this ACTH fragment without stimulating cortisol. It is reported to raise BDNF and its receptor TrkB in the hippocampus and cortex, supporting plasticity and neuron survival. In rodents it increases dopamine and serotonin turnover, and in rat stroke models it shifts gene expression toward protection and repair. Melanocortin receptor action is claimed but unestablished. Most data come from Russian groups.

## What has been tested

## Who shouldn't take it
44. People with a history of seizures: Russian prescribing info lists convulsive disorders as a contraindication. (reported)
45. People with acute psychosis or severe anxiety: Raises dopamine and serotonin turnover; Russian label excludes acute psychotic states. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
46. Selank: Semax brings drive and focus through BDNF and dopamine, Selank brings calm through enkephalin and claimed GABA effects, so Selank softens Semax's edge. Sold pre-mixed as Semax + Selank, but the pair has not been tested together in trials.
47. Dihexa: Semax works on BDNF and monoamines, Dihexa on HGF/c-Met synapse building, two growth signals aimed at memory and focus. People stack them for cognition. The pairing is user logic, not trial data, and Dihexa has only rat data.

## Where it comes from
48. A seven-amino-acid peptide: a piece of ACTH (residues 4-7) plus a stabilizing Pro-Gly-Pro tail, developed in Russia as a neuroprotective nootropic.
49. First studied for: Stroke recovery and cognitive enhancement; used in Russian emergency medicine for stroke.
50. Name story: Reportedly a Russian acronym for 'SEMь AminoKiSlot', seven amino acids. It is the ACTH(4–7) start, MEHF, with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail added in place of the hormone's own residues 8–10. [S5] (reported)
51. 1980s to 1990s: ACTH(4-7) plus Pro-Gly-Pro, built in Russia; used in stroke care
52. Jul 2026: FDA compounding panel recommended it; non-binding, no rule yet
53. Today: Registered in Russia; among the most-used nootropic peptides

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from [lab]
54. Semax was built in Moscow from the ACTH(4-7) fragment, Met-Glu-His-Phe, which matches part of alpha-MSH, with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail for stability [5] (verified). It was first described in the literature in 1991 and placed on Russia's list of vital and essential drugs on 7 December 2011 [3] (verified). It has a real national drug history, which sets it apart from most compounds in this library.
55. What that history does not include is a defined mechanism. Even the licensing country's literature describes the mechanism as unknown [3] (reported). The Russian label itself sells the compound in two very different strengths for two different jobs: 0.1% for cognitive and neurological indications, and 1% for acute ischaemic stroke only [2] (verified).

### What is actually in a vial [lab]
56. The registered product is nasal drops with methylparaben as preservative, in 3 mL bottles [2] (verified). What US nominators proposed instead was injection or nasal spray at 7.5 mg or 1 mg per millilitre [1] (verified).
57. The FDA's July 2026 review found the naming unreliable: certificates titled "semax acetate" carried the identifiers of the free base. The acetate certificates listed identity, purity and water, and allowed single unidentified impurities of up to 1% or 2%, but none reported aggregates, bioburden or endotoxin [1] (verified). Both forms were judged not well characterised.
58. Solubility is a concrete problem. The free base dissolves in water to about 2 mg/mL and in phosphate buffer to about 10 mg/mL [1] (verified). A 7.5 mg/mL product would therefore need buffer or the salt form, since plain water would not hold it (inferred). Lyophilised powder is quoted as stable four years at minus 20 degrees, but reconstituted solution only 2 to 7 days in the fridge [1] (verified), far shorter than most handling assumes.
59. No nasal spray device information, meaning container, closure or pump, was supplied, which the agency flagged as a strong concern for a nasal product [1] (verified).

### Mechanism, one step at a time, with the model each step came from [mixed]
60. Melanocortin receptors, in cells and animals. Semax competitively blocked alpha-MSH at MC4 and MC5 receptors and did not block MC3 [3] (reported). So the receptor action, where it exists, looks like antagonism or partial agonism rather than switching receptors on.
61. Enzymes, in human serum. Semax inhibited enkephalin-degrading enzymes with an IC50 of about 10 micromolar [4] (verified), a concentration well above anything nasal dosing is shown to reach (inferred).
62. Stroke, in rats. A single nasal dose of 250 micrograms per kilogram after photothrombotic stroke gave infarcts about 20% smaller at day 8, and intraperitoneal dosing reduced neuronal damage after carotid occlusion [1] (verified). Transcriptome work after ischaemia shows inflammatory genes turned down and neurotransmission genes turned up, and the Pro-Gly-Pro tail alone does not reproduce it, so the ACTH part matters [5] (verified).
63. Blood clotting, in rats. This is the least-known part of the profile. Nasal 1 mg/kg a day for five days raised fibrinolytic activity, another group found a hypocoagulation profile, and platelet aggregation fell about 35%. The breakdown product Pro-Gly-Pro was itself anticoagulant [1] (verified). No dose-response was examined.
64. Dopamine, in mice. Semax potentiated amphetamine-induced dopamine release in the striatum, which the FDA called concerning for abuse potential; no abuse or reinforcement study exists [1] (verified).

### What the body does with it [mixed]
65. The label states nasal uptake of up to 60 to 70%, arrival in the brain within about 4 minutes, rapid breakdown in blood and urinary excretion, with active fragments, and an effect lasting 20 to 24 hours after one dose [2] (verified). No half-life is given.
66. The rat numbers put the brain claim in proportion. After intravenous dosing, peak brain level was about 0.005% of the dose; after nasal dosing, about 0.072% at 2 minutes, with brain-to-blood ratios of 0.24 and 0.67 [1] (verified). Nasal delivery does favour the brain, but the amount arriving is a tiny fraction of what is given. The main metabolite is Pro-Gly-Pro [1].
67. No human pharmacokinetic study exists for any route, and no subcutaneous data exist in any species [1] (verified). So injection, the route many people now use, has no exposure data behind it.

### The human record [people]
68. The stroke use is the oldest claim. A 2018 Russian study of patients at different stages of ischaemic stroke is cited for faster and greater recovery of neurological function [5,6] (reported). The full paper was not opened for this read.
69. The FDA found only two usable references for the three uses it reviewed. One 2002 meeting abstract in chronic ischaemic brain disease gave clotting labs without full results and no functional outcome [1]. The other, from 1996, was open-label and uncontrolled. In 12 people with migraine, a single nasal dose cut a pain-sensitivity score from 78% to 32%, and 4 of 12 reported the headache stopping within two hours. In 16 people with typical trigeminal neuralgia, nothing changed. In 9 with dental nerve pain, 6 resolved, but attack frequency and duration did not fall, and the authors concluded Semax has no analgesic activity by itself [1] (verified).
70. Across the safety literature, roughly 33 to 47 healthy adults, 69 adults with medical conditions and 451 children with depression or tics have been exposed, all nasally. Three of the sources were meeting abstracts, one reported no adverse events, and the rest did not discuss them [1] (verified). There is no established human dose outside the Russian label.

### Safety: what has been looked at, and what has not [mixed]
71. Absent: acute toxicity, repeat-dose toxicity, reproductive toxicity and two-year carcinogenicity studies [1] (verified). The one cancer-related study gave 3 mg/kg intraperitoneally to female mice that already had mammary tumours; tumours ended smaller (7.4 against 15.0 cubic centimetres) and lifespan longer, but with one dose, one sex and under three months it cannot assess cancer risk [1] (verified).
72. The label contraindicates Semax in pregnancy, breastfeeding, acute psychosis, anxiety disorders and a history of seizures [2] (verified). That anxiety line matters, because Semax is often sold paired with an anti-anxiety peptide for people who feel overstimulated.
73. The FDA's adverse event system held one report: a consumer who bought 0.1% nasal drops online reported eye pain and burning, hospitalisation, and pain still unresolved a year later [1] (verified). The antithrombotic animal data raise a bleeding question for anyone on blood thinners or facing surgery; it has not been studied in people (inferred).

### Who produced the evidence [lab]
74. Almost all of it comes from the Moscow institutes that developed the drug and their clinical partners [1,5] (verified). That is typical for a national medicine, and the work is not trivial: there are transcriptome and proteome studies in rat stroke models. What is missing is any independent replication outside Russia, and any trial built to current standards with a placebo arm and pre-registered endpoints (verified as absent in the FDA review).

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
75. Russia: a registered medicine on the essential drugs list [2,3] (verified). US: listed in the do-not-compound category as of 2024 [11], then reviewed by the FDA, whose staff concluded the criteria weighed against both forms [1]. On 24 July 2026 the advisory committee nonetheless voted 8 to 5 with one abstention to recommend it, for cerebral ischaemia, migraine and trigeminal neuralgia [7,8] (verified). The vote is advisory, and a bulks-list listing still needs rulemaking.
76. An interim access policy for compounding certain peptides went to White House regulatory review on 14 October 2026; it had not been issued and the peptides it covers are not public [9] (verified).
77. Sport: not named on the 2026 Prohibited List. The catch-all class covers substances with no current government approval anywhere [10]; Semax is approved in Russia, so that class arguably does not fit, but the list does not settle it (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
78. Does it help stroke recovery in a placebo-controlled trial? This is the most answerable question and the one with the oldest claim.
79. What do the antithrombotic effects mean in people? Unstudied, and relevant to anyone bleeding-prone.
80. Does the dopamine potentiation carry abuse risk? No study exists.
81. What happens with injection, and with long-term use? No exposure or toxicity data exist for either.
