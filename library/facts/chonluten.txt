# Chonluten
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/chonluten
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, EDG, Glu-Asp-Gly
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: EDG (3 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Chonluten is a lab-made tripeptide (Glu-Asp-Gly) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the lungs and bronchial tissue. It is used mainly by the longevity community for respiratory support, and it is claimed to tune airway genes and damp inflammation. Evidence is animal work and Russian reports with no Western validation, and user reports are very few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson's three-amino-acid lung bioregulator, claimed to tune airway genes and calm inflammation. Russian data.
7. Used for: Lung and airway support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Organ function: Lung and airway support
9. Inflammatory signaling: Calms airway inflammation
10. Tissue repair: Lining repair claims

## How it works
11. Claimed to tune airway tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Organ function
   Chonluten is claimed to act on DNA in airway cells and adjust which genes they switch on, a route that bypasses surface receptors. The aim is to keep bronchial and lung tissue working like younger tissue. This comes only from the Khavinson group, in animal work.
12. Claimed to damp airway inflammation · Inflammatory gene expression · role Inflammatory signaling
   The group claims it turns down inflammatory genes in the bronchial lining. Less inflammatory signalling would mean calmer, less reactive airways. This has not been confirmed outside Russian studies, and user reports are very few.
13. Mechanism overview: Chonluten is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide aimed at the airways. The group claims it adjusts gene expression in airway tissue and damps inflammatory genes in the bronchial lining. These mechanisms come from one research group and are not independently confirmed.

## What has been tested
29. Organ function: Respiratory and lung tissue support (animal)
30. Russian studies only.
31. What it helps: Evidence is animal work and Russian reports of respiratory and lung tissue support, all from the Khavinson tradition. There is no Western validation. First-hand reports from users are very few, so real-world effects are hard to judge. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
32. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
33. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
34. People treating a diagnosed lung disease with it: No controlled trials show benefit; delays proven care. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
35. Epitalon: Epitalon targets aging as a whole, with claims on telomerase and melatonin rhythm, while Chonluten targets the airways. Longevity users stack them so a general bioregulator runs beside a lung one. Both rest on Khavinson-group claims and the pair has not been tested together.
36. Thymalin: Thymalin rebalances T and B cells and lowered inflammation markers in COVID-19 reports, while Chonluten is aimed at airway inflammation. People pair them for lung and immune recovery after respiratory illness. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
37. A tripeptide (Glu-Asp-Gly) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the lungs/bronchial tissue.
38. First studied for: Respiratory and lung-tissue support.
39. Name story: Despite the cartilage-sounding 'chon', Chonluten is filed as a bronchial and lung bioregulator. EDG is its one-letter code, Glu-Asp-Gly. No source found explains the name itself. [S5] (inferred)
40. Late 20th c.: Tripeptide Glu-Asp-Gly from the Khavinson program, aimed at the lungs
41. Today: Sold as a bioregulator; not an approved drug anywhere
