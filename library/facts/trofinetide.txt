# Trofinetide
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/trofinetide
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Daybue, Daybue Stix, Daybu (EU), NNZ-2566, G-2MePE, glycyl-L-2-methylprolyl-L-glutamic acid
2. Origin: Lab-modified fragment of a natural protein: IGF-1's first three amino acids
3. Shown with an FDA approved badge
4. Legal and approval status: FDA approved March 2023 for Rett syndrome from age 2; Daybue Stix powder approved December 2025. EU authorised as Daybu on 20 August 2026 from age 5. Also approved in Canada (reported).

## Summary
5. Trofinetide is a tiny three-amino-acid fragment of IGF-1 (GPE) with one methyl added so it survives in the body. It became the first drug approved for Rett syndrome in 2023, thought to support synapse maturation and calm brain inflammation. A 12-week Phase 3 showed modest but real gains in caregiver and clinician ratings. Diarrhea affected 82% versus 20% on placebo, and how it works is officially unknown.
6. Punch line: Daybue, a stabilised IGF-1 fragment and the first approved Rett drug. Taken twice daily; diarrhea is the hurdle.
7. Used for: Rett syndrome

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Neural plasticity: Thought to help synapses form and mature
9. Neural protection: Calms brain cell stress in Rett models
10. Cognition: Small gains in communication and alertness
11. Inflammatory signaling: Reduces brain inflammation (proposed)

## How it works
12. Mimics GPE, a natural IGF-1 fragment in the brain · IGF-1 N-terminal tripeptide analogue · role Neural plasticity
   Trofinetide is a modified copy of GPE, the three amino acid piece cut from the start of IGF-1 in the brain. The change makes it last longer than natural GPE. It is approved for Rett syndrome on human trial data, though how GPE acts is still being worked out.
13. Proposed to support synapse maturation · Synaptic function · role Neural plasticity
   It is proposed to help synapses, the connections between neurons, mature and work properly, which is impaired in Rett syndrome. That would fit the modest gains in caregiver and clinician ratings. The synapse mechanism comes from preclinical work, not human measurements.
14. Proposed to dampen microglial inflammation · Neuroinflammation · role Inflammatory signaling
   It is also proposed to calm microglia, the brain's immune cells, and the inflammation they drive. Lower neuroinflammation may protect neurons in Rett syndrome. This is a proposed mechanism from preclinical studies, not shown in patients.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
15. Typical: 5 to 12 g by body weight (12 g at 50 kg and up) · Twice daily · cycle Ongoing · route Oral, or by G-tube (label)
16. Half-life: About 1.5 hours
17. Most people take: Weight-based, twice daily by mouth or G-tube: 5 g (9 to 12 kg), 6 g (12 to 20 kg), 8 g (20 to 35 kg), 10 g (35 to 50 kg), 12 g (50 kg and up).

## What people report
18. Caregivers describe better eye contact, hand use and alertness in some children, against hard-to-manage diarrhea and weight loss; dose reductions and stopping laxatives are common advice.
19. Liked / worked: First approved Rett syndrome drug
20. Liked / worked: Caregiver and clinician scores improved
21. What worked: LAVENDER: caregiver-rated Rett symptoms (RSBQ) improved by 4.9 points versus 1.7 on placebo, and clinicians rated overall change slightly better (CGI-I 3.5 versus 3.8). Communication also improved, and gains tended to keep growing in extensions.
22. Went wrong / did not work: Diarrhea in 82%
23. Went wrong / did not work: Modest effect size
24. What went wrong: The effect is modest. Diarrhea in 82% versus 20% on placebo and vomiting in 29% versus 12%, so many families struggle to stay on it. How it works is officially unknown.
25. Most reported: Better eye contact and hand use for some
26. Most reported: Diarrhea and weight loss hard to manage

## What has been tested
27. Neural plasticity: Rett syndrome, ages 5 to 20, LAVENDER Phase 3 (trial)
28. Neural plasticity: Rett syndrome, ages 2 to 4, DAFFODIL (trial)
29. Phase 3 LAVENDER in 187 girls and women aged 5 to 20 over 12 weeks, with LILAC open-label extensions over several years, and DAFFODIL in 15 children aged 2 to 4.
30. What it helps: Approved for Rett syndrome on a 12-week Phase 3 with modest but real gains in caregiver and clinician ratings, which tended to grow in multi-year extensions. Diarrhea and vomiting are the main limit.
31. Trial: none · Approved; pivotal Phase 3 complete · NCT04181723. Its pivotal Phase 3 LAVENDER trial led to approval; no new major trial was found in the sources checked. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1]

## Synergies (paired with)
32. IGF-1 LR3: IGF-1 LR3 is a long-acting full IGF-1 analogue aimed at muscle growth, while trofinetide copies only the small brain fragment. They share a parent molecule but have different targets. The pairing is user logic and has not been tested.
33. Cerebrolysin: Cerebrolysin is a pig brain peptide mix with nerve growth activity, and trofinetide targets synapse function and brain inflammation. Both are used for brain support from different angles. The pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
34. Developed by Neuren Pharmaceuticals in New Zealand from GPE, a tiny piece cut from IGF-1 in the brain, then licensed to Acadia Pharmaceuticals.
35. First studied for: Rett syndrome, a genetic disorder in girls that takes away speech and hand use. It is the first approved treatment for it.
36. 2000s: Neuren develops NNZ-2566 from the IGF-1 fragment GPE
37. 2015: EU orphan designation for Rett syndrome
38. Mar 2023: FDA approves Daybue, first Rett syndrome drug
39. Dec 2025: FDA approves Daybue Stix powder
40. Aug 2026: EU authorises it as Daybu
41. Today: Standard Rett treatment in the US; GI side effects limit use
