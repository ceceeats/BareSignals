# Vilon
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/vilon
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, KE, Lys-Glu
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: KE (2 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Vilon is a lab-made dipeptide (Lys-Glu) Khavinson bioregulator, one of the shortest, studied for immune and gene-regulating effects. It is used for immune support and aging, and it is claimed to loosen chromatin and boost thymus and spleen cell growth. Russian animal studies report immune restoration and longer lifespan, but none of it is independently replicated.
6. Punch line: Among the shortest Khavinson bioregulators, two amino acids. Taken for thymus and aging; claimed to open chromatin.
7. Used for: Immune and longevity support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Immunity: Thymus support
9. DNA & telomeres: Gene-regulating claims
10. Inflammatory signaling: Calms inflammation

## How it works
11. Claimed to tune immune and gene activity · Khavinson group · role Immunity
   The Khavinson group proposes that the dipeptide Lys-Glu enters cells and acts directly on DNA to adjust gene activity, rather than through a surface receptor. The claimed effect is better immune and tissue function with age. Russian cell and animal studies report this, without independent replication.
12. Claimed to loosen chromatin, reawakening genes · Chromatin decondensation · role DNA & telomeres
   Chromatin is DNA wrapped around proteins, and tightly packed regions keep genes silent, a pattern that grows with age. The group claims Vilon loosens that packing so silenced genes switch back on. This comes from Russian cell work and has not been confirmed elsewhere.
13. Claimed to boost thymus and spleen cell growth · Lys-Glu, lymphoid cells · role Immunity
   The group reports that Lys-Glu increases growth of lymphoid cells in the thymus and spleen, the organs that raise and house T and B cells. More of these cells would mean stronger immune responses in aging. This is shown only in Russian animal studies, which also report longer lifespan.
14. Mechanism overview: Vilon is the Khavinson dipeptide Lys-Glu. The group claims it decondenses chromatin, reawakening silenced genes, and boosts growth of thymus and spleen lymphoid cells. These mechanisms come from one research group and have not been independently replicated. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## What has been tested
32. Immunity: Immune restoration (animal)
33. Aging & senescence: Lifespan extension (animal)
34. DNA & telomeres: Gene expression effects (cell)
35. Russian studies claim gene-expression and immune effects and lifespan extension in animals; not independently replicated.
36. What it helps: Russian animal studies report immune restoration and longer lifespan, with cell work on gene expression. None of this is independently replicated or validated in the West. First-hand reports are few, with some interest on longevity forums. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
37. Trial: none. Its parent drug Thymalin is an injectable used in Russia, but Vilon itself has no registered trial. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S6] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
38. Transplant recipients on anti-rejection drugs: Sold as an immune stimulant, which works against anti-rejection drugs. (inferred)
39. People with active or recent cancer: Claimed gene-regulating effects; untested in tumours. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
40. Epitalon: Epitalon is claimed to switch on telomerase and restore melatonin rhythm, while Vilon is claimed to loosen chromatin and support the thymus. People run these two Khavinson peptides together in longevity courses. Both rest on one group's data, and the pair has not been tested together.
41. Thymalin: Thymalin, a thymus extract, has Russian human data on raising and rebalancing T and B cells, and Vilon is claimed to grow thymus and spleen cells. People pair them to work on immune aging from two angles. The combination has not been studied.

## Where it comes from
42. A dipeptide (Lys-Glu) Khavinson bioregulator, one of the shortest, studied for immune and gene-regulating effects.
43. First studied for: Immune restoration and aging, in the Russian bioregulator program.
44. Name story: Lys-Glu, among the shortest drugs in the bioregulator line. Unlike Thymogen it was not isolated from Thymalin: Khavinson's group designed it from the extract's amino acid makeup, then named it 'Vilon'; the name itself is unexplained. [S7] (verified)
45. 1990s to 2000s: Lys-Glu dipeptide from the Russian bioregulator program, for immunity
46. Today: A minimal Khavinson bioregulator with longevity-forum interest
