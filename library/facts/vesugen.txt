# Vesugen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/vesugen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, T-38, KED, Lys-Glu-Asp
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: KED (3 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Vesugen is a lab-made tripeptide (Lys-Glu-Asp) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at blood vessels. It is used mainly by the longevity community for vascular aging, and it is claimed to bind promoter DNA and keep aging vessel cells dividing. Russian animal work is the only evidence, with no independent trial, and its effects on circulation in people are undocumented.
6. Punch line: Khavinson's blood vessel tripeptide. Longevity users take it for vascular aging; claimed to reset vessel genes.
7. Used for: Blood vessel support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Heart & vessels: Blood vessel wall support
9. DNA & telomeres: Gene-regulating claims

## How it works
10. Claimed to tune vessel wall genes · Khavinson group · role Heart & vessels
   Vesugen is claimed to tune gene activity in the vessel wall, aiming to keep blood vessels behaving like younger tissue. The Khavinson group says it does this from inside the cell rather than through a surface receptor. It rests on Russian animal work and lacks replication.
11. Claimed to bind gene promoter DNA · Promoter binding · role DNA & telomeres
   The group claims it binds gene promoters, the DNA stretches that act as on switches for each gene. Binding there would change how much of a protein the cell makes. This comes from one group and is not confirmed elsewhere.
12. Claimed to keep aging vessel cells dividing · Endothelial renewal · role Heart & vessels
   The group claims it keeps aging endothelial cells, the inner lining of vessels, dividing. A renewed lining keeps vessels flexible and less prone to damage. This is reported in Russian studies only, with no data on circulation in people.
13. Mechanism overview: Vesugen is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide for blood vessels. The group claims it binds gene promoter DNA, tunes vessel wall genes and keeps aging endothelial cells dividing. These claims rest on one research group and lack outside replication. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## What has been tested
28. Heart & vessels: Vessel wall health, vascular aging (animal)
29. Russian studies only.
30. What it helps: Russian animal work reports support for vessel wall health and vascular aging. There is no Western validation and no independent trial. First-hand user reports are very few, so practical effects on circulation in people are undocumented. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
31. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
32. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
33. People treating a diagnosed vascular disease with it: No controlled trials show benefit; delays proven care. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
34. Epitalon: Epitalon brings the general anti-aging claims, while Vesugen is aimed at vessel walls and vascular aging. Longevity users pair them so a whole-body bioregulator runs beside a vascular one. Both rest on one group's claims and the pair has not been tested together.
35. Thymalin: Thymalin supports immune balance and Vesugen the vessel lining, two systems that age side by side. Users rotate them through the same bioregulator courses. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
36. A tripeptide (Lys-Glu-Asp) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at blood vessels.
37. First studied for: Vascular-wall health and cardiovascular aging.
38. Name story: The vascular entry in Khavinson's organ-named line; its lab code was T-38. KED is Vilon's KE plus an aspartate, and adding a glycine gives Testagen. [S5] (inferred)
39. Late 20th c.: Tripeptide Lys-Glu-Asp from the Khavinson program, for blood vessels
40. Today: Sold as a bioregulator; not an approved drug anywhere
