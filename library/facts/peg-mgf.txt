# PEG-MGF
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/peg-mgf
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: mgf mechano growth factor, PEGylated MGF, IGF-1Ec, IGF-IEc E-peptide
2. Origin: Lab-modified natural chain
3. Sequence: YQPPSTNKNTKSQRRKGSTFEEHK (24 amino acids). MGF E-domain peptide with a PEG chain attached, not shown
4. Legal and approval status: Unapproved research chemical. WADA-banned. On the FDA compounding advisory agenda for a meeting due before end of February 2027.

## Summary
5. PEG-MGF is a pegylated form of Mechano Growth Factor, an IGF-1 splice variant that muscle releases after mechanical load. It is studied as the local signal that wakes muscle stem cells after damage, and the PEG tag extends its short natural life. Evidence is cell and animal only, and a small user base reports it for recovery, though the pegylated form has never been studied in people.
6. Punch line: Muscle's own post-load repair signal, PEG-tagged to last longer. Wakes muscle stem cells; dosed around workouts.
7. Used for: Muscle recovery after training

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Muscle & performance: Wakes muscle stem cells
9. Tissue repair: Muscle repair after damage

## How it works
10. Wakes muscle stem cells after damage · Satellite cells · role Muscle & performance
   MGF is a version of IGF-1 that muscle makes after mechanical damage, and it switches on satellite cells, the resting stem cells beside muscle fibres. Once active they divide and fuse into damaged fibres. This has been shown in cell and animal studies, not in people.
11. Claimed to act through its own E-peptide tail · MGF E-domain · role Tissue repair
   MGF carries its own C-terminal tail, the E-peptide, which is claimed to signal separately from the main IGF-1 receptor. That would let it start repair through a route distinct from IGF-1. The idea comes from cell work and is not settled.
12. PEG tag stretches a short natural life · Pegylation · role Tissue repair
   Native MGF is cleared within minutes. Attaching a polyethylene glycol chain makes the molecule bigger and slower to clear, a standard drug-design method. The pegylated form has never been studied in people.
13. Shields stressed heart and nerve cells in animals · Anti-apoptotic signalling · role Tissue repair
   In animal studies MGF switches on anti-apoptotic signalling, which stops stressed cells from self-destructing. This protected heart and nerve cells in injury models. No human work has tested it.
14. Mechanism overview: Mechano growth factor is a splice variant of IGF-1 produced in muscle after mechanical damage. Its distinct C-terminal E-peptide is claimed to act separately from IGF-1R, activating satellite cells, the muscle stem cells that fuse into damaged fibres. Native MGF lasts minutes, so the pegylated version adds a polyethylene glycol chain to slow clearance. Animal work also shows anti-apoptotic signalling that protects stressed heart and nerve cells.

## Where it comes from
38. A pegylated version of Mechano Growth Factor, a splice variant of IGF-1 released by muscle after mechanical load; the PEG tag extends its short natural life.
39. First studied for: Studied as the local signal that activates muscle stem (satellite) cells after damage.
40. Name story: Geoff Goldspink's group named it Mechano Growth Factor because muscle makes this IGF-1 splice variant in response to mechanical stretch and load. PEG is the polyethylene glycol chain bolted on to make it last longer. [S5] (verified)
41. 1990s: MGF found as an IGF-1 splice variant released by loaded muscle
42. 2000s: Studied as the signal that wakes muscle stem cells
43. Today: Pegylated grey-market form, unstudied in people; WADA-banned; on FDA compounding agenda
