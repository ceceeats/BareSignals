# Vitamin B12
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/vitamin-b12
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: cobalamin, cyanocobalamin, methylcobalamin, hydroxocobalamin, B12 shot, mecobalamin (INN for methylcobalamin), cobamamide (adenosylcobalamin), extrinsic factor
2. Origin: Not a peptide: cobalt-containing vitamin
3. Shown with an FDA approved badge
4. Legal and approval status: Cyanocobalamin injection is FDA-approved (Rx, 1,000 mcg/mL); hydroxocobalamin is approved too. Injectable methylcobalamin has no FDA-approved product and is compounded; final 503A bulk status not confirmed. Not on the WADA list.

## Summary
5. Vitamin B12 is a cobalt-centred vitamin, not a peptide, needed to make red blood cells, myelin and DNA. Injections are approved for pernicious anaemia and other deficiency, and are also popular as an energy shot in wellness and weight-loss clinics. Benefit is mostly seen in people who were low, and acne flares are the most common complaint.
6. Punch line: Cobalt vitamin for blood, nerves and DNA. The clinic energy shot; real lift mostly if you're low.
7. Used for: Deficiency, energy, weight-loss clinic add-on

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Cellular energy: Fixes deficiency fatigue
9. Neural protection: Protects nerves and myelin
10. Cognition: Clears deficiency brain fog
11. Mood: Low B12 linked to low mood
12. Skin & aesthetics (backed only by animal or user reports): Acne flares reported
13. Sleep & rhythms (backed only by animal or user reports): Methyl form tied to sleep rhythm

## How it works
14. Helps make red blood cells and DNA · Methionine synthase, folate cycle · role Cellular energy
   As methylcobalamin, B12 is the cofactor for methionine synthase, which keeps folate moving through the DNA-making cycle. Without it, dividing cells in bone marrow cannot copy DNA properly and red cells come out large and few. Correcting this fixes deficiency anaemia in people.
15. Keeps nerve insulation healthy · Methylmalonyl-CoA mutase, myelin · role Neural protection
   As adenosylcobalamin, B12 runs methylmalonyl-CoA mutase in mitochondria, a step needed for healthy fat handling in myelin, the insulation around nerves. Deficiency damages nerves and spinal cord. Treating it early stops neuropathy from progressing in people, while late damage can stay.
16. Lowers homocysteine · Homocysteine to methionine · role Cognition
   Methionine synthase also turns homocysteine back into methionine. Low B12 lets homocysteine build up in blood, which is a marker of deficiency and linked to vascular and brain risk. Giving B12 lowers homocysteine, mainly in people who were low.
17. Feeds skin bacteria porphyrins · C. acnes porphyrin output · role Skin & aesthetics
   Skin bacteria called C. acnes take up B12 and shift their metabolism toward making porphyrins, molecules that trigger inflammation in follicles. That explains acne-like flares after injections. The evidence is case reports and lab work on the bacteria.
18. Mechanism overview: Cobalamin is a corrinoid ring around a cobalt atom. In cells it works as two cofactors: methylcobalamin for methionine synthase, which recycles homocysteine to methionine and keeps folate moving through DNA synthesis, and adenosylcobalamin for methylmalonyl-CoA mutase in mitochondria, which matters for myelin. Cyanocobalamin and hydroxocobalamin are converted to both forms in the body. Absorption from the gut needs intrinsic factor, which injections bypass.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
19. Typical: 1,000 mcg (1 mg) community; 100 mcg (label) · weekly (community); label loading then monthly · cycle Ongoing · route IM or deep Sub-Q (not IV) (verified)
20. Half-life: Most of large doses excreted in 48 h; stores last months
21. Most people take: Label for pernicious anaemia: 100 mcg IM/deep SC daily for 6–7 days, then alternate days, then every 3–4 days, then 100 mcg monthly for life. Common practice is 1,000 mcg weekly to monthly. High-dose oral (1–2 mg/day) and nasal gel are alternatives.

## What people report
22. Weekly shots for energy and in weight-loss clinics, often compounded with GLP-1s. People report an energy lift (mostly those who were low), red-tinged urine with hydroxocobalamin, and acne on the face and back.
23. Liked / worked: Reverses deficiency anaemia
24. Liked / worked: Halts early deficiency nerve damage
25. Liked / worked: Bypasses absorption problems
26. What worked: Reverses deficiency anaemia and can halt nerve damage when caught early. Injections bypass the intrinsic-factor problem of pernicious anaemia, metformin users and gut surgery.
27. Went wrong / did not work: No energy boost if levels are normal
28. Went wrong / did not work: 50–98% of a big dose lost in urine
29. Went wrong / did not work: Acne flares
30. Went wrong / did not work: Rare anaphylaxis, low potassium early in severe anaemia
31. What went wrong: No proven energy or weight benefit when B12 levels are already normal. Most of a large injected dose leaves in the urine within 48 h. Acne flares, rare anaphylaxis, and low potassium when treating severe anaemia.
32. Most reported: Energy lift, mostly if low
33. Most reported: Acne on face and back
34. Most reported: Methyl form preferred for 'MTHFR' (no proof)
35. Most reported: Common in GLP-1 clinic blends

## What has been tested
36. Cellular energy: Pernicious anaemia and deficiency (human)
37. Neural protection: Deficiency neuropathy (human)
38. Skin & aesthetics: Acneiform eruption case reports (human)
39. Decades of clinical use for deficiency; large vitamin trials found no harm at 0.4–1 mg/day.
40. What it helps: Injections treat pernicious anaemia, malabsorption after gut surgery and deficiency from metformin, acid blockers, veganism or age, fixing anaemia and stopping early nerve damage. NIH notes no proven difference between oral forms; methylcobalamin superiority is a marketing claim. People take weekly shots for energy and focus, often in weight-loss clinics; benefit is mostly in those who were low. Acne flares are the most common complaint.
41. Trial: active · Phase 4 · B12 deficiency. Approved and cheap, so academic trials of oral versus injected or sublingual B12 run continuously, though one example checked had already completed. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
42. People with Leber's hereditary optic neuropathy: Cyanocobalamin can speed optic nerve damage. (verified)
43. People with cobalt allergy: Cobalt sits at the centre of the B12 molecule; the label contraindicates use. (verified)
44. People with very low potassium: Treating deficiency can drop potassium fast. (verified)
45. People prone to acne or rosacea: High-dose B12 linked to acne flares. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
46. Semaglutide: Semaglutide drives weight loss through the GLP-1 receptor, while B12 is added in weight-loss clinics for energy and to cover lower intake while eating less. Benefit is mostly in people who are low. The pairing is clinic practice, not trial data.
47. Tirzepatide: Tirzepatide brings large weight loss through GIP and GLP-1 receptors, while B12 is a common clinic add-on for energy and to guard against deficiency during reduced eating. Gain is mainly for people who were low. The pair has not been tested together.
48. NAD+: NAD+ is used as a cell energy coenzyme, while B12 supports red cells and nerves. Both are sold as energy shots, often given together. The pairing is user and clinic logic, and the combination has not been tested.
49. Glutathione: Glutathione is an antioxidant used for skin brightening, while B12 covers red cells, nerves and energy in deficient people. They are often given together in drip and injection clinics. The combination is clinic practice, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
50. A cobalt-centred vitamin needed to make red blood cells, myelin and DNA. Cyanocobalamin is the stable pharmacy form; methylcobalamin is an active form sold mainly through compounders.
51. First studied for: Pernicious anaemia and other B12 malabsorption.
52. Name story: Cobalamin is named for the cobalt atom at its core. The cyanide in cyanocobalamin is a purification accident: it latched on during charcoal clean-up in the 1948 isolation, and that form stayed the commercial 'vitamin B12'. [S2] (verified)
53. 1926: Raw liver diet rescues pernicious anaemia (inferred date)
54. 1948: Vitamin B12 isolated (inferred date)
55. 2021: Compounding panel votes to keep methylcobalamin available
56. 2023: Case report: B12 injections trigger acne
57. Today: Ubiquitous clinic shot, often blended with GLP-1s
