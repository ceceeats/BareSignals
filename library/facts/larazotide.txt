# Larazotide
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/larazotide
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: at-1001 larazotide acetate, INN-202
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: GGVLVQPG (8 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not approved; Phase 3 stopped for futility in 2022.

## Summary
5. Larazotide is a lab-designed eight-amino-acid peptide that tightens the junctions between gut-lining cells, blocking the zonulin pathway behind so-called leaky gut. It was developed for celiac disease to reduce symptoms from gluten exposure despite a gluten-free diet. Earlier trials showed symptom relief, but the Phase 3 trial was stopped for futility in 2022, and grey-market users report variable results.
6. Punch line: Oral gut-barrier peptide that blocks zonulin to reseal leaky gut lining. Earlier trials eased celiac symptoms.
7. Used for: Celiac and leaky gut symptoms

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Inflammatory signaling: Calms gut reaction to gluten
9. Digestive & gut: Tightens the gut lining

## How it works
10. Tightens seals between gut-lining cells · Tight junctions · role Digestive & gut
   Larazotide acts on tight junctions, the protein seals between gut-lining cells. It helps the junction proteins and the actin skeleton beneath them reassemble, closing gaps in the barrier. It acts locally in the gut rather than through the bloodstream.
11. Blocks the 'leaky gut' signal · Zonulin pathway · role Digestive & gut
   In celiac disease, gliadin from gluten triggers release of zonulin, which loosens tight junctions. Larazotide blocks this zonulin-driven opening. Earlier human trials reduced symptoms in celiac patients still affected despite a gluten-free diet, but the Phase 3 trial was stopped for futility in 2022.
12. Less gluten leaking in, less inflammation · Reduced antigen uptake · role Inflammatory signaling
   With the barrier tighter, fewer gluten fragments cross into the gut wall and reach immune cells. Less immune activation means less inflammation in the lining. This chain is well described in celiac biology, though the clinical benefit was never confirmed at scale.
13. Mechanism overview: Larazotide is an eight amino acid peptide that acts on the tight junctions sealing the gaps between gut-lining cells. In celiac disease, gliadin triggers release of zonulin, which loosens these junctions so gluten fragments slip through and activate immune cells. Larazotide blocks this zonulin-driven opening and helps tight junction proteins and the actin cytoskeleton reassemble. Fewer gluten fragments cross the barrier, so less immune activation and inflammation follow. It acts locally in the gut rather than systemically.

## Where it comes from
32. Larazotide acetate (AT-1001), a synthetic octapeptide that tightens the junctions between gut-lining cells, reversing the 'leaky gut' zonulin pathway.
33. First studied for: Celiac disease, to reduce symptoms from gluten exposure despite a gluten-free diet.
34. Name story: Built from a sliver of cholera biology: Zot (zonula occludens toxin) from Vibrio cholerae loosens gut tight junctions, and an eight-residue stretch it shares with human zonulin became this blocker. The "-zot-" in the name appears to nod to it. [S2] (verified)
35. 2000s: Synthetic 8-amino-acid peptide (AT-1001) built to tighten the gut lining
36. 2010s: Celiac trials show symptom reduction
37. 2022: Phase 3 stopped after interim analysis showed likely futility
38. Today: Not approved; a novel tight-junction idea, grey-market for leaky gut
