# GLOW
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/glow
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: GLOW blend, GLOW stack, GLOW 70, GHK-Cu + BPC-157 + TB-500
2. Origin: Blend of 3 peptides
3. Legal and approval status: Not approved anywhere. US: BPC-157, TB-500 and injectable GHK-Cu left FDA Category 2 in April 2026 when nominations were withdrawn; non-injectable GHK-Cu sits in Category 1; the July 2026 panel voted for BPC-157 and TB-500 compounding, no rule yet. WADA: BPC-157 and TB-500 banned at all times.

## Summary
4. GLOW is a three-peptide vial combining the copper peptide GHK-Cu with BPC-157 and TB-500 (usually full thymosin beta-4) in a 5:1:1 ratio. People combine the collagen-building skin peptide with the two Wolverine stack repair peptides for skin quality and healing. No study of the blend exists, and the copper share causes stinging and redness at the injection site.
5. Punch line: GHK-Cu plus the Wolverine repair pair, 5:1:1. Copper for skin texture and scars, the pair for healing.
6. Used for: Skin quality, collagen, wound and injury repair

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Skin & aesthetics: Copper peptide aimed at collagen and skin quality
8. Tissue repair: BPC-157 and TB-500 cover wound and tendon repair
9. Inflammatory signaling: All three lower inflammation in models
10. Hair: Hair and nail gains reported, GHK-Cu side
11. Pain & sensory (backed only by animal or user reports): Joint comfort reported
12. Digestive & gut (backed only by animal or user reports): BPC-157 side, gut lining in rats

## How it works
13. Signals skin to build collagen and remodel · GHK-Cu: copper delivery, gene expression · role Skin & aesthetics
   GHK-Cu delivers copper to tissue and shifts gene expression toward collagen, elastin and glycosaminoglycan synthesis and remodelling. It is the only skin and collagen signal in the vial. Its support is cell, animal and small topical human skin studies, not injection.
14. Helps blood vessels grow into damage · BPC-157: VEGFR2, eNOS · role Tissue repair
   BPC-157 activates VEGFR2 and eNOS to grow new blood vessels into damaged tissue. Better blood supply brings oxygen and repair cells to slow-healing sites like tendon. This is shown in rat injury models.
15. Moves repair cells into wounds · Tβ4: G-actin sequestering · role Tissue repair
   Thymosin beta-4 binds G-actin so repair cells can build their scaffold and crawl into wounds. Full Tβ4 has human wound trials. In the 2026 rat study, TB-500 alone helped tendon strength but adding BPC-157 gave no extra benefit.
16. Damps inflammation · GHK-Cu and Tβ4: NF-κB down · role Inflammatory signaling
   GHK-Cu and thymosin beta-4 both lower NF-κB, the main switch for inflammatory genes. Less inflammation lets repair proceed with less tissue damage. This is shown in cell and animal studies, and no study has checked how the three interact.
17. Mechanism overview: GHK-Cu delivers copper to tissue and shifts gene expression toward collagen, elastin and glycosaminoglycan synthesis and remodelling; it binds Cu(II) very tightly at neutral pH and more weakly as pH drops. BPC-157 adds angiogenesis (VEGFR2, Akt, eNOS) and fibroblast migration; thymosin beta-4 adds actin-driven cell migration and NF-κB suppression. The intended split is GHK-Cu for skin matrix, the other two for deeper tissue repair. No study has checked for interaction, and the 2026 rat study found the BPC-157 and TB-500 pair added nothing over either alone.

## What is inside the blend
18. GHK-Cu: 50 mg · Collagen, skin remodelling, hair
19. BPC-157: 10 mg · Blood vessel growth, gut lining
20. TB-500 (Thymosin Beta-4): 10 mg · Cell migration into wounds
21. Most common ratio: GHK-Cu 50 · BPC-157 10 · TB-500 10 = 70 mg (18 of 25 listings (7 state the split, 11 sell 70 mg))
22. Other ratio seen: GHK-Cu 35 · TB-500 10 · BPC-157 5 = 50 mg (3 listings)
23. Other ratio seen: GHK-Cu 30 · BPC-157 10 · TB-500 10 = 50 mg (1 listing (slug), 2 more 50 mg with no split)
24. Other ratio seen: 30 mg and 60 mg totals, split not stated = 30 / 60 mg (2 listings)
25. Version Common retail: GHK-Cu 50 · BPC-157 10 · TB-500 10 = 70 mg
26. Why GHK-Cu is in it: The only skin and collagen signal in the vial; hair and nail effects
27. Why BPC-157 is in it: Angiogenesis and gut protection
28. Why TB-500 is in it: Cell migration; human wound trial data (full Tβ4)
29. Mixing chemistry: GHK-Cu holds its copper tightly near neutral pH; acidic BPC-157 acetate liquid can weaken that grip.
30. Mixing chemistry: Copper plus a reducing agent (vitamin C, glutathione) oxidises peptides: never add them to the vial.
31. Mixing chemistry: Blue is normal. A shift to green, teal or brown suggests the copper has come off (reported, thresholds unverified).
32. Mixing chemistry: Release COAs show drift: BPC-157 at 117% of label in one, totals 94% in another.
33. Mixing chemistry: Stability after mixing is only claimed by sellers and affiliates, from 21 days to 3 months; nothing independent.
34. Self-mixing: Self-mixing puts an acidic BPC-157 acetate solution into the copper vial and pushes pH toward where GHK-Cu starts losing copper; separate vials or same-syringe draws avoid that.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
35. Typical: 10–15 units (70 mg in 3 mL) · 1× daily or 5×/week · cycle 8–12 weeks · route Sub-Q (reported)
36. Half-life: per component
37. Most people take: No standard dose. Most size it to land BPC-157 near 330 to 500 mcg: 10 to 15 units of a 70 mg vial mixed with 3 mL, which also delivers 1.7 to 2.5 mg GHK-Cu. One aggregator lists only 200 to 500 mcg of total blend, about a tenth of that.

## What people report
38. Sub-Q daily or 5 days a week. Common draw is 10 to 15 units of a 70 mg vial in 3 mL (about 1.7 to 2.5 mg GHK-Cu and 330 to 500 mcg each of BPC-157 and TB-500). Courses 8 to 12 weeks, then a break or 2 to 3 shots a week. If it irritates, people halve to 5 units for 1 to 2 weeks.
39. Liked / worked: Smoother skin and softer lines reported by weeks 4 to 6
40. Liked / worked: Faster wound and scab healing reported
41. Liked / worked: COAs show components near label
42. What worked: Users report better skin texture, hydration and smaller fine lines from weeks 4 to 6, faster scab and wound healing, joint comfort and stronger nails and hair. Guides describe early dewiness in week 1 and clearer change by weeks 5 to 8.
43. Went wrong / did not work: Never tested as a blend
44. Went wrong / did not work: Burning welts and itching from the copper share
45. Went wrong / did not work: Timelines come from affiliate guides without counts
46. What went wrong: No controlled data at all; the timelines come from affiliate guides without counts. The copper share stings: users describe burning welts lasting a day and intense itching, not fixed by changing needle length or angle. Redness at the site for 30 to 60 minutes is common. Finnrick: 1 of 2 classifiable GLOW tests passed (Jul to Sep 2026).
47. Most reported: Skin texture, hydration, scars
48. Most reported: Hair and nail gains
49. Most reported: Sting and redness at the site
50. Most reported: Breaks after 8 to 12 weeks

## What has been tested
51. Skin & aesthetics: GHK-Cu topical skin studies (human)
52. Tissue repair: GHK-Cu wound healing (animal)
53. Tissue repair: BPC-157 tendon and gut healing (rat)
54. Tissue repair: Tβ4 wound trials (trial)
55. Skin & aesthetics: Texture, fine lines, scars (users)
56. The blend has never been tested in animals or people. Component evidence: GHK-Cu in cell, animal and small topical skin studies; BPC-157 in rats and one uncontrolled knee series; thymosin beta-4 in rats and human wound trials. Lab work exists only on the powder: release COAs show components near label, with drift (BPC-157 +17% in one).
57. What it helps: Evidence is component-level and user-reported. GHK-Cu has cell, animal and small topical human skin data; BPC-157 and thymosin beta-4 have animal repair data and, for full Tβ4, human wound trials. No study of the blend exists. Users report better skin texture, faster healing and joint comfort over 4 to 8 weeks.

## Who shouldn't take it
58. Wilson's disease or copper overload: Each dose delivers copper; people who cannot clear copper accumulate it in liver and brain. (inferred)
59. People with active or recent cancer: Raises VEGFR2 signalling and new vessel growth in animal work; tumours use the same pathway. (inferred)
60. Drug-tested athletes: TB-500 is named on the WADA list as a thymosin beta-4 derivative. (verified)
61. People with cholestatic liver disease: Copper leaves the body through bile; PBC, PSC and bile duct blockage raise body copper. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
62. KPV: KPV adds a direct NF-κB block in gut and immune cells, stronger than the anti-inflammatory side of GLOW. Adding it turns GLOW into the KLOW stack. That combination has not been tested in animals or people.
63. GHK-Cu: GLOW already contains GHK-Cu, so adding more raises the skin and collagen share and the copper load. People do this for skin-first goals. There is no data on extra benefit.
64. BPC-157: GLOW already contains BPC-157, so adding more raises the vessel growth and gut-protection share. In the only controlled tendon study, BPC-157 alone did not reach significance on strength. There is no data on extra benefit.
65. Thymosin Beta-4: GLOW already contains thymosin beta-4 as TB-500, so adding more raises the cell-migration share. It was the stronger half in the one rat tendon study. There is no data on extra benefit from the added dose.

## Where it comes from
66. A three-peptide vial: GHK-Cu 50 mg, BPC-157 10 mg and TB-500 10 mg, 70 mg total, a 5:1:1 ratio. GHK-Cu, the copper peptide, makes up about 71% of the powder. On the common retail version TB-500 is full thymosin beta-4.
67. First studied for: Put together by the research-peptide market around 2024 to 2025 as a skin and repair blend: the collagen copper peptide plus the two Wolverine stack peptides. Popularised by newsletters and wellness clinics.
68. Name story: No source explains the name. It most likely refers to the skin glow it is marketed for, and it became the template for KLOW (GLOW plus KPV). [S1] (inferred)
69. 1973: GHK-Cu found in human plasma by Loren Pickart
70. 2010s to 2020s: BPC-157 and TB-500 paired as the Wolverine stack
71. 2024 to 2025: GLOW sold as a 50+10+10 mg vial for skin and repair
72. Apr 2026: BPC-157, TB-500 and injectable GHK-Cu leave FDA Category 2
73. Jun to Sep 2026: Lab COAs and Finnrick tests of GLOW published

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### What combining these three adds in evidence terms [mixed]
74. Nothing has been measured. The blend has never been tested in an animal or a person, and no study of any three-component ratio exists (verified as absent).
75. What has been tested is two of the three, together, in one experiment. A 2026 rat Achilles transection study ran four groups of eight: vehicle, the gastric peptide alone, the thymosin component alone, and both, dosed intraperitoneally for 30 days after a Kessler repair [1]. The authors hypothesised an additive benefit and found none, stating that combining the two conferred no additional benefit over either agent alone [1] (verified).
76. Two details from that study bear on this blend specifically. On load to failure, the gastric peptide alone did not reach significance against vehicle, while the thymosin component alone did [1]. And the combination's mechanical result sat numerically below both single agents, which with four tendons per group is within noise but is the opposite of the pattern the pairing is sold on [1].
77. So the deeper two-thirds of this product rests on one exploratory experiment that looked for addition and did not find it. The authors offered overlapping downstream pathways, a ceiling effect, or non-optimal dosing as explanations, and tested none [1] (verified).
78. The copper component was not in that study. Its interaction with either of the others has never been examined in any system.

### The three components do not have comparable evidence [mixed]
79. A blend inherits the reputation of its best-regarded ingredient. Read separately, the three sit at very different places.
80. The copper tripeptide has the most human data and the most discouraging large result: a pivotal programme in diabetic foot ulcers enrolling 511 patients across 30 centres at two strengths against placebo found the drug no better than placebo [2,3] (verified). Its positive human evidence is topical, small and short. The injected route, which is what a blend uses, has never been studied in humans.
81. The gastric peptide has one controlled human trial, a 2005 meeting abstract in ulcerative colitis whose between-group confidence interval crossed zero, and which regulators judged inadequate for lacking endpoint definitions, inclusion criteria and statistics [4] (verified).
82. The thymosin component is the thinnest and the most often assumed strongest. Regulators found no clinical studies, no human exposure data, no human pharmacokinetic data by any route, and no toxicology studies of any kind for the marketed fragment [5] (verified). When that fragment was tested directly in a fibroblast wound-closure assay at 50 micrograms per millilitre, it did not significantly promote closure [5].
83. On the common retail version the thymosin component is the full 43-residue protein rather than the fragment. Regulators stated in 2026 that these are not the same substance [5] (verified), so the component list cannot be used to look up which evidence applies.

### The chemistry three components have to satisfy at once [lab]
84. This is where documented facts about the separate components say something concrete about the mixture, and it is the most practically useful part of this entry.
85. The components prefer different acidity. The copper tripeptide binds its copper ion very tightly at neutral pH and more weakly as pH falls, while the two repair peptides have different preferred ranges from each other, one toward neutral-to-slightly-alkaline and one toward mildly acidic (as the card records). A single solution sits at one pH. It therefore cannot sit at all three optima, and whichever compromise is struck, at least one component is held outside its preferred range (inferred).
86. The specific concern that follows is copper. If the solution is held toward the acidic end to suit the peptide that prefers it, the copper complex's binding is weaker there, which is the condition under which copper is less fully held (inferred). The reason bound copper is tolerable in the first place is that complexation silences its reactivity; a documented 1981 study showed that redox activity is effectively silenced when the metal is held by this tripeptide (verified, in vitro, as indexed in the copper peptide reference literature). Weaker binding is therefore the one chemical variable in this product with a clear mechanism for harm, and no study has measured free copper in any multi-peptide blend (verified as absent).
87. Storage requirements also conflict. The gastric peptide's lyophilised powder is recommended below minus 18 degrees Celsius, with reconstituted solution documented as stable only two to three weeks refrigerated [4]. The thymosin fragment is stored below minus 20 as free base and below minus 15 as acetate [5]. A single vial can only be stored one way, and after reconstitution the shortest-lived component sets the real shelf life of the whole product (inferred).
88. Release certificates on the powder show components near label with drift, including the gastric peptide running about 17% over in one batch, and independent testing is thin: one of two classifiable results passed between July and September 2026 (reported, as logged in the card record and not re-verified in this pass).

### What the copper share means that the other components do not [lab]
89. At the common composition the copper tripeptide is about five parts to one of each other component, which makes this, in mass terms, mostly a copper peptide product with two additions. Nothing in the literature establishes that this ratio is right for any purpose, because no ratio has been studied (verified as absent).
90. An injected copper complex raises questions a plain peptide does not: how much copper is delivered per dose, where it goes once the peptide is degraded, and what repeated dosing does to copper handling over weeks. Nothing in the available record addresses this for the single agent, let alone in a mixture (verified as absent).
91. There is also a regulatory asymmetry that bears on this. For the gastric peptide and the thymosin fragment, regulators published detailed 2026 assessments covering identity, certificates, impurities, endotoxin, aggregates, toxicology and immunogenicity [4,5]. For injectable copper tripeptide, no such document exists, because its advisory review was deferred to a later meeting [6] (verified). So the component making up most of the mass is the one with no published agency assessment of what is in it.
92. The stinging and welts users describe at injection sites are attributed to the copper share and are consistent with a reactive metal complex meeting tissue (reported). They are also the clearest signal in the whole product that something is happening locally that nobody has characterised.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
93. No approved product contains this combination anywhere, and the three components sit in three different regulatory positions.
94. The gastric peptide and the thymosin fragment left the US do-not-compound category in April 2026 because nominations were withdrawn, not because of a revised safety finding, leaving them outside any recognised category rather than cleared [6] (verified). On 23 July 2026 the advisory committee recommended both for the 503A bulks list, each by 8 to 6 with one abstention, against agency staff who had proposed adding neither [4,5,7].
95. The copper tripeptide is placed differently again. Its non-injectable form was restored to Category 1 on 5 May 2026 after a nominator clarified it had meant to withdraw only the injectable nomination, and it sits there under temporary enforcement discretion. The injectable route was left unclassified and has not been reviewed by the committee at all; that review is expected before the end of February 2027 [6] (verified).
96. So an injectable blend of all three draws on two substances recommended but not yet permitted, plus one whose injectable form has never been assessed. Addition to the list requires formal rulemaking, which has not begun [8]. An interim enforcement-discretion policy went to the White House budget office on 14 October 2026 and was unpublished as of mid-October, with its covered substances and conditions not public [8] (verified). Nothing in any of this addresses premixed combinations.
97. In sport, two of the three are prohibited at all times.

### Open questions [mixed]
98. Does any three-way combination add anything? The only pairwise test among these components looked for addition and found none, and the copper component has never been combined with either in a study.
99. How tightly is the copper held in a multi-peptide solution at whatever pH the product sits at, and is any of it free? This is the single most consequential unmeasured variable, and it is answerable by analytical chemistry rather than by a trial.
100. What happens to delivered copper over a multi-week course? Unstudied for the single agent and unexamined in combination, in the component that makes up most of the mass.
101. What is the real shelf life after reconstitution? The components' documented stability windows differ and the shortest governs, but nobody has measured the mixture.
102. Which thymosin molecule is present? Regulators say the fragment and the full protein are different substances, and no routine identity test distinguishes them in grey-market supply.
103. If a user responds, to what? Three simultaneous components at a vendor-fixed ratio make attribution impossible by design.
104. Why does the copper component sting, and is the local reaction telling us something about the chemistry? Nobody has looked.
