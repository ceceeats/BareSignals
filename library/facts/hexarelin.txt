# Hexarelin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/hexarelin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: examorelin, EP-23905, MF-6003
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: HWAWFK (6 amino acids). 2-methyl-D-Trp and D-Phe shown as W and F; amide end
4. Legal and approval status: Unapproved research chemical. WADA-banned.

## Summary
5. Hexarelin is a synthetic hexapeptide that mimics ghrelin and is the most potent of the classic growth hormone releasers. People use it for the GH effect, and it also acts directly on heart tissue, which draws separate interest. Its GH response fades with repeated use, it raises cortisol and prolactin, and it has no outcome trials.
6. Punch line: The most potent classic GH releaser. Also acts directly on heart tissue; the response fades with steady use.
7. Used for: Strong GH release, cardiac interest

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Endocrine & growth: The strongest GH release
9. Heart & vessels: Direct heart protection
10. Muscle & performance: Lean mass reported

## How it works
11. Mimics ghrelin to trigger a strong GH pulse · Ghrelin receptor, GHSR-1a · role Endocrine & growth
   Hexarelin binds the ghrelin receptor, GHSR-1a, in the pituitary and hypothalamus. It produces one of the strongest GH pulses of any GHRP. Human studies confirm the size of the release.
12. Acts directly on heart tissue · CD36 receptor · role Heart & vessels
   Separately from GH, hexarelin binds CD36, a receptor found on heart muscle and blood vessel cells. This gives it a direct route into heart tissue that other GHRPs mostly lack. The effect works even without GH, shown in animal and early human work.
13. Protects heart muscle in injury models · CD36 cardioprotection · role Heart & vessels
   Through CD36, hexarelin protected heart muscle in injury models, such as reduced blood flow followed by reflow. Small human studies have looked at heart function, but there are no outcome trials. The cardiac interest rests mainly on animal work.
14. GH response fades with repeated use · Receptor desensitization · role Endocrine & growth
   With repeated use, the ghrelin receptor becomes less responsive, a process called receptor desensitisation. The GH pulse shrinks, and it fades faster with hexarelin than with other GHRPs. This is widely noted in studies and by users.
15. Raises cortisol and prolactin more than ipamorelin · ACTH, prolactin release · role Endocrine & growth
   The ghrelin receptor also prompts the pituitary to release ACTH, which raises cortisol, and prolactin. Hexarelin raises both more than ipamorelin does. Human studies show these hormone rises.
16. GH pulse lifts IGF-1, aiding muscle · GH/IGF-1 axis · role Muscle & performance
   Each GH pulse prompts the liver and tissues to make IGF-1, the main growth messenger of GH. IGF-1 drives protein building in muscle. The GH rise is shown in people, while long-term muscle outcomes are not.
17. Mechanism overview: Hexarelin activates the ghrelin receptor, GHSR-1a, and produces one of the strongest GH pulses of any GHRP, lifting IGF-1. It also binds CD36 directly on heart tissue, a route separate from GH, which protects heart muscle in injury models. It raises cortisol and prolactin more than ipamorelin. With repeated use the GH response fades through receptor desensitisation, faster than with other GHRPs.

## What has been tested
34. Endocrine & growth: GH release (human)
35. Heart & vessels: Heart protection (animal)
36. Heart & vessels: Heart function, small studies (human)
37. Human studies show strong GH release; animal and small human cardiac work suggests direct heart-protective effects independent of GH.
38. What it helps: Human studies show potent GH release, and small human studies look at heart function. Animal work shows a distinct heart-protective effect. There are no outcome trials. Users take it for the GH effect, the cardiac angle draws interest, and desensitisation is widely noted.
39. Trial: none · growth hormone release, heart function. Human studies were academic and mostly Italian, ending in the 2000s; no registered trial found. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
40. People with Cushing’s or high cortisol: Raises cortisol and ACTH alongside GH. (reported)
41. People with prolactinoma or high prolactin: GHRPs push prolactin up alongside GH, worsening prolactin problems. (reported)
42. People with active or recent cancer: It raises GH and IGF-1, growth signals that tumour cells also respond to. (inferred)
43. Drug-tested athletes: GH-releasing peptides are named on the WADA banned list. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
44. CJC-1295 no DAC: CJC-1295 no DAC works through the GHRH receptor and hexarelin through the ghrelin receptor, so together they push a larger GH pulse. The GHRH and GHRP synergy is established, but this exact pair has not been trialled, and hexarelin still desensitises.
45. CJC-1295 with DAC: The DAC version holds GHRH signalling up for about a week while hexarelin adds strong pulses. People pair them to raise GH and IGF-1 further, but the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
46. Sermorelin: Sermorelin supplies a short, natural GHRH signal and hexarelin amplifies the GH pulse it starts. The receptors work together in principle, but the pair itself has not been tested together.
47. Tesamorelin: Tesamorelin is a stabilised GHRH with trial data for visceral and liver fat, and hexarelin adds GH release through the ghrelin receptor. Users combine them for more GH, but the pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
48. A synthetic GH-releasing hexapeptide, the most potent of the classic GHRPs, with additional direct cardiac activity.
49. First studied for: A GH-releasing agent; separately studied for the heart.
50. Name story: Officially examorelin. An Italian rework of GHRP-6 with a methylated tryptophan for more punch. "Hexa" most likely nods to its six amino acids, joined to the "-relin" releasing-hormone stem. [S5] (inferred)
51. 1990s: Built as the most potent classic GHRP
52. 1990s to 2000s: Heart work shows protection independent of GH
53. Today: Strong GH releaser with a cardiac angle; unapproved, WADA-banned
