# Modafinil
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/modafinil
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Provigil, Modiodal, Modalert, Modvigil, CRL 40476, moda; armodafinil (Nuvigil, Waklert, R-modafinil)
2. Origin: Not a peptide: wakefulness promoting agent (dopamine reuptake inhibitor)
3. Shown with an FDA approved badge
4. Legal and approval status: FDA approved: Provigil (modafinil) 1998, Nuvigil (armodafinil) June 2007, for narcolepsy, obstructive sleep apnoea and shift work disorder. US Schedule IV. In the EU limited to narcolepsy since 2010. Cognitive enhancement in healthy people is off label. WADA prohibited in competition (stimulant).

## Summary
5. Modafinil is a wakefulness drug from Lafon Laboratories in France, not a peptide, and the successor to adrafinil. It is approved for sleepiness in narcolepsy, sleep apnoea and shift work, and used for long focus days, thought to work by keeping dopamine around and raising wake-centre activity. In rested healthy adults it gives modest gains on complex tasks only. Lost sleep is the most common complaint.
6. Punch line: The classic smart drug, not a peptide. Approved for narcolepsy; used off label for long workdays with Semax.
7. Used for: Focus, wakefulness, long work days

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Drive & mental stamina: Hours of alert, motivated focus
9. Cognition: Small gains on complex tasks
10. Sleep & rhythms: Pushes back sleepiness

## How it works
11. Keeps more dopamine around · Dopamine transporter blocked · role Drive & mental stamina
   Modafinil weakly blocks the dopamine transporter, the pump that clears dopamine from the synapse. More dopamine stays active in the striatum and prefrontal cortex. This is its best understood action and underlies the alertness seen in human trials.
12. Switches on the brain's wake centres · Orexin and histamine activity up · role Sleep & rhythms
   Downstream it raises activity in the orexin and histamine systems, the brain's main wake-promoting centres. This keeps people awake without the jittery push of classic stimulants. It is approved and effective for sleepiness in narcolepsy, sleep apnoea and shift work.
13. Sharpens planning and decisions · Prefrontal catecholamines up · role Cognition
   It raises catecholamines such as dopamine and noradrenaline in the prefrontal cortex, the region for planning and decisions. In rested healthy adults, a review of human studies found gains on long, complex tasks. Simple tasks and working memory changed little.
14. Mechanism overview: Modafinil's best understood action is weak blockade of the dopamine transporter, raising dopamine in the striatum and prefrontal cortex. Downstream it increases activity in orexin and histamine wake systems and raises noradrenaline and glutamate while lowering GABA in some regions. It is a racemate: armodafinil is the R enantiomer, which clears more slowly, so it holds higher levels late in the day. The serious but rare risks on the label are severe rashes, including Stevens Johnson syndrome, and psychiatric reactions.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
15. Typical: 50–200 mg (armodafinil 50–150 mg) · once in the morning · cycle As needed; many limit to a few days a week · route Oral (reported)
16. Half-life: ~12–15 hours (armodafinil longer late levels)
17. Most people take: Off label users commonly take 100 mg, ranging 50 to 200 mg, early in the morning. Label dose is 200 mg once each morning (or before a shift); armodafinil 150 to 250 mg.
18. Usually taken on demand rather than daily to limit tolerance and sleep loss. Taking it after midday commonly wrecks sleep.

## What people report
19. One of the most used off label 'smart drugs', taken for long work or study days, often stacked with Semax or caffeine. A 2020 Reddit survey (Teodorini) had 219 of 404 respondents report using it. Users describe hours of locked in focus and motivation; insomnia is the top complaint, then headache, diarrhoea, low appetite, mood changes, and less often palpitations and tremor. Some report tolerance and a flat or irritable feel with daily use. No aggregator counts located.
20. Liked / worked: Approved wakefulness drug
21. Liked / worked: Holds performance during sleep loss
22. Liked / worked: Modest gains on complex tasks in rested people
23. What worked: Reliably reduces sleepiness in narcolepsy and shift work and props up performance during sleep loss. In rested people, modest gains in attention and executive function on demanding tasks.
24. Went wrong / did not work: Little effect on working memory
25. Went wrong / did not work: EU restricted it to narcolepsy
26. Went wrong / did not work: Insomnia and headache common
27. What went wrong: Little benefit on simple tasks and working memory in rested people, and a few studies saw worse divergent creative thinking. The EMA judged risks outweighed benefits outside narcolepsy and removed the sleep apnoea and shift work uses in Europe in 2010.
28. Most reported: 100 mg mornings for long focus days
29. Most reported: Insomnia the top complaint
30. Most reported: Headache, low appetite, diarrhoea
31. Most reported: Tolerance with daily use

## What has been tested
32. Sleep & rhythms: Narcolepsy, sleep apnoea, shift work (trial)
33. Cognition: Rested healthy adults, 24 studies (human)
34. Drive & mental stamina: Sleep deprived military personnel (trial)
35. Many trials in narcolepsy, sleep apnoea, shift work and sleep deprived soldiers and pilots. In rested healthy people, a 2015 Oxford and Harvard review of 24 studies (Battleday and Brem) found consistent gains on longer, complex tasks such as planning and decision making, but not on working memory or flexible thinking.
36. What it helps: Approved and effective for sleepiness in narcolepsy, sleep apnoea and shift work. In rested healthy adults the evidence shows modest gains on long, complex tasks, little change on simple ones. Users take it for long focus days and most often complain of lost sleep.
37. Trial: recent · NCT07830056. A US Army study of modafinil in cold stressed, sleep deprived personnel is registered; current status was not confirmed. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1]

## Synergies (paired with)
38. Bromantane: Bromantane raises dopamine production, while modafinil keeps existing dopamine around longer. People stack them for drive and focus from two directions. Both lean on dopamine, and the pair has not been tested together.
39. Semax: Semax is reported to raise brain growth factors and monoamine turnover, while modafinil drives wakefulness and focus. The pairing aims at alertness plus longer-term brain support. It is user logic, not trial data.
40. Selank: Selank is used to calm anxiety without sedation, which can offset the edginess some feel on modafinil. People pair them for focus with calm. The pair has not been tested together.
41. Methylene blue: Methylene blue acts on mitochondrial energy and blocks MAO-A, while modafinil works on dopamine and wake systems. Users stack them for focus and mental energy. The MAO-A effect is a reason for caution, and the pair has not been tested together.
42. NA Semax Amidate: NA Semax Amidate is a longer-lasting Semax form assumed to raise BDNF and monoamine turnover, adding to modafinil's wake and focus effect. People stack them for long work days. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
43. A French wakefulness drug from Lafon Laboratories, the successor to adrafinil, which keeps people alert without the jittery rush of amphetamine. Armodafinil is its longer lasting R half, sold separately as Nuvigil and covered here in the same entry.
44. First studied for: Narcolepsy, then excessive sleepiness from obstructive sleep apnoea and shift work sleep disorder.
45. Name story: 'Moda' plus 'finil' from its parent adrafinil; CRL stands for Centre de Recherche Lafon, the French lab that made it. Armodafinil is the R (rectus) mirror form. [S2] (reported)
46. 1970s: Lafon Laboratories in France develops adrafinil, then its active metabolite modafinil
47. 1994: Launched in France as Modiodal
48. 1998: FDA approves Provigil for narcolepsy
49. 2007: Armodafinil (Nuvigil) approved in the US
50. 2010: EMA restricts EU use to narcolepsy
51. 2015: Oxford and Harvard review finds benefit on complex tasks in rested people
52. Today: Generic worldwide; a staple off label focus drug
