# Abaloparatide
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/abaloparatide
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Tymlos, BA058, BIM-44058, Eladynos (EU), Abaloparatide-SC, Eladynos
2. Origin: Lab-modified natural chain: PTHrP(1–34) analogue
3. Sequence: AVSEHQLLHDKGKSIQDLRRRELLEKLLAKLHTA (34 amino acids). Position 29 is Aib, shown as A; C-terminal amide
4. Shown with an FDA approved badge
5. Legal and approval status: FDA-approved as Tymlos (women 2017, men Dec 2022); boxed warning removed Dec 2021; hypersensitivity label update Aug 2026. Earliest US generic entry estimated Oct 2027 (reported). Not on the WADA list by name.

## Summary
6. Abaloparatide (Tymlos) is a 34-amino-acid analogue of PTHrP, engineered to favour the receptor state that builds bone. A daily pen shot is FDA-approved for high-risk osteoporosis, and trials showed far fewer new spine fractures than placebo. Dizziness, nausea and high urine calcium recur, lifetime use is capped at two years, and a bone drug usually follows.
7. Punch line: Daily pen shot built on PTHrP, not PTH. Approved for high-risk osteoporosis; trials cut new spine fractures 86%.
8. Used for: Osteoporosis, low bone density

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
9. Bone & cartilage: Builds new bone, fewer fractures
10. Tissue repair: Fracture healing interest
11. Endocrine & growth: Acts on the PTH receptor
12. Aging & senescence: Reverses age-related bone loss
13. Heart & vessels (backed only by animal or user reports): Dizziness, fast heartbeat after dose

## How it works
14. Pulses switch on bone-building cells · PTH1 receptor (PTH1R), RG conformation · role Bone & cartilage
   Abaloparatide is a PTHrP analogue that binds the PTH1 receptor and favours its RG conformation, a state that gives short bursts of cAMP signalling. Given as a daily pulse, this switches on osteoblasts, the bone-building cells. Phase 3 trials in people confirm the bone gains.
15. Builds more than it breaks down · Anabolic window, osteoblast > osteoclast · role Bone & cartilage
   Each pulse opens an anabolic window, where osteoblasts lay down new bone before osteoclasts, the bone-removing cells, catch up. Net bone is gained. In ACTIVE this cut new vertebral fractures in postmenopausal women, and ATOM showed density gains in men.
16. Less calcium spike than teriparatide · Transient cAMP signalling · role Endocrine & growth
   Because its cAMP signal is brief, it mobilises less calcium from bone and kidney than teriparatide. Blood calcium rises less as a result. High urine calcium can still occur and is one of the recurring problems.
17. Widens blood vessels briefly · Vascular PTH1R · role Heart & vessels
   PTH1 receptors also sit on blood vessels, and activating them relaxes the vessel wall. This causes a brief drop in blood pressure and a faster heartbeat after a dose. It explains the dizziness some people report.
18. Mechanism overview: Abaloparatide is a 34-residue synthetic analogue of parathyroid hormone-related protein (PTHrP 1–34) with substitutions including an Aib at position 29. It binds the PTH1 receptor (PTH1R) and favours the RG conformation, which gives short cAMP bursts. Given as a daily pulse, this tilts bone remodelling towards osteoblasts laying down new bone before osteoclasts catch up, the so-called anabolic window, with less sustained calcium mobilisation than teriparatide. Vascular PTH1R activation explains the transient blood pressure drop and faster heartbeat.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
19. Typical: 80 mcg · once daily · cycle Max 2 years lifetime, then antiresorptive · route Sub-Q (abdomen) (verified)
20. Half-life: ~1 h
21. Most people take: 80 mcg SC daily into the periumbilical abdomen, sitting or lying for early doses; up to 2 years lifetime, then denosumab or a bisphosphonate. A 300 mcg transdermal patch was tested but abandoned.

## What people report
22. Grey-market users take the clinical 80 mcg dose for bone density, usually then switching to an antiresorptive. Dizziness on early doses is the common report.
23. Liked / worked: 86% fewer new spine fractures
24. Liked / worked: Bone density up in spine and hip
25. Liked / worked: Works in men too
26. What worked: New vertebral fractures 0.6% vs 4.2% on placebo at 18 months (86% relative reduction), with gains in spine and hip density; approved for men to raise bone density.
27. Went wrong / did not work: Dizziness and orthostatic hypotension
28. Went wrong / did not work: Nausea, headache, palpitations
29. Went wrong / did not work: High urine calcium
30. Went wrong / did not work: 2-year lifetime cap
31. Went wrong / did not work: Patch version failed
32. What went wrong: Dizziness, nausea, headache, palpitations and orthostatic hypotension after doses; high urine calcium. Rat osteosarcoma; lifetime use capped at 2 years. The patch reached 7.1% vs 10.9% spine gain and was dropped.
33. Most reported: Dizziness on first doses
34. Most reported: Used before switching to denosumab
35. Most reported: Injection-site redness
36. Most reported: Few grey-market reports

## What has been tested
37. Bone & cartilage: Vertebral fractures, ACTIVE (human)
38. Bone & cartilage: Bone density in men, ATOM (human)
39. Tissue repair: Fracture healing (animal)
40. Phase 3 ACTIVE in postmenopausal women and ATOM in men; a microneedle patch failed its Phase 3 comparison.
41. What it helps: In ACTIVE it cut new vertebral fractures from 4.2% to 0.6% over 18 months in postmenopausal women and raised spine and hip density; ATOM showed density gains in men. It is used for high-risk osteoporosis, followed by an antiresorptive to keep the gain. Grey-market interest is small and mostly mirrors the clinical dose. Dizziness, nausea and high urine calcium are the recurring problems.
42. Trial: active · Phase 2 · spinal fusion in lumbar degenerative disease · NCT07587775. Approved as Tymlos; a new Phase 2 spine surgery study is not yet recruiting and two others are active but closed to enrollment. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
43. Paget's disease, unexplained high ALP, prior bone radiation: Higher baseline osteosarcoma risk; label warns. (verified)
44. People prone to dizziness on standing: Orthostatic hypotension around first doses. (verified)
45. People with kidney stones or high urine calcium: It raises calcium in the urine, which can grow new stones. (verified)

## Where it comes from
46. A 34-amino-acid analogue of PTHrP, the parathyroid-hormone-related protein, engineered to favour the receptor state that builds bone over the one that drives calcium up. A daily pen shot that grows new bone rather than just slowing loss.
47. First studied for: Postmenopausal osteoporosis at high fracture risk; men added in 2022.
48. Name story: Discovered at Ipsen as BIM-44058, developed by Radius Health as BA058. The '-paratide' ending marks it as kin to teriparatide, but it copies PTHrP, the tumour-linked cousin of parathyroid hormone, with which it shares 76% of its sequence. [S2] (verified)
49. Apr 2017: FDA approves Tymlos for postmenopausal osteoporosis
50. Dec 2021: Rat-osteosarcoma boxed warning removed
51. Dec 2021: Patch misses non-inferiority in wearABLe; dropped Jun 2022
52. Dec 2022: Approved to raise bone density in men
53. Aug 2026: Label adds hypersensitivity reaction warning
