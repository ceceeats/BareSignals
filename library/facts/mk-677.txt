# MK-677
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/mk-677
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: ibutamoren nutrobal, MK-0677, L-163,191, LUM-201, ibutamoren mesylate
2. Origin: Not a peptide
3. Legal and approval status: Not approved. Back in development as LUM-201, in a Phase 3 trial for children with growth hormone deficiency. WADA-banned (S2). Sold grey-market as a research chemical.

## Summary
4. MK-677 (ibutamoren) is an orally active small molecule, not a peptide, that mimics ghrelin to raise growth hormone and IGF-1 around the clock. Merck developed it for GH deficiency and frailty, and users take it for sleep, recovery and lean mass. It is not approved, and appetite, water retention and blood sugar rise, while a hip-fracture trial saw more heart-failure events.
5. Punch line: Oral ghrelin mimic, not a peptide. Lifts GH and IGF-1 all day; deeper sleep, bigger hunger, water retention.
6. Used for: GH/IGF-1 boost, sleep, lean mass

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Appetite & satiety: Raises hunger
8. Sleep & rhythms: Deeper sleep reported
9. Endocrine & growth: Raises GH and IGF-1 all day
10. Muscle & performance: Adds lean mass in older adults

## How it works
11. Mimics ghrelin to raise GH all day · Ghrelin receptor, GHSR-1a · role Endocrine & growth
   MK-677 is an oral small molecule that activates the ghrelin receptor GHSR-1a. In the pituitary this triggers growth hormone release, and in the hypothalamus it boosts GHRH and blunts somatostatin. Its long action pushes larger GH pulses across the day, shown in human trials.
12. Lifts IGF-1 downstream · GH/IGF-1 axis · role Endocrine & growth
   The higher growth hormone output drives the liver to make more IGF-1, which stays raised with daily dosing. Human trials reliably show IGF-1 rising to young-adult levels in older people. A trial in Alzheimer's disease showed that raising IGF-1 alone did not slow the disease.
13. Raises hunger · Hypothalamic ghrelin signal · role Appetite & satiety
   Ghrelin is the body's hunger hormone, so activating its receptor in the hypothalamus raises appetite. Users commonly report a strong increase in hunger. This is a direct effect of the target, not a side effect that can be separated from it.
14. Deepens slow-wave and REM sleep · GH pulse at night · role Sleep & rhythms
   Larger night-time GH pulses go along with deeper slow-wave sleep and more REM sleep. One small human study found both rose at the higher dose. With fourteen people in total, that evidence base is thin for the sleep reputation it carries.
15. Adds lean mass, not proven strength · GH/IGF-1 anabolic · role Muscle & performance
   Through raised GH and IGF-1, it added lean mass in a year-long trial in healthy older adults. Strength and physical function did not change in that trial. Fasting glucose rose, and a hip-fracture trial was stopped early over heart-failure events.
16. Mechanism overview: MK-677, or ibutamoren, is an oral small molecule, not a peptide, that activates the ghrelin receptor GHSR-1a. In the pituitary this triggers GH release, and in the hypothalamus it boosts GHRH and blunts somatostatin. Its long half-life raises GH pulses across the day and lifts IGF-1 with daily dosing. Ghrelin receptor activation also drives hunger. GH's anti-insulin action raises fasting glucose, and sodium retention causes water weight. Larger night-time GH pulses accompany deeper slow-wave and more REM sleep.

## Who shouldn't take it
44. People with heart failure or heart disease: A hip-fracture trial in older adults was stopped early for more heart failure. (reported)
45. People with prediabetes or poorly controlled diabetes: Raised fasting glucose and HbA1c in a 2-year trial. (reported)
46. People with active or recent cancer: Raises IGF-1 around the clock for as long as it is taken. (inferred)
47. Drug-tested athletes: Ibutamoren (MK-677) is named on the WADA banned list as a GH secretagogue. (verified)

## Where it comes from
49. Ibutamoren, an orally active small-molecule ghrelin-receptor agonist (not a peptide) that raises GH and IGF-1 around the clock.
50. First studied for: Developed by Merck for GH deficiency and for frailty/muscle wasting in the elderly.
51. Name story: MK is Merck's in-house compound prefix; the same molecule was also Merck's L-163,191. Despite living on peptide lists, it is a small molecule that mimics ghrelin, and its INN is ibutamoren. [S8] (reported)
52. 1990s: Merck builds an oral ghrelin mimic for GH deficiency and frailty
53. 2000s: Hip-fracture trial in older adults stopped over heart failure
54. Today: Popular oral GH-optimizer; in Phase 3 as LUM-201 for children; WADA-banned

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from: a Merck drug that never found its indication [people]
55. Merck designed it in the mid-1990s as L-163,191, later MK-0677, with a tentative brand name of Oratrope [1] (verified). The goal was a pill that would do what growth hormone injections do for frail older people, by persuading the pituitary to release more of its own hormone rather than replacing it. That framing explains the shape of the human record: the trials were in older adults, hip fracture patients, people with Alzheimer's disease and children with short stature, not in healthy young adults.
56. Merck ran it through several indications and stopped each one, for reasons covered below. The molecule then passed through licensees and is now back in development under a new code, LUM-201, with the original company still listed as originator [2] (verified). So the compound sold as a gym product and the one in a 2026 phase 3 for children are the same molecule with very different paperwork behind them.

### Mechanism: why it raises growth hormone all day rather than in a pulse [people]
57. It binds the ghrelin receptor, the same receptor the hunger hormone uses, which is why appetite and growth hormone release travel together on this drug. Natural ghrelin is a peptide with a short life in blood. This is a small, orally absorbed molecule with a much longer one, so instead of brief spikes the receptor is pushed for most of the day [1] (verified).
58. The useful detail is the gap between drug half-life and effect. In dogs the molecule itself has an elimination half-life of about four to six hours, yet in humans IGF-1 stays elevated for up to 24 hours after a single dose [1] (verified, animal half-life; human effect duration). Human plasma half-life figures were not found in an accessible primary source for this review. What matters for interpreting the trials is that daily dosing produces a sustained IGF-1 rise, and IGF-1, not growth hormone, is what most of the downstream effects and side effects track.
59. Because it acts on the body's own pituitary, the growth hormone it produces still comes in pulses, just larger and more frequent ones; the 1990s work described amplitude rising rather than a flat plateau (inferred from the studies of the class; the Nass trial reported pulsatile secretion restored to young-adult levels [3]).

### The human record: what each trial measured and found [people]
60. Healthy older adults, the best trial. Sixty-five healthy adults aged 60 to 81 took 25 mg or placebo daily for a year. Growth hormone and IGF-1 rose to young-adult levels. Fat-free mass rose 1.1 kg on drug and fell 0.5 kg on placebo, a 1.6 kg difference [3,4] (verified). Strength and physical function did not change [4] (reported). Fasting glucose rose by about 0.3 mmol per litre and insulin sensitivity fell [3,4] (verified). This is the source of the claim that it builds lean mass, and the same paper is the source of the claim that the lean mass did not translate into function.
61. Hip fracture recovery. A Merck phase 2b randomised 123 elderly hip fracture patients to 25 mg or placebo for 24 weeks. IGF-1 rose by about 51 ng per mL over placebo. Gait speed improved slightly, stair-climbing power and other functional measures did not significantly change, and falls trended lower without reaching significance. The trial was stopped early for a safety signal of congestive heart failure in a small number of patients, and the authors called the safety profile unfavourable in that population [5] (verified).
62. Alzheimer's disease. A Merck-funded year-long trial in people with mild to moderate disease raised IGF-1 as expected and did nothing to slow progression [6,7] (verified). Coverage at the time put enrolment at 416 [7] (reported). It is the cleanest demonstration in the record that raising IGF-1 is not the same as producing a clinical benefit.
63. Sleep. One small study: eight young men on 5 mg, 25 mg and placebo for a week each, and six older adults on 2 mg then 25 mg for two weeks each. In the young group, stage IV sleep lasted about 50% longer and REM rose over 20% at the high dose; in the older group REM rose nearly 50% and REM latency fell [8] (verified). The older arm had no placebo period, and fourteen people in total is a thin base for the sleep reputation the compound has.
64. Children with growth hormone deficiency, the live programme. Phase 2 studies reportedly met their endpoints, one enrolling 104 children, and a 12-month placebo-controlled phase 3 in treatment-naive prepubertal children is recruiting, with a long-term extension planned [2] (reported).

### Safety: what the trials actually showed [people]
65. Glucose is the most consistent signal. Every longer trial in the record moved fasting glucose up and insulin sensitivity down [3,4] (verified). The mechanism is ordinary growth hormone physiology, which opposes insulin action, so this is expected rather than idiosyncratic, and it is dose- and duration-dependent rather than a rare event (inferred).
66. The heart failure signal is the one most often left out. It appeared in frail elderly patients recovering from surgery, a population prone to fluid overload, and the published abstract does not give the case count by arm [5]. It cannot be generalised to young healthy users, and it cannot be dismissed either: fluid retention is a known effect of raising growth hormone, and the drug did enough of it in that group to stop a trial (verified that the trial stopped; inferred on mechanism).
67. Appetite rises early and, in the older adult trial, settled in most people within about three months [3] (reported). Cortisol rises acutely with dosing [1] (reported).
68. What has not been studied: no trial has looked at healthy adults under 60 for more than weeks, none has examined cancer incidence despite the sustained IGF-1 rise, and none has followed users beyond about two years.

### What is actually in the bottle [lab]
69. It is a small molecule, not a peptide, so the identity and stability problems that dominate peptide products are less severe here: it is a stable salt (the mesylate) that survives being made into capsules or liquids (inferred from chemistry). The quality problem is different. Outside the LUM-201 programme there is no pharmaceutical-grade supply, so every grey-market product is a research chemical with whatever purity and dose accuracy its maker provides. No published independent survey of retail MK-677 content was found for this review.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
70. Not approved anywhere. In the US it is an investigational drug, in phase 3 as LUM-201 for paediatric growth hormone deficiency, with orphan designations in the US and EU [1,2] (verified).
71. In sport it is named in the WADA Prohibited List under S2 as a growth hormone secretagogue, prohibited at all times [9] (verified). Urinary metabolites are the detection target in anti-doping work.

### Open questions [mixed]
72. Does lean mass gained this way ever become strength or function? The best trial says no at one year in older adults; nobody has tested younger people properly.
73. What is the glucose cost over years? Every trial moved glucose the wrong way, and none ran long enough to show whether that becomes diabetes in susceptible people.
74. How many heart failure cases were there, and in which arm? The answer sits in the full hip fracture paper and the trial's internal data.
75. Will a paediatric approval change access? If LUM-201 is approved, the molecule gets a label, a reference standard and a pharmacovigilance record for the first time, which would change how the rest of the evidence can be read.
