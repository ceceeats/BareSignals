# GHK-Cu + KPV
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ghk-cu-kpv
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: GHK-Cu/KPV 60 mg, GHK-Cu/KPV 70 mg (Beauty Blend), copper peptide + KPV, KLOW without BPC-157 and TB-500
2. Origin: Blend of 2 peptides
3. Legal and approval status: US: not approved. GHK-Cu (injectable) and KPV were removed from FDA Category 2 on 15 Apr 2026; KPV won an 8-6-1 PCAC vote in July 2026, GHK-Cu goes to a panel before end of Feb 2027. No blend has any approval. EU, UK, Australia: no approved product. WADA: both fall under S0.

## Summary
4. This blend combines the copper tripeptide GHK-Cu with KPV, an anti-inflammatory tripeptide, in one vial. People pair them so GHK-Cu supports collagen, skin and hair while KPV calms inflammation in skin and gut. Evidence is per component, with small topical human skin trials for GHK-Cu and mouse colitis models for KPV, and nobody has tested the pair.
5. Punch line: KLOW minus BPC-157 and TB-500. Copper peptide rebuilds collagen and skin; KPV calms skin and gut inflammation.
6. Used for: Skin quality, scars, inflamed skin and gut

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Skin & aesthetics: Collagen and skin repair from GHK-Cu
8. Inflammatory signaling: KPV switches off inflammation signals
9. Tissue repair: Wound and scar closure
10. Digestive & gut: KPV calms colitis in mice
11. Hair (backed only by animal or user reports): GHK-Cu used for hair density
12. Immunity (backed only by animal or user reports): Steadies overreacting immune cells

## How it works
13. Turns on collagen building in skin · Fibroblast collagen and decorin synthesis · role Skin & aesthetics
   GHK-Cu signals fibroblasts to make collagen, decorin and glycosaminoglycans, the building blocks of skin matrix. It also shifts the balance between matrix-remodelling enzymes and their inhibitors. This is shown in cell and animal studies and small topical human skin trials.
14. Carries copper to healing tissue · Cu(II) bound to Gly-His-Lys · role Tissue repair
   GHK-Cu binds a copper ion tightly and delivers it to rebuilding tissue, where copper enzymes cross-link collagen. KPV has no His, Met or Asn, so it does not compete for that copper. The two can sit in one vial without disturbing the copper complex.
15. Blocks a key inflammation switch · NF-κB down · role Inflammatory signaling
   KPV, residues 11 to 13 of alpha-MSH, blocks activation of NF-κB, the main switch for inflammatory genes. It does this without the MC1R tanning receptor. This is shown in cell studies and mouse colitis models, with no human trial.
16. Gets into gut and immune cells · PepT1 transporter · role Digestive & gut
   KPV enters gut and immune cells through PepT1, a transporter for small peptides in the gut lining. That lets it act inside the cells driving inflammation. In mice this eased colitis and helped the colon lining heal.
17. Mechanism overview: GHK-Cu is the Gly-His-Lys tripeptide holding a Cu(II) ion; it raises collagen, decorin and glycosaminoglycan synthesis in fibroblasts and shifts the balance of matrix metalloproteinases and their inhibitors. KPV is Lys-Pro-Val, residues 11 to 13 of alpha-MSH; it enters cells by PepT1 and blocks NF-κB activation independent of MC1R. The pair covers matrix rebuilding (GHK-Cu) and inflammation control (KPV). KPV has no His, Met or Asn, so it does not compete for copper and is chemically quiet beside Cu-GHK.

## What is inside the blend
18. GHK-Cu: 50 mg · Collagen, skin and hair repair; carries copper
19. KPV: 10 mg · Switches down inflammation in skin and gut
20. Most common ratio: GHK-Cu 50 · KPV 10 = 60 mg (10 of 15 listings)
21. Other ratio seen: GHK-Cu 50 · KPV 20 ("Beauty Blend") = 70 mg (5 listings, mostly one factory's naming)
22. Other ratio seen: GHK-Cu 25 · KPV 5 = 30 mg (reported for some boutique sellers, no listing seen)
23. Version Most listings: GHK-Cu 50 · KPV 10 = 60 mg
24. Version Beauty Blend: GHK-Cu 50 · KPV 20 = 70 mg
25. Why GHK-Cu is in it: Matrix rebuilding: collagen, skin firmness, hair; KPV does none of this
26. Why KPV is in it: Direct anti-inflammatory signal (NF-κB) for flares, gut and skin; GHK-Cu's anti-inflammatory effect is weaker and indirect
27. Mixing chemistry: KPV (Lys-Pro-Val) has no His, Met or Asn, so it does not grab copper or oxidise easily: the quietest partner GHK-Cu can have.
28. Mixing chemistry: Copper stays bound above about pH 4; a blue solution means the complex is intact, a green or brown shift suggests copper has come off.
29. Mixing chemistry: Keep vitamin C, glutathione and other reducers out of the vial and syringe: copper plus a reducer drives oxidation.
30. Mixing chemistry: GHK-Cu over-fill of around 20% has been seen on KLOW lab certificates; ask for per-component mg, not one purity number.
31. Self-mixing: Mixing separate vials lets you change the 5:1 ratio, but pulls acidic KPV or BPC-style acetate liquid into the copper vial, which can lower pH toward the zone where copper loosens.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
32. Typical: 1–2 mg GHK-Cu equivalent · 1× daily · cycle 4–8 weeks · route Sub-Q (reported)
33. Half-life: per component
34. Most people take: No standard blend dose. People size it on GHK-Cu: 60 mg in 3 mL, 10 units gives about 1.7 mg GHK-Cu and 0.33 mg KPV.

## What people report
35. Usually Sub-Q, once daily, sized off GHK-Cu (1 to 2 mg). At that size KPV lands at 0.2 to 0.4 mg, the low end of its usual range (0.4 to 0.8 mg on the 50 + 20 vial). Some users split it into two daily shots to soften the sting.
36. Liked / worked: Each component has animal or small human data
37. Liked / worked: KPV said to soften GHK-Cu sting
38. Liked / worked: Covers skin repair and inflammation in one shot
39. What worked: No blend-specific report pool. Users of the parent KLOW blend and of each component report smoother, calmer skin, faster scar and wound closure, and calmer gut and eczema flares. KPV is often added because it is said to reduce the injection sting of GHK-Cu.
40. Went wrong / did not work: Never tested as a pair
41. Went wrong / did not work: GHK-Cu sting and flushing with every dose
42. Went wrong / did not work: No reconstituted stability data
43. What went wrong: GHK-Cu dominates the vial (50 of 60 mg), so the injection sting, skin flushing and tiredness people report from copper peptide come with every dose. KPV users report early headache or flu-like feeling at low doses. No human efficacy data for either peptide injected, and none for the pair.
44. Most reported: Dose set by GHK-Cu, 1 to 2 mg
45. Most reported: Some split into two shots a day
46. Most reported: Bought as a cheaper KLOW alternative

## What has been tested
47. Never tested as a combination in animals or people. GHK-Cu has cell, animal and small topical human skin studies; KPV has mouse colitis and cell studies. No published stability study of the two reconstituted together; lab certificates exist for the larger KLOW blend, not this pair.
48. What it helps: Evidence is per component. GHK-Cu: cell and animal wound studies and small topical human skin trials. KPV: mouse colitis models, no human trial. No study has tested them together; users report calmer, smoother skin and fewer flares, mostly from the related KLOW blend.

## Who shouldn't take it
49. Wilson's disease or copper overload: Each dose delivers copper; people who cannot clear copper accumulate it in liver and brain. (inferred)
50. People with IBD on biologics or immunosuppressants: KPV damps NF-kB signalling; stacking it onto immune drugs is untested. (inferred)
51. People with cholestatic liver disease: Copper leaves the body through bile; PBC, PSC and bile duct blockage raise body copper. (inferred)
52. People fighting an active infection: Blunting inflammation can blunt the response clearing the infection. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
53. BPC-157: BPC-157 adds vessel growth, tendon and gut repair, while this blend covers skin matrix and inflammation control. Adding it moves toward the KLOW blend. No study has tested these together, so it is user logic, not trial data.
54. Thymosin Beta-4: Thymosin beta-4 adds cell migration and anti-inflammatory action for deeper wounds. Combined with this blend and BPC-157 it forms the KLOW stack. The combination has not been tested in animals or people.
55. GHK-Cu: This blend already contains GHK-Cu, so adding more raises the collagen and copper share, and with it the copper load. People do this for scars or hair. There is no data on extra benefit.
56. KPV: This blend already contains KPV, so adding more raises the NF-κB-blocking anti-inflammatory share. People do this for gut or skin flares. There is no data on extra benefit.

## Where it comes from
57. A two-peptide vial: the copper tripeptide GHK-Cu (50 mg) with the anti-inflammatory tripeptide KPV (10 mg); a 50 + 20 mg version also sells. It is KLOW with BPC-157 and TB-500 taken out.
58. First studied for: Sold as a skin and calming blend: GHK-Cu for collagen, skin and hair, KPV to switch down inflammation in skin and gut.
59. 1973: GHK-Cu isolated from human plasma (Pickart)
60. 1990s to 2000s: KPV studied as the calming tail of alpha-MSH
61. 2020s: GLOW and KLOW blends spread; the two-peptide GHK-Cu + KPV vial follows
62. Apr 2026: Both removed from FDA Category 2
63. Today: Sold as a skin and inflammation blend, mostly 50 + 10 mg (60 mg); 50 + 20 also sold
