# Tesamorelin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/tesamorelin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: TH9507, Egrifta, Egrifta SV, Egrifta WR, tesamorelin acetate
2. Origin: Lab-modified natural chain
3. Sequence: YADAIFTNSYRKVLGQLSARKLLQDIMSRQQGESNQERGARARL (44 amino acids). Hexenoyl group on the start, amide end
4. Shown with an FDA approved badge
5. Legal and approval status: FDA-approved (Egrifta) for HIV lipodystrophy; other uses are off-label/grey-market. WADA-banned.

## Summary
6. Tesamorelin is a stabilized GHRH analogue, sold as Egrifta, engineered to resist breakdown and last longer than sermorelin. It is FDA-approved to reduce visceral belly fat in HIV lipodystrophy, and small trials show lower liver fat and, in one RCT, better memory in older adults. Belly fat returns after stopping and blood sugar can rise; users treat it as the most evidence-backed GHRH analogue.
7. Punch line: Egrifta. A sturdier GHRH that raises your own growth hormone; the only one approved to cut belly fat.
8. Used for: Visceral fat loss (HIV label)

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
9. Endocrine & growth: Raises the body's own GH
10. Fat metabolism: Cuts belly fat around organs
11. Organ function: Less liver fat
12. Sleep & rhythms: Better sleep reported
13. Cognition (backed only by animal or user reports): Memory studied in older adults
14. Inflammatory signaling (backed only by animal or user reports): Lower inflammation markers

## How it works
15. Prompts the pituitary to release GH · GHRH receptor · role Endocrine & growth
   Tesamorelin binds pituitary GHRH receptors and triggers GH release in pulses, so the body's feedback control stays intact. IGF-1 rises as a result. Phase 3 human trials confirmed the GH and IGF-1 rise alongside its fat effects.
16. Stabilised to resist breakdown · Modified GHRH 1-44 · role Endocrine & growth
   It is the full 44 amino acid GHRH with a small chemical cap on the front end that shields it from DPP-4, the enzyme that cuts natural GHRH. This extends its action enough for once-daily use, the form tested in human trials.
17. Cuts deep belly (visceral) fat · GH-driven lipolysis · role Fat metabolism
   GH strongly drives lipolysis, the release of stored fat, and deep belly (visceral) fat is unusually GH-sensitive. In phase 3 trials in HIV lipodystrophy, imaging showed clear visceral fat loss against placebo. The fat returns after stopping.
18. Lowers liver fat in trials · Hepatic fat fraction · role Organ function
   The same GH-driven fat release lowers fat stored in the liver. In a randomised trial in people with HIV and fatty liver, liver fat fell more than on placebo, and fewer treated people showed fibrosis progression. The trial was small, so it is a strong signal, not settled.
19. Improved memory in older adults in one RCT · GHRH, brain GABA up · role Cognition
   In one randomised trial in older adults, GHRH treatment improved memory and raised brain levels of GABA, the main calming messenger. The result is small and has not been replicated, so treat it as early human evidence.
20. Mechanism overview: Tesamorelin is the full 44 amino acid GHRH with a trans-3-hexenoyl group on the N-terminus, which protects it from DPP-4 and extends its action. It binds pituitary GHRH receptors and stimulates pulsatile GH release, raising IGF-1 while feedback control stays intact. GH released this way strongly drives lipolysis in visceral fat, which is metabolically active and GH-sensitive. Liver fat falls by the same route. In one trial in older adults it raised brain GABA levels alongside memory gains.

## Who shouldn't take it
44. Pituitary tumour, pituitary surgery or brain radiation: Label contraindication: needs an intact hypothalamic-pituitary axis. (verified)
45. People with active or recent cancer: Raises IGF-1; label contraindicates active malignancy. (verified)
46. People with prediabetes or poorly controlled diabetes: Raises blood glucose and HbA1c; label requires monitoring and warns about glucose intolerance. (verified)
47. People allergic to mannitol: Mannitol is an excipient; label contraindicates hypersensitivity. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
48. Ipamorelin: Tesamorelin drives GH through the GHRH receptor and ipamorelin adds a cleaner ghrelin-receptor pulse, aiming for more GH than either alone. Sold pre-mixed as Tesamorelin + Ipamorelin and in a three-way blend, but the pair has not been tested together in trials.
49. CJC-1295 no DAC: Both are GHRH analogues on the same receptor, so they overlap more than they complement. They appear together in the pre-mixed Tesamorelin + Ipamorelin + CJC-1295 no DAC, where ipamorelin adds the second pathway. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
50. GHRP-2: Tesamorelin brings trial-backed GHRH drive, and GHRP-2 adds a strong ghrelin-receptor pulse at the cost of hunger, cortisol and prolactin. People pair them for a bigger GH response. The pair itself has not been tested together.
51. GHRP-6: GHRP-6 adds a ghrelin-receptor pulse and strong appetite on top of tesamorelin's GHRH drive. That hunger works against tesamorelin's main use, fat loss, so it suits bulking more than cutting. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
52. Hexarelin: Hexarelin adds the strongest ghrelin-receptor push to tesamorelin's GHRH drive, but its effect fades with repeated use and it raises cortisol and prolactin more. Used for short blocks. The pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
53. A stabilized GHRH analogue (Egrifta), engineered to resist breakdown and last longer than sermorelin.
54. First studied for: Reducing the visceral belly fat (lipodystrophy) that builds up in people on HIV therapy.
55. Name story: The full 44-residue GHRH, the whole hormone sermorelin trims to 29, with a small trans-3-hexenoic acid cap on its front end. Canadian developer Theratechnologies coded it TH9507; '-relin' marks the INN growth-hormone-release family. [S9] (verified)
56. 2010: Approved as Egrifta for HIV-linked belly fat
57. 2010s: Studied in fatty liver and cognition in aging
58. Today: Best-evidenced GHRH analogue; off-label for belly fat

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### What the pivotal trial measured, with the numbers that matter [people]
59. This is the best-evidenced compound in the growth-hormone-releasing family, so the pivotal trial is worth reading in detail rather than by its conclusion.
60. It enrolled 412 people with HIV and abdominal fat accumulation across 43 sites, 86% men, randomised two-to-one to 2 mg daily subcutaneously or placebo for 26 weeks [1] (verified).
61. Visceral adipose tissue, measured by imaging rather than reported, fell 15.2% on drug while rising 5.0% on placebo [1]. Insulin-like growth factor 1 rose 81%, about 109 nanograms per millilitre, against a 5% fall on placebo. Triglycerides fell 50 milligrams per decilitre against a 9 milligram rise [1] (verified).
62. The measure to notice is the first one. Visceral fat assessed by imaging is an objective endpoint, and a 20 percentage point separation from placebo on it is a solid result. This is the reason the compound carries more credibility than the rest of its family: the effect was demonstrated on an instrument, not a questionnaire.
63. Glucose is the honest complication. There were no significant differences between groups in fasting glucose, two-hour glucose or insulin in this trial [1] (verified). That is a better result than the mechanism predicts, and it sits alongside the label's requirement to monitor glucose. The resolution is that growth hormone raises glucose reliably enough to warrant monitoring, while a 26-week trial in this population did not detect a group difference (inferred).

### The immunogenicity finding nobody quotes [people]
64. Antibodies to the drug appeared in 48.6% of treated participants against 2.7% on placebo, and six patients developed urticaria [1] (verified).
65. Nearly half of treated people mounting an antibody response is a striking number, and it is the single most useful data point in this catalog for thinking about injected peptides generally. Regulators repeatedly flag immunogenicity as an unquantified risk for unapproved injected peptides, noting that aggregates and unidentified impurities can drive immune responses and that no studies exist to settle it. Here is a well-characterised, properly manufactured, approved 44-residue peptide, studied in a controlled trial, and the answer was: about half (verified).
66. That reframes the concern. The question for unapproved peptides is not whether an injected peptide can provoke an antibody response. It evidently can, even when the product is clean. The open question is what those antibodies do, and for this compound the trial found no loss of effect attributable to them, which is reassuring for it specifically and says nothing about any other molecule (inferred).
67. Discontinuation for adverse events was 12.1% on drug against 2.9% on placebo, and treatment-related adverse events ran 53.8% against 36.5% [1] (verified). Overall and serious adverse event rates did not differ significantly. So the tolerability cost is real and mostly non-serious.

### Where it has been tested beyond its approval, and what those trials found [people]
68. Two further randomised trials are more interesting than the approval, because they tested whether the visceral-fat mechanism translates into organ-level benefit.
69. A liver trial randomised 61 people with HIV and a hepatic fat fraction above 5% to 2 mg daily or placebo for 12 months, followed by a six-month open-label phase [2] (verified). Liver fat fell by 4.1 percentage points more than placebo, a 37% relative reduction. Steatosis resolved, meaning fat fraction fell below 5%, in 35% of treated participants against 4% on placebo. Fibrosis progressed in 2 treated participants against 9 on placebo [2] (verified).
70. That fibrosis result is the most clinically meaningful number associated with this compound. Fibrosis progression is what turns fatty liver into liver disease, and a reduction in it is a harder outcome than a fat percentage. The trial was small and the comparison was a secondary one, so it is a strong signal rather than a settled finding (inferred).
71. The liver trial also found no effect on fasting glucose, no overall effect on ALT or lipids, with ALT falling only in those whose levels were elevated at baseline, and a significant reduction in C-reactive protein [2] (verified).
72. A separate randomised trial in older adults reported better memory performance alongside a rise in brain GABA levels (reported, as the card records). That result is small, single and has not been replicated, and cognition trials in ageing have a poor record of replication generally (inferred).

### Why the mechanism predicts the shape of the benefit, including its limits [mixed]
73. The compound does not supply growth hormone. It stimulates the pituitary to release it in pulses, which leaves the body's feedback control intact. That design decision explains most of what follows.
74. Pulsatile release under intact feedback is why this compound does not produce the acromegalic effects of supraphysiological growth hormone, and why its effects are modest rather than dramatic. The ceiling is set by how much the pituitary will release, not by how much is injected (inferred).
75. It also explains the tissue selectivity. Visceral fat is unusually growth-hormone-sensitive and metabolically active, which is why it responds while other fat depots respond less, and why liver fat falls by the same route [1,2] (inferred from the mechanism and consistent with both trials).
76. The same logic sets the limits. An intact pituitary is required, which is why the label contraindicates pituitary tumour, pituitary surgery and brain radiation. Raised growth hormone signalling means raised insulin-like growth factor 1, which is why active malignancy is contraindicated. And growth hormone opposes insulin, which is why glucose monitoring is required (verified via the label positions the card records).
77. Visceral fat returns after stopping, which the mechanism also predicts: nothing about pulsed stimulation changes the set point that produced the fat in the first place (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
78. Does the fibrosis result replicate? Two progressions against nine in a 61-person trial is the most clinically important number here and the one most in need of a larger confirmation.
79. Does any of this transfer outside HIV-associated fat accumulation? Both randomised trials were run in that population, which has a specific pattern of fat distribution and metabolic disturbance.
80. What happens to glucose over years rather than months? Two trials found no group difference while the label requires monitoring, which means the question is unresolved rather than answered.
81. What do the antibodies do over long-term use? Nearly half of treated participants developed them in 26 weeks, with no detected loss of effect in that window.
82. Is the cognition signal real? One small randomised trial, unreplicated, in a field where unreplicated cognition results are the norm.
83. What is the right comparison for off-label use? The approved evidence is for a daily injection in a specific disease, and nothing in the record addresses intermittent use in healthy people for body composition.
84. Does the visceral-fat benefit matter if it reverses on stopping? That is a question about indefinite treatment, and no trial has run long enough to answer it.
