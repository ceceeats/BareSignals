# Cerebrolysin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/cerebrolysin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: brain peptide mixture, FPF-1070, cerebroprotein hydrolysate, CAS 12656-61-0
2. Origin: Not a peptide
3. Shown with an FDA approved badge
4. Legal and approval status: Approved in many countries (not the US) for stroke/dementia. Not FDA-approved.

## Summary
5. Cerebrolysin is not one peptide but a mixture of small peptides and amino acids from pig brain tissue. It is approved in many countries, though not by the FDA, and given by IV or IM courses for stroke, dementia and brain injury. Some trials show modest gains, but a 2023 Cochrane review found no reduction in stroke deaths and a possible rise in serious side effects.
6. Punch line: Pig brain peptide mixture given as IV or IM courses after stroke and brain injury. Approved abroad only.
7. Used for: Stroke, brain injury, cognition

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)

9. Neural plasticity: Nerve growth factor-like action
10. Neural protection: Studied after stroke and brain injury
11. Mood: Users report steadier mood
12. Tissue repair (backed only by animal or user reports): Nerve repair after injury
13. Sleep & rhythms (backed only by animal or user reports): Some users report better sleep

## How it works
14. Peptide mix with nerve-growth-like activity · Neurotrophic activity · role Neural protection
   Cerebrolysin is a mix of small peptides and free amino acids from digested pig brain. In cell and animal models it shows neurotrophic activity resembling BDNF and NGF, the brain's own growth factors. That supports neuron survival, though which molecules do this is unknown.
15. Active components undefined · Pig brain peptide mix · role Neural protection
   No active ingredient has been identified, so there is no sequence or reference standard. Batches are released against process controls and activity tests, and some variation between batches is inherent. Research chemical powders sold under the name cannot be assumed to match the approved solution.
16. Supports new connections and neurons · Synaptogenesis, neurogenesis · role Neural plasticity
   In models it supports synaptogenesis, the forming of new connections, and neurogenesis, the birth of new neurons. The aim is to back the brain's own repair after injury and in degenerative disease. This rests on cell and animal work.
17. Limits nerve cell death after injury · Anti-apoptotic · role Neural protection
   After ischaemia or trauma, it limits apoptosis and excitotoxic damage in animal models, so fewer nerve cells die. In human stroke trials this did not translate: a 2023 Cochrane review found no reduction in deaths and more non-fatal serious side effects.
18. Modest memory gains in dementia trials · Cognitive outcomes · role Cognition
   Some dementia trials report modest gains on memory and cognitive scales. A Cochrane review in vascular dementia found benefit but rated the evidence very low quality, with industry funding and risk of bias. Traumatic brain injury trials report small functional gains in limited numbers.
19. Mechanism overview: Cerebrolysin is a mixture of low molecular weight peptides and free amino acids from enzymatically digested pig brain. Its individual active components are undefined. In models it shows neurotrophic activity resembling BDNF and NGF signalling, supporting neuron survival, synapse formation and neurogenesis. It limits apoptosis and excitotoxic damage after ischaemia or trauma. The idea is to support the brain's own repair programme in the window after injury and in degenerative disease.

## What has been tested
37. Neural protection: Stroke outcomes (trial)
38. Cognition: Dementia (trial)
39. Tissue repair: Traumatic brain injury (trial)
40. Many human trials, mostly in Europe and Asia. The 2023 Cochrane review found it probably does not reduce deaths after stroke and may add non-fatal serious side effects. Some dementia and brain-injury trials report modest benefit.
41. What it helps: It is approved in many countries and given as IV or IM courses. Some dementia trials show modest memory and cognitive gains, and some traumatic brain injury trials report better recovery. ## Synergies (paired with)
46. Dihexa: Dihexa boosts HGF and c-Met signalling to build synapses, while cerebrolysin brings broad neurotrophic activity resembling BDNF and NGF. People pair them for memory and focus. Dihexa has only rat data, the same pathway drives many cancers, and the pair has not been tested together.
47. Semax: Semax is reported to raise BDNF and is used for stroke in Russia, while cerebrolysin offers a wider neurotrophic mix used after stroke and brain injury. People pair them for cognition and recovery from two angles. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
48. Selank: Selank is used for calm focus and is reported to raise BDNF, while cerebrolysin targets neuron survival and new connections. People pair them to support cognition with less anxiety. The pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
49. Not a single peptide but a mixture of small peptides and amino acids derived from pig brain tissue, made by Ever Pharma, with neurotrophic (nerve-growth-like) activity.
50. First studied for: Stroke, dementia and traumatic brain injury.
51. Name story: Cerebro (brain) plus lysis (breaking down): it is a mix of peptides made by enzymatically digesting pig brain protein. Its Chinese name translates literally as 'cerebroprotein hydrolysate'. [S6] (inferred)
52. Late 20th c.: Pig brain peptide mix used for stroke, dementia and brain injury
53. 2023: Cochrane: probably no fewer stroke deaths; possible added harms

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### What is actually in the ampoule, and why that is the central question [lab]
55. This is not a peptide, it is a manufacturing process. Purified brain tissue from pigs approved for human food is digested with enzymes under controlled conditions, then ultrafiltered so that what passes through is mostly below 10 kilodaltons [1] (reported). The resulting solution is roughly a quarter low molecular weight peptides and three quarters free amino acids, standardised at about 215 mg per mL of nitrogen-containing substances [1] (reported).
56. No active ingredient has been identified. There is no sequence, no single molecular weight and no reference standard, so batches are released against process controls, composition tests and biological activity assays rather than against a defined molecule [1] (reported). Batch-to-batch variability is inherent to that approach and has been raised by regulators [1] (reported). An independent assessment described it plainly: because it is prepared from brain homogenate, content and concentration can vary between preparations and between manufacturers [2] (verified).
57. Two practical consequences. First, an animal-derived injectable carries contamination risk in a way a synthetic peptide does not, and that risk was explicitly noted in the same assessment [2] (verified). Second, products sold as "cerebrolysin" powder by research chemical suppliers cannot be the same thing as the approved European solution, because the approved product is defined by its manufacturer's process; nothing connects an unrelated powder to the trial evidence (inferred).

### Mechanism, and the gap in it [mixed]
58. The proposal is neurotrophic activity resembling the body's own nerve growth factors, supporting neuron survival, synapse formation and neurogenesis, and limiting cell death after injury. That proposal rests on cell and animal work, and the card covers it.
59. What the mechanism story lacks is a bridge to the dose. With no identified active component, there is no way to say which molecule in the mixture produces which effect, no structure-activity relationship, and no way to measure drug exposure in a patient (inferred from the absence of an identified active substance). Pharmacokinetics in the ordinary sense cannot be done on an undefined mixture, which is why no half-life, distribution or clearance data exist for the product as such.
60. Free amino acids make up most of the mass and are not considered pharmacologically active [1] (reported). If that is right, the active fraction is a minority of what is injected, and its composition is controlled statistically rather than exactly.

### The human record, by indication [people]
61. Acute ischaemic stroke is the best-studied and the least encouraging. A 2023 Cochrane review covering seven randomised trials and 1,773 participants found no effect on all-cause death, with a risk ratio of 0.96 (95% CI 0.65 to 1.41), at moderate certainty [3] (verified). On harms it found the opposite of reassuring: non-fatal serious adverse events were more common on treatment, risk ratio 2.39 (95% CI 1.10 to 5.23) across three trials and 1,335 participants, and 2.87 (95% CI 1.24 to 6.69) in the subgroup given 30 mL for ten days [3] (verified).
62. The same review recorded that the manufacturer supported three of the multicentre studies, by funding them, supplying drug and placebo or randomisation codes, providing grants or supplying statisticians, and noted this may have affected design, conduct and reporting [3] (verified).
63. Vascular dementia reads better but weaker. A 2019 Cochrane review of six trials and 597 participants found benefit on pooled cognitive scales (standardised mean difference 0.36, 95% CI 0.13 to 0.58) and on global clinical response (risk ratio 2.69, 95% CI 1.82 to 3.98), with no difference in adverse events. Every key outcome was rated very low quality, the reviewers flagged high risk of bias and heterogeneity, warned the benefit may be too small to matter clinically, and recorded that all three trials with known funding were industry-supported [4] (verified).
64. Traumatic brain injury has the most favourable published result and the smallest numbers. A prospective meta-analysis of two phase 3b/4 placebo-controlled trials analysed 185 patients with admission Glasgow Coma Scale 6 to 12, given 50 mL a day for ten days then two further ten-day cycles at 10 mL. On a multidimensional ensemble of functional and neuropsychological scales it reported a small-to-medium effect favouring treatment at day 30 (standardised mean difference 0.31, p = 0.0156) and day 90 (0.34, p = 0.0146), with comparable safety [5] (verified). Note the endpoint design: a combined ensemble analysed with pre-ordered hypotheses rather than a single conventional outcome, which is a legitimate but unusual approach and one that makes comparison with other trials difficult (inferred).
65. Alzheimer's disease has meta-analyses reporting benefit on cognition and global change in mild to moderate disease, using 30 mL daily by infusion for four weeks to six months; an independent assessment judged several component trials small, the effects modest, one key Russian trial unreviewable because it was published only in Russian and never replicated, and the overall evidence insufficient to recommend routine use [2] (verified).

### Safety: a rare case where the harm signal is better quantified than the benefit [people]
66. Most sources describe it as well tolerated, with transient headache, dizziness, agitation, feeling hot and weight loss, and adverse event rates similar to placebo over courses up to three years [2] (verified).
67. Against that, the stroke review's finding of roughly 2.4-fold more non-fatal serious adverse events, at moderate certainty, is the single most statistically solid clinical result in the whole record, and it points the wrong way [3] (verified). It was also dose-related: the signal was strongest in the 30 mL arm. The honest summary is that in acute stroke the evidence probably shows no benefit on survival and a possible increase in serious non-fatal harm.
68. Separate from the molecule: it is given intravenously or intramuscularly in multi-week courses, and it is biological material of animal origin, so sterility and viral or bacterial contamination are live concerns rather than theoretical ones [2] (verified).

### Who produced the evidence [people]
69. The product has one manufacturer, and the manufacturer's involvement in the trial base is documented rather than suspected: funding, drug and placebo supply, randomisation codes, grants and statistical support across several multicentre studies, with Cochrane reviewers in two separate reviews noting industry support and its possible effect on reporting [3,4] (verified).
70. The trial network is also concentrated in a small group of investigators across Europe and Asia, with overlapping authorship between the stroke, dementia and brain injury programmes [5] (verified by authorship). That is normal for a single-sponsor drug and it means replication by unrelated groups is largely missing.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
71. Approved and marketed in many European, Asian, Latin American and post-Soviet countries for stroke, traumatic brain injury and dementia; not approved in the United States for anything [2] (verified). It is a prescription parenteral medicine where approved, administered in courses under supervision.
72. In sport, the substance is not named on the WADA Prohibited List. The route matters more than the molecule: intravenous infusions or injections totalling more than 100 mL per 12 hours outside hospital treatment, surgery or clinical investigation are a prohibited method, and clinical courses of 20 to 50 mL sit below that threshold while repeated same-day dosing could approach it (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
73. What is the active component? Without it there is no dose-response, no exposure measurement and no way to make a defined synthetic equivalent.
74. Why do the stroke and brain injury results diverge? Different injury type, different timing, different endpoint design and different trial sizes are all candidates, and nobody has reconciled them.
75. What drove the excess non-fatal serious adverse events in the stroke trials, and is it dose-related? The subgroup pattern suggests dose; the mechanism is unknown.
76. Would an independently funded, adequately powered trial reproduce the dementia and brain injury findings? Both Cochrane reviews asked for exactly that, and it has not happened.
77. What is in a non-manufacturer product sold under this name? Nothing links such material to the approved preparation or to any of the evidence above.
