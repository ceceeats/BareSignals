# Thymosin Beta-4
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/thymosin-beta-4
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: tb-500 tb500 tb4 thymosin beta-4 full, Tβ4, TMSB4X (gene)
2. Origin: Made by the body
3. Sequence: SDKPDMAEIEKFDKSKLKKTETQEKNPLPSKETIEQEKQAGES (43 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Investigational; the eye program is furthest along. Injectable use is unapproved and WADA-banned (S2).

## Summary
5. Thymosin beta-4 is a protein made by nearly every cell that holds actin ready to rebuild the cell's scaffolding. It is investigated for wound healing, heart repair and eye-surface disease, with reliable repair in animal studies. Human eye trials disappointed, and grey-market TB-500 is often this full molecule, used for tendon and muscle injury recovery.
6. Punch line: The full repair protein behind TB-500. Weekly shots for tendon and muscle injuries; slower, whole-body healing.
7. Used for: Injury and soft tissue repair

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Tissue repair: Keeps cells ready to rebuild
9. Inflammatory signaling: Lowers inflammation signals
10. Cellular protection: Helps heart cells survive in mice
11. Pain & sensory: Less injury pain reported
12. Heart & vessels (backed only by animal or user reports): Heart repair in mice
13. Bone & cartilage (backed only by animal or user reports): Joint recovery reported
14. Immunity (backed only by animal or user reports): Immune signalling studied in animals

## How it works
15. Holds actin ready for cell rebuilding · G-actin sequestering · role Tissue repair
   Inside nearly every cell, Tβ4 binds single actin units (G-actin) and keeps them in reserve. When a cell has to move or reshape, that stock is released to build new filaments fast. This is its best-established job, shown in biochemistry and cell work.
16. Moves repair cells into wounds · Cell migration · role Tissue repair
   Outside cells, Tβ4 promotes migration of endothelial and skin cells, the ones that line vessels and close wounds. Repair cells reach damaged tissue faster, so wounds close sooner. This is reliable in animal wound and cornea models, but eye drop trials in people missed their main endpoints.
17. Grows new blood vessels · Angiogenesis · role Tissue repair
   Tβ4 promotes angiogenesis, the growth of new blood vessels into healing tissue. Better blood supply brings oxygen and repair cells to the injury. This is shown in animal wound and heart models.
18. Lowers inflammatory signals · NF-κB, TNF-α down · role Inflammatory signaling
   Tβ4 lowers NF-κB activation and TNF-α, central drivers of inflammation. A calmer injury site can heal with less secondary damage. This is shown in cell and animal studies.
19. Helps heart muscle survive injury, in animals · ILK, Akt survival · role Heart & vessels
   In heart injury models, Tβ4 activates ILK (integrin-linked kinase) and Akt, survival signals inside heart muscle cells. More cells survive the injury. This is shown in animals, not in people.
20. Releases an anti-scarring fragment · Ac-SDKP cleavage · role Cellular protection
   The front end of Tβ4 can be cut to release Ac-SDKP, a tetrapeptide that limits fibrosis. This adds an anti-scarring effect to the repair process. The seven-residue TB-500 fragment sold on the grey market lacks this end.
21. Mechanism overview: Tβ4 is a 43 amino acid protein, the main G-actin sequestering molecule in cells. By holding actin monomers, it controls how quickly cells can remodel their cytoskeleton and migrate. Outside cells it promotes endothelial and skin cell migration and angiogenesis, and lowers NF-κB and TNF-α signalling. In heart injury models it activates ILK and Akt, improving heart muscle cell survival. Its N-terminus is cleaved to release Ac-SDKP, an anti-scarring tetrapeptide. Together these explain the reliable wound, cornea and heart repair in animals.

Grey-market TB-500 is often this full molecule, used by people for tendon and muscle injury recovery.

## Who shouldn't take it
50. People with active or recent cancer: Tβ4 drives new blood vessels and cell migration; high levels track with tumour spread. (reported)
51. Drug-tested athletes: Thymosin beta-4 and TB-500 are named on the WADA list; doping cases exist. (verified)
52. People with proliferative retinopathy or vascular malformations: Pro-angiogenic peptides could feed abnormal vessel growth. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
53. BPC-157: BPC-157 works on growth signalling and blood flow at the injury, while Tβ4 moves repair cells in and supplies the actin they rebuild with. People stack them for tendon and muscle injuries and they are sold pre-mixed as BPC-157 + TB-500 and in several wider blends, but the pair has not been tested together in people.
54. KPV: Tβ4 handles cell movement, vessel growth and actin supply for repair, while KPV turns down inflammation inside gut and immune cells. They are sold pre-mixed in KLOW and Tβ4 + BPC + KPV. The pair has not been tested together in people.
55. Cartalax: Cartalax is claimed by the Khavinson group to tune connective tissue genes in cartilage, while Tβ4 brings repair cells and new vessels. They are sold pre-mixed as Cartalax + Tβ4 + BPC-157 for joints. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
56. Named for the thymus, where it was found in 1981, though nearly every cell makes it and platelets carry the most. Its job is holding actin, the cell's scaffolding, ready to rebuild. Its start carries a natural acetyl cap.
57. First studied for: Wound healing, heart repair and eye-surface disease.
58. Name story: 'Beta' means it sat in the near-neutral charge band of calf-thymus fraction 5, not that it is related to the alpha thymosins. 'TB-500' is just market shorthand for 'thymosin beta'; strictly it names the 17–23 fragment. [S9] (verified)
59. 1981: Found in the thymus; later seen in nearly every cell
60. 2000s: Strong animal data for heart, wound and corneal repair
61. 2010: IV safety study in healthy volunteers
62. 2025: Eye-drop Phase 3s miss; SEER-1 stopped early, SEER-3 missed
63. Today: Investigational eye drug, SEER-2 due late 2026; injectable use WADA-banned

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Two different molecules, one market name [lab]
64. This is the thing to settle before anything else, because almost every confused claim about this compound starts here. The full protein is 43 residues. The material nominated and sold as TB-500 is a seven-residue fragment, residues 17 to 23, carrying an acetyl group on its front end. Reviewing them for the US compounding list, the FDA stated plainly that TB-500 and thymosin beta-4 are not the same substance [1] (verified).
65. That has a practical consequence that cuts against the usual reassurance. People often say grey-market TB-500 is "actually the full molecule, which is better studied". Sometimes it is. But the two have different evidence bases, different stability, different molecular weights and different regulatory records, and a label naming one while containing the other is a mislabelling either way. The free base fragment is listed at 889.01 g/mol under one CAS number, the acetate at 949.1 under another, and the nomination's own stated identifier matched neither of them [1] (verified).
66. The identity confusion was not limited to buyers. In the nomination the agency reviewed, the substance named was the free base while the submitted certificate of analysis was for the acetate salt, listing the acetate's CAS number, formula and molecular weight [1]. Like the other peptides in this group, TB-500 is a common name rather than an approved generic name, which the agency flagged as a patient safety risk because there is no reference standard to test a batch against [1] (verified).

### What was actually nominated, and what the paperwork showed [lab]
67. The nominated product was a 3 mg per millilitre lyophilised powder for subcutaneous and intramuscular injection, for wound healing [1] (verified). That is the specific claim the agency evaluated, not recovery, not tendon repair.
68. For the free base, no certificate of analysis was submitted at all. A publicly available certificate tested appearance, identity by mass spectrometry and HPLC, and peptide purity, and did not test assay, impurities, bacterial endotoxins or aggregates [1]. For the acetate, the supplied certificate was more thorough, covering appearance, solubility, amino acid composition, water, acetate content, impurity limits of 2.0% total and 1.0% for the largest single impurity, and purity, but it did not report identification, assay, aggregates or bacterial endotoxins [1] (verified).
69. As with the other peptides in this review round, the impurity limits leave impurities of unknown identity, and the agency's concern is that unidentified impurities and aggregation can drive immune responses to an injected peptide. It judged injectable routes to pose a significant immunogenicity risk on that basis, with no immunogenicity studies available either way [1] (verified). Both forms were concluded to be not physically and chemically well characterised.
70. Storage is cold: below minus 20 degrees Celsius for the free base, below minus 15 for the acetate [1].

### Mechanism, and which molecule each step was shown with [mixed]
71. The actin story belongs to the full protein. Holding actin monomers in reserve is what sets how fast a cell can rearrange its own scaffolding and crawl, which is the common root of the wound, cornea and heart findings. Everything downstream of that is a consequence of cells being able to move and survive, not of a receptor being switched on.
72. The heart survival step came from a 2004 paper in Nature working in mouse cardiac tissue and cells, which reported that the full protein activates integrin-linked kinase and promotes cardiac cell migration and survival after injury [2] (verified, animals and cells). This is the most cited mechanistic result in the field and it was done with the 43-residue protein.
73. Here is where the fragment's own record gets uncomfortable. The wound-healing data most often invoked for TB-500 came from a 2003 study using the non-acetylated seven-residue sequence applied topically to aged mice. The agency's position is that this cannot be extrapolated to the acetylated injected fragment, because it differs in both the molecule and the route [1] (verified).
74. And when the fragment itself was tested directly, in a 2024 in vitro fibroblast scratch assay, TB-500 free base at 50 micrograms per millilitre did not significantly promote wound closure. A metabolite, the N-acetylated five-residue fragment, showed a small but statistically significant effect at the same concentration [1] (verified, cells). A negative result in a simple assay at a high concentration is not the end of the question, but it is the only direct test of the marketed fragment's core claim, and it did not support it.
75. Pharmacokinetics for the fragment amount to one animal study in a different species: horses given 10 mg subcutaneously reached plasma peaks of 0.05 and 0.08 nanograms per millilitre [1]. There is no intramuscular pharmacokinetic data, and none in humans, for either the fragment or by any route [1] (verified).

### The human record for the full protein [people]
76. Systemic human exposure exists, and it was well tolerated. A Phase 1 programme on an injectable formulation of the full protein ran a double-blind, placebo-controlled single ascending intravenous dose stage in 40 healthy volunteers and a 14-day dosing stage in a further 40, reporting no adverse events judged related to the drug [3] (verified). Doses and pharmacokinetics were not reported in the public announcement, which is a real limitation when that study is cited as the safety basis for injected use.
77. A separate first-in-human randomised, double-blind single- and multiple-dose Phase 1 study of recombinant human thymosin beta-4 in healthy volunteers has been published [4] (reported). Together these establish that intravenous dosing of the full protein has been tolerated in small, short, monitored settings. They do not establish anything about subcutaneous dosing of a seven-residue fragment over weeks, which is the actual pattern of use.
78. For the fragment, the agency found no clinical studies, no human exposure data and no human pharmacokinetic data by any route [1]. Its adverse event database returned nothing, and a separate complaint system returned two cases involving a blended fragment-and-BPC-157 product with no safety assessment attached [1] (verified). There is no established human dose for the fragment, and no study from which one could be derived.
79. The eye programme is where the full protein has been tested properly, and it is the most informative part of the record precisely because it is rigorous. The third pivotal trial in neurotrophic keratitis completed its last patient visit in August 2026 and entered data cleaning and analysis, run across 50 sites, 41 in the United States and 9 across Italy, Spain and Poland, with topline results aimed for November 2026 [5,6] (reported). Given that the earlier pivotal trials in this programme did not deliver a clear win, this readout is the single most consequential pending result for the molecule, in either form.

### Safety: an absence rather than a finding [lab]
80. For the fragment, the toxicology section of the record is empty. No acute toxicity studies, no repeat-dose studies, no genotoxicity studies, no developmental or reproductive studies and no carcinogenicity studies were submitted or identified for either the free base or the acetate [1] (verified).
81. That is a different situation from a compound that has been tested and raised concerns. It means the ordinary questions, what dose causes harm, what organ is affected first, whether it damages DNA, whether it affects pregnancy, whether it causes tumours, have not been asked of this molecule at all. The agency noted the absence of carcinogenicity data without drawing a conclusion from it [1] (verified).
82. The widely repeated line that this peptide has an excellent safety profile with no reported side effects is, read against the record, a statement about the absence of studies and of surveillance reports rather than about the presence of safety data. Both readings produce the same sentence; only one of them is informative.
83. One consideration specific to the biology is worth stating carefully. A molecule whose job is to make cells migrate and survive is, in principle, acting on capabilities that matter in tumour spread as well as in repair. No study has examined this for either form (speculated). It is an argument from mechanism, not an observation.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
84. The agency proposed not adding either the free base or the acetate to the 503A compounding bulks list. Its reasons were, in order: neither substance is well characterised; there is no history of use in compounding; there is no evidence of effectiveness for wound healing; there is no human safety data; injectable routes carry immunogenicity risk; and approved wound therapies already exist [1] (verified).
85. The advisory committee recommended adding it anyway, voting 8 to 6 with one abstention on 23 July 2026, for wound healing [7,8]. The same committee recommended BPC-157 and KPV by the same margin, against the same staff opposition [8,9].
86. That recommendation is advisory and changes nothing by itself. Adding the substance requires formal notice-and-comment rulemaking, which has not begun and carries no deadline [7] (verified). The interim path under consideration is an "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides", sent to the White House budget office for review on 14 October 2026 and unpublished as of mid-October, with its covered substances and conditions not public [7] (verified). It would be enforcement discretion rather than approval, and could be withdrawn without rulemaking.
87. In sport, injectable use of either form is prohibited under the peptide hormones and growth factors class. That is a straightforward position and has not changed.

### Open questions [mixed]
88. Does the acetylated seven-residue fragment do anything? The one direct test of it against its own nominated indication was negative, and the positive wound data belongs to a different molecule applied a different way. This is the field's central unexamined assumption.
89. If the fragment works through a metabolite, as the 2024 assay faintly suggested, then dose, timing and route logic all change, and none of the existing conventions were built on that premise.
90. What is in the vial, the fragment or the full protein? There is no routine identity testing that distinguishes them in grey-market supply, and the two are regulated and evidenced separately.
91. Does the full protein work in the eye? The November 2026 readout will largely answer this, and it is the only well-powered test of the molecule in humans anywhere.
92. Does intravenous tolerability in 80 monitored volunteers over 14 days say anything about repeated subcutaneous dosing over months? Nothing in the record connects the two.
93. Everything about long-term safety, in both forms. There is no toxicology at all for the fragment, which makes this less an open question than an unopened one.
