# Cartalax
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/cartalax
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, AED, Ala-Glu-Asp
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: AED (3 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Cartalax is a lab-made tripeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at cartilage, bone and connective tissue. Users take it for joints and joint pain, and it is claimed to tune connective tissue genes and slow aging in cartilage cells. Evidence is Russian animal work with no independent trials, and first-hand reports are few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson's cartilage tripeptide. Taken for aging joints and joint pain; claimed to reset connective tissue genes.
7. Used for: Joints and cartilage

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Bone & cartilage: Cartilage support
9. Aging & senescence: Joint aging
10. Pain & sensory: Less joint pain reported

## How it works
11. Claimed to tune connective tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Bone & cartilage
   The Khavinson group says this tripeptide acts inside connective tissue cells and shifts gene expression, working at the DNA level rather than through a receptor. The claimed result is better upkeep of cartilage, bone and joint tissue with age. This comes from Russian animal work and has not been independently replicated.
12. Claimed to slow aging in cartilage cells · Chondrocyte senescence · role Aging & senescence
   Chondrocytes are the only cells that build and maintain joint cartilage, and with age they drift into senescence, a state where they stop dividing and repair poorly. The group claims Cartalax slows that shift, which would help cartilage hold up in aging joints. Support is from Russian animal studies only.
13. Mechanism overview: Cartalax is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide for connective tissue. The group claims it tunes gene expression in cartilage and slows senescence in chondrocytes, the cells that maintain joint cartilage. These claims come from one group without independent replication. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## What has been tested
29. Tissue repair: Cartilage and connective tissue (animal)

31. Russian studies claim connective-tissue-regulating effects; no independent trials.
32. What it helps: Russian animal work reports support for cartilage, connective tissue, bone and joints in aging. There are no independent trials and no Western validation. Users take it for joints and joint pain, but first-hand reports are few. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
33. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S3] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
34. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
35. People treating a diagnosed joint disease with it: No controlled trials show benefit; delays proven care. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
36. Thymosin Beta-4: Cartalax is claimed to tune gene activity in cartilage cells, while Tβ4 moves repair cells into damaged tissue and lowers inflammatory signals. People combine them for joint and connective tissue repair, and they are sold pre-mixed in Cartalax + Tβ4 + BPC-157, but the pair has not been tested together.
37. BPC-157: BPC-157 grows new blood vessels and draws repair cells into injured tendon and joint tissue, while Cartalax is claimed to work on the cartilage cells themselves. They are sold pre-mixed in Cartalax + Tβ4 + BPC-157. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
38. A tripeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at cartilage and bone/connective tissue.
39. First studied for: Supporting cartilage, bone and joint repair in aging.
40. Name story: Cartilage plus 'lax', from the Khavinson bioregulator line; AED (Ala-Glu-Asp) is linked to cartilage and skin fibroblast function. It is Epitalon (AEDG) minus the final glycine. [S4] (inferred)
41. 1990s to 2000s: Ala-Glu-Asp tripeptide from the Khavinson program
42. Today: A cartilage and joint bioregulator
