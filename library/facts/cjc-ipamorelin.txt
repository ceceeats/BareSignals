# CJC-1295 no DAC + Ipamorelin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/cjc-ipamorelin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: CJC/Ipa, Mod GRF 1-29 + Ipamorelin, CJC-1295 no DAC/Ipamorelin 5/5, CJC + Ipa blend
2. Origin: Blend of 2 peptides
3. Legal and approval status: US: neither half approved. Both sit on FDA's Category 2 safety-risk list (ipamorelin added Sep 2023); the PCAC voted against both in Oct and Dec 2024, and neither was among the peptides removed in April 2026. Sold as a research chemical. WADA: prohibited (S2).

## Summary
4. This blend pairs CJC-1295 without DAC, a GHRH analogue, with ipamorelin, a selective ghrelin-receptor agonist. People combine them because pressing both pituitary buttons releases far more growth hormone than either alone, and they use it for sleep, recovery and body composition. The synergy is shown in humans, but no trial tested this pair for those outcomes.
5. Punch line: The gold-standard GH stack. Presses both pituitary buttons for one bigger pulse; often a single shot before bed.
6. Used for: GH release: sleep, recovery, body composition

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Endocrine & growth: Two inputs, one bigger GH pulse
8. Sleep & rhythms: Deeper sleep, vivid dreams
9. Muscle & performance: Recovery and lean mass, reported
10. Fat metabolism: Slow fat loss over months
11. Skin & aesthetics (backed only by animal or user reports): Skin quality, reported
12. Tissue repair (backed only by animal or user reports): Soreness and recovery, reported

## How it works
13. Presses the GHRH button on the pituitary · GHRH-R, cAMP · role Endocrine & growth
   Mod GRF 1-29 binds the GHRH receptor on pituitary somatotroph cells and raises cAMP inside them. That triggers release of the GH those cells have stored. Each half raising GH is shown in humans, and this is the GHRH route.
14. Lasts about 30 minutes, keeps the natural pulse · Mod GRF 1-29, 4 substitutions · role Endocrine & growth
   Four substitutions, including Asn8 to Gln and Met27 to Leu, slow enzyme breakdown, but without DAC it still clears in about 30 minutes. That gives a short spike that mimics the body's own GH pulse rather than a constant rise. Keeping the pulse pattern is the reason for choosing the no DAC form.
15. Presses the ghrelin button, a second route · GHS-R1a, PLC and calcium · role Endocrine & growth
   Ipamorelin binds GHS-R1a, the ghrelin receptor, which signals through phospholipase C and calcium. This is a second, independent way to make the pituitary release GH. Its GH release is shown in rats and pigs, and it was tried in people after bowel surgery.
16. Releases GH without cortisol or prolactin spikes · Selective GHS, animal data · role Sleep & rhythms
   In animals ipamorelin released GH without raising ACTH, cortisol or prolactin, unlike GHRP-2 and GHRP-6. That selectivity is why it is chosen for this pair, aiming at sleep and recovery without stress hormone or hunger spikes. Human selectivity data are limited.
17. Together, a bigger pulse than either alone · GHRH + GHRP synergy · role Muscle & performance
   Because the GHRH and ghrelin routes act through separate receptors, together they release more GH than either alone. This synergy was shown in normal men in 1990 using GHRP-6. No trial has measured this exact pair or its effect on body composition, sleep or recovery.
18. Mechanism overview: Mod GRF 1-29 is the first 29 residues of GHRH with four substitutions (including Asn8 to Gln and Met27 to Leu) that slow enzyme breakdown; it binds GHRH receptors on somatotrophs and raises cAMP for a short, pulse-like GH release. Ipamorelin (Aib-His-D-2Nal-D-Phe-Lys-NH2) binds GHS-R1a, signalling through phospholipase C and calcium, and in animals releases GH without raising ACTH, cortisol or prolactin. Because the two pathways are independent, GHRH plus GHRP produces a synergistic GH pulse, shown with GHRP-6 in normal men in 1990.

## What is inside the blend
19. CJC-1295 no DAC (Mod GRF 1-29): 5 mg · GHRH signal: sets pulse size
20. Ipamorelin: 5 mg · Ghrelin-receptor signal: triggers the pulse, selectively
21. Most common ratio: CJC-1295 no DAC 5 · Ipamorelin 5 = 10 mg (15 of 15 listings (7 state 5/5, 8 sell a 10 mg vial))
22. Other ratio seen: CJC-1295 no DAC 10 · Ipamorelin 10 = 20 mg (5 listings, always as a second size)
23. Version Common retail: CJC-1295 no DAC 5 · Ipamorelin 5 = 10 mg
24. Why CJC-1295 no DAC is in it: Amplifies the pulse through the GHRH route; ipamorelin alone gives a smaller pulse
25. Why Ipamorelin is in it: Adds the ghrelin route without the cortisol, prolactin and hunger of GHRP-2 or GHRP-6
26. Mixing chemistry: The most forgiving pair on this list: Mod GRF lost native Met27 and Asn8, the two sites most prone to oxidation and deamidation.
27. Mixing chemistry: Ipamorelin is a short pentapeptide with no Met or Cys; little to oxidise.
28. Mixing chemistry: No published stability study of the reconstituted blend; community claims of 90 days are unsourced. Most guides say 2 to 3 weeks refrigerated.
29. Mixing chemistry: FDA flagged ipamorelin as sensitive to formulation and prone to aggregation, an immunogenicity concern; aggregates can form without cloudiness.
30. Mixing chemistry: Discard if cloudy, gelled or particulate.
31. Self-mixing: Drawing both separate vials into one syringe right before injecting is long-standing practice and low risk; pre-mixing them into one vial for weeks has no stability data, and separate vials let you change the ratio.

33. Half-life: per component
34. Most people take: Sized by the CJC dose: 100 mcg of each per shot. A 10 mg (5/5) vial in 2 mL gives 5 mg/mL, so 4 units = 100 + 100 mcg and 8 units = the 200 + 200 mcg pre-bed dose. Fasted 1 to 2 hours before, no food for 30 to 60 minutes after.

Side effects reported: hand and foot tingling, water retention, flushing, hunger spikes, headache, rising fasting glucose.

## What has been tested
48. Endocrine & growth: GHRH + GHRP synergy, normal men (human)
49. Endocrine & growth: CJC-1295 DAC, GH and IGF-1 rise (human)
50. Endocrine & growth: Ipamorelin GH release, rats and pigs (animal)
51. Digestive & gut: Ipamorelin IV after bowel surgery (human)
52. Sleep & rhythms: Deeper sleep, vivid dreams (users)
53. Never tested as a pair in a trial. Each half has human data: CJC-1295 (the DAC version) raised GH 2 to 10 fold and IGF-1 1.5 to 3 fold in healthy adults; ipamorelin released GH without cortisol or prolactin in animals and was tried IV after bowel surgery. GHRH plus GHRP synergy was shown in normal men in 1990.
54. What it helps: Evidence is mechanistic plus user reports. Synergy of the two signal types is shown in humans; each component raises GH, and the DAC form of CJC-1295 raised IGF-1 1.5 to 3 fold. No trial has measured body composition, sleep or recovery with this pair. Users report better sleep first, then recovery, then slow body composition change over months.

## Who shouldn't take it
55. Pituitary tumour, pituitary surgery or brain radiation: Works by pushing the pituitary; disease there makes effects unpredictable. (inferred)
56. People with active or recent cancer: Raises GH and IGF-1, which promote cell growth. (inferred)
57. People with prediabetes or poorly controlled diabetes: GH raises blood glucose and insulin resistance. (inferred)
58. Drug-tested athletes: GHRH analogues are banned under WADA S2. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
59. Sermorelin: Sermorelin is another GHRH analogue on the same receptor as the CJC-1295 half. Adding it duplicates that route rather than adding a new one, so people usually swap it in. The combination has not been tested.
60. Tesamorelin: Tesamorelin is a stabilised GHRH with human trial data for visceral and liver fat. It overlaps the CJC-1295 half, so it is used instead of, or rotated with, this blend for fat loss. The pair has not been tested together.
61. BPC-157: BPC-157 works on vessel growth and tendon repair and raises growth hormone receptor on tendon cells, while the blend raises GH pulses. People pair them for injury recovery. This is user logic, not trial data.
62. GHRP-2: GHRP-2 hits the same ghrelin receptor as ipamorelin but with more hunger, prolactin and cortisol. Adding it duplicates the ghrelin route and loses the selectivity ipamorelin was chosen for. The pairing has not been tested.
63. MK-677: MK-677 is an oral ghrelin mimic that raises GH all day, while this blend gives short pulses. People combine them for higher IGF-1, at the cost of hunger and blunting the pulse pattern. The combination has not been tested.

## Where it comes from
64. A one-vial pairing of the two most used growth hormone releasers: CJC-1295 without DAC (Mod GRF 1-29, a GHRH analogue) and ipamorelin (a selective ghrelin-receptor agonist). Sold as 5 mg of each in one vial.
65. First studied for: The pairing comes from 1990 work showing a GHRH signal and a GHRP signal together release far more growth hormone than either alone. The community turned that into a daily stack, then sellers pre-mixed it.
66. Name story: Sold as "CJC-1295 no DAC", but the molecule is Mod GRF 1-29 (tetrasubstituted GRF 1-29). The name CJC-1295 belongs to the DAC version, which carries a drug affinity complex that binds albumin and lasts about a week. Check the label: the dosing is completely different. [S1] (verified)
67. 1990: GHRP plus GHRH shown to release GH synergistically in normal men
68. 1998: Ipamorelin described as the first selective GH secretagogue
69. 2006: CJC-1295 (DAC) raises GH and IGF-1 for days in healthy adults
70. 2023: Ipamorelin added to FDA Category 2
71. 2024: PCAC votes against ipamorelin (Oct) and CJC-1295 (Dec)
72. 2026: Blend testing offered by Finnrick; both still Category 2
73. Today: Most sold GH secretagogue blend, 5 + 5 mg

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### A solubility problem that undercuts the product itself [lab]
74. This is the most concrete finding in the regulatory record for this blend, and it is a chemistry problem rather than an evidence problem.
75. Reviewing the ghrelin-receptor half for the US compounding list, regulators recorded the free base's water solubility as 0.0032 milligrams per millilitre, against a nominated product strength of 2 milligrams per millilitre, and questioned whether that strength is achievable at all [1] (verified). The acetate salt's solubility is 5 milligrams per millilitre [1].
76. That is a gap of roughly six hundred-fold between what the free base dissolves to in water and what the proposed product claims to contain. The implication is direct: a product at that strength is either made from the acetate salt, or formulated with something other than water, or not fully in solution (inferred from the cited figures).
77. It also means the two salt forms are not interchangeable here in any practical sense, and regulators found the nominators inconsistent about which one they meant, with certificates and stated identifiers not matching the named substance [1] (verified). Neither form has an approved generic name, which regulators treat as a safety issue because there is no reference standard to check a batch against.
78. Storage differs between the forms too: the free base is described as stable at room temperature for about three weeks and recommended below minus 18 degrees Celsius, while the acetate is listed at 2 to 8 degrees with stability up to four years at minus 20 [1].

### The one controlled trial of the ghrelin-receptor half, which failed [people]
79. There is a controlled human efficacy trial here, which is more than most peptides in this catalog have, and it did not succeed.
80. A Phase 2 randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in postoperative ileus, the slowed gut that follows abdominal surgery, enrolled 117 and randomised 114: 56 to the peptide at 0.03 mg per kilogram intravenously twice daily, 58 to placebo [1] (verified). Median time to first tolerated meal was 25.3 hours against 32.6 hours.
81. That difference was not statistically significant, and the secondary endpoints showed no difference [1] (verified). Development was subsequently discontinued [1].
82. A seven-hour numerical advantage that fails significance in a 114-person trial is the classic shape of either a small real effect or noise, and the sponsor's decision to stop tells you how they read it. Either way, the only controlled test of this molecule for any indication did not meet its endpoint.
83. Regulators also found no clinical trials at all for growth hormone deficiency, the other indication they evaluated [1] (verified). So the compound most associated with raising growth hormone has never been tested in a trial for a growth hormone condition.

### What is known about exposure, and what that implies about route [mixed]
84. Human pharmacokinetics exist for the ghrelin-receptor half, from a 1999 study in 48 healthy males across five intravenous dose groups of six, with two placebo subjects per group, spanning roughly a thirty-fold dose range. Half-life was about two hours, clearance 0.078 litres per hour per kilogram, volume of distribution 0.22 litres per kilogram. No effectiveness data and no adverse events were reported [1] (verified).
85. The important absence: there is no subcutaneous pharmacokinetic data, in any species [1] (verified). Rat intravenous data gives a half-life of about 27 minutes with 60 to 80% excreted unchanged [1]. So the only route with human exposure data is intravenous, and the only route in use is subcutaneous.
86. For the growth-hormone-releasing half, the identity question shapes what evidence applies. The human data people cite, a several-fold rise in growth hormone and a 1.5 to 3-fold rise in insulin-like growth factor 1 lasting days, belongs to the version carrying an albumin-binding complex. The material sold under that name without the complex is a different molecule with a short duration of action and a different dosing logic entirely (verified, as the card's naming note records). Regulators evaluated five separate substances under this name family, covering both the complexed and uncomplexed forms as free bases and salts [2] (verified).
87. The synergy premise, that a releasing-hormone signal and a ghrelin-receptor signal together produce a larger growth hormone pulse than either alone, was demonstrated in humans in 1990 using a different ghrelin-receptor peptide from the one in this blend (verified, as the card records). Whether it holds for this specific pair at the amounts used has not been tested (verified as absent).

### Two concerns regulators raised that are specific to this mechanism [mixed]
88. First, abuse potential. Ghrelin receptors are present in reward-related brain regions, and regulators explicitly raised the possibility that the ghrelin-receptor half may have reinforcing properties [1] (verified). This is not a concern that applies to the repair peptides, and it is rarely mentioned in discussion of this blend. It has not been studied in humans.
89. Second, reproductive effects. No reproductive or developmental studies of the compound itself exist. Regulators cited a mouse study of ghrelin and a ghrelin antagonist showing effects on fertilisation and embryofetal development, and stated this may apply here [1] (verified, as a read-across rather than a direct finding).
90. Beyond those, the toxicology is empty: no acute toxicity, no repeat-dose toxicity, no genotoxicity and no carcinogenicity studies were found for the ghrelin-receptor half [1] (verified). Regulators concluded the nonclinical data were too limited to assess safety for clinical use.
91. Adverse event reporting amounts to two non-serious cases involving compounded products, one a nasal spray with headache and increased tearing, the other a subcutaneous combination with joint pain [1] (verified). As with every blend in this catalog, the combination case cannot be attributed to either ingredient.
92. Immunogenicity could not be ruled out from unidentified impurities or aggregates, and regulators noted injectable routes present a particular risk for both forms [1].

### Where the law stands, October 2026, and why this blend sits differently from the others [people]
93. This is the one blend in the catalog whose components have been through the advisory process and lost.
94. Regulators proposed that neither form of the ghrelin-receptor half be added to the 503A bulks list, on all four criteria: neither is well characterised, with critical data on impurities, aggregates and endotoxins missing; effectiveness evidence is limited for any route with nothing supporting the subcutaneous route for either indication reviewed; professional guidelines do not mention it for either condition; approved alternatives exist for both; and the nonclinical data cannot support a safety assessment [1] (verified).
95. The committee agreed. It voted against the ghrelin-receptor half in October 2024 and against the releasing-hormone half in December 2024, in both cases unanimously or near-unanimously (verified, as the card records the tallies). The December 2024 session covered five substances in the releasing-hormone name family, with the agency proposing that none be included [2] (verified).
96. Neither component was among the twelve peptides removed from the do-not-compound category in April 2026, and both remain on it (verified, as the card records). That is a materially different position from the repair peptides, which left that category and then received favourable advisory recommendations in July 2026.
97. So while the broader regulatory direction in 2026 has been toward opening access, these two moved the other way and stayed there. Any interim access policy would have to cover substances the agency and its committee have both already declined (inferred).
98. In sport, releasing-hormone analogues and growth hormone secretagogues are prohibited.

### Open questions [mixed]
99. Can the nominated strength even be made from the free base? The cited solubility figure and the claimed concentration differ by roughly six hundred-fold, which makes this a question about what is physically in the vial.
100. Which molecule is the releasing-hormone half, complexed or not? The dosing logic and duration differ completely, and the human growth hormone data belongs to the complexed version.
101. Does the 1990 synergy finding hold for this specific pair? It was shown with a different ghrelin-receptor peptide, and the premise of the whole product rests on it transferring.
102. What does subcutaneous dosing actually produce? There is no subcutaneous pharmacokinetic data in any species for the ghrelin-receptor half, and that is the only route in use.
103. Is there reinforcing potential? Regulators raised it from receptor distribution, and nobody has looked.
104. What are the reproductive effects? None have been studied; the concern is a read-across from ghrelin biology.
105. Does raising growth hormone and insulin-like growth factor 1 over months do anything measurable to body composition in healthy adults? No trial has measured it with this pair, and the reported effects are sequenced exactly as expectation would predict.
