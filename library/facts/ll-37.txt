# LL-37
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ll-37
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: FALL-39 (original name), cathelicidin, hCAP-18 (precursor protein), CAMP (gene)
2. Origin: Fragment of a natural protein
3. Sequence: LLGDFFRKSKEKIGKEFKRIVQRIKDFLRNLVPRTES (37 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Research chemical, no approval. On the FDA compounding advisory agenda for a meeting due before end of February 2027.

## Summary
5. LL-37 is the only human cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide, a 37-residue chain that is part of innate immunity. It is studied as a natural antibiotic and wound-healing agent because it kills bacteria, breaks up biofilms and speeds wound closure in models. Its strongest results are in cells and animals, and too much of it is linked to rosacea and psoriasis, so injected use is unstudied.
6. Punch line: The body's only cathelicidin, a natural antibiotic. Kills bacteria and breaks biofilms in models; excess inflames.
7. Used for: Immune support, infections

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Immunity: Natural antibiotic peptide
9. Inflammatory signaling: Too much drives inflammation
10. Tissue repair: Helps wounds close
11. Organ function: Defends skin, lungs and gut

## How it works
12. Punches holes in bacterial membranes · Membrane disruption · role Immunity
   LL-37 is a positively charged spiral peptide that slots into the negatively charged membranes of bacteria and tears them open. Human cell membranes carry less negative charge, which gives it some selectivity. Broad killing is shown in cell and animal work.
13. Calls in and tunes immune cells · FPR2, TLR signalling · role Immunity
   LL-37 also signals to the host. Through FPR2, a receptor on immune cells, it calls in neutrophils and other cells, and it tunes TLR signalling, the germ-sensing system. These effects come mostly from cell and animal studies.
14. Speeds wound closure · Keratinocyte migration · role Tissue repair
   LL-37 drives keratinocytes, the main skin cells, to migrate across a wound and close it. Faster wound closure is shown in animal models, and early human wound studies exist but are limited.
15. Neutralises bacterial endotoxin · LPS binding · role Inflammatory signaling
   LL-37 binds LPS, the endotoxin on the outer wall of some bacteria, so it cannot trigger TLR4 and set off a strong inflammatory reaction. This dampening is shown in cell and animal work.
16. Breaks up bacterial biofilms · Biofilm disruption · role Immunity
   Biofilms are sticky colonies that shield bacteria from antibiotics and immune attack. LL-37 breaks them up in lab models. This is cell and animal evidence, and it has not been shown for injected use in people.
17. Drives skin inflammation in psoriasis · Self-DNA, pDC activation · role Inflammatory signaling
   LL-37 binds the body's own DNA released from damaged cells, which lets plasmacytoid dendritic cells sense it and pour out interferon. In people, excess LL-37 is tied to psoriasis and rosacea. This is the clearest human finding about it.
18. Mechanism overview: LL-37 is the only human cathelicidin, cut from its precursor hCAP18 by proteases in skin and neutrophils. Its positive, amphipathic helix inserts into negatively charged bacterial membranes and tears them open, and it binds bacterial LPS so endotoxin cannot trigger TLR4. It also signals to the host. Through FPR2 it recruits immune cells, and it drives keratinocyte migration to close wounds. The same activity has a cost. LL-37 complexes with self-DNA, pushing plasmacytoid dendritic cells to make interferon, which feeds psoriasis and rosacea.

## What has been tested
36. Immunity: Direct antimicrobial action (cell)
37. Tissue repair: Wound healing (animal)
38. Inflammatory signaling: Rosacea and psoriasis links (human)
39. Tissue repair: Early wound and infection studies (human)
40. Extensive cell and animal work: direct antimicrobial action, wound healing, immune modulation; a role in some skin diseases (rosacea) where it is over-expressed. Early human wound and infection studies exist.
41. What it helps: Its strongest results are in cells and animals: broad killing of bacteria, biofilm breakup and faster wound closure in models. Human data link it to skin disease more than treatment, with excess LL-37 tied to rosacea and psoriasis. Early human wound and infection studies exist. Injected use is unstudied, and user reports centre on chronic infection, gut and immune problems, with mixed results.
42. Trial: none · Phase 2 · venous leg ulcers and diabetic foot ulcers. Last trials were a Swedish Phase 2b for leg ulcers (ropocamptide, 2022) and an Indonesian diabetic foot ulcer cream study published 2023; nothing active now. (checked 2026-10-23) [S3] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
43. People with psoriasis, lupus or rosacea: LL-37 is overexpressed in these and helps drive them by activating immune cells against DNA. (reported)
44. People with active or recent cancer: Promotes growth of ovarian, breast and lung tumours in lab studies. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
45. KPV: LL-37 attacks bacteria and biofilms while also stirring immune signalling, and KPV calms inflammation through NF-κB, mostly in mouse colitis models. People pair them for gut and chronic infection problems. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
46. The only human cathelicidin antimicrobial peptide, a 37-residue chain that starts with two leucines, part of innate immunity, active against bacteria and in wound healing.
47. First studied for: Studied as a natural antibiotic and wound-healing/immune-modulating agent.
48. Name story: Named by its first two residues (Leu-Leu) and its length, 37. It was first reported in 1995 as FALL-39, a guessed 39-residue form; a year later the real peptide turned out two amino acids shorter and got renamed. [S4] (verified)
49. 1995: Identified as the only human cathelicidin
50. 2000s: Wound, infection and skin-disease roles mapped
51. Today: Research chemical; on FDA compounding agenda before end of Feb 2027
