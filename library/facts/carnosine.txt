# Carnosine
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/carnosine
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: l-carnosine beta-alanyl histidine, beta-alanyl-L-histidine, L-carnosine
2. Origin: Made by the body, by enzymes
3. Sequence: AH (2 amino acids). First link is β-alanine, not regular alanine, shown as A
4. Legal and approval status: Supplement; not an approved drug.

## Summary
5. Carnosine is a small compound made from beta-alanine and histidine by an enzyme, stored mostly in muscle, where it buffers acid and acts as an antioxidant. People use it for muscle endurance and anti-aging, since it also blocks sugar damage to proteins. The human benefit comes mainly through beta-alanine loading, oral carnosine is broken down before reaching muscle, and the anti-aging claims remain preclinical.
6. Punch line: Muscle's acid buffer, made from beta-alanine and histidine. Also blocks sugar damage to proteins, hence anti-aging.
7. Used for: Antiglycation, healthy aging

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Aging & senescence: Blocks sugar damage to proteins
9. Cellular protection: Antioxidant in muscle and brain
10. Muscle & performance: Buffers acid in hard efforts

## How it works
11. Soaks up acid in working muscle · pH buffer · role Muscle & performance
   Carnosine's imidazole ring has a pKa close to muscle pH, so it absorbs hydrogen ions as acid builds during intense effort. That delays the acidosis that shuts down contraction. Raising muscle carnosine through beta-alanine gives modest gains in short, hard efforts in human trials.
12. Mops up reactive sugars and oxidants · Antiglycation, antioxidant · role Aging & senescence
   Carnosine quenches reactive carbonyls, the sticky sugar breakdown products that glycate proteins and stiffen tissue. It also scavenges oxidants. These anti-aging effects are shown in cell and animal work, not in human outcome trials.
13. Muscle levels set by its building block · Beta-alanine limits supply · role Muscle & performance
   Muscle builds carnosine from beta-alanine and histidine, and beta-alanine supply is the limiting step. Oral carnosine is largely broken down by the enzyme carnosinase before it reaches muscle. That is why beta-alanine, not carnosine itself, is the practical way to raise stores.
14. Binds metals that drive oxidation · Copper and zinc chelation · role Cellular protection
   Carnosine binds copper and zinc, metals that can drive oxidation reactions when free. Holding them reduces that source of oxidative damage in tissue. This chelation is shown in lab chemistry and animal work, and its human relevance is not established.
15. Mechanism overview: Carnosine is a dipeptide of beta-alanine and histidine, concentrated in skeletal muscle and brain. Its imidazole ring has a pKa close to muscle pH, so it soaks up hydrogen ions during intense effort and delays the acidosis that shuts down contraction. It also quenches reactive carbonyls that would otherwise glycate proteins, scavenges oxidants, and chelates copper and zinc. Muscle levels are limited by beta-alanine supply, and oral carnosine is largely broken down by carnosinase before reaching muscle, which is why beta-alanine is the practical way to raise stores.

## What has been tested
30. Muscle & performance: High-intensity output, via beta-alanine (trial)
31. Aging & senescence: Anti-aging effects (animal)
32. Human trials mostly use beta-alanine (its rate-limiting precursor), which raises muscle carnosine and modestly improves high-intensity performance. Carnosine itself is poorly absorbed orally.
33. What it helps: The human benefit comes through beta-alanine loading, which reliably raises muscle carnosine and gives modest gains in short, high-intensity efforts. Anti-aging effects are animal-only. Users report it for endurance and anti-aging; injectable carnosine is a niche use with few detailed reports.
34. Trial: active · Phase 1/2 · peripheral arterial disease · NCT06480760 · Shahid Baba (University of Louisville). Six months of 2 g daily carnosine is being tested in leg artery disease, running to 2028. (checked 2026-10-23) [S5] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
35. People on blood pressure medicines: Carnosine may lower blood pressure slightly; effects could add up. (inferred)

## Where it comes from
36. Found in 1900 in meat extract (the name is from Latin for flesh). Made by an enzyme, not a gene, and stored mostly in muscle, where it buffers acid.
37. First studied for: A muscle buffer and antioxidant; studied for performance and aging.
38. Name story: From Latin carnis, flesh: Russian chemist Vladimir Gulevich found it around 1900 in meat extract. It shares the root with carnitine and carnivore. Chemically it is just beta-alanine joined to histidine. [S6] (verified)
39. 1900: Found in meat extract; named from Latin for flesh
40. Decades: Known as a muscle acid buffer, made by an enzyme, not a gene
41. 2000s to 2010s: Beta-alanine loading shown to raise muscle carnosine
42. Today: A supplement for performance and aging; injectable form is niche
