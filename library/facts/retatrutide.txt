# Retatrutide
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/retatrutide
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: reta ly3437943 triple agonist glp-3, LY-3437943
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: YAQGTFTSDYSILLDKKAQAAFIEYLLEGGPSSGAPPPS (39 amino acids). Positions 2 and 20 are Aib and 13 is α-methyl-leucine, shown as A and L; fat chain on K17 and amide end not shown
4. Legal and approval status: Not approved anywhere. Phase 3 has read out; no approval yet. Sold only grey-market as a research chemical.

## Summary
5. Retatrutide is Eli Lilly's investigational lab-designed peptide that activates three gut-hormone receptors at once: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. It is aimed at obesity, type 2 diabetes and liver fat, by quieting hunger and burning more energy. Phase 3 trial data show very large weight loss, and grey-market users report strong appetite suppression, but it is not approved and nausea and GI upset are the main drawbacks.
6. Punch line: Triple-agonist weekly shot. Hits GLP-1, GIP and glucagon; trials saw over a quarter of body weight lost.
7. Used for: Weight loss

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Appetite & satiety: Strong appetite control
9. Fat metabolism: Glucagon arm burns fat
10. Glucose & insulin: Lowers blood sugar and A1c
11. Reward & urges: Fewer cravings reported
12. Sleep & rhythms (backed only by animal or user reports): Studied in sleep apnea
13. Pain & sensory (backed only by animal or user reports): Less knee pain in a trial
14. Cognition (backed only by animal or user reports): Users report less food noise

## How it works
15. Quiets hunger in the brain · GLP-1 receptor · role Appetite & satiety
   Retatrutide activates the GLP-1 receptor in the hypothalamus and brainstem, the brain's appetite centres. That cuts hunger and slows stomach emptying, so meals end sooner. In human trials this drives large weight loss, along with the nausea and vomiting that rose with dose.
16. Boosts insulin after meals · GIP receptor · role Glucose & insulin
   At the GIP receptor it boosts insulin release when blood sugar is high after meals, and it acts on fat tissue. This helps lower glucose without causing lows on its own. In human trials A1c fell, and it is being tested separately in type 2 diabetes.
17. Burns more energy and liver fat · Glucagon receptor · role Fat metabolism
   The glucagon receptor arm is the distinctive part. It raises energy expenditure and drives fat burning in the liver, which is why liver fat drops so sharply in trials. Glucagon activity can also raise resting heart rate, a signal reported in earlier trial work.
18. Dampens food cravings and reward · Mesolimbic GLP-1R · role Reward & urges
   GLP-1 receptors in the brain's mesolimbic reward circuit also respond, lowering the pull of high-reward food. Users report fewer cravings, not only less hunger. The reward effect is drawn from GLP-1 biology in animals and people, rather than measured directly for this drug.
19. Mechanism overview: Retatrutide is a single peptide that activates three receptors: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. GLP-1 receptor activation in the hypothalamus and brainstem cuts hunger and food reward, and slows stomach emptying. GIP receptor activation enhances glucose-dependent insulin release and acts on fat tissue. The glucagon arm is the distinctive part: it raises energy expenditure and drives fat oxidation in the liver, which is why liver fat drops so sharply. A fatty acid side chain binds albumin, allowing once-weekly dosing.

## What has been tested
39. Fat metabolism: Obesity, 48 weeks (trial)
40. Fat metabolism: Obesity, 80 weeks, TRIUMPH-1 (trial)
41. Pain & sensory: Obesity with knee osteoarthritis (trial)
42. Glucose & insulin: Type 2 diabetes, TRANSCEND-T2D (trial)
43. Organ function: Liver fat (trial)
44. Phase 2 obesity trial: about 24% weight loss at 48 weeks on the top dose. Phase 3 began reading out in late 2025: TRIUMPH-4 (obesity with knee osteoarthritis, Dec 2025, reported) and TRIUMPH-1 (obesity, May 2026: 28.3% weight loss at 80 weeks on 12 mg, against 2.2% on placebo). Diabetes is tested in a separate program, TRANSCEND-T2D.
45. What it helps: Trial data only. In obesity, TRIUMPH-1 showed 28.3% weight loss at 80 weeks against 2.2% on placebo, and weight was still falling. Trials also show lower liver fat, blood pressure and A1c, and it is being tested in type 2 diabetes and in obesity with knee osteoarthritis. Grey-market users report strong appetite suppression and fat loss felt first, with nausea and GI upset during titration.
46. Trial: active · Phase 3 · obesity, type 2 diabetes, fatty liver · NCT07165028 · Eli Lilly. Eleven open trials as of October 2026, including a head to head against tirzepatide (TRIUMPH-5) and a recruiting fatty liver outcomes trial. (checked 2026-10-23) [S6] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
47. Personal or family history of MTC or MEN2: GLP-1 receptor agonists cause C-cell tumours in rodents; trials exclude these people. (inferred)
48. People relying on the contraceptive pill: Contains GIP agonism like tirzepatide, whose label warns pill absorption drops after starts and dose increases. (inferred)
49. People with arrhythmia or a fast resting pulse: The glucagon arm raised resting heart rate in trials, peaking around week 24. (reported)
50. Anyone booked for surgery, endoscopy or sedation: Very slow stomach emptying raises the risk of inhaling stomach contents under anaesthesia. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
51. BPC-157: Retatrutide drives weight and liver fat loss, while BPC-157 is used for tendon and gut repair. Some users add BPC-157 hoping to ease GI upset during dose increases, based on gut protection seen in rats. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
52. TB-500 frag 17–23: Retatrutide works on appetite and metabolism, while the TB-500 fragment is taken for tissue repair and recovery. People combine them while training through a fat loss phase. The goals are separate, and the pair has not been tested together.
53. GHK-Cu: Retatrutide drives fast fat loss, while GHK-Cu is used to support collagen and skin repair. People pair them hoping to limit loose skin during large weight loss. That use is plausible but untested, and the pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
54. Eli Lilly's investigational chain (LY3437943) that hits three gut-hormone receptors at once: GLP-1, GIP and glucagon. Still in trials, but its structure is published.
55. First studied for: Built for obesity and type 2 diabetes, with liver fat and metabolic disease close behind.

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### What the pivotal trial actually measured [people]
61. TRIUMPH-1 randomised 2,339 adults with obesity, or overweight plus at least one weight-related condition, and without diabetes, one-to-one-to-one-to-one across three doses and placebo, dosed weekly for 80 weeks [1] (verified). A pre-specified extension carried 532 participants with a starting body mass index of 35 or above out to 104 weeks.
62. The primary measure was percent body weight change at 80 weeks. Results ran 19.0% at the low dose, 25.9% at the middle dose and 28.3% at the top dose, against 2.2% on placebo [1]. In the extension, the top dose reached 30.3% at 104 weeks, and participants switched from placebo onto drug reached 19.2% [1] (verified).
63. Two secondary measures are more informative than the headline. Waist circumference fell 16.3, 21.8 and 24.1 centimetres across the three doses against 3.6 on placebo, which matters because waist tracks visceral fat more closely than total weight does [1]. And the proportion losing at least 30% of starting body weight was 15.3%, 37.9% and 45.3% against 0.5% on placebo [1] (verified). That last figure is the one that moved clinical conversation, because losing 30% of body weight was previously the territory of surgery.
64. What the trial did not measure: it was not a cardiovascular outcome trial, it excluded diabetes, and at 80 weeks weight was still falling, so the plateau has not been located.

### The dose-response is the real finding, in both directions [people]
65. A clean dose-response is what separates a drug effect from noise, and this one is clean. Weight loss, waist reduction and the proportion hitting large thresholds all rose in order across the three doses [1].
66. Tolerability moved in the same order. Discontinuation for adverse events ran 4.1%, 6.9% and 11.3% across the three doses against 4.9% on placebo [1] (verified). So at the top dose, roughly one in nine participants stopped because of side effects, and the placebo rate tells you some of that would have happened anyway.
67. The gastrointestinal pattern scaled too: nausea 28.6%, 38.4% and 42.4% against 14.8% on placebo; vomiting 10.6%, 22.8% and 25.3% against 4.8% [1] (verified).
68. Read together, those two gradients are the practical substance of the trial. The top dose bought roughly 9 percentage points more weight loss than the low dose and roughly tripled the chance of stopping. That is a genuine trade-off rather than a free gain, and it is the kind of detail that disappears when a compound is described by its best number alone.

### One signal that is not in the class playbook [people]
69. Dysesthesia, meaning altered or unpleasant skin sensation, appeared in 5.1%, 12.3% and 12.5% of the three dose groups against 0.9% on placebo [1] (verified). That is a roughly thirteen-fold difference from placebo at the higher doses, with a dose relationship.
70. This is worth flagging because it is not a familiar feature of the established incretin drugs, so it is plausibly tied to the third receptor arm rather than to the shared mechanism (inferred). The trial sponsor described these events as generally mild to moderate, with most resolving during treatment [1] (reported).
71. Heart rate is the other arm-specific question. Glucagon receptor activation raises energy expenditure, and it also raises resting heart rate; Phase 2 work reported an increase of roughly 3 to 5 beats per minute [2] (reported). The pivotal release did not report heart rate figures [1], which is a gap rather than a reassurance.
72. Neither point argues against the compound. Both argue that a third receptor means a third side-effect profile, and that the class-wide intuitions people carry over from older drugs will be incomplete here.

### The other three trials, and what a hazard ratio of 0.82 does and does not show [people]
73. All four Phase 3 trials had reported topline results by October 2026 [2] (reported).
74. In obesity with type 2 diabetes, results reported in July 2026 showed up to 20.8% weight loss with HbA1c falling by as much as 1.6 percentage points [2]. Weight loss is reliably smaller in diabetes across this whole drug class, so the gap from the non-diabetes trial is expected rather than disappointing.
75. In obesity with knee osteoarthritis, results reported in December 2025 showed 28.7% weight loss at 68 weeks alongside significant improvement in knee pain [2] (reported).
76. The cardiovascular trial, in people with a body mass index of 35 or above and established cardiovascular disease, reported up to 22.6% weight loss and a pre-specified composite cardiovascular hazard ratio of 0.82 against placebo [2] (reported). That number deserves careful handling. A hazard ratio of 0.82 points in the right direction, but a pre-specified analysis inside a weight-loss trial is not the same as a dedicated, adequately powered cardiovascular outcome trial, and nothing in the topline establishes that it crossed a significance threshold with a trial designed to test it. Treat it as encouraging and not as established (inferred).

### Where approval stands, and why that matters here more than usual [people]
77. As of October 2026 the compound was not approved anywhere, and no marketing application had been submitted. The developer has indicated an intent to file in the first quarter of 2027, after completing manufacturing data, with any decision following well after that [2] (reported).
78. That timeline creates a situation worth naming plainly. The efficacy evidence here is unusually strong for an unapproved compound: four Phase 3 trials, thousands of participants, a clean dose-response, publication in a major journal [1]. The manufacturing and quality evidence for anything circulating outside that programme is entirely separate from it, and none of the trial data speaks to what is in an unapproved vial.
79. For every other compound in this catalog, weak trial evidence and uncertain product quality travel together. Here they come apart: the molecule has been tested properly, and the supply has not. Those are two different questions and conflating them in either direction produces a wrong conclusion (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
80. Where does weight loss plateau? It was still falling at 80 weeks and reached 30.3% by 104 weeks in the extension, so the ceiling has not been observed.
81. What happens on stopping? No published data address regain after this compound specifically, and the class pattern suggests it should be expected.
82. Is the dysesthesia signal durable, dose-limiting or mechanistic? It is dose-related and roughly thirteen-fold over placebo, and its cause has not been established.
83. What does the glucagon arm do to the heart over years? Resting heart rate rose in Phase 2 and the pivotal release did not report it. A long cardiovascular trial is the only way to settle this.
84. Does the cardiovascular signal hold up in a trial built to test it? A pre-specified composite inside a weight trial is a reason to run that trial, not a substitute for it.
85. How much muscle comes off at 28 to 30% total weight loss? Body composition is the obvious question at this magnitude of loss and is not in the topline reporting.
