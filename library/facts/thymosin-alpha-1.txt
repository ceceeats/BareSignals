# Thymosin Alpha-1
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/thymosin-alpha-1
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: thymalfasin, Zadaxin, Timosina, Tα1
2. Origin: Fragment of a natural protein
3. Sequence: SDAAVDTSSEITTKDLKEKKEVVEEAEN (28 amino acids). Acetylated start, not shown
4. Shown with an FDA approved badge
5. Legal and approval status: Approved in about 35 countries (China and Italy among them) as Zadaxin; not FDA-approved.

## Summary
6. Thymosin Alpha-1 is a 28-amino-acid peptide from the thymus that tunes the immune response, especially T-cells and dendritic cells. It is approved in about 35 countries as Zadaxin, including for hepatitis B, and is used as an immune adjuvant, with users reporting immune resilience. It is not FDA-approved, and trials in melanoma, COVID-19 and sepsis were inconclusive or mixed.
7. Punch line: Thymus peptide that trains T cells. Approved as Zadaxin in about 35 countries, including for hepatitis B.
8. Used for: Immune support, chronic infection

## How it works
15. Matures and activates T-cells · T-cell maturation · role Immunity
   Thymosin alpha-1, a natural peptide from the thymus, helps young T-cells mature and become active. T-cells are the immune cells that target infected and abnormal cells. This underpins its approval as thymalfasin in many countries outside the US.
16. Primes dendritic cells · TLR2, TLR9 · role Immunity
   It acts at TLR2 and TLR9, sensors on dendritic cells that normally detect germs, and switches on MyD88 signalling. That primes dendritic cells, the scouts that show threats to T-cells, strengthening the immune response. This comes from cell and animal work.
17. Boosts antiviral interferon and NK activity · IFN-α/γ, NK cells · role Immunity
   Through this priming it raises interferon alpha and gamma, key antiviral messengers, and increases natural killer (NK) cell activity. This supports antiviral and antitumour defence, shown in lab studies and reflected in its use as a vaccine and chemotherapy adjuvant.
18. Rebalances inflammation in sepsis trials · Cytokine balance · role Inflammatory signaling
   In hyperinflammation it rebalances cytokines, the immune signalling molecules, rather than simply boosting them. This is why it was tested in sepsis. Human trials gave mixed results, and a large sepsis trial reportedly showed no survival gain.
19. Helps clear hepatitis B; approved outside the US · HBV, immune adjuvant · role Organ function
   By strengthening T-cell and interferon responses it helps the body clear hepatitis B virus. Phase 3 use led to approval for chronic hepatitis B in many countries, though US trials were not definitive.
20. Mechanism overview: Thymosin alpha-1 is a 28 amino acid peptide first isolated from thymus tissue. It acts as an agonist at TLR2 and TLR9 on dendritic cells and other immune cells, triggering MyD88 signalling. That primes dendritic cells, promotes T-cell maturation and raises interferon alpha and gamma and NK cell activity, strengthening antiviral and antitumour defence. It also rebalances cytokines during hyperinflammation, which is why it was studied in sepsis. It is approved as thymalfasin in many countries.

## Where it comes from
48. A 28-amino-acid peptide from the thymus (Zadaxin), a tuner of the immune response, especially T-cells and dendritic cells.
49. First studied for: Supporting immune responses in infection and cancer, and as a vaccine adjuvant.
50. Name story: 'Alpha' is about electric charge, not structure: Goldstein's group sorted calf-thymus 'fraction 5' peptides by isoelectric point (alpha acidic, beta near-neutral, gamma basic). This was the first acidic one, sequenced in 1977; the synthetic drug is thymalfasin. [S4] (verified)
51. 1970s: Isolated from the thymus as an immune tuner
52. 1990s to 2000s: Approved abroad as Zadaxin for hepatitis and as an adjuvant
53. 2020s: Small, mixed COVID studies; large sepsis trial no survival gain (reported)
54. Today: Approved in about 35 countries; not FDA-approved

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from, and why it is different from most peptides here [lab]
55. Thymosin alpha-1 is a 28-residue peptide first isolated from a calf thymus extract; the drug version, thymalfasin, is synthetic [6] (verified). It is a natural human peptide: healthy adults carry about 0.1 to 1 ng/mL in serum, highest in the thymus [1] (verified).
56. What sets it apart is that a licensed, manufacturer-controlled product exists. Zadaxin is supplied as a 1.6 mg vial for subcutaneous injection, made in Italy, with a licence holder in Singapore and indications in chronic hepatitis B and, with interferon, hepatitis C [7] (verified). Its approvals span Asia-Pacific, Latin America, Eastern Europe and the Middle East, plus Italy as a flu vaccine adjuvant for immunocompromised people, India for hepatitis B, and an EMA orphan designation in liver cancer [1] (reported; the FDA could not verify the manufacturer's full country list).

### What is actually in a vial, licensed versus not [lab]
57. The licensed product is reconstituted with its own 1 mL of sterile water just before use, and the label is clear it is subcutaneous only, not intravenous [7] (verified).
58. Material outside that channel is a different question. Reviewing the US nomination, the FDA found free base and acetate confused in the paperwork, certificates without impurity, aggregate, bioburden or endotoxin data, and doubted that the proposed 3 mg/mL could be reached given limited water solubility; the highest concentration it found used clinically was 2 mg/mL [1,2] (verified). The research-grade powder does not inherit the licensed product's quality record (inferred).

### Mechanism, one step at a time, with the model each step came from [mixed]
59. Dendritic cells, in cells and mice. In dendritic cells exposed to the fungus Aspergillus, the peptide drove maturation and IL-12 production through p38 MAPK and NF-kappaB, dependent on MyD88 and Toll-like receptor signalling; in mice it strengthened Th1 antifungal immunity and protected highly susceptible transplant recipients from aspergillosis [5] (verified). This is the study most often cited for the TLR mechanism, and it is a fungal infection model.
60. T cells and cytokines, in people. Clinical and lab work summarised for hepatitis B describes effects on T-cell development, higher interferon-gamma and IL-2, and more NK cell killing [6] (reported).
61. Vaccines, in mice. Anti-tetanus antibodies were about 25 to 35% higher in young mice given the peptide, but there was no boost with a pneumococcal vaccine [1] (verified). The adjuvant effect is real in some settings and absent in others.

### What the body does with it [people]
62. After 1.6 to 2.2 mg under the skin in healthy volunteers, peak levels were 32.6 to 78.9 ng/mL at 0.67 to 2 hours, half-life was 1.8 to 2.6 hours, and five daily doses did not accumulate [1] (verified). Levels return near baseline within about a day.
63. Set against the endogenous range, one standard dose raises blood levels roughly 30 to several hundred times above normal for a few hours (inferred from the two ranges). The twice-weekly schedule therefore produces pulses, not a steady state, and the immune effects are presumed to outlast the drug.

### The human record, where size changes the answer [people]
64. Hepatitis B is the indication that built its reputation. Seven randomised trials of 1.6 mg twice weekly for six months reported a significantly higher sustained response than no treatment, with a peculiar timing: benefit was not obvious on treatment and complete virological response rose gradually after it stopped [6] (reported). These trials predate today's antivirals.
65. Sepsis is where the record turned. A 2025 meta-analysis of 11 randomised trials, all from China, found lower 28-day mortality overall (odds ratio 0.73), but the benefit came from the six lower-quality trials (odds ratio 0.41, 359 patients); the five higher-quality trials showed no significant effect (0.82, 95% CI 0.65 to 1.03), and the test for publication bias was positive [4] (verified).
66. The largest trial settles most of that. TESTS randomised 1,106 adults with sepsis at 22 Chinese centres to 1.6 mg every 12 hours or placebo for seven days. Day-28 mortality was 23.4% against 24.1%, hazard ratio 0.99 (95% CI 0.77 to 1.27); 90-day mortality was also no different [3] (verified). Exploratory subgroups pointed both ways, worse under 60 (hazard ratio 1.67) and better with diabetes (0.58), neither surviving correction for multiple testing [3].
67. In the US it was trialled in cancer from the early 1980s; a phase 1 escalation in 14 patients reached 9.6 mg/m2 without a dose-limiting toxicity [1] (verified). More than 4,400 trial subjects are cited across infections, cancers and vaccines [1], but no US approval followed.

### Safety: a real record, with real limits [mixed]
68. Of all the peptides in this library, this one has the most human safety data. In TESTS, adverse events (66.4% against 67.6%) and serious adverse events (26.8% against 29.3%) matched placebo [3] (verified). The manufacturer reports over 17 million doses given with infrequent, mild adverse experiences, an estimate from manufacturer-affiliated authors [1] (reported). Injection-site redness and discomfort are the common complaint [1].
69. The licensed label contraindicates it in people being deliberately immunosuppressed, such as transplant recipients, unless benefit clearly outweighs risk, because it is designed to stimulate immunity [7] (verified). Anyone with an autoimmune condition faces the same logic, though that has not been formally studied (inferred).
70. The gaps: no carcinogenicity study, and the acute and 13-week toxicology the manufacturer reports (no signals up to 20 mg/kg once, or 6 mg/kg a day) was never provided to the FDA as data [1] (verified). The FDA's adverse event system held one report, in a hepatitis C patient also on peginterferon [1].

### Who produced the evidence [people]
71. The licence holder co-funded TESTS, and several authors reported grants or consulting fees from it [3] (verified). That trial is also the one that found no effect, which counts in favour of its credibility. The sepsis meta-analysis found that the positive signal sat in small single-centre trials [4], a familiar pattern for industry-adjacent fields.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
72. Not FDA-approved; no NDA has been approved, though four US orphan designations exist [1,2] (verified). It left the do-not-compound category when its nominator withdrew [8], and at the December 2024 advisory committee the members voted 17 to 4 against adding either form to the bulks list, the acetate vote citing a lack of convincing effectiveness data [2] (verified). It was not part of the 2026 peptide votes.
73. Sport: not named on the 2026 Prohibited List [9]. Because it is approved in several countries, the catch-all non-approved class does not obviously apply (inferred). Thymosin beta-4 and its fragments are a different molecule and are explicitly banned [9] (verified).

### Open questions [mixed]
74. Does it help hepatitis B on top of modern antivirals? The original trials compared it to nothing.
75. Is there any group in sepsis that benefits? TESTS hints at subgroups but cannot prove one.
76. Is research-grade powder equivalent to the licensed product? Unknown without impurity and endotoxin testing.
77. What does repeated immune stimulation do over years in healthy people? Unstudied.
