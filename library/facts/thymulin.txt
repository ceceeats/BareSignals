# Thymulin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/thymulin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: facteur thymique sérique, FTS, serum thymic factor, zinc-thymulin
2. Origin: Made by the body
3. Sequence: EAKSQGGSN (9 amino acids). Starts with pyroglutamate, shown as E; carries zinc
4. Legal and approval status: Research chemical; no approval.

## Summary
5. Thymulin is a natural nonapeptide hormone made by the thymus that matures T cells and only works with a zinc atom attached. It is studied as a regulator of immune balance and inflammation, and in animal models it lowers inflammatory signals and reduces pain hypersensitivity. Evidence is animal work, there are no modern human efficacy trials, and it has no approval.
6. Punch line: The thymus hormone that matures T cells, active only with zinc bound. Calms inflammation and pain in animals.
7. Used for: Immune support and inflammation

## How it works
15. Matures T-cells · Thymic hormone · role Immunity
   Thymulin is a hormone made by thymic epithelial cells that signals T-cell precursors to mature and specialise. That keeps up the supply of working T-cells, which falls as the thymus shrinks with age. Its role is shown in animal work, with no modern human efficacy trials.
16. Only active with bound zinc · Zinc-dependent · role Immunity
   Thymulin only works when bound to a zinc ion, which locks the peptide into its active shape. Without zinc it is present but inactive, so zinc status shapes its effect. This dependence complicates practical use of the peptide on its own.
17. Lowers inflammatory cytokines in models · TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-6 · role Inflammatory signaling
   In animal models, thymulin lowers TNF-alpha, IL-1 beta and IL-6, three main signals that drive inflammation. Less of these signals means a calmer immune response in tissue. These results come from animals, not human trials.
18. Cuts pain hypersensitivity in animal models · Anti-hyperalgesia · role Pain & sensory
   In animal pain models, thymulin reduces hyperalgesia, the state where nerves overreact to normal or mild stimuli. This fits with its lowering of the inflammatory cytokines that sensitise nerves. It has not been tested for pain in people.
19. Two-way link with pituitary hormones · Neuroendocrine-thymus axis · role Endocrine & growth
   Pituitary hormones regulate how much thymulin the thymus releases, and thymulin in turn affects pituitary output. This two-way neuroendocrine loop ties the immune and hormone systems together. It is described from animal and some human work.
20. Mechanism overview: Thymulin is a nine-amino-acid hormone secreted by thymic epithelial cells. It is only active when bound to zinc, which locks it into its working shape. It drives T-cell maturation and differentiation. In animal models it lowers TNF-alpha, IL-1 beta and IL-6 and reduces pain hypersensitivity. It sits in a two-way loop with the pituitary: pituitary hormones regulate its secretion and thymulin in turn affects pituitary output.

## What has been tested
36. Immunity: Immune modulation (animal)
37. Inflammatory signaling: Anti-inflammatory effect (animal)
38. Pain & sensory: Pain relief (animal)
39. Animal and some human work: zinc-thymulin influences immune and neuroendocrine signaling and has anti-inflammatory and pain effects in models.
40. What it helps: Evidence is animal work showing immune modulation, anti-inflammatory effects and pain relief. There are no modern human efficacy trials, and the zinc dependence complicates practical use. First-hand reports are few, mostly from people using it for immune support. Pain models show reduced hypersensitivity.
41. Trial: none. Studied in small human reports in the 1980s and 1990s; no registered interventional trial found. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
42. Transplant recipients on anti-rejection drugs: Thymic hormone that matures T cells. (inferred)
43. People with autoimmune disease: Turning up immune activity can flare an autoimmune condition. (inferred)

## Where it comes from
44. A natural zinc-dependent thymus hormone (nonapeptide) that matures T-cells; its activity depends on bound zinc.
45. First studied for: A marker and modulator of thymic/immune function, studied in immune deficiency and inflammation.
46. Name story: Discovered by Jean-François Bach's group in 1977 as 'facteur thymique sérique' (FTS), a thymus factor found in blood serum. It only works with a zinc atom bound. Easily confused with Thymalin, a whole-gland extract. [S5] (verified)
47. 1970s: Found as a zinc-dependent thymus hormone
48. Today: Research chemical; no approval
