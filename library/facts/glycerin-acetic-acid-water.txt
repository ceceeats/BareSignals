# Glycerin Acetic Acid Water
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/glycerin-acetic-acid-water
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: GAC
2. Origin: Sterile mixing water, not a peptide
3. Class: water
4. Legal and approval status: Sold as a mixing liquid for freeze-dried peptides. Not taken on its own.

## Summary
5. Punch line: Sterile water with acetic acid and glycerin. The acid dissolves stubborn peptides; the glycerin stabilizes the mix.

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
6. Mixing water: A sterile water based liquid containing a small amount of acetic acid and glycerin. The acid helps certain peptides dissolve and the glycerin is added as a stabilizing and thickening agent.

## How it works

## Water
7. Made of: Sterile water, balance
8. Made of: Acetic acid, not stated on label; dilute, likely in the 0.5 to 1% range (unverified)
9. Made of: Glycerin (glycerol), not stated on label (unverified)
10. pH: Not published. If the acetic acid content is around 0.6%, the pH would be roughly 2.9 to 3, calculated from acetic acid's pKa of 4.76. Glycerin is neutral and does not change pH meaningfully.
11. When used: Peptides that dissolve poorly or clump in plain or bacteriostatic water and do better in mild acid, the same group as acetic acid water (for example AOD-9604 and HGH fragment 176-191).
12. When used: Glycerin is used in protein and peptide formulation as a co-solvent that can reduce clumping (aggregation) of hydrophobic molecules, which is the likely reason it is included.
13. When used: Only when the peptide's own instructions or supplier call for an acidic diluent. It is not a general purpose replacement for bacteriostatic water.
14. not_for: Peptides that are acid sensitive or that the supplier says to dissolve in neutral water or bacteriostatic water.
15. not_for: Situations requiring a preservative for many punctures over weeks, unless the product's certificate of analysis states a preservative is present.
16. not_for: Anyone sensitive to acidic injections, since low pH liquids are more likely to sting.
17. not_for: Any use where the exact composition matters and the certificate of analysis is unavailable. The concentrations are not standardized across makers.
18. storage: Unopened: cool, dry place away from light, within the printed expiry date. Refrigeration (2 to 8°C) is a reasonable default if no instructions are given.
19. storage: After first puncture: no published standard exists for this liquid. Treat as preservative-free unless stated otherwise: refrigerate and use within a short period. The USP 28-day multi-dose limit applies only to vials with a preservative and should be treated as an upper bound.
20. storage: Once a peptide is dissolved in it, follow the peptide's own storage rules, usually refrigerated at 2 to 8°C.
21. storage: Do not freeze unless the label allows it.
22. how: Confirm the label, expiry date and clarity. Write the date of first puncture on the vial.
23. how: Wipe the stopper with an alcohol swab and let it dry before every entry. Use a new sterile needle and syringe each time.
24. how: Draw slowly. The glycerin makes the liquid slightly thicker than water, so it may draw and push a little more slowly.
25. how: Add it gently down the inside wall of the peptide vial and swirl, do not shake.
26. how: If the peptide does not fully dissolve, let it rest in the refrigerator before deciding it has failed.
27. watch: Cloudiness, haze or color change: discard.
28. watch: Floating particles or strands: discard.
29. watch: A noticeable vinegar smell is expected. Any other odor is not.
30. watch: Stinging or burning at the injection site is more likely with acidic liquids; persistent redness, swelling or heat is a warning sign.
31. watch: A gel or clumps forming after mixing suggests the peptide has aggregated.
32. facts: Glycerin (glycerol, C3H8O3) is one of the oldest protein stabilizers in biochemistry labs. Research on plant proteins showed it prevents acid-induced clumping in proportion to how much it shields water-repelling surfaces.
33. facts: Acetic acid is the same acid in vinegar. Glacial acetic acid is the pure, water-free form, named for the ice-like crystals it forms just below room temperature (16.6°C).
34. facts: Unlike bacteriostatic water, there is no official pharmacopeia monograph for a 'glycerin plus acetic acid' reconstitution water, so composition varies by maker.

## Synergies (paired with)
42. AOD-9604: The mild acid keeps AOD-9604 dissolved, and glycerin is added to reduce clumping further. It is purely a mixing choice and has no part in the fragment's claimed fat loss or cartilage effects.
43. HGH frag 176–191: For HGH frag 176-191, the acid helps it dissolve and the glycerin is added to cut clumping. This is about keeping the solution clear, not about the fragment's fat loss action.
