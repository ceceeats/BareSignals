# Somatropin (HGH)
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/somatropin-hgh
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: hgh growth hormone somatropin gh, somatotropin, rhGH, Genotropin, Norditropin, Humatrope, Nutropin, Omnitrope, Saizen, Zomacton
2. Origin: Made by the body
3. Sequence: FPTIPLSRLFDNAMLRAHRLHQLAFDTYQEFEEAYIPKEQKYSFLQNPQTSLCFSESIPTPSNREETQQKSNLELLRISLLLIQSWLEPVQFLRSVFANSLVYGASDSNVYDLLKDLEEGIQTLMGRLEDGSPRTGQIFKQTYSKFDTNSHNDDALLKNYGLLYCFRKDMDKVETFLRIVQCRSVEGSCGF (191 amino acids). Two bridges between C pairs hold the fold; not shown
4. Legal and approval status: FDA-approved for specific deficiencies; anti-aging/athletic use is off-label and illegal to distribute for that purpose. WADA-banned (S2).

## Summary
5. Somatropin is recombinant human growth hormone, the full 191-amino-acid protein the pituitary makes, now produced by engineered bacteria. It is approved for growth hormone deficiency and wasting, where it restores growth and raises lean mass while cutting fat, working through the GH receptor and IGF-1. Anti-aging and physique use is off-label and WADA-banned, and water retention, carpal tunnel and rising blood sugar are common.
6. Punch line: The real pituitary hormone, not a secretagogue. What GH peptides chase: more lean mass, less fat, water retention.
7. Used for: Adult GH deficiency, body composition

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Tissue repair: Supports tissue and bone repair
9. Fat metabolism: Breaks down stored fat
10. Endocrine & growth: Replaces growth hormone
11. Muscle & performance: Adds lean mass

## How it works
12. Signals growth and fat breakdown · GH receptor, JAK2/STAT5 · role Endocrine & growth
   Somatropin binds the GH receptor, which activates the JAK2 enzyme, which then switches on STAT5 to change gene activity. That signal drives growth in children and shifts metabolism toward fat breakdown in adults. It is identical to the body's own growth hormone, with decades of human trials.
13. Drives IGF-1 from the liver · IGF-1 axis · role Endocrine & growth
   In the liver, STAT5 drives production of IGF-1, which carries most of growth hormone's growth and building effects. IGF-1 acts on bone, muscle and connective tissue throughout the body. Raised IGF-1 is the main marker clinicians track during treatment.
14. Releases fat from fat cells · Lipolysis · role Fat metabolism
   Growth hormone acts directly on fat cells to trigger lipolysis, the release of stored fat for fuel. This is part of why trials show less fat mass in adults with deficiency and HIV wasting. GH also opposes insulin, which raises blood sugar.
15. Builds lean mass and protein · Protein synthesis · role Muscle & performance
   Through IGF-1, growth hormone raises protein synthesis and lean mass. Trials show clear gains in true GH deficiency and HIV wasting. In older adults without deficiency, anti-aging trials found the risks outweighed the gains, and some of the added mass is retained fluid.
16. Supports collagen and tissue repair · IGF-1, collagen synthesis · role Tissue repair
   IGF-1 drives collagen formation in tendon, ligament, bone and skin. That is the basis of the recovery reputation among users. Clear human evidence exists for growth and body composition in deficiency, while repair claims in healthy adults rest mostly on user reports.
17. Mechanism overview: Somatropin is recombinant human growth hormone, identical to the 191 amino acid pituitary hormone. It binds the GH receptor, which activates JAK2, which phosphorylates STAT5. In the liver, STAT5 drives IGF-1 production, and IGF-1 carries most of the growth and anabolic effects: protein synthesis, bone growth and collagen formation. GH also acts directly on fat cells to trigger lipolysis, and it opposes insulin, raising blood glucose. That anti-insulin action, plus sodium retention, explains the common side effects of high blood sugar and fluid retention.

## What has been tested
34. Endocrine & growth: GH deficiency, children and adults (trial)
35. Bone & cartilage: Turner syndrome, short stature (trial)
36. Organ function: Kidney disease in children (trial)
37. Muscle & performance: HIV wasting (trial)
38. Aging & senescence: Anti-aging in older adults (trial)
39. Decades of trials: clear benefit in true GH deficiency, Turner syndrome, chronic kidney disease in children, and HIV wasting. Anti-aging use studied and largely cautioned against.
40. What it helps: In trials it restores growth in children with GH deficiency, Turner syndrome and chronic kidney disease, and improves body composition in adult deficiency and HIV wasting. Broadly it adds lean mass and cuts fat. Anti-aging trials in older adults found risks outweighing gains without deficiency. Users report strong physique and recovery effects, with water retention, carpal tunnel and rising blood sugar common.
41. Trial: active · growth hormone deficiency and related conditions · Pfizer and others. Approved since 1985; the latest confirmed trial was a Pfizer Genotropin pen study that ended July 2026, and daily somatropin is the comparison arm in several recruiting weekly GH trials. (checked 2026-10-23) [S7] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
42. People with active or recent cancer: Label contraindicates active malignancy; childhood cancer survivors on GH had more second tumours. (verified)
43. People acutely ill after major surgery or trauma: Two large ICU trials found higher death rates on GH; label contraindication. (verified)
44. People with diabetic retinopathy: Label contraindicates active proliferative or severe non-proliferative retinopathy. (verified)
45. Prader-Willi with severe obesity or breathing problems: Sudden deaths reported; label contraindication. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
46. BPC-157: Growth hormone raises IGF-1 and collagen building body wide, while BPC-157 works locally on blood flow and, in lab work, makes tendon cells more responsive to growth hormone. People pair them for injury recovery. The combination has not been tested together.
47. TB-500 frag 17–23: Growth hormone supplies a body wide anabolic and collagen signal, while the TB-500 fragment is used to move repair cells into injured tissue. People stack them for recovery from tendon and muscle injuries. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
48. Recombinant human growth hormone, a 191-amino-acid protein. Until 1985 GH was collected from the pituitary glands of cadavers; that stopped when it was found to spread a prion disease, and engineered bacteria have made it since.
49. First studied for: Growth hormone deficiency in children and adults; later wasting conditions and short stature.
50. Name story: From Greek soma, 'body', plus -tropin, 'acting on'. The INN is strict: Genentech's first 1985 version carried one extra methionine and got its own name, somatrem, while the exact human sequence is somatropin. [S8] (verified)
51. 1958 to 1985: GH taken from cadaver pituitaries; stopped over prion disease
52. 1985: Recombinant GH made by engineered bacteria approved
53. 1990: Small anti-aging study, later criticised
54. Today: Approved for deficiency; widely used off-label for physique; WADA-banned
