# Tβ4 + BPC + KPV
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/tb4-bpc-kpv
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Tri-HealMax, Tri-Heal Max, Coremend, Tβ4 + BPC-157 + KPV, Tβ4 + KPV + BPC-157, TB-500 + BPC-157 + KPV, CORE blend
2. Origin: Blend of 3 peptides
3. Legal and approval status: Not approved anywhere as a drug or as a combination. US: BPC-157, TB-500 and KPV left FDA 503A Category 2 in April 2026 when nominations were withdrawn; the July 2026 advisory panel voted in favour of compounding all three, no rule yet. Australia: BPC-157 and TB-500 Schedule 4. WADA: BPC-157 and TB-500 banned at all times; KPV under S0.

## Summary
4. This blend adds KPV to the so-called Wolverine pair of thymosin beta-4 (Tβ4) and BPC-157, sold as Tri-HealMax or Coremend. It is used for injury recovery and inflammation, with BPC-157 and Tβ4 supporting repair and KPV damping NF-κB inflammation. Evidence is by component, mostly in rats and mice, and the one combination study found no extra benefit. Users report better recovery and calmer gut, while others see little.
5. Punch line: The Wolverine pair plus KPV. Tβ4 and BPC-157 rebuild tissue while KPV damps the inflammation around it.
6. Used for: Injury repair with inflammation control

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Tissue repair: BPC-157 and Tβ4 for tendon, muscle and wound repair
8. Inflammatory signaling: KPV and Tβ4 lower NF-κB signalling
9. Digestive & gut: KPV and BPC-157 aimed at gut lining
10. Pain & sensory: Soreness and injury pain ease in reports
11. Immunity (backed only by animal or user reports): KPV steadies immune cells
12. Muscle & performance (backed only by animal or user reports): Used for muscle tears and training recovery

## How it works
13. Moves repair cells into wounds · Tβ4: G-actin sequestering · role Tissue repair
   Thymosin beta-4 binds single actin units (G-actin) and holds them in reserve. When a repair cell needs to move, the stock is released to build filaments fast, so cells migrate into the wound. Shown in cell and animal work; human wound and eye trials used the full protein.
14. Helps blood vessels grow into damage · BPC-157: VEGFR2, eNOS · role Tissue repair
   BPC-157 acts on VEGFR2 and eNOS, the receptor and enzyme that drive new blood vessel growth and nitric oxide release. Better blood supply carries oxygen and repair cells into damaged tissue. This is shown in rat injury models, not human trials.
15. Blocks a key inflammation switch inside cells · KPV: PepT1 uptake, NF-κB down · role Inflammatory signaling
   KPV, the last three amino acids of alpha-MSH, enters gut and immune cells through PepT1, a peptide transporter. Inside, it blocks NF-κB, a master switch for inflammatory genes. This damps inflammation at the injury or gut wall, shown in mice only.
16. Protects gut lining · BPC-157 and KPV, colitis models · role Digestive & gut
   BPC-157 shields the stomach and gut lining from damage, and KPV calms inflammation in the colon wall. Each one helped the lining heal in rodent colitis models. The two have not been tested together for gut effects.
17. Mechanism overview: Thymosin beta-4 sequesters G-actin to drive cell migration and lowers NF-κB signalling; BPC-157 adds VEGFR2, Akt and eNOS driven angiogenesis and FAK and paxillin fibroblast migration; KPV, alpha-MSH 11 to 13, enters cells via PepT1 and blocks NF-κB. Tri-HealMax weights Tβ4 at 2.5 times the others, which brings a daily dose closer to standalone Tβ4 use than a 1:1 stack; Coremend is even. KPV is chemically quiet; full Tβ4 carries an oxidisable methionine.

## Who shouldn't take it
57. People with active or recent cancer: Tβ4 drives new blood vessels and cell migration; high levels track with tumour spread. (reported)
58. People with IBD on biologics or immunosuppressants: KPV damps NF-kB signalling; stacking it onto immune drugs is untested. (inferred)
59. Drug-tested athletes: Thymosin beta-4 and TB-500 are named on the WADA list; doping cases exist. (verified)
60. People fighting an active infection: Blunting inflammation can blunt the response clearing the infection. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
61. BPC-157: BPC-157 is already in this blend. Extra BPC-157 tilts it toward blood vessel growth and gut protection. In the 2026 rat Achilles study, BPC-157 plus Tβ4 did no better than either alone.
62. Thymosin Beta-4: Thymosin beta-4 is already in this blend, weighted heaviest in some versions. Extra Tβ4 raises the cell migration share. Tβ4 alone outperformed vehicle in the 2026 rat Achilles study, while the BPC-157 plus Tβ4 pair added nothing.
63. KPV: KPV is already in this blend. Extra KPV raises the anti-inflammatory share for gut and skin. KPV has mouse colitis data only, and the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
64. GHK-Cu: GHK-Cu adds copper delivery and collagen signalling for skin and matrix repair, which this blend lacks. Adding it turns the stack into KLOW. The four together have not been tested.

## Where it comes from
65. The Wolverine pair plus KPV, sold in two ratios: Tri-HealMax, Tβ4 25 + BPC-157 10 + KPV 10 = 45 mg (2.5:1:1, heavy on thymosin beta-4), and Coremend, 10 + 10 + 10 = 30 mg. A similar 10+10+10 kit is sold elsewhere as a CORE blend.
66. First studied for: Assembled by the research-peptide market as an injury stack with an anti-inflammatory third peptide: BPC-157 and Tβ4 for repair, KPV to damp inflammation and, users say, take the sting out.
67. Name story: Tri-HealMax: three healing peptides, with Max likely pointing to the larger thymosin beta-4 share. Coremend is a product name for the even 10+10+10 version. Neither name is explained by its makers. [S1] (inferred)
68. 1981: Thymosin beta-4 isolated

71. 2020s: Sold premixed as Tri-HealMax 25+10+10 and Coremend 10+10+10

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Two of the three were finally tested together, and the combination lost [animals]
73. For once a blend's central premise has been examined, at least in part. A 2026 study in a peer-reviewed orthopaedic surgery journal took 32 male Sprague-Dawley rats, cut the Achilles tendon fully and repaired it with a Kessler technique, then split them into four groups of eight: vehicle, BPC-157 at 10 micrograms per kilogram daily, thymosin beta-4 at 60 micrograms per kilogram daily, and both together, all intraperitoneal, from the first day after surgery for 30 days [1] (verified, in animals).
74. Maximum load to failure at four weeks, reported as medians, was 26.91 newtons for vehicle, 37.16 for BPC-157, 37.41 for thymosin beta-4, and 34.54 for the combination [1] (verified). Only thymosin beta-4 against vehicle survived correction for multiple comparisons, adjusted p=0.0406, with BPC-157 against vehicle at adjusted p=0.389 [1].
75. Read the combination column again. It is not just that two plus two failed to make four; the combination arm sat numerically below both single agents on the mechanical endpoint. On the histological scores the pattern repeated: thymosin beta-4 differed from control on both total scores, the combination differed from control on one, BPC-157 on neither, and there were no significant differences among the treatment groups at all [1] (verified).
76. The authors' conclusion is the sentence that matters here: combined treatment did not confer additional benefits compared with either agent alone [1] (verified). They offered overlapping downstream pathways, a ceiling effect and suboptimal dosing as possible explanations and labelled all three speculative, since no molecular or pharmacokinetic analyses were done [1].
77. The study's own limits are stated plainly and are worth carrying: a single four-week time point, healthy young male rats, no functional testing, histology subgroups of only four tendons per group with no power calculation, no blinding of surgery or dosing, and biomechanics limited to peak load without stiffness or energy to failure [1] (verified). It received no financial support and declared no conflicts [1]. So this is not a definitive negative. It is the only direct test of the combination in existence, and it points the opposite way to the product's premise.
78. Note also what it did not test: the third component. No study of any kind has examined KPV alongside the other two (verified as absent).

### The three components have very unequal, and unevenly thin, evidence [mixed]
79. A blend inherits the reputation of its best-regarded ingredient. Component by component, that reputation does not survive contact with the regulatory reviews of 2026.
80. BPC-157 has exactly one controlled human trial, reported only as a 2005 meeting abstract in ulcerative colitis, whose between-group difference had a confidence interval crossing zero, and which the FDA found inadequate for lacking endpoint definitions, inclusion criteria and statistical methods [2] (verified, as recorded in this library's component entry). Everything else human is uncontrolled.
81. Thymosin beta-4 is the component most often assumed strongest and is the weakest on paper for the marketed fragment. The FDA found no clinical studies, no human exposure data and no human pharmacokinetic data by any route for the nominated substance, and no toxicology studies of any kind: no acute, no repeat-dose, no genotoxicity, no reproductive, no carcinogenicity [3] (verified, as recorded in this library's component entry).
82. KPV has no human studies, no pharmacokinetic data in any species and no toxicology studies. Its nomination cited nine references, eight animal studies of related compounds and one cadaver-skin study, none testing it in a living person, and it was nominated as a 0.1% topical cream rather than for injection [4] (verified, as recorded in this library's component entry).
83. So the blend combines one component with a single inadequate and non-significant controlled trial and two with no human data whatsoever, one of which was never proposed for injection at all.

### The identity problem, and the one mismatch specific to this vial [lab]
84. None of the three has an approved generic name, so there is no reference standard to test a batch against, which regulators flagged as a patient safety issue in its own right. Each exists as both a free base and an acetate salt with different molecular weights, and in each case nominators were inconsistent about which form they meant [2,3,4] (verified, as recorded in this library's component entries).
85. One mismatch belongs specifically to any blend containing thymosin beta-4. Regulators stated that the marketed seven-residue fragment and the full 43-residue protein are not the same substance [3] (verified). Products in this family commonly contain the full protein under the fragment's name, or the reverse. That is not a labelling quirk: the two molecules have different evidence, different stability and different regulatory records, so the ingredient list cannot be used to look up what is actually present.
86. It also changes the chemistry of the vial. The full protein carries an oxidisable methionine; the short fragment does not. A vial built on full protein at the larger share is therefore the most oxidation-sensitive version of this blend, which is why light and cold storage matter more here than in a fragment-based mix (inferred).
87. KPV is the chemically quiet one: no histidine, methionine or asparagine, so it adds no new reactive site to the solution (verified as a structural observation). The pH preferences of the components do not match, with the gastric peptide's range sitting above the full protein's, so a single blended solution cannot be optimal for both (inferred).
88. No public certificate of analysis for either common ratio was found in this review (verified as absent), which leaves the mass of each component in a given vial unverifiable from outside.

### The ratio is a product decision, not a finding [mixed]
89. At the leading composition the thymosin share is two and a half times each of the others, so by mass this is predominantly a thymosin product with two additions. The even version is a different product in everything but name.
90. Nothing in the literature establishes that either ratio is right for any purpose, because no study of any ratio exists (verified as absent). The one combination study used doses in a six-to-one ratio favouring thymosin by weight, closer to the weighted vial than to the even one, and found no added benefit at that ratio [1]. That is a single data point, at one dose pair, in one species, against one injury.
91. The practical consequence of a fixed ratio is attribution. If something improves, three components could be responsible, and the two with no human data are as plausible as the one with a single inadequate trial. If something goes wrong, the ambiguity runs the same way. Nobody using a fixed blend learns which component they respond to, which is the quiet cost of every blend and is sharper here because the rat study suggests the components may not even be additive (inferred).
92. There is a real argument on the other side, which is convenience: one reconstitution, one injection, one vial to store. That is a genuine benefit and it is a handling benefit, not a pharmacological one.

### Safety, as an absence rather than a finding [mixed]
93. The three components drew the same concern from US regulators in 2026: peptide-related impurities and aggregates from synthesis can drive immune responses, and no immunogenicity studies exist for any of them [2,3,4] (verified, as recorded in this library's component entries). Dissolving three peptides in one vial multiplies the impurity and aggregate surface without adding any data about it (inferred).
94. Beyond immunogenicity, the absences are the story. For the thymosin fragment and for KPV there is no toxicology at all, of any kind, in any species [3,4]. For BPC-157 there are no repeat-dose studies longer than 28 days in any species, and none by the routes people actually use [2]. A combination cannot be safer than the sum of what has not been studied about its parts.
95. Nothing here says the blend is dangerous. It says that in evidence terms the blend is a fourth product with no file of its own, and that its three parts between them have one inadequate controlled human trial and no toxicology for two of the three.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
96. No approved product contains this combination anywhere, and no approved product contains any of the three components as a single agent.
97. The US sequence in 2026 is easy to misread. On 15 April 2026 the FDA said it would remove twelve peptides from the do-not-compound Category 2 within seven days, because the original nominations had been withdrawn; the agency was explicit that lifting that designation did not make any peptide eligible for compounding, and it simultaneously scheduled advisory committee review [5] (verified). On 29 June the agency's own staff urged that none of the seven peptides under review be added to the 503A bulks list, citing poorly characterised substances, limited or absent human data and immunogenicity risk; the same day a reconstituted advisory committee was announced, with roughly half its members reported to have ties to peptide businesses or clinics [5] (verified).
98. At the meeting on 23 and 24 July 2026 the committee recommended six peptides for addition, including all three components of this blend, each passing 8 to 6 with one abstention, and declined one [5] (verified). Those recommendations are advisory only. The agency must still decide whether to accept them, publish a proposed rule, take comments and issue a final rule, with no statutory deadline; for scale, a 2016 proposed rule on ten bulk substances was not finalised until 2019 [5] (verified).
99. The live development is an interim route. An Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides went to the White House budget office on 14 October 2026 and had not been issued as of mid-October. Its expected shape is temporary enforcement discretion for state-licensed 503A pharmacies compounding for individual patients on prescription; it would approve nothing, would not cover research-use-only, supplement or outsourcing-facility channels, could be restricted by route, and could be narrowed or withdrawn without rulemaking [5] (reported). Until it is published, nobody can say whether a three-peptide blend would fall inside it.
100. In sport, BPC-157 is named explicitly on the 2026 Prohibited List and thymosin beta-4 and its derivatives are named under peptide hormones and growth factors, both prohibited at all times; KPV is not named and falls under the catch-all class for substances without approval for human use [6] (verified for the two named, inferred for the third).

### Open questions [mixed]
101. Does the combination do anything the components do not? The one direct test, in two of the three, found no added benefit and a numerically lower peak load than either alone [1]. That needs repeating at other doses and time points before it is treated as settled, in either direction.
102. Why would two repair peptides fail to add? Overlapping downstream pathways and a ceiling effect are the obvious candidates, and the study that raised them could not test either [1].
103. What has KPV got to do with tendon repair? It was nominated as a topical cream, has no injectable human data, and has never been studied alongside the other two [4].
104. Does the vial contain the fragment or the full protein? Regulators say they are different substances [3], and no public certificate of analysis for either common ratio was found.
105. What is the real shelf life after reconstitution for a three-peptide solution whose components have different pH preferences and one of which oxidises? Unmeasured for the mixture.
106. Would a blend fall inside the interim compounding policy once published? Unknowable until it appears, and a mixture is exactly the case such a policy may not address [5].
