# Gonadorelin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/gonadorelin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: gnrh lhrh, Factrel, Lutrepulse, Hoe 471, AY 24031, RU 19847, Abbott 41070, LH-RH, gonadorelin acetate, gonadorelin hydrochloride
2. Origin: Made by the body
3. Sequence: QHWSYGLRPG (10 amino acids). First link is pyroglutamate, a curled ring, shown as Q; amide end
4. Legal and approval status: Was FDA-approved (Factrel/Lutrepulse); now mostly compounded. Not a controlled substance.

## Summary
5. Gonadorelin is synthetic GnRH, the brain's ten-amino-acid signal to the reproductive system, once FDA-approved and now mostly compounded. Pulsed delivery switches on LH and FSH release, and men on testosterone therapy use it in place of HCG to keep testicular size, natural testosterone and fertility. Its very short half-life demands frequent injections, and steady exposure paradoxically switches the system off.
6. Punch line: Synthetic GnRH, the brain's LH and FSH trigger. An HCG swap on TRT; needs frequent, pulsed dosing.
7. Used for: Keep testes working on TRT

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Connection & desire: Supports natural testosterone
9. Endocrine & growth: Pulses switch on the reproductive axis

## How it works
10. Pulses switch on LH and FSH release · GnRH receptor, pituitary · role Endocrine & growth
   Gonadorelin is synthetic GnRH. It binds GnRH receptors on pituitary gonadotroph cells, which then release LH and FSH. Its half-life is minutes, so it acts as a brief pulse, like the natural hormone. Pulsed delivery restores fertility in people whose hypothalamus fails to make GnRH.
11. Steady exposure switches the axis off · Receptor desensitization · role Endocrine & growth
   Steady exposure to GnRH desensitises its receptors, and the pituitary stops releasing LH and FSH. Long-acting GnRH drugs use this on purpose to suppress hormones. The timing of doses decides whether gonadorelin switches the axis on or off.
12. LH keeps testes making testosterone · Leydig cell stimulation · role Connection & desire
   LH drives Leydig cells in the testes to make testosterone. On testosterone therapy the body's own LH falls, so these cells go quiet. Gonadorelin aims to keep them active, a use supported by user reports rather than formal trials in men on TRT.
13. Helps keep testes size and function on TRT · FSH, spermatogenesis · role Reproductive
   FSH supports Sertoli cells, which nurse sperm production. Keeping LH and FSH active helps preserve testicular size and fertility while on testosterone. Men use gonadorelin in place of HCG for this, and user reports describe it as effective but demanding to dose.
14. Mechanism overview: Gonadorelin is synthetic GnRH, the ten amino acid hypothalamic hormone. It binds GnRH receptors on pituitary gonadotroph cells, which release LH and FSH. LH drives Leydig cells in the testes to make testosterone; FSH supports Sertoli cells and sperm production. The pattern matters: pulses keep the receptors responsive, while steady exposure desensitises them and shuts the axis down. Long-acting GnRH agonists exploit exactly that to suppress hormones. Gonadorelin's half-life is minutes, so frequent dosing is needed to keep pulses meaningful.

## Who shouldn't take it
37. Men with prostate cancer or hormone-sensitive tumours: Pulses raise LH and testosterone and can flare hormone-driven tumours. (inferred)
38. People who reacted to it before: Anaphylaxis reported after repeated doses. (reported)
39. Drug-tested athletes (men): Named on the WADA list as an LH-releasing factor. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
40. TRT: Testosterone therapy shuts down the body's own LH and FSH, so the testes shrink and fertility drops. Gonadorelin restores those pituitary signals to keep the testes working. Men use it alongside TRT based on its pulsed fertility record, but this pairing rests on user reports, not trials.

## Where it comes from
41. Synthetic GnRH itself, the brain's ten-link signal to the reproductive system. Its discovery shared the 1977 Nobel Prize between rivals Guillemin and Schally. Pulses switch the system on; steady exposure switches it off.
42. First studied for: A diagnostic of pituitary function and a fertility treatment; also used (continuously) to suppress hormones.
43. Name story: The official name for synthetic GnRH, the brain hormone that tells the pituitary to release LH and FSH. "-relin" is the stem for releasing hormones; decoding its structure helped earn Schally and Guillemin the 1977 Nobel Prize. [S4] (verified)
44. 1971: GnRH structure decoded
45. 1977: Nobel Prize shared by rivals Guillemin and Schally
46. 1980s: Approved as Factrel and Lutrepulse for testing and fertility
47. Today: Mostly compounded; popular HCG swap in TRT
