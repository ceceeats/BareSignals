# SS-31

## Identity
1. Also called: elamipretide, Forzinity, Bendavia, MTP-131
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: RYKF (4 amino acids). D-Arg shown as R; Dmt (dimethyl tyrosine) shown as Y; amide end
4. Shown with an FDA approved badge
5. Legal and approval status: Elamipretide (Forzinity) received FDA accelerated approval for Barth syndrome on 19 September 2025. Every other use is unapproved, and grey-market SS-31 is not the approved product.

## Summary
6. SS-31 (elamipretide) is a synthetic four-amino-acid peptide that concentrates in the inner mitochondrial membrane by binding cardiolipin, stabilizing the machinery that makes cellular energy. It is aimed at mitochondrial disease and heart failure, and received FDA accelerated approval for Barth syndrome in 2025. Other trials were mixed or missed their endpoint, and grey-market users report energy and recovery, with injection-site reactions.
7. Punch line: Elamipretide. Binds the inner mitochondrial membrane to steady energy output; FDA approved for Barth syndrome.
8. Used for: Barth syndrome; mito energy

## How it works
15. Sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane · Cardiolipin binding · role Cellular energy
   SS-31 (elamipretide) is a small peptide that crosses cell membranes and concentrates in the inner mitochondrial membrane. There it binds cardiolipin, a fat that anchors and organises the energy-making machinery. This targeting is well shown in cell and animal studies.
16. Steadies the energy-making machinery · Electron transport chain · role Cellular energy
   By stabilising cardiolipin, SS-31 keeps the folds of the inner membrane (cristae) intact and the electron transport chain complexes working together. Electron flow is more efficient and ATP output rises. This is shown in models of heart, kidney and muscle.
17. Cuts mitochondrial oxidative stress · ROS down · role Cellular protection
   More efficient electron flow means fewer electrons leak out to form reactive oxygen species, the damaging by-products of energy production. Lower oxidative stress protects mitochondria and the cells around them. This is shown in animal and cell studies.
18. Improves muscle strength in Barth syndrome · FDA approval 2025 · role Muscle & performance
   Barth syndrome is caused by defective cardiolipin, which makes it the clearest target. The FDA gave accelerated approval in 2025 for muscle strength, but the randomised phase of the 12-person trial missed its endpoints; gains appeared only in a long open-label extension. A confirmatory trial is required.
19. Protects heart cells in injury models · Cardiac mitochondria · role Heart & vessels
   In heart injury models SS-31 protects cardiac mitochondria and limits cell damage. Animal results for heart and kidney are strong. Human heart failure trials gave mixed results, so the animal benefit has not clearly carried over.
20. Mechanism overview: SS-31, elamipretide, is a tetrapeptide of alternating aromatic and basic residues that crosses cell membranes and concentrates in the inner mitochondrial membrane. There it binds cardiolipin, the phospholipid that anchors and organises the electron transport chain complexes. Stabilising cardiolipin keeps cristae intact and electron flow efficient, raising ATP output and cutting the electron leak that forms reactive oxygen species. Barth syndrome is a disease of defective cardiolipin, which makes it the clearest target. In models it protects heart, kidney and muscle mitochondria.

## Who shouldn't take it
46. People with severe kidney impairment: Elamipretide is cleared by the kidneys; the approved label lowers the dose. (reported)
47. People prone to injection-site skin reactions: Injection-site reactions were the most common side effect in trials. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
48. Humanin: SS-31 protects the energy machinery inside mitochondria, while humanin, a peptide made by mitochondria, blocks cell-death signals and improves insulin sensitivity in rodents. People pair them for mitochondrial ageing from two angles. The pair has not been tested together.
49. MOTS-c: SS-31 keeps the mitochondrial membrane and energy chain intact, while MOTS-c, another mitochondrial signal, shifts metabolism through AMPK in mice. People stack them for energy and metabolic ageing. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
50. Tesofensine: These serve different goals: tesofensine cuts appetite by keeping noradrenaline, serotonin and dopamine around longer, while SS-31 targets mitochondrial energy. Some users pair them for energy during weight loss. There is no data on the combination, and tesofensine raises heart rate.

## Where it comes from
51. A synthetic 4-amino-acid peptide (elamipretide/MTP-131) that concentrates in the inner mitochondrial membrane by binding cardiolipin, stabilizing the machinery that makes cellular energy.
52. First studied for: Mitochondrial diseases and heart failure, by protecting energy production in damaged tissue.
53. Name story: SS stands for Szeto–Schiller: Hazel Szeto (Cornell) and Peter Schiller (IRCM Montreal), who designed the series. It became elamipretide and in September 2025 Forzinity, the first FDA-approved Barth syndrome drug. [S8] (verified)
54. 2000s: Mitochondria-targeted peptide; strong animal heart and kidney data
55. 2010s to 2020s: Trials in heart failure, myopathy, dry AMD; myopathy Phase 3 misses
56. Sep 2025: Accelerated FDA approval for Barth syndrome as Forzinity
57. Today: Niche approved drug; grey-market energy and recovery peptide

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### What the approval was actually based on, which is not what it sounds like [people]
58. This is the most important thing to understand about this compound, and the summary version gets it backwards.
59. The pivotal trial, TAZPOWER, enrolled 12 males with genetically confirmed Barth syndrome in a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design: six to drug and six to placebo for 12 weeks, a four-week washout, then crossover [1] (verified). The primary endpoints were six-minute walk distance and a total fatigue score.
60. In that randomised phase, there was no statistically significant difference in walk distance, no difference in fatigue score, and no improvement in knee extensor strength [1] (verified). The controlled portion of the pivotal trial missed everything it set out to measure.
61. The approval rests on what came afterward: a 168-week open-label extension, in which all participants received drug. Knee extensor strength first appeared to improve at week 36 but was not sustained. By week 168, all subjects showed significant improvement, with a median change of 63 newtons [1] (verified). Accelerated approval followed in September 2025 for improving muscle strength in patients weighing at least 30 kilograms, at 40 mg subcutaneously once daily, with a required post-approval randomised placebo-controlled confirmatory trial [1].
62. So the evidence is: twelve people, a failed randomised phase, and a benefit visible only in an uncontrolled extension after more than three years of continuous treatment. The agency accepted that for an ultra-rare fatal disease with no other treatment, which is a defensible regulatory judgement. It is not a demonstration that the molecule reliably improves muscle strength (verified).

### Why an open-label extension after three years is weak evidence, and why it was accepted anyway [people]
63. In an open-label extension everyone knows they are on drug, there is no comparison group, and the people still enrolled at week 168 are the ones who chose to stay. Each of those independently biases toward an apparent benefit. Natural history also runs in the background: a measure taken repeatedly over three years in a dozen young people will move for reasons unrelated to treatment (inferred).
64. The strength signal's own shape is a caution. It appeared at week 36, then was not sustained, then was present at week 168 [1]. A genuine pharmacological effect usually does not vanish and return; a noisy measure in a tiny sample does exactly that.
65. What makes the approval reasonable despite all that is the disease. Barth syndrome is a rare genetic disorder of cardiolipin, the specific phospholipid this molecule binds, and there was no approved treatment. Accelerated approval exists for precisely this situation: accept weaker evidence of a surrogate benefit, require a proper trial afterwards. The confirmatory trial is mandated and has not reported [1] (verified).
66. The practical consequence for anyone reading the approval as validation of the molecule's broader promise: it is the narrowest possible approval, in the one disease where the target is the known defect, on the weakest evidence the pathway permits.

### The broader programme, and what it failed to show [people]
67. This molecule has been tested in considerably more than its approved indication, and the pattern of those results is the most informative thing about it.
68. A Phase 3 trial in primary mitochondrial myopathy missed its main endpoint. Heart failure work produced mixed results. Dry age-related macular degeneration has been trialled (verified, as the card records). Extensive animal work shows protection of heart, kidney and muscle mitochondria.
69. That shape, strong and consistent preclinical protection followed by a failed Phase 3 in the most obvious human indication, is one of the most common patterns in drug development and usually means one of three things: the animal models do not capture the human disease, the exposure achieved in people is insufficient at the target, or the endpoint cannot detect the effect size present (inferred). None of those has been established here.
70. The mechanism remains the compound's strongest asset. Binding cardiolipin in the inner mitochondrial membrane is a specific, structurally grounded proposal, and it explains why Barth syndrome, a disease of defective cardiolipin, is the clearest target. It also implies the opposite of a general-purpose benefit: the clearer the mechanism, the narrower the population in whom it should matter (inferred).

### What the label says about handling the drug, and why that is relevant outside the label [people]
71. Because an approved version exists, there is a characterised safety and handling profile here that no other unapproved peptide in this catalog has. It is worth reading for what it reveals.
72. Dose is 40 mg subcutaneously once daily, with a reduction to 20 mg daily in severe renal impairment, meaning estimated filtration below 30 and not on dialysis [1] (verified). That renal adjustment tells you the compound is cleared by the kidneys and that exposure rises when they are impaired.
73. The most common adverse events are injection-site reactions, specifically redness, induration, itching and pain. In the open-label extension, 2 of 10 participants discontinued because of injection-site reactions [1] (verified). For a daily subcutaneous injection, that is a meaningful discontinuation driver rather than a nuisance.
74. Warnings include hypersensitivity reactions, including serious allergic reactions. The formulation is not approved for neonates because it contains benzyl alcohol [1] (verified).
75. The label also makes an unusual statement about time course: measurable benefit was observed only after more than three years of treatment, and long-term use is needed [1]. That is a direct consequence of the evidence base described above, and it is irreconcilable with the way the compound is discussed as a short-course energy intervention (inferred).
76. Grey-market material is not the approved product, and the approved product's characterisation does not transfer to it (verified).

### Open questions [mixed]
77. Does the confirmatory trial replicate the strength benefit? That trial is required and has not reported, and it is the single result that would convert an accelerated approval into an established one.
78. Why did the randomised phase fail while the extension succeeded? Sample size, duration, measure choice and open-label bias are all candidates, and distinguishing them matters for every other indication.
79. Why did the large mitochondrial myopathy Phase 3 miss? Without an answer, there is no rational basis for expecting benefit in any other mitochondrial condition.
80. Does the mechanism predict benefit outside cardiolipin-defect disease? Binding cardiolipin is specific, which argues for a narrow population rather than a broad one.
81. What exposure does the approved daily dose achieve at the inner mitochondrial membrane, and is that the step the failed trials fell at? Not publicly established.
82. Does anything in the approval support the energy and recovery uses it is bought for? Nothing in the record addresses healthy people, short courses, or any outcome resembling those claims.
83. What happens over years in people without the disease? The only long-duration human data comes from twelve patients with a specific genetic defect.
