# Dihexa
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/dihexa
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: PNB-0408, N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6) aminohexanoic amide, N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6)-aminohexanoic amide, fosgonimeton (prodrug)
2. Origin: Lab-designed from angiotensin IV
3. Sequence: YI (2 amino acids). Hexanoyl cap on Tyr, then Ile and a 6-aminohexanoic amide tail; a modified dipeptide
4. Legal and approval status: Not approved; FDA nomination for compounding withdrawn; on the April 2026 list moved off FDA Category 2 (as of Oct 2026).

## Summary
5. Dihexa is a small modified dipeptide built from angiotensin IV at Washington State University. It boosts a growth factor signal (HGF and c-Met) that builds synapses, and was developed as an oral drug lead for Alzheimer's. Evidence is rat and cell work, no human study exists, and users report headaches and anxiety.
6. Punch line: Oral Alzheimer's lead built from angiotensin that grows new synapses. Rat data only; headaches are common.
7. Used for: Memory, focus

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Cognition: Memory restored in rats
9. Neural plasticity: New synapses in cells
10. Neural protection: Studied for dementia
11. Drive & mental stamina: Sharper focus reported
12. Cancer biology (backed only by animal or user reports): Activates a tumour growth pathway

## How it works
13. Boosts a growth factor signal that builds synapses · HGF / c-Met potentiation · role Neural plasticity
   Dihexa, a modified piece of angiotensin IV, boosts signalling by hepatocyte growth factor, HGF, through its receptor c-Met. In neurons this pathway grows dendritic spines, the contact points for synapses. This is shown in cell studies.
14. Helps HGF pair up and switch on its receptor · HGF dimerisation · role Neural plasticity
   HGF needs to pair up, or dimerise, before it can switch on c-Met. Dihexa binds HGF and helps those pairs form, so a small amount of HGF signals more strongly. This is the proposed mechanism from lab work by its developers.
15. Restores memory in impaired rats · Morris water maze, scopolamine model · role Cognition
   In rats with memory impaired by scopolamine, Dihexa restored performance in the Morris water maze, a standard memory test. The proposed basis is new synapse growth. No human study exists, and a related compound, fosgonimeton, failed its Alzheimer's trial.
16. Same pathway drives many cancers · c-Met overexpression in tumours · role Cancer biology
   HGF and c-Met signalling is overactive in many tumours, where it drives growth and spread. Boosting the same pathway carries a theoretical cancer and metastasis risk. No carcinogenicity study has been done, so the risk is untested.
17. Mechanism overview: Dihexa is a capped, modified dipeptide derived from angiotensin IV. It binds hepatocyte growth factor (HGF) and helps it form the dimers that switch on its receptor, c-Met. In neurons, HGF/c-Met signalling grows dendritic spines and new synapses, which is the proposed basis for memory rescue in rats. The same HGF/c-Met pathway is overactive in many tumours, so stronger signalling carries a theoretical cancer and metastasis risk; no carcinogenicity study exists.

## What has been tested
34. Cognition: Memory in impaired rats (animal)
35. Neural plasticity: Dendritic spine growth (cell)
36. Rat and mouse memory models and cell work on synapse growth; no human trials.
37. What it helps: Evidence is rat, mouse and cell work from the Harding lab and its spin-out companies. It rescued memory in impaired rats and grew synapses in cells. No human study exists, and the related compound fosgonimeton failed its Alzheimer's trial. Users take it for memory and focus, and headaches and anxiety are frequent complaints.
38. Trial: recent · Phase 2/3 · Alzheimer's disease · NCT04488419 · Athira Pharma. Dihexa itself was never tested in people; the related prodrug fosgonimeton failed its Phase 2/3 Alzheimer's trial (LIFT-AD) in September 2024 and development was paused. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
39. People with active or recent cancer: Boosts HGF/c-Met signalling, a known oncogene pathway. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
40. Semax: Dihexa works on synapse growth through HGF and c-Met, while Semax raises BDNF and monoamine turnover in rodents. People stack them for memory and focus from two growth-factor angles, but the pair has not been tested together.
41. Selank: Dihexa targets memory through synapse building, and Selank is used to calm anxiety without sedation. Users pair them to offset the anxiety some report on Dihexa, but the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
42. NA Semax Amidate: NA Semax Amidate is a longer-lasting Semax, assumed to raise BDNF and lift drive, while Dihexa works through HGF and c-Met for synapse growth. The pathways differ and could add up, but the pair has not been tested together.
43. PE-22-28: PE-22-28 blocks TREK-1 to make serotonin neurons fire more and was linked to new neuron growth in animals, while Dihexa grows synapses through c-Met. People pair them for mood plus memory, but this rests on animal and cell data only.

## Where it comes from
44. A small modified dipeptide built from angiotensin IV at Washington State University (Harding lab).
45. First studied for: An oral, brain-entering drug lead for Alzheimer's dementia.
46. Name story: The 'di-hexa' reads as two six-carbon hexanoic pieces capping a Tyr-Ile core: a hexanoyl at the front and an aminohexanoic amide at the back. Built at Washington State University from angiotensin IV; its phosphate prodrug became fosgonimeton. [S2] (inferred)
47. 2000s: Harding lab studies angiotensin IV fragments for memory
48. 2012–2013: Dihexa published: oral, brain-entering, synapse-building in rats
49. 2013: Press release claims 'seven orders of magnitude' stronger than BDNF, never compared in the paper
50. 2020s: Related drug fosgonimeton (Athira) fails in Alzheimer's; company pivots to ALS
51. Today: Sold as an oral nootropic; FDA compounding nomination withdrawn
