# TB-500 frag 1–4
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/tb-500-frag-1-4
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Ac-SDKP, Goralatide, seraspenide, AcSDKP, Thymosin β4 (1-4), N-acetyl-Ser-Asp-Lys-Pro
2. Origin: Fragment of a natural protein
3. Sequence: SDKP (4 amino acids). Acetylated start (Ac-SDKP), not shown
4. Legal and approval status: Unapproved research chemical. (Identity note: confirm your vial is 1-4, not 17-23.)

## Summary
5. TB-500 frag 1-4 is Ac-SDKP, the four-amino-acid N-terminal piece of thymosin beta-4 that an enzyme cuts off naturally. It is studied as the body's own anti-scarring and blood-vessel-growth signal, working by turning down TGF-beta signalling. Animal studies show anti-fibrotic effects in heart and kidney, but there is no human efficacy data, and it is often confused with the 17-23 fragment sold as TB-500.
6. Punch line: Ac-SDKP, the anti-scarring tip cut from thymosin beta-4. Protects heart and kidney in models; TB-500 does not.

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Heart & vessels: Protects heart from scarring in animals
8. Inflammatory signaling: Lowers inflammation
9. Tissue repair: Anti-scarring signal
10. Pain & sensory: Less pain reported

## How it works
11. Turns down scarring signals · TGF-beta, Smad · role Heart & vessels
   Ac-SDKP inhibits TGF-beta signalling through the Smad proteins, the main relay that tells fibroblasts to activate and lay down collagen. With that relay turned down, less scar tissue builds up after injury. Animal studies show protection against fibrosis in the heart and kidney.
12. Grows new blood vessels · Angiogenesis · role Tissue repair
   Ac-SDKP promotes angiogenesis, the sprouting of new blood vessels from existing ones. More vessels can bring oxygen and repair cells into damaged tissue. This has been shown in cell work and has not been tested as a treatment in people.
13. Broken down by ACE; rises on ACE inhibitors · ACE substrate · role Heart & vessels
   Angiotensin-converting enzyme, the same enzyme that ACE inhibitor blood pressure drugs block, is the main enzyme that breaks Ac-SDKP down. When ACE is blocked, Ac-SDKP levels rise. That is why it serves as a marker in ACE inhibitor research and may contribute to those drugs' anti-fibrotic effect.
14. Calms immune cell influx into damaged tissue · Macrophage infiltration · role Inflammatory signaling
   Ac-SDKP limits macrophage infiltration, the influx of immune cells that pile into injured tissue and release inflammatory and scar-promoting signals. Fewer of these cells means less ongoing inflammation feeding fibrosis. This has been shown in animal models of heart and kidney injury.
15. Keeps blood stem cells resting · Haematopoietic stem cells · role Immunity
   Ac-SDKP keeps haematopoietic stem cells, the bone marrow cells that make all blood cells, in a resting, quiescent state. It acts as a natural brake that holds them back from dividing. This comes from lab and animal work, with no human efficacy data.
16. Mechanism overview: This is Ac-SDKP, the N-terminal tetrapeptide of thymosin beta-4, a separate molecule from the 17 to 23 fragment. It inhibits TGF-beta signalling through Smad proteins, which turns down the fibroblast activation and collagen deposition behind scarring. It promotes new vessel growth and limits macrophage influx into injured tissue. It also keeps haematopoietic stem cells quiescent. ACE breaks it down, so levels rise in people taking ACE inhibitors, which may contribute to their anti-fibrotic effect.

## What has been tested
28. Organ function: Kidney fibrosis (animal)
29. Heart & vessels: Heart fibrosis and protection (animal)
30. Tissue repair: New vessel growth (cell)
31. Animal and cell work: Ac-SDKP reduces fibrosis by turning down TGF-beta, protects the heart and kidney, and promotes new vessels. It is also a marker used in ACE-inhibitor research. No grey-market human trials.
32. What it helps: Animal studies show clear anti-fibrotic effects in the heart and kidney, with protection against fibrosis and organ damage, and cell work shows new vessel growth. There is no human efficacy data for injected Ac-SDKP. A small user base takes it for recovery and anti-scarring, and vial identity is often confused with the 17 to 23 fragment.
33. Trial: none · Phase 1/2 · protecting bone marrow during chemotherapy · Ipsen. As goralatide it reached early chemotherapy trials in the 1990s, then was abandoned; nothing since. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
34. People on ACE inhibitors: ACE breaks down Ac-SDKP; ACE inhibitors already raise levels 4- to 5-fold. (reported)
35. People with active or recent cancer: Ac-SDKP promotes new blood vessel growth, which tumours exploit. (reported)

## Where it comes from
36. Ac-SDKP, the N-terminal four-amino-acid piece of thymosin beta-4, cut off naturally by an enzyme. A different molecule from TB-500 (the 17-23 fragment).
37. First studied for: Studied as the body's own anti-fibrotic and blood-vessel-growth signal.
38. Name story: Ac-SDKP is one-letter code for acetyl-Ser-Asp-Lys-Pro (INN goralatide): thymosin beta-4's first four residues, which the body snips off naturally. Sellers file it under 'TB-500' because that became the trade label for anything from Tβ4, though it shares nothing with the 17–23 'TB-500' heptapeptide. [S2] (verified)
39. 1980s: Ac-SDKP found as a natural blood-cell regulator
40. 1990s to 2000s: Studied as the body's own anti-scarring and vessel signal
41. Today: Research chemical; distinct from TB-500 (17 to 23)
