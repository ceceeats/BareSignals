# Epitalon
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/epitalon
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Epithalon, Epithalone, AEDG peptide, Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: AEDG (4 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug. The July 2026 FDA compounding advisory panel recommended it; non-binding, no rule yet.

## Summary
5. Epitalon is a synthetic four-amino-acid peptide designed from epithalamin, a pineal gland extract, in Khavinson's lab. It is the best-known longevity peptide, claimed to activate telomerase and restore melatonin rhythm, and users inject it in short cycles and report better sleep. The telomerase and lifespan claims rest on one research tradition and are not independently confirmed.
6. Punch line: The best-known longevity peptide, from a pineal extract. Short cycles for sleep; telomerase claims stay unproven.
7. Used for: Longevity, sleep

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Aging & senescence: Claimed telomerase activation
9. Sleep & rhythms: Restores melatonin rhythm
10. DNA & telomeres: Telomere claims from cell work
11. Mood: Calmer mood reported
12. Neural plasticity (backed only by animal or user reports): Brain cell studies
13. Pain & sensory (backed only by animal or user reports): Less aches reported
14. Cognition (backed only by animal or user reports): Clearer head reported

## How it works
15. Claimed to switch on telomerase · Telomerase, claimed · role Aging & senescence
   The Khavinson group claims Epitalon switches on telomerase, the enzyme that rebuilds telomeres, the protective caps on chromosome ends. This would let cells divide more times before ageing. It comes almost entirely from one group and has not been independently confirmed.
16. Claimed to lengthen telomeres in human cells · Telomere length, claimed · role DNA & telomeres
   In human cell cultures the same group reports telomeres lengthened after Epitalon. This is cell data only. The FDA's 2026 review found no human study of the synthetic peptide showing this in the body.
17. Claimed to restore melatonin rhythm · Pineal gland, claimed · role Sleep & rhythms
   Russian reports describe restored night-time melatonin rhythm and better sleep in older people. Many of these human results, including the mortality cohorts, used epithalamin, a crude pineal extract, not the synthetic tetrapeptide sold today.
18. Claimed to reopen silenced genes in aged cells · Chromatin, claimed · role DNA & telomeres
   In aged cells, chromatin, the packaging around DNA, becomes condensed and silences genes. The group claims Epitalon loosens it and reactivates those genes. This is cell work from the originating group without outside replication.
19. Mechanism overview: Epitalon is the tetrapeptide Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly, a synthetic version of a claimed active part of epithalamin, a pineal gland extract. The Khavinson group claims it activates telomerase and lengthens telomeres in human cell cultures, restores night-time melatonin rhythms in older people, and reopens condensed chromatin in aged cells, reactivating silenced genes. If true, this would slow replicative aging and restore circadian signalling. These claims come almost entirely from one group, and telomerase activation has not been independently confirmed.

## Who shouldn't take it
44. People with active or recent cancer: Activates telomerase in cell studies; most cancers rely on telomerase to keep dividing. (inferred)
45. People on melatonin or sedatives: Raised melatonin output in aged monkeys; effects may add up. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
46. Thymalin: Thymalin targets the ageing immune system through T and B cells, Epitalon targets the pineal gland and telomeres, and both come from the same Russian bioregulator programme. Users run them as a combined longevity course. The pair has not been tested together.
47. Pinealon: Both are Khavinson bioregulators, Epitalon for pineal rhythm and ageing, Pinealon for claimed neuron protection, so users run them together for sleep and brain ageing. Neither has independent trials. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
48. DSIP: Epitalon is used for claimed melatonin rhythm and DSIP for deeper sleep, approaching sleep from two angles. Both have weak, inconsistent evidence. The pair itself has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
49. A synthetic tetrapeptide (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly) designed from epithalamin, a peptide extract of the pineal gland, in Khavinson's lab; the flagship longevity peptide of the Russian school.
50. First studied for: Slowing aging, by (claimed) telomerase activation and pineal/melatonin normalization.
51. Name story: A synthetic four-residue stand-in (Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly, hence "AEDG") for Epithalamin, a crude extract of the pineal gland, which sits in the brain region called the epithalamus. Khavinson's St Petersburg lab designed it from the extract's amino acid makeup. [S7] (verified)
52. 1980s to 1990s: Designed from epithalamin, a pineal extract, in Khavinson's lab
53. 2000s: Multi-year Russian human cohorts on aging and mortality
54. Jul 2026: FDA compounding panel recommended it; non-binding, no rule yet
55. Today: The best-known longevity peptide

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from, and the substitution hidden in the story [lab]
56. Epithalamin, a crude polypeptide extract of cattle pineal gland, appears in accessible research from 1973. Epitalon, the four-residue Ala-Glu-Asp-Gly, was later synthesised from the extract's amino acid makeup, and its presence in human pineal extract was first reported only in 2017 [2] (verified).
57. That order matters more than any single study. The most quoted human results, the multi-year mortality cohorts, were done with the extract, not with the synthetic tetrapeptide sold today [4,5] (verified). Treating them as evidence for Epitalon assumes the four residues are the whole active ingredient of a crude extract. That has never been shown (inferred). When the FDA reviewed Epitalon in 2026, it found no human cohort or mortality study of the synthetic peptide at all [1] (verified).

### What is actually in a vial [lab]
58. The FDA found that one nominator's certificate listed a molecular formula matching neither the free base nor the acetate, and another submitted an acetate certificate under free-base identifiers [1] (verified). The acetate certificate that did exist reported 99.5% purity with total impurities up to 2% and single impurities up to 1%, none identified, and no bioburden, endotoxin or aggregate testing [1] (verified). No certificate at all was submitted for the free base.
59. Water solubility of the free base is limited, and the agency doubted that a 3 mg/mL product could be made in water [1]. Reconstituted solution is reported stable 2 to 7 days at 4 degrees [1] (verified).
60. Two identity traps are specific to this peptide. Eight stereoisomers are possible and only the all-L form has been studied, and market products have been checked by mass spectrometry to separate genuine material from the swapped-sequence isomer EADG [2] (verified). Products are sold as acetate or trifluoroacetate salts, which differ in weight and in what else ends up in the vial [2].

### Mechanism, one step at a time, with the model each step came from [mixed]
61. Telomerase, in the originating group's cells. Human fetal lung fibroblasts given 50 ng/mL for four days showed higher telomerase expression and activity and longer telomeres, and treated cultures kept dividing past passage 44 while controls stopped at 34 [1,2] (reported).
62. Telomerase, in an independent lab. A 2025 London university study is the first outside replication and is more complicated than either side tends to quote. In normal fibroblasts and mammary epithelial cells, three weeks at 1 microgram per millilitre raised telomerase activity about 4-fold and 26-fold and lengthened telomeres; four days did nothing. In two breast cancer lines, telomeres also lengthened, but telomerase activity did not rise; instead the alternative, telomerase-independent lengthening pathway (ALT) rose about 10-fold and 3-fold [3] (verified). The authors suggested this supports safe use in healthy people, a claim their experiments did not test [3].
63. Melatonin, in monkeys. Older rhesus monkeys given 2 micrograms per kilogram intramuscularly for 10 days had evening melatonin more than 4-fold higher and evening cortisol about 30% lower; younger monkeys showed no melatonin change [1] (verified). Yet in isolated rat pineal tissue, Epitalon at up to 100 micromolar did not increase melatonin release [1] (verified), so whatever raises melatonin probably does not act on the gland directly.
64. Chromatin, in cells. Decondensation of heterochromatin in lymphocytes from donors aged 76 to 80 is reported from the same group [5] (reported).

### What the body does with it [animals]
65. There is no pharmacokinetic data, including half-life, for either form in any species, people included [1] (verified). The only distribution work gave a fluorescent label to pregnant rabbits; signal appeared in liver, lungs and gut at one hour and weakly in brain, and the agency noted it does not prove the signal was the peptide [1]. The commonly quoted two-hour half-life has no source in the regulatory record (verified as absent).

### The human record [people]
66. For the synthetic peptide, the one randomised placebo-controlled study enrolled 75 women aged 40 to 50, gave about 0.5 mg a day under the tongue for 20 days, and found urinary melatonin metabolite about 1.6 to 1.7-fold higher plus changes in clock gene expression [1,2] (verified). Blinding was not specified, no sleep outcome was measured, and the reported mood improvement (83% against 25%) came without a described method [1]. A retinitis pigmentosa series of 162 patients used 5 micrograms per eye by injection beside the eyeball for 10 days [2] (reported).
67. For the extract, the cohorts are larger and older. A study of 266 people over 60 reported 1.6 to 1.8-fold lower mortality over six years, and up to 4.1-fold with the thymic extract added and yearly courses [5] (reported). A comparison of 39 elderly coronary patients on six courses of epithalamin over three years against 40 on standard therapy reported preserved endurance, normalised melatonin rhythm and lower mortality [4] (verified). The paper's own title says pituitary gland and 15-year follow-up while the abstract describes a pineal peptide over three years [4], which is the kind of inconsistency that should lower confidence.
68. No trial in insomnia, the use reviewed in 2026, has ever been run, and no trial has used injection under the skin [1] (verified). There is no established human dose for the synthetic peptide.

### Safety: what has been looked at, and what has not [mixed]
69. Absent: acute toxicity, repeat-dose toxicity, reproductive toxicity and two-year carcinogenicity [1] (verified). The mouse tumour and lifespan studies used females only, one fixed dose, and at most about 22 weeks of exposure, which the agency said cannot assess cancer risk [1]. No adverse event reports reached the FDA or its food complaint system [1].
70. The cancer question is the honest centre of this compound. A drug designed to lengthen telomeres pushes in the direction that unusually long telomeres point to in population studies, where they are linked to several cancers, which the agency named as an unresolved concern [1] (verified). The independent 2025 data add a specific wrinkle: in cancer cells it switched on a different lengthening pathway [3]. Neither finding shows harm; together they explain why long-term safety data are not optional here (inferred).

### Who produced the evidence [lab]
71. Nearly all pharmacology and every human study come from one St Petersburg group and its partners, much of it in Russian and not verified by the FDA [1,2] (verified). The 2025 review that catalogued this work came from an independent Warsaw pharmacy faculty with no external funding, and it lists inconsistent reporting, very low or non-monotonic doses and unreplicated 1990s data among the limitations [2] (verified). The 2025 telomere replication is genuinely independent [3]. That makes two outside groups engaging seriously, which is more than most compounds of this kind have.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
72. Not an approved drug anywhere, with no EMA-authorised product and no approval in the major European markets or Canada and Australia [1] (verified). In 2024 it sat in the US do-not-compound category [10]. On 24 July 2026 the advisory committee recommended adding it to the bulks list for insomnia, against staff advice [1,6]. The count is reported as 7 to 4 with one abstention by one trade summary and 7 to 5 by a law firm [6,7] (reported); either way it is advisory and needs rulemaking. An interim access policy for certain peptides went to White House review on 14 October 2026, with covered peptides not public [8] (verified).
73. Sport: not named, but with no government approval it falls in the catch-all non-approved class, prohibited at all times [9] (inferred application, verified wording).

### Open questions [mixed]
74. Is the synthetic tetrapeptide the active ingredient of the extract that produced the cohort results? Never tested.
75. What happens to telomeres and cancer risk over years of courses? Unstudied in any species past about five months.
76. Does it improve sleep, measured as sleep? No trial has looked.
77. What reaches the blood after injection or under the tongue, and for how long? No data exist.
