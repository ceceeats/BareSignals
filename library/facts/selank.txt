# Selank
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/selank
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Selanc, Селанк, TKPRPGP
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: TKPRPGP (7 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Approved and used in Russia; research chemical elsewhere. Not FDA-approved.

## Summary
5. Selank is a lab-made peptide built from tuftsin, a natural immune peptide, with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail added to resist breakdown. It was developed in Russia as a non-sedating anxiety treatment and is thought to work partly by slowing the breakdown of calming enkephalins and raising BDNF. Russian studies report calm and focus, but the evidence is almost entirely Russian and unconfirmed elsewhere.
6. Punch line: Russian anti-anxiety nasal spray built from an immune peptide. Calm without sedation; often stacked with Semax.
7. Used for: Anxiety, focus, calm

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Mood: Calm without sedation
9. Immunity: Built from an immune peptide
10. Cognition: Clearer focus reported
11. Sleep & rhythms: Better sleep reported
12. Inflammatory signaling (backed only by animal or user reports): Shifts inflammation signals

## How it works
13. Immune peptide tuftsin, made longer-lasting · Tuftsin analogue · role Immunity
   Selank is tuftsin, a four amino acid immune fragment of antibody protein, with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail added to slow breakdown. No receptor for it has been identified, so its effects are measured downstream changes rather than a known binding target.
14. Calms anxiety without sedation, mechanism debated · GABA system, claimed · role Mood
   Its calming effect is attributed to tuning the GABA system, the brain's main brake. That claim rests on gene-expression patterns in rats and human cell lines, not receptor binding, and the cell work only partly supports it. Small Russian trials report anxiety relief without sedation, with no placebo arm.
15. Raises a brain growth factor · BDNF, reported · role Cognition
   Selank is reported to raise BDNF, a growth factor that supports nerve connections and learning, in the rat hippocampus. This comes from Russian work that is hard to access in full. No human study has shown it raises brain BDNF.
16. Slows breakdown of calming enkephalins · Enkephalinase inhibition · role Mood
   In human serum in a test tube, Selank blocked enkephalinase, the enzymes that break down enkephalins, the body's own calming opioid peptides. One trial found blood enkephalin lasted longer on Selank. The lab concentration needed is high, and brain levels are unknown.
17. Shifts immune cytokine balance · IL-6, Th1/Th2 genes · role Immunity
   Through its tuftsin portion, Selank shifts immune gene activity, lowering IL-6, an inflammatory messenger, and changing the Th1/Th2 balance between immune arms. These findings come from Russian work in patients' blood cells and in rats, without independent replication.
18. Mechanism overview: Selank is a synthetic analogue of tuftsin, an immune tetrapeptide, extended with Pro-Gly-Pro to resist breakdown. Its anti-anxiety action is attributed to positive modulation of GABA-A signalling, a claim that is debated and comes mainly from Russian groups. It inhibits enkephalinase, slowing breakdown of calming enkephalins, and is reported to raise BDNF. Through the tuftsin portion it shifts IL-6 and Th1/Th2 gene expression. The combination is meant to calm anxiety without sedation or dependence.

## What has been tested
36. Mood: Anxiety vs benzodiazepines (human)
37. Cognition: Focus and mild cognitive effects (human)
38. Neural plasticity: BDNF levels (human)
39. Immunity: Immune markers (human)
40. Russian human studies report anxiety reduction comparable to benzodiazepines without sedation or dependence, plus effects on BDNF and immune markers; little Western replication.
41. What it helps: Russian human studies report anxiety relief comparable to benzodiazepines, without sedation or dependence, plus focus and mild cognitive gains. Evidence is almost entirely Russian, and durability is unconfirmed. Users take it nasally or by injection and report calm, focus and better sleep, and it is generally well tolerated.
42. Trial: none. Approved in Russia for anxiety; published Russian trials exist but none found in registries, and Russian registry checks were not possible. (checked 2026-10-23) [S5] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
43. People on benzodiazepines or other sedatives: Selank modulates GABA-A receptors; effects may stack. (inferred)
44. People on opioids: Selank slows enkephalin breakdown, prolonging the body’s own opioids. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
45. Semax: Semax brings drive and focus through growth factor and dopamine effects, Selank brings calm through enkephalin and claimed GABA effects, so one offsets the other's edge. Sold pre-mixed as Semax + Selank, but the pair has not been tested together in trials.
46. Dihexa: Dihexa targets memory through the HGF/c-Met synapse pathway in rats, Selank adds calm and focus from a different system. People pair them for a calm but sharp cognitive stack. The pairing is user logic, not trial data, and Dihexa has no human data.

## Where it comes from
47. The natural immune peptide tuftsin with a stabilizing Pro-Gly-Pro tail added, developed in Russia as an anxiety treatment.
48. First studied for: Anxiety and related mood disorders, as a non-sedating anxiolytic.
49. Name story: Built from tuftsin (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg), a small immune peptide snipped from antibodies, plus a Pro-Gly-Pro tail, by Moscow's Institute of Molecular Genetics. The name's origin is not documented in English sources. [S6] (verified)
50. 1990s to 2000s: Tuftsin plus a Pro-Gly-Pro tail, built in Russia for anxiety
51. Today: Approved in Russia as a non-sedating anxiolytic; research chemical elsewhere

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from, and the gap in the story [lab]
52. Selank was designed at the Institute of Molecular Genetics of the Russian Academy of Sciences together with the Zakusov Research Institute of Pharmacology in Moscow [1,2] (verified). The starting point was tuftsin, a four-residue fragment of human immunoglobulin G with immune-stimulating activity. The Pro-Gly-Pro tail was added to slow breakdown and stretch the duration of action [1] (verified).
53. The part of the story that is never explained in print is the jump from an immune fragment to an anti-anxiety drug. The early clinical claim, that it calms like a tranquiliser without the amnesia, withdrawal or dependence of benzodiazepines, is cited in later papers to Russian reports from 1990 and 1998 rather than shown in an accessible trial [1] (reported). No receptor for the molecule has been identified, so every mechanism below is an observed downstream change, not a binding target.

### What the licensed product is, and what circulates instead [lab]
54. The registered Russian medicine is a 0.15% nasal drop: 1.5 mg of peptide per millilitre, preserved with methylparaben, in purified water. The registration holder and manufacturer is a single Moscow company, and the label restricts it to adults, excludes pregnancy and breastfeeding because no studies were done, and runs it as a 14-day course [3] (verified).
55. What circulates elsewhere is mostly lyophilised powder, often sold as an acetate salt or as modified versions such as an amidated or acetylated form. None of those modified forms is the registered drug, and none has its own published clinical record (inferred). In the US nomination system, a nomination for "Selank acetate (TP-7)" was removed from the do-not-compound category after the nominator said it had meant a different substance [8] (verified). That is a small detail with a large meaning: even the people asking regulators to allow it were unsure what they were naming.
56. Unlike Semax, Selank was not on the July 2026 advisory committee agenda, so no regulator has published a review of its purity, impurities or endotoxin testing [9] (verified as absent).

### Mechanism, one step at a time, with the model each step came from [mixed]
57. Enzyme inhibition, in human serum in a test tube. Selank inhibited the enzymes that break down enkephalins with an IC50 of about 20 micromolar, against 10 micromolar for Semax; its pentapeptide fragment also worked, shorter fragments did not [4] (verified). The catch is concentration. Twenty micromolar is a high, lab-scale level, and nothing in the record shows a nasal dose producing anything close to it in blood or brain (inferred).
58. The GABA question, in rats. One intranasal dose of 300 micrograms per kilogram changed expression of 45 neurotransmission genes in rat frontal cortex at one hour, and the pattern correlated strongly with the pattern GABA itself produced (r = 0.86). By three hours the correlation had turned slightly negative (r = minus 0.39), and the biggest single change was an orexin gene rising about 128-fold [1] (verified). The authors read this as possible allosteric action on GABA signalling and asked for more work.
59. The GABA question, in human cells. In human neuroblastoma cells that carry GABA-A receptors, Selank alone changed none of 69 measured genes, but it almost completely suppressed the changes GABA caused when the two were given together [2] (verified). The authors concluded the data only partially support a GABA-A mechanism. So the most-repeated claim about Selank, that it is a GABA-A modulator, rests on gene-expression patterns, not on receptor binding or electrophysiology.
60. Immune and growth-factor effects, in people and rats. Suppression of IL-6 gene expression in blood cells of depressed patients, a shifted Th1/Th2 cytokine balance, and a rapid rise in hippocampal BDNF in rats are all reported in Russian-language work [11] (reported). These were not opened in full for this read.

### What the body does with it [people]
61. The Russian label gives the only human-facing pharmacokinetic figures: absolute bioavailability of 92.8% after nasal use, detection in plasma about 30 seconds after dosing, plasma levels falling away over 5 to 5.5 minutes, and neither intact drug nor metabolites found in 24-hour urine because tissue peptidases break it down completely [3] (verified). The label says it reaches brain tissue but gives no figure for how much.
62. The label does not state a half-life. The commonly quoted figure of about two minutes is not on it (verified as absent). Whatever the exact number, exposure is measured in minutes, while the reported effects are said to last days, including about a week after the last dose in one trial [6]. That gap is either a sign of downstream signalling outlasting the drug, or a sign the effect is softer than reported. Nothing in the record separates the two (inferred).

### The human record, read closely [people]
63. The core trial randomised 62 people with generalised anxiety disorder or neurasthenia to Selank (30) or the benzodiazepine medazepam (32), scored on Hamilton, Zung and clinical global scales. Anxiety relief was similar in both arms, and Selank added anti-fatigue and mildly stimulating effects [5] (verified). The trial also measured how fast blood broke down leu-enkephalin; that half-life was short at baseline and lengthened on Selank, which fits the enzyme mechanism above [5]. There was no placebo arm.
64. A 2014 comparison against phenazepam in 60 people with phobic-anxiety and somatoform disorders reported pronounced anti-anxiety and mild nootropic effects, lasting about a week after the last dose, with better quality of life [6] (verified). A 2012 congress abstract followed 20 people with anxiety on 2.7 mg a day: 40% improved within three days (Hamilton score 20.3 to 7.0), the rest gradually by day 14 (16.1 to 6.2), and the fast responders showed EEG changes after a single dose [7] (verified). It had no control group.
65. Put together: every accessible human study is small, Russian, either uncontrolled or compared against another active drug, and none has a placebo arm. Comparing against a benzodiazepine and finding "similar" results without a placebo cannot tell a working drug from a strong placebo response, which is large in anxiety trials (inferred). There is no registered trial outside Russia and no established dose outside the Russian label.

### Safety: what is known, and what is just absent [people]
66. The label lists allergic reactions, including delayed ones, and an unpleasant taste when drops run into the throat, both of unknown frequency, and records no overdose cases [3] (verified). That is a short list, and short lists in old national registrations often reflect thin monitoring rather than proven safety (inferred).
67. No English-language repeat-dose toxicology, reproductive toxicology or carcinogenicity study was located. Use in pregnancy, breastfeeding and under 18 is excluded on the label precisely because it was never studied [3] (verified). Higher-than-label doses, injection, and modified forms have no safety data at all.

### Who produced the evidence [lab]
68. The developer institute appears on both sides of the record. The 2014 clinical comparison lists among its authors the developer group's senior chemist and the scientists who ran the 2001 enzyme study [4,6] (verified). The gene-expression work comes from the same institute [1,2], and the 2012 trial from the co-developing pharmacology institute [7]. This is normal for a national drug programme. It does mean there is no independent replication of either the clinical or mechanistic findings in the accessible literature (verified as absent).

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
69. Russia: a registered prescription medicine as 0.15% nasal drops, for anxiety states and adjustment disorders in adults [3] (verified). US: not approved; outside any compounding category since its nomination was withdrawn; not reviewed by the July 2026 advisory committee [8,9] (verified).
70. Sport: it is not named on the 2026 Prohibited List. The catch-all non-approved class covers substances with no current approval by any government health authority for human use [10]. Selank does hold a national approval, so that class arguably does not fit it, but nothing in the list settles the point (inferred). Athletes cannot assume it is permitted.

### Open questions [mixed]
71. Does it beat placebo? No accessible trial asks. One adequately sized placebo-controlled study would matter more than every result above.
72. How does a peptide that is gone from plasma in minutes produce effects reported to last a week? Unexplained.
73. Is it a GABA-A modulator at all? Binding or electrophysiology data would answer this; gene-expression patterns cannot.
74. Do modified forms behave like the registered drug? Unstudied.
