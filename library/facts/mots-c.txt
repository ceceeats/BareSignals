# MOTS-c
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/mots-c
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: MOTSc, mitochondrial ORF of the 12S rRNA-c
2. Origin: Made by the body
3. Sequence: MRWQEMGYIFYPRKLR (16 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Unapproved research chemical. The July 2026 FDA compounding advisory panel recommended it; the vote is non-binding and needs a rule before it takes effect.

## Summary
5. MOTS-c is a peptide encoded in the mitochondria's own DNA, which in mice behaves like exercise in a molecule by acting on AMPK. It is studied for metabolic health, insulin sensitivity and endurance. Evidence is mainly mouse, with no human trial of MOTS-c itself; users report energy, endurance and fat loss, often stacking it with 5-Amino-1MQ and NAD+.
6. Punch line: Exercise in a molecule, encoded in mitochondrial DNA. Mouse data on endurance and fat; often stacked with NAD+.
7. Used for: Energy, endurance, fat loss

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Cellular energy: Exercise-like signal in cells
9. Glucose & insulin: Better sugar handling in mice
10. Fat metabolism: Resists weight gain in mice
11. Drive & mental stamina: More endurance reported
12. Muscle & performance (backed only by animal or user reports): Older mice ran farther
13. Immunity (backed only by animal or user reports): Immune balance in mice
14. Pain & sensory (backed only by animal or user reports): Less soreness reported

## How it works
15. Mimics the metabolic effect of exercise · AMPK activation · role Cellular energy
   MOTS-c activates AMPK, the cell's main energy sensor, indirectly. Once on, AMPK pushes cells toward glucose uptake and fat burning, the same shift exercise produces. This is shown in mice and cells, largely from one lab, and no receptor has been identified.
16. Moves to the nucleus under stress · Stress-response genes · role Cellular energy
   Under metabolic stress, MOTS-c moves from the mitochondria into the nucleus and regulates stress-response genes. This makes it a message from the mitochondria to the rest of the cell. It is shown in cell studies and was a key part of the original discovery.
17. Improves muscle glucose uptake, in mice · Folate cycle, AICAR · role Glucose & insulin
   MOTS-c inhibits the folate cycle and the purine pathway tied to it, which builds up AICAR, a natural AMP mimic. AICAR switches on AMPK, improving muscle glucose uptake. This is shown in mice and remains the proposed model, not a settled pathway.
18. Resists diet-induced fat gain, in mice · Fat oxidation · role Fat metabolism
   In mice fed a high-fat diet, MOTS-c raised fat oxidation and limited weight gain while keeping glucose handling better. The effect fits its AMPK activation. No completed human trial of MOTS-c exists; an analogue, CB4211, finished a small early trial in fatty liver and obesity.
19. Rises with exercise, falls with age · mtDNA 12S rRNA gene · role Muscle & performance
   MOTS-c is encoded inside the mitochondrial 12S rRNA gene, part of the mitochondria's own DNA. Blood levels rise with exercise and fall with age in people. These are observational human data; they do not show that giving it reverses ageing.
20. Mechanism overview: MOTS-c is a 16 amino acid peptide encoded inside the mitochondrial 12S rRNA gene. It inhibits the folate cycle, which builds up AICAR, an AMP mimic that activates AMPK. AMPK switches cells toward glucose uptake and fat oxidation, the same shift exercise produces. Under metabolic stress MOTS-c moves into the nucleus and regulates stress-response genes. Blood levels rise with exercise and fall with age. Most mechanism work comes from one lab and mouse models.

## Who shouldn't take it
46. Insulin or sulfonylurea users: Raises insulin sensitivity via AMPK; adding it to glucose-lowering drugs can drop sugar too far. (inferred)
47. Drug-tested athletes: WADA S4.4 bans AMPK activators as metabolic modulators; MOTS-c works through AMPK. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
48. 5-Amino-1MQ: MOTS-c activates AMPK for fat burning and glucose uptake, while 5-Amino-1MQ blocks the fat cell enzyme NNMT, which spares NAD. People stack them for fat loss, and they are sold pre-mixed as NAD+ + MOTS-c + 5-Amino-1MQ. Both rest on mouse data, and the combination is untested.
49. NAD+: MOTS-c pushes cells toward fuel use through AMPK, while NAD+ is the coenzyme that energy metabolism and sirtuins run on. They are sold pre-mixed as NAD+ + MOTS-c + 5-Amino-1MQ for energy and metabolic ageing. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
50. BPC-157: MOTS-c works on whole-body metabolism and endurance in mice, while BPC-157 targets tendon and tissue repair in rats. People combine them in training stacks for performance plus recovery. The pair has not been tested together.
51. TB-500 frag 17–23: MOTS-c works on energy and endurance through AMPK, while the TB-500 fragment is used for injury repair. People run them together in training and recovery stacks. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
52. GHK-Cu: MOTS-c targets metabolism and energy, while GHK-Cu targets collagen and skin repair. People combine them in longevity stacks covering two separate systems. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
53. Found in 2015 by Changhan Lee in Pinchas Cohen's USC lab, hiding inside the mitochondria's own DNA in a ribosome gene. In mice it behaves like exercise in a molecule, acting on AMPK and metabolic stress pathways.
54. First studied for: Studied for metabolic health, insulin sensitivity and exercise capacity.
55. Name story: MOTS-c = Mitochondrial Open reading frame of the Twelve S rRNA, type c. It is coded inside mitochondrial DNA, hidden within the 12S rRNA gene. [S5] (verified)

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Why the discovery was interesting, independent of whether the compound works [lab]
60. The 2015 paper that introduced this peptide made a claim about biology that was more startling than any claim about therapy: that the mitochondrial genome, long treated as a small set of genes for respiratory-chain components, also encodes short signalling peptides that act on the rest of the cell [1] (verified).
61. That reframes the mitochondrion from a power plant taking orders to a compartment that sends messages outward. The idea has held up well enough to generate a field, and it is the main reason this molecule attracts serious researchers rather than only enthusiasts.
62. The mechanism proposed in that paper is worth stating precisely, because the popular version compresses it into something simpler. The peptide was proposed to act on the folate-dependent one-carbon pool and to inhibit the de novo purine synthesis pathway tethered to it. Blocking that pathway causes an intermediate to accumulate, and that intermediate is itself a potent activator of the cell's main energy sensor. So the energy-sensor activation is proposed to be indirect, a downstream consequence of a metabolic bottleneck, not a direct binding event [1] (verified as the paper's proposed model).
63. The 2015 paper presents this as a proposed model in its own figure legend [1]. It is a hypothesis with supporting data, not a settled pathway, and no receptor has been identified.

### Why a short peptide encoded inside a ribosomal RNA gene is contested [lab]
64. There is a genuine scientific argument here that rarely surfaces in consumer material, and it is worth understanding because it bears on how seriously to take the biology.
65. The reading frame sits inside a gene whose product is a structural RNA, in a genome that uses a different genetic code from the nucleus and has its own translation machinery. For the peptide to be a real signalling molecule, a short open reading frame inside that RNA gene has to be translated, and the product has to leave the organelle and act elsewhere. Each of those steps is plausible and each has been questioned (verified that the mechanism is proposed rather than established).
66. This is not a reason to dismiss the molecule. Synthetic peptide given to an animal can have effects whether or not the body makes it the same way. It is a reason to treat the framing of this compound as a natural hormone that declines with age as the least secure part of the story, because that framing depends on the endogenous biology being exactly as proposed (inferred).
67. The observed associations, that circulating levels rise with exercise and fall with age, are correlations in human samples. They do not establish that supplying the peptide reproduces what exercise does, and the gap between an exercise-associated molecule and an exercise substitute is the whole question (inferred).

### What was actually measured in animals [animals]
68. The animal work targets skeletal muscle and metabolic handling, and the effects reported are metabolic rather than structural: better glucose handling, resistance to diet-induced obesity, greater running endurance (verified as the literature's consistent findings, in mice).
69. What is missing from the animal literature is the thing that would make the exercise analogy real. Exercise produces cardiovascular remodelling, mitochondrial biogenesis, bone loading, and effects on mood and sleep. The animal data here speak to glucose and fat handling and to endurance capacity, which is a subset (inferred).
70. There is also a dose-response and exposure gap shared with most peptides in this catalog. No pharmacokinetic data in any species was identified in the regulatory review, so there is no way to connect an injected amount to a tissue concentration, and the animal doses cannot be translated (verified as absent in the regulatory record [2]).

### The human record, and the analogue that was tested instead [people]
71. No completed trial of this peptide itself exists. The closest human evidence belongs to a different molecule: a modified analogue developed by a company, which completed a small early-phase study in fatty liver disease and obesity around 2021 (verified as the card records; details of that programme were not retrievable in this pass).
72. An analogue is a different compound. A developer modifies a natural sequence precisely because the natural one has properties, usually stability or exposure, that make it unsuitable as a drug. So the existence of an analogue programme is simultaneously evidence that the target interested a drug developer and evidence that the unmodified peptide was not considered the right molecule to develop (inferred).
73. There is no human efficacy data for the peptide, no human safety dataset, and no established human dose. Every circulating figure derives from community convention or from converting mouse doses (verified as absent).
74. The reported user experience, energy, drive and improved glucose readings, is the pattern one would expect from a compound acting on the cell's energy sensor, and it is also the pattern one would expect from almost any intervention undertaken deliberately by someone paying attention to their metabolic numbers. Self-reported energy is the least specific endpoint in this entire field (inferred).

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
75. In the July 2026 advisory round, agency staff proposed that neither the free base nor the acetate be added to the 503A compounding bulks list, applying the four standard criteria of characterisation, safety, effectiveness and history of compounding use [2] (verified). The original nomination had been withdrawn, and the agency proceeded anyway.
76. The committee recommended it regardless, by 7 to 5 with two abstentions on 23 July 2026, for obesity and osteoporosis, the indications the agency had reviewed [3] (verified). That margin is narrower and the abstentions more numerous than for the repair peptides considered the same week.
77. As with the others, the recommendation is advisory and changes nothing by itself. Addition requires formal notice-and-comment rulemaking, not begun and with no deadline [4] (verified). An interim enforcement-discretion policy covering certain peptides went to the White House budget office on 14 October 2026 and was unpublished as of mid-October, with its covered substances not public [4].
78. In sport, the position follows from the mechanism rather than from a named listing: metabolic modulators acting on the cell's main energy sensor fall within a prohibited class, so a compound whose entire proposed mechanism is activation of that sensor sits inside it (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
79. Is the peptide genuinely translated and secreted in humans, or is the therapeutic effect of synthetic peptide unrelated to an endogenous signalling system? The replacement framing depends entirely on the former.
80. Is energy-sensor activation really downstream of a purine-synthesis bottleneck? That is the proposed model in the original paper and remains a model.
81. Does anything reach muscle after injection? With no pharmacokinetic data in any species, this is unanswered for every route in use.
82. Why did development proceed with a modified analogue rather than the natural sequence? The answer would say a lot about the unmodified peptide's drug-likeness.
83. Do exercise-associated levels mean anything causal? Falling with age and rising with exercise is consistent with the peptide being a marker of mitochondrial activity rather than a driver of it.
84. What is the toxicology? None was identified in the regulatory review, so long-term safety is unopened rather than open.
85. Does it do anything measurable in a human? A small, short, placebo-controlled trial with glucose handling as the endpoint would settle more than another decade of mouse work.
