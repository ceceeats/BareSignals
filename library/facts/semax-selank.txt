# Semax + Selank
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/semax-selank
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Semax/Selank 20 mg, Semax/Selank 10 mg, Russian nootropic stack, Selank-Semax nasal spray
2. Origin: Blend of 2 peptides
3. Legal and approval status: Russia: both approved medicines (Semax on the national essential drug list since 2011). US: not approved; Semax removed from FDA Category 2 on 15 Apr 2026 and won an 8-5-1 PCAC vote in July 2026; Selank was not on that list. WADA: neither named; S0 applies.

## Summary
4. Semax + Selank combines two Russian heptapeptides: Semax, an ACTH(4-10) analogue, and Selank, a tuftsin analogue. People stack them for calm, focused thinking, with Semax raising BDNF and alertness and Selank slowing the breakdown of calming peptides. Each has Russian clinical data, but the pair has only one rat study and user reports. A fixed blend stops you adjusting each one, and Semax late in the day can disrupt sleep.
5. Punch line: The Russian nootropic stack, usually a morning nasal spray. Semax drives focus, Selank removes the jitter.
6. Used for: Focus with calm

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Cognition: Semax raises BDNF in animal brain
8. Mood: Selank calms anxiety (Russian trials)
9. Drive & mental stamina: Semax activating, dopamine-linked
10. Neural protection: Semax used after stroke in Russia
11. Sleep & rhythms (backed only by animal or user reports): Late Semax disrupts sleep
12. Immunity (backed only by animal or user reports): Selank shifts cytokine balance

## How it works
13. Raises brain growth factor · BDNF and TrkB up in hippocampus · role Cognition
   Semax raises BDNF, a growth factor for nerve cells, and its receptor TrkB in the hippocampus, the memory centre. More BDNF signalling favours learning, new connections and nerve cell survival after injury. This is shown in animal studies; Russian clinical use covers stroke recovery and cognition.
14. Lifts alertness · Dopamine and serotonin modulation · role Drive & mental stamina
   Semax adjusts dopamine and serotonin activity, the brain chemicals for drive and mood. That gives the activating, alert side of the pair. The neurotransmitter effects come from animal studies; Russian clinical data cover Semax, but Western replication is limited.
15. Slows breakdown of calming peptides · Enkephalinase inhibition · role Mood
   Both peptides slow enkephalinase, the enzymes that break down enkephalins, the body's own calming opioid peptides. In human serum in a test tube, both blocked these enzymes. Whether two blockers of the same enzymes add up has never been measured.
16. Shifts immune signalling · IL-6 and T-helper cytokine balance · role Immunity
   Selank shifts immune messengers, including IL-6 and the T-helper cytokine balance. It was developed from tuftsin, an immune peptide. A 2022 rat social-stress study of the pair reported restored immune responses; there are no human data for this effect.
17. Mechanism overview: Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro) is ACTH(4-10) with a Pro-Gly-Pro tail for stability; in animals it raises hippocampal BDNF and TrkB and modulates dopamine and serotonin. Selank (Thr-Lys-Pro-Arg-Pro-Gly-Pro) extends tuftsin with the same tail; it inhibits enkephalin-degrading enzymes, shifts IL-6 and T-helper cytokine balance and alters serotonin metabolism. Both share enkephalinase inhibition; Semax leans activating, Selank anxiolytic.

18. Semax: 10 mg · Focus, alertness, BDNF

25. Version High-Selank vial (one seller): Selank 30 · Semax 10 = 40 mg
26. Version Nasal spray: Semax 50 mcg · Selank 50 mcg per spray = 11 mg / 110 sprays
27. Why Semax is in it: The activating, focus side; Selank alone can feel sedating
28. Why Selank is in it: Takes the edge off Semax's stimulation and anxiety
29. Mixing chemistry: Semax carries Met and His, so it is the one that oxidises; keep the vial cold and dark (guides say wrap in foil).
30. Mixing chemistry: Selank (TKPRPGP) has no Met, His or Asn and is chemically quiet.
31. Mixing chemistry: Both end in Pro-Gly-Pro, added for resistance to breakdown; similar size and charge suggests they share a vial comfortably.
32. Mixing chemistry: Nasal sprays are liquid from the start; formulation and preservative are rarely disclosed.
33. Mixing chemistry: Avoid freeze-thaw cycles.
34. Self-mixing: Separate vials or sprays let you titrate each and take Selank alone at night; most guides prefer this, and the two-spray format is the more common purchase.

36. Half-life: per component

## What people report
38. Nasal or Sub-Q, mornings. Common: about 300 mcg of each, 1:1. Selank often started alone for a week before Semax is added. Cycles 2 to 4 weeks on, then a break.
39. Liked / worked: Both approved medicines in Russia
40. Liked / worked: Users report focus without jitter
41. Liked / worked: Selank matched medazepam in a 62-patient anxiety trial
42. What worked: Users report clearer thinking, calm sustained focus, steadier mood and better stress handling; one writer says Semax alone feels anxious in meetings while the pair suits collaborative work. Aggregator scores: Semax 7.1, Selank 6.8 out of 10.
43. Went wrong / did not work: No human trial of the pair
44. Went wrong / did not work: Fixed ratio blocks separate titration
45. Went wrong / did not work: Semax late in the day hurts sleep
46. What went wrong: Semax: overstimulation, headache, sleep problems if dosed after about 2 pm, nasal irritation. Selank: drowsiness, appetite increase, emotional flattening at higher doses. A fixed blend stops you titrating each one; guides suggest running each alone first.
47. Most reported: About 300 mcg of each, mornings
48. Most reported: Selank first, Semax added later
49. Most reported: Nasal spray most common

## What has been tested
50. Each has Russian clinical studies (Selank: a 62-patient anxiety trial against medazepam). Together: a 2022 rat study gave both in a social-stress model and reported restored immune responses. No human trial of the pair.
51. What it helps: Each has Russian clinical data: Semax for stroke recovery and cognition, Selank for generalised anxiety (62 patients vs medazepam). The pair has one rat social-stress study and user reports of calm focus. Western replication is limited.

## Who shouldn't take it
52. People with a history of seizures: Russian prescribing info lists convulsive disorders as a contraindication. (reported)
53. People on benzodiazepines or other sedatives: Selank modulates GABA-A receptors; effects may stack. (inferred)
54. People with acute psychosis or severe anxiety: Raises dopamine and serotonin turnover; Russian label excludes acute psychotic states. (reported)
55. People on opioids: Selank slows enkephalin breakdown, prolonging the body’s own opioids. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
56. NA Semax Amidate: NA Semax Amidate is Semax capped at both ends to last longer, assumed to act like Semax. Adding it doubles the focus side of the pair. No study tests the capped form or the stack.
57. NA Selank Amidate: NA Selank Amidate is Selank capped at both ends for longer action, assumed to act like Selank. Adding it strengthens the calm side. The capped form has no trial data, and the stack has not been tested.
58. Pinealon: Pinealon is a Khavinson bioregulator studied for neuron protection under oxidative stress and sleep rhythm, adding a protection angle to focus and calm. A four-peptide blend pairs it with capped Semax and Selank, but the combination has not been tested.
59. PE-22-28: PE-22-28 blocks the TREK-1 potassium channel, a mood route neither Semax nor Selank uses, with antidepressant-like effects in mice. People add it for mood. The pairing has not been tested together.
60. Cerebrolysin: Cerebrolysin is a pig brain peptide mix with nerve growth factor-like activity and stroke and dementia trial data, overlapping Semax's BDNF effect. People stack them for brain recovery. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
61. Two Russian heptapeptides in one vial or spray: Semax, an ACTH(4-10) analogue for focus, and Selank, a tuftsin analogue for calm.
62. First studied for: Each was developed and approved in Russia for clinical use (Semax for stroke and cognition, Selank for anxiety). Paired by users as a focused-but-calm stack.
63. 1980s to 1990s: Semax developed from ACTH(4-10) in Moscow
64. 2000s: Selank developed from tuftsin; approved in Russia for anxiety
65. 2011: Semax added to Russia's essential drug list
66. 2022: Rat study tests both in social stress
67. Apr 2026: Semax removed from FDA Category 2
68. Today: Sold mostly as 10 + 10 mg vials (5 + 5 smaller) and 1:1 nasal sprays

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Was the pair ever studied together? [mixed]
69. Not as a combination in people. No human trial of the two given together has been located, and no registered trial exists (verified as absent in the sources reviewed). The two come from the same Moscow institute and often appear in the same papers, but side by side rather than together: in rats with Parkinson-like damage, for example, each peptide was tested on its own, neither changed motor activity, and only Selank reduced anxiety [4] (verified).
70. The one direct head-to-head measurement is biochemical. In human serum in a test tube, both inhibited enkephalin-degrading enzymes, Semax at an IC50 of about 10 micromolar and Selank at about 20 [3] (verified). Whether two inhibitors of the same enzymes add, overlap or simply saturate the same target has never been measured (inferred).

### The two licensed labels pull against each other [people]
71. This is the most concrete fact about the pairing, and it rarely gets mentioned. The Russian Semax label lists anxiety disorders, acute psychosis and a history of seizures among its contraindications [1] (verified). The Russian Selank label exists for anxiety states and adjustment disorders [2] (verified).
72. The usual reason for pairing them, Selank to take the edge off Semax's stimulation, therefore runs into Semax's own label: in its country of approval it is not meant for people with an anxiety disorder at all (verified that the contraindication exists; what it means for the pair is inferred). Someone who feels anxious on Semax is, by that label, someone the drug was not meant for, and adding a second peptide is a workaround nobody has tested.

### Licensed products versus the blended vial [lab]
73. Both registered medicines are separate nasal drops from the same Russian manufacturer, each preserved with methylparaben: Semax at 0.1% or 1%, Selank at 0.15% [1,2] (verified). No licensed product combines them (inferred, none located).
74. The solubility arithmetic is worth doing. A 10 + 10 mg vial in 4 mL holds 2.5 mg/mL of each. The documented water solubility of Semax free base is about 2 mg/mL, rising to about 10 mg/mL in phosphate buffer [5] (verified). On those figures, free-base Semax would sit just above its limit in plain water, so a vial that dissolves cleanly is most likely using the salt form or a buffered diluent (inferred, assuming the figures carry over into the mixture).
75. Shelf life is set by the shorter-lived part. Reconstituted Semax is quoted as stable only 2 to 7 days in the fridge [5] (verified), and no comparable figure has been published for Selank. A mixed vial used over weeks is outside any documented stability window (inferred).

### Two peptides that are both gone in minutes [mixed]
76. Selank's label gives 92.8% nasal bioavailability, plasma detection at about 30 seconds and levels falling away over 5 to 5.5 minutes [2] (verified). Semax's label gives up to 60 to 70% nasal uptake and arrival in the brain within about 4 minutes, while claiming an effect lasting 20 to 24 hours [1] (verified). In rats, peak brain Semax after nasal dosing was about 0.072% of the dose [5].
77. Both are cleared by the same tissue peptidases, and both inhibit enkephalin-degrading peptidases. Whether either slows the other's breakdown, lengthening exposure in a way neither label describes, is a plausible question that has never been asked (speculated). By injection, which many blends assume, neither has any human exposure data [5] (verified for Semax).

### The evidence is uneven between the two [people]
78. Semax has had a full regulatory review. The FDA found two usable clinical references for its proposed uses, both small and uncontrolled; in the pain study the authors themselves concluded Semax has no analgesic activity by itself [5] (verified). Its rodent profile includes antithrombotic effects and potentiation of amphetamine-induced dopamine release, both unstudied in people [5].
79. Selank has no regulatory review in English. Its accessible human record is a handful of small Russian trials, compared against benzodiazepines or uncontrolled, none with a placebo arm (reported; see the Selank entry). So one half of the blend has been examined and found thin, and the other half has not been examined.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
80. Semax: recommended for the US bulks list by the advisory committee on 24 July 2026, 8 to 5 with one abstention, against FDA staff advice; still needs rulemaking [5,6] (verified). Selank: not reviewed; its acetate nomination was removed from the do-not-compound category earlier because the nominator meant a different substance [7] (verified). An interim access policy for certain peptides went to White House review on 14 October 2026, peptides not yet named [8] (verified). A blend therefore pairs one substance recommended but not yet permitted with one sitting outside every category (inferred).
81. Sport: neither is named on the 2026 list. Both hold Russian approvals, which arguably takes them outside the catch-all non-approved class [9], but the list does not address them and athletes cannot assume either is allowed (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
82. Does the pair do anything either does not do alone? Untested in any species as a combination.
83. Should someone who gets anxious on Semax be taking it at all? Its own label says not, and that question deserves an answer before a second peptide is added.
84. How long does a mixed vial stay potent? Unmeasured, with one component documented at days.
85. Do the two change each other's breakdown? A simple plasma study could answer it.
