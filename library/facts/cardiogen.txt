# Cardiogen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/cardiogen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, AEDR, Ala-Glu-Asp-Arg
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: AEDR (4 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Cardiogen is a lab-made Khavinson bioregulator peptide aimed at heart and vascular tissue. It is used mainly by the longevity community for heart aging, and it is claimed to tune heart tissue genes and favour heart cell renewal over scarring. The evidence is animal work from the Khavinson group with no independent trials, and first-hand reports are few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson heart bioregulator, claimed to tune heart tissue genes. A longevity stack pick; evidence mostly Russian.
7. Used for: Heart support, longevity stacks

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Heart & vessels: Heart muscle support
9. Tissue repair: Heart tissue repair claims

## How it works
10. Claimed to tune heart tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Heart & vessels
   Cardiogen is claimed to enter heart cells and act on DNA directly, shifting which genes are active in heart tissue, rather than working through a surface receptor. The idea comes from the Khavinson group in Russia. It rests on their animal work and has not been independently replicated.
11. Claimed to favour heart cell renewal over scar · Cardiomyocyte vs fibroblast · role Tissue repair
   The group claims it favours renewal of cardiomyocytes, the heart's beating muscle cells, over fibroblasts, which lay down scar. Less scar after injury would mean a stronger, more elastic heart. This is reported only in Russian animal studies.
12. Mechanism overview: Cardiogen is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide. The group claims it enters cells and tunes gene expression in heart tissue, favouring renewal of cardiomyocytes over fibroblast scar formation. These mechanisms are claimed by one research group and lack independent replication.

## What has been tested
28. Heart & vessels: Heart-muscle repair, cardio aging (animal)
29. Russian studies only; no independent trials.
30. What it helps: Evidence is animal work from the Khavinson group on heart muscle repair and cardiac aging, with Russian reports of cardiac support. No independent trials exist. First-hand reports are few, coming mostly from people with longevity and cardiovascular interest. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
31. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S3] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
32. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
33. People treating a diagnosed heart disease with it: No controlled trials show benefit; delays proven care. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
34. Epitalon: Epitalon is the best known Khavinson peptide, claimed to switch on telomerase and restore melatonin rhythm, while Cardiogen is aimed at heart tissue. Longevity users combine a whole-body bioregulator with an organ one in the same course. All claims come from one group, and the pair has not been tested together.
35. Thymalin: Thymalin works on immune cells, raising and rebalancing T and B cells, while Cardiogen is aimed at the heart. They are paired in longevity courses that cycle several bioregulators to cover different organs. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
36. A Khavinson bioregulator peptide aimed at heart and vascular tissue.
37. First studied for: Supporting heart-muscle repair and cardiovascular aging, in the Russian program.
38. Name story: Cardio (heart) plus Khavinson's '-gen' suffix for his synthetic short bioregulators. AEDR is the one-letter code for Ala-Glu-Asp-Arg, assigned to cardiovascular function. [S4] (verified)
39. Late 20th c.: Short bioregulator peptide from the Khavinson program, aimed at the heart
40. Today: Sold as a bioregulator; not an approved drug anywhere
