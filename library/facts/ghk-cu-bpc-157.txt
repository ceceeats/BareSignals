# GHK-Cu + BPC-157
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ghk-cu-bpc-157
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: GHK-Cu/BPC-157 70 mg, copper peptide + BPC, GLOW without TB-500
2. Origin: Blend of 2 peptides
3. Legal and approval status: US: not approved. Both removed from FDA Category 2 on 15 Apr 2026 (GHK-Cu for injection). BPC-157 won an 8-6-1 PCAC vote in July 2026; GHK-Cu goes to a panel before end of Feb 2027. WADA: BPC-157 banned since 2022 (S0). EU, UK, Australia: no approved product.

## Summary
4. This blend combines GHK-Cu, a copper tripeptide, with BPC-157 in one vial, essentially GLOW without TB-500. People pair them so BPC-157 covers tendon, muscle and gut healing while GHK-Cu covers collagen, skin and scars. Evidence is per component and mostly animal, no study tests the two together, and GHK-Cu makes up most of the vial and its sting.
5. Punch line: GLOW minus TB-500. BPC-157 heals tendon, muscle and gut while the copper peptide rebuilds collagen, skin and scars.
6. Used for: Injury repair with skin and scar support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Tissue repair: BPC-157 tendon, muscle and wound healing in animals
8. Skin & aesthetics: GHK-Cu collagen and skin repair
9. Digestive & gut: BPC-157 gut lining protection in animals
10. Inflammatory signaling: Both dampen inflammation
11. Hair (backed only by animal or user reports): GHK-Cu used for hair density

## How it works
12. Speeds tendon, muscle and gut healing in animals · Angiogenesis, FAK-paxillin, NO system · role Tissue repair
   BPC-157 drives new vessel growth, moves fibroblasts into the injury through FAK-paxillin, and steadies nitric oxide signalling. That speeds tendon, muscle and gut healing in rodent models. Human data are a small knee report and an unfinished Phase 1.
13. Protects gut lining · Gastric cytoprotection · role Digestive & gut
   BPC-157 protects the gut lining, an effect called gastric cytoprotection, shown against damage such as NSAID injury in rats. It is where the peptide was first studied. The one human trial was a rectal colitis study that regulators judged inadequate.
14. Turns on collagen building in skin · Fibroblast collagen and decorin synthesis · role Skin & aesthetics
   GHK-Cu signals fibroblasts to make more collagen and decorin, a protein that organises collagen fibres. It also shifts the balance of matrix metalloproteinases, the enzymes that remodel tissue. This is shown in cell, animal and small topical human skin studies.
15. Carries copper to healing tissue · Cu(II) bound to Gly-His-Lys · role Tissue repair
   GHK-Cu is Gly-His-Lys holding a copper ion tightly, and it delivers that copper to rebuilding tissue. Copper is needed by enzymes such as lysyl oxidase that cross-link collagen. BPC-157 has no strong copper site, so it does not strip copper from GHK in the vial.
16. Mechanism overview: BPC-157 is a 15-residue fragment of a gastric protein; in rodent injury models it promotes angiogenesis, tendon fibroblast migration (FAK-paxillin) and nitric oxide balance, and protects the gut lining. GHK-Cu is Gly-His-Lys bound to Cu(II); it raises collagen and decorin synthesis and modulates matrix metalloproteinases. BPC-157 has no His and no strong copper site, so it does not strip copper from GHK. The pair targets deep tissue repair plus surface matrix and skin.

## What is inside the blend
17. GHK-Cu: 50 mg · Collagen, skin and scar remodelling; carries copper
18. BPC-157: 20 mg · Tendon, muscle and gut healing
19. Most common ratio: GHK-Cu 50 · BPC-157 10 = 60 mg (1 listing (URL slug only))
20. Version Common retail: GHK-Cu 50 · BPC-157 20 = 70 mg
21. Why GHK-Cu is in it: Skin, scar and hair matrix work that BPC-157 is not used for
22. Why BPC-157 is in it: Deep injury and gut repair; GHK-Cu has little evidence for tendon or gut
23. Mixing chemistry: BPC-157 is most stable around pH 6.5 to 8.5 by its own patent; GHK-Cu keeps its copper above about pH 4. A vial at near-neutral pH suits both reasonably.
24. Mixing chemistry: BPC-157 acetate liquid can sit near pH 3.9; drawn into a copper vial in volume it pushes pH down toward where copper loosens.
25. Mixing chemistry: Blue means the copper complex is intact; green or brown suggests copper has come off.
26. Mixing chemistry: No vitamin C or glutathione in the same vial or syringe: copper plus a reducer drives oxidation.
27. Mixing chemistry: Ask for per-component mg; GLOW certificates show BPC-157 running up to 17% over label.
28. Self-mixing: Mixing separate vials lets you set your own ratio, but each vial carries its own salt and pH, so the combined liquid lands at an unknown pH and overfill errors stack.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
29. Typical: 250–500 mcg BPC-157 equivalent · 1× daily · cycle 4–8 weeks · route Sub-Q (reported)
30. Half-life: per component
31. Most people take: No standard blend dose. 70 mg in 3 mL: 10 units gives about 1.7 mg GHK-Cu and 0.67 mg BPC-157, so many draw 5 to 8 units to land BPC-157 at 330 to 530 mcg.

## What people report
32. Usually Sub-Q once daily. People size it on BPC-157 (250 to 500 mcg), which brings 0.6 to 1.25 mg GHK-Cu, or on GHK-Cu (1 to 2 mg), which brings 0.4 to 0.8 mg BPC-157. Often injected near the injury.
33. Liked / worked: Covers injury repair and skin in one shot
34. Liked / worked: Strong animal repair data for BPC-157
35. Liked / worked: Copper stays bound at near-neutral pH
36. What worked: No blend-specific report pool. Stack users give each peptide a lane: BPC-157 for injury and gut, GHK-Cu for skin, scars and hair. Reports from the related GLOW blend describe faster wound and scar healing and better skin texture.
37. Went wrong / did not work: No trial of the pair
38. Went wrong / did not work: Different pH comfort zones
39. Went wrong / did not work: GHK-Cu sting with every dose
40. What went wrong: GHK-Cu is most of the vial, so its sting, redness and flushing come with every dose. BPC-157 and GHK-Cu prefer different pH ranges, so a shared vial is a compromise. No human efficacy data for injected use of either.
41. Most reported: Sized on BPC-157, 250 to 500 mcg
42. Most reported: Injected near the injury
43. Most reported: Picked for scars and skin after surgery

## What has been tested
44. Never tested as a combination. A stack guide updated 29 Sep 2026 states no controlled human trial has tested BPC-157 with GHK-Cu for any use. BPC-157 has animal injury models and very thin human data; GHK-Cu has cell, animal and small topical human studies.
45. What it helps: Evidence is per component and mostly animal. BPC-157 speeds tendon, ligament, muscle and gut healing in rodents; human data is a 12-patient knee report and an unfinished phase I. GHK-Cu has cell, animal and small topical human skin studies. No study tests the two together.

## Who shouldn't take it
46. Wilson's disease or copper overload: Each dose delivers copper; people who cannot clear copper accumulate it in liver and brain. (inferred)
47. People with active or recent cancer: Raises VEGFR2 signalling and new vessel growth in animal work; tumours use the same pathway. (inferred)
48. People with cholestatic liver disease: Copper leaves the body through bile; PBC, PSC and bile duct blockage raise body copper. (inferred)
49. Drug-tested athletes: Banned since 2022 under S0 (non-approved substances); positives have led to bans. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
50. Thymosin Beta-4: Thymosin beta-4 adds actin-driven cell migration and anti-inflammatory action to the blend's vessel growth and collagen work. Adding it makes the three-part GLOW stack. In the only rat study, BPC-157 plus TB-500 added nothing over either alone.
51. KPV: KPV blocks NF-κB in gut and immune cells, while the blend covers deep repair and skin matrix. People add it for inflamed skin or gut. Adding it gives the KLOW-style stack, but this pairing has not been tested.
52. TB-500 frag 17–23: The fragment carries the actin-binding region of thymosin beta-4 and is used for cell migration into wounds. It fills the same role as full TB-500 in GLOW. Data come mostly from the full 43-mer, and the pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
53. A two-peptide vial: GHK-Cu (50 mg) with BPC-157 (20 mg). It is GLOW without TB-500, with more BPC-157 per vial.
54. First studied for: Sold as a repair and skin blend: BPC-157 for tendon, muscle and gut healing, GHK-Cu for collagen, skin and scars.
55. 1973: GHK-Cu isolated from human plasma
56. 1993: BPC-157 first published by Sikiric's Zagreb group
57. 2020s: GLOW blend spreads; two-peptide GHK-Cu + BPC-157 vials follow
58. Apr 2026: Both removed from FDA Category 2
59. Jul 2026: BPC-157 wins PCAC vote 8 to 6
60. Today: Sold as a repair and skin blend, 50 + 20 mg

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where the pairing came from [mixed]
61. Neither compound was developed with the other in mind. The copper tripeptide comes out of 1970s plasma research and a cosmetic and wound-care programme [1]; the gastric peptide comes out of a Zagreb laboratory's gut and injury work from 1993 onward [2]. The two-peptide vial is a retail descendant of larger multi-peptide repair blends, built by subtraction rather than by any study showing that these two belong together (inferred from the order in which the products appeared; no scientific paper proposes this pair).
62. The rationale offered for it is division of labour: one for deep tissue, one for skin and matrix. That is a reasonable way to describe what each has been studied for. It is not evidence that combining them does more than either alone, and nobody has tested that (verified as absent; no study of the combination in cells, animals or people was found).

### The two halves have different kinds of evidence, by different routes [mixed]
63. The gastric peptide's evidence is almost entirely rodent injury models from one founding group, with one controlled human trial: a 2005 meeting abstract in ulcerative colitis using an enema, whose result regulators judged inadequate [2] (verified). No human pharmacokinetic data exist for injection, and in the rectal studies where plasma was measured, levels were undetectable or below quantification [2].
64. The copper tripeptide's human evidence is topical, small and short, and its one large test failed. Two phase 3 trials in diabetic foot ulcers, 511 patients across 30 centres at 0.5% and 2% against placebo, found it no better than placebo [3,4] (verified). The injected route has never been studied in people.
65. So the blend is injected, while the human data that exist were gathered rectally for one component and on the skin for the other. In route terms, the vial has no human evidence behind either half (inferred from the above, and the most important thing to understand about it).

### What the copper adds that a plain peptide blend does not [lab]
66. The copper tripeptide is roughly 16% copper by mass, from its formula (inferred, calculated). A 50 mg share of copper peptide therefore carries about 8 mg of elemental copper across the whole vial. For scale, the adult dietary requirement is 0.9 mg a day and the upper intake limit is 10 mg a day, a limit set on liver damage [5] (verified).
67. The comparison is imperfect in a way that matters. The body controls copper mainly at the gut, by adjusting how much it absorbs, and at the liver, by excreting it into bile [5] (verified). An injection skips the first of those controls. Whether the copper stays bound to its peptide long enough to be handled differently from free copper, and where it goes once the peptide is broken down, has not been studied for injection (verified as absent).
68. The chemistry of the bound complex is the best-established part of the copper story: held by the tripeptide, copper's ability to drive damaging redox reactions is largely silenced [1] (verified, in vitro). That protection depends on the copper staying bound. Anything in the solution that competes for copper or reduces it works against that, which is the underlying reason the mixing rules on the card exist (inferred).

### Mixing and stability: what is known and what is assumed [lab]
69. For the gastric peptide, regulators recorded that the lyophilised powder should be kept below minus 18 degrees and that reconstituted solution is stable for two to three weeks refrigerated [2] (verified). That is the shortest documented window of the two components and, in a combined vial, it sets the realistic life of the whole solution once mixed (inferred).
70. No stability study of the two together exists. Whether the gastric peptide, which carries two neighbouring aspartates and a lysine, takes up any copper from the complex at the concentrations in a vial has not been measured; the card's statement that it has no strong copper site is a reasonable reading of its sequence, not a tested result (inferred).
71. The quality problems recorded for the gastric peptide on its own, unidentified impurities and no endotoxin, bioburden or aggregate testing on the certificates regulators reviewed [2], carry over unchanged into a blend. Injectable copper tripeptide has had no equivalent public review at all [6] (verified).

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
72. No approved product contains either component, alone or together. In the US the gastric peptide was removed from the do-not-compound category in April 2026 after nominations were withdrawn, then recommended for the 503A bulks list by an advisory committee in July by 8 to 6 with one abstention, against the agency's own staff recommendation [2,7]. That vote is advisory and requires rulemaking that has not begun [8] (verified).
73. Injectable copper tripeptide is in a different position: the topical form was restored to Category 1 in May 2026, the injectable route was left unclassified, and its advisory review is expected before the end of February 2027 [6,8] (verified). So a combined injectable draws on one substance recommended but not yet permitted and one whose injectable form has not been assessed.
74. In sport, the gastric peptide is prohibited at all times under WADA's non-approved substances class [2]; the copper tripeptide is not named, but injected use of an unapproved substance falls under the same catch-all (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
75. Does the pair do anything the components do not? Untested.
76. Where does injected copper go, and does repeated dosing shift copper balance? Unstudied for either the single agent or the blend, and it is the larger component by mass.
77. Does the gastric peptide interact with the copper complex in solution? Answerable with routine analytical chemistry, never published.
78. If someone responds, to which half? A fixed-ratio vial makes that impossible to tell.
