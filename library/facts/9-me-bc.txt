# 9-Me-BC
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/9-me-bc
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: 9-methyl-beta-carboline, 9-methyl-β-carboline, 9-methylnorharmane, 9MBC
2. Origin: Not a peptide: beta-carboline
3. Legal and approval status: Not approved anywhere and no registered human trial as of Oct 2026. Sold as a research chemical; not scheduled in most countries. Not named on the WADA list.

## Summary
4. 9-Me-BC is a small beta-carboline molecule, a family found in coffee and tobacco smoke, not a peptide. German researchers found it increased dopamine neurons and growth factors in cell cultures, and online users take it for motivation as a dopamine regenerator. All evidence is from cell cultures and rodents, and no human trial exists. It inhibits MAO-A and can damage DNA under UV light.
5. Punch line: A beta-carboline, not a peptide. Regrew dopamine neurons in cells; taken sublingually in short cycles for drive.
6. Used for: Motivation, mood, dopamine

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Drive & mental stamina: Motivation and drive reported
8. Neural protection: Protects dopamine neurons in lab models
9. Neural plasticity: Raises BDNF and other growth factors in cells

## How it works
10. Increases dopamine neuron numbers in culture · Tyrosine hydroxylase up · role Drive & mental stamina
   In primary midbrain cultures, 9-Me-BC raises tyrosine hydroxylase, the enzyme for the first step of dopamine production, and the number of neurons carrying it. That points to more dopamine making capacity. This is shown in cell cultures only, with no human data.
11. Raises growth factors for neurons · BDNF and other neurotrophins up · role Neural plasticity
   The same cultures show higher expression of BDNF and other neurotrophic genes, signals that help neurons survive and form connections. The effect disappears when PI3K/Akt is blocked and does not need D2/D3 receptors. This is cell and rodent evidence, not human.
12. Blocks dopamine breakdown · MAO-A over MAO-B inhibition · role Drive & mental stamina
   9-Me-BC inhibits monoamine oxidase A more than MAO-B, the enzymes that break down dopamine, serotonin and noradrenaline. Slower breakdown leaves more of these signals active, one likely source of the motivation users describe. It also means interaction risk with other drugs that raise monoamines.
13. Protects neurons from toxins · PI3K/Akt dependent · role Neural protection
   Its protective effect runs through PI3K/Akt, a survival pathway that tells stressed cells not to self destruct. In cultures and a rat Parkinson's model it shielded dopamine neurons from toxins and aided recovery. No human trial exists, and photoexcited beta-carbolines damage DNA in vitro.
14. Mechanism overview: 9-Me-BC is a methylated beta-carboline. It is taken up into dopamine neurons via the dopamine transporter, inhibits MAO-A more than MAO-B, and in primary mesencephalic cultures increases tyrosine hydroxylase positive neurons and expression of BDNF and other neurotrophic genes, an effect abolished by blocking PI3K/Akt and independent of D2/D3 receptors. Beta-carbolines absorb UV light and generate reactive oxygen species, and photoexcited 9-methyl-beta-carbolines damage DNA in vitro.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
15. Typical: 5–15 mg/day · once in the morning · cycle 2–4 weeks on, 2–4 weeks off · route Sublingual, oral (reported)
16. Half-life: Unknown in humans
17. Most people take: No human dose exists. Community use is 5 to 15 mg once in the morning, held under the tongue.
18. Users run 2 to 4 week cycles with equal time off and avoid sun exposure while taking it.

## What people report
19. Discussed heavily on nootropic forums, mostly at 5 to 15 mg a day under the tongue in 2 to 4 week cycles. Users report better motivation, mood and ability to start tasks, sometimes lasting after stopping, and some use it after stimulant burnout. Complaints are insomnia, headache, anxiety and sunburn sensitivity; users avoid sun and MAO or serotonin drugs. No aggregator counts located.
20. Liked / worked: Grows and protects dopamine neurons in lab models
21. What worked: In lab models, protects and regrows dopamine neurons and raises neurotrophic factors.
22. Went wrong / did not work: No human data
23. Went wrong / did not work: Phototoxic under UV light
24. Went wrong / did not work: MAO-A inhibitor; interaction risk
25. What went wrong: No human data at all, and no human half life or dose. It is a photosensitiser that can damage DNA under UV light, and it inhibits MAO-A. Higher concentrations reduced dopamine neurons in culture.
26. Most reported: 5 to 15 mg sublingual mornings
27. Most reported: Motivation and mood lift
28. Most reported: Insomnia, headache, sun sensitivity

## What has been tested
29. Drive & mental stamina: Dopamine neuron cultures (cell)
30. Neural protection: Parkinson's model (rat)
31. Cell cultures and rodents only: more tyrosine hydroxylase positive dopamine neurons, roughly double BDNF expression, recovery in a Parkinson's model. No human trial.
32. What it helps: All evidence is from cell cultures and rodents, where it protects and promotes dopamine neurons. Community users report motivation and mood benefits at 5 to 15 mg a day, with insomnia and sun sensitivity as the main concerns. No human trial exists.
33. Trial: none. No registered human trial found. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1]

## Synergies (paired with)
34. Bromantane: Both raise tyrosine hydroxylase, 9-Me-BC in cell cultures and bromantane in Russian studies, and 9-Me-BC adds MAO-A inhibition on top. People stack them for dopamine driven motivation from overlapping angles, but the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
35. Dihexa: 9-Me-BC targets dopamine neurons and BDNF, while Dihexa boosts HGF and c-Met signalling that builds synapses in rats. Users pair them for growth factor support from two pathways. Both rest on cell and animal work, and the pair has not been tested together.
36. Semax: Semax is reported to raise BDNF and monoamine turnover, overlapping with 9-Me-BC's neurotrophin and dopamine effects in cultures. People combine them for mood and drive, but neither has Western trials and the pair has not been tested together.
37. Methylene blue: Methylene blue supports mitochondrial electron transport, while 9-Me-BC works on dopamine neurons and their growth factors. Both inhibit MAO-A, so combining them stacks the breakdown block, and the pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
38. A beta-carboline studied by German neuroscientists (Gille's group in Dresden, from 2008) that, in cell cultures, increased dopamine neurons and their growth factors. Online nootropic users picked it up as a 'dopamine regenerator'.
39. First studied for: Proposed as a lead for Parkinson's disease based on cell and rodent work.
40. Name story: Plain chemistry: a beta-carboline ring with a methyl group on position 9. [S2] (verified)
41. 2008: Dresden group reports effects on dopamine neurons in culture
42. 2010: J Neurochem paper: stimulates dopamine neuron differentiation and growth factors
43. 2010s: Phototoxicity and microglia studies
44. Today: Popular research chemical; still no human trial
