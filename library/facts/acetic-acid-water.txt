# Acetic Acid Water
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/acetic-acid-water
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: HAC
2. Origin: Sterile mixing water, not a peptide
3. Class: water
4. Legal and approval status: Sold as a mixing liquid for freeze-dried peptides. Not taken on its own.

## Summary
5. Punch line: Sterile water with a little acetic acid, usually 0.6%. Dissolves peptides that clump or gel in plain water.

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
6. Mixing water: Sterile water containing a small amount of acetic acid, most commonly sold at 0.6%. The mild acidity helps certain peptides that clump or gel in neutral water dissolve cleanly.

## How it works

## Water
7. Made of: Sterile water, about 99.4%
8. Made of: Acetic acid, commonly 0.6% (retail range about 0.6 to 1%; confirm on certificate of analysis)
9. pH: About 2.9 for 0.6% acetic acid (about 0.1 mol/L), calculated from acetic acid's pKa of 4.76. Not stated on most labels.
10. When used: AOD-9604 and HGH fragment 176-191, which are commonly reported to gel or clump in bacteriostatic water and dissolve more cleanly in dilute acid.
11. When used: Other positively charged (basic) peptides that do not dissolve in water. Peptide manufacturers' solubility guides recommend trying acetic acid for basic peptides when water fails.
12. When used: Sometimes used as a first step: the peptide is dissolved in a small amount of acid, then diluted with water or bacteriostatic water.
13. not_for: Peptides that dissolve fine in bacteriostatic water. Acid adds sting without benefit.
14. not_for: Acid-sensitive peptides, or peptides the supplier says to keep at neutral pH.
15. not_for: Repeated draws over weeks if the product has no preservative. Most acetic acid waters list none.
16. not_for: Mixing with strongly buffered or alkaline liquids in the same vial, which can neutralize the acid and cause the peptide to fall out of solution.
17. storage: Unopened: cool place away from light, within the printed expiry date. Refrigeration (2 to 8°C) is a reasonable default if no instructions are given.
18. storage: After first puncture: treat as preservative-free unless the label says otherwise. Refrigerate and use within a short period. The USP 28-day multi-dose limit is meant for preserved vials and should be treated as an upper bound, not a target.
19. storage: Once a peptide is dissolved in it, follow that peptide's storage rules, usually refrigerated at 2 to 8°C.
20. storage: Do not freeze unless the label allows it.
21. how: Check the label, concentration, expiry date and clarity. Write the date of first puncture on the vial.
22. how: Wipe the stopper with an alcohol swab and let it dry. Use a new sterile needle and syringe each time.
23. how: Add the liquid slowly down the inside wall of the peptide vial.
24. how: Swirl gently until clear. Do not shake.
25. how: If the solution gels or stays cloudy, do not use it; a different solvent or lower concentration may be needed.
26. watch: Cloudiness, haze or particles in the plain liquid: discard.
27. watch: A light vinegar smell is expected. Any other odor is not.
28. watch: Stinging or burning on injection is common with acidic liquids; worsening redness, swelling, heat or pain at the site is a warning sign.
29. watch: Gel, strands or white flakes after mixing indicate the peptide has aggregated.
30. facts: 'HAc' is a standard chemistry shorthand for acetic acid, where Ac stands for the acetate group (AcOH and HOAc are also used). The retail label is almost certainly this abbreviation.
31. facts: AOD-9604 is a 16 amino acid piece of human growth hormone with two arginines and a ring formed by two cysteines. In acid, the molecules carry a stronger positive charge and repel each other, which is the likely reason they stop clumping.
32. facts: 0.6% acetic acid is roughly seven to twelve times more dilute than table vinegar (4 to 7%), yet still several hundred times more acidic than bacteriostatic water (pH about 2.9 versus 5.7).
33. tile: 0.6% acetic acid
34. tile: pH about 2.9 · no preservative
35. cells: In it, 0.6% acetic acid
36. cells: pH, about 2.9
37. cells: Opened, short, fridge
38. cells: Route, Mixing only

## Synergies (paired with)
40. AOD-9604: Some GH fragments clump or dissolve poorly in plain water, and a mild acetic acid solution helps keep AOD-9604 in solution. It is a mixing choice and plays no part in the fragment's claimed fat loss effects.
41. HGH frag 176–191: HGH frag 176-191 can clump in plain water, so some users dissolve it in mild acetic acid water to keep it clear. The acid is there for solubility only and does not change how the fragment acts on fat.
