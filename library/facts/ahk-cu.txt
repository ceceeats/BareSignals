# AHK-Cu
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ahk-cu
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: L-alanyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine copper(II), Ala-His-Lys-Cu, copper tripeptide AHK
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: AHK (3 amino acids). Bound to one copper ion; weight shown is AHK alone
4. Legal and approval status: Cosmetic ingredient; not an approved drug.

## Summary
5. AHK-Cu is a lab-made copper tripeptide (Ala-His-Lys plus copper), a cousin of GHK-Cu studied mostly for hair follicles. People apply it topically for thinning hair, and it is thought to stimulate follicle cells and new blood vessels. The real data are cell studies, human use is small and cosmetic, and first-hand reports are few.
6. Punch line: GHK-Cu's hair-focused cousin, a copper tripeptide for the scalp. Studied to wake follicle cells; few user reports.
7. Used for: Thinning hair

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Hair: Stimulates hair follicle cells
9. Skin & aesthetics: Collagen support
10. Cellular protection: Copper delivery

## How it works
11. Stimulates hair-follicle cells · Dermal papilla cells · role Hair
   AHK-Cu acts on dermal papilla cells, the signalling hub at the base of each hair follicle that steers how the hair grows. In cell work it stimulates these cells to grow and multiply. Human use is small and cosmetic, with no serious trials.
12. Feeds follicles with new vessels · VEGF, vascularization · role Hair
   It raises VEGF, a growth factor that tells small blood vessels to sprout. More vessels around the follicle means more blood supply to feed hair growth. This is shown in cell studies.
13. Slows follicle cell death · Bcl-2 up, Bax down · role Cellular protection
   It tips the balance between Bcl-2, which keeps cells alive, and Bax, which pushes them to self-destruct, toward survival. Follicle cells then die off more slowly. This is cell data only.
14. Delivers copper for collagen cross-linking · Lysyl oxidase · role Skin & aesthetics
   The copper it carries supports lysyl oxidase, an enzyme that cross-links collagen and elastin into a firm network. That strengthens the skin around the follicle. This is textbook copper biology, not shown for AHK-Cu in people.
15. Mechanism overview: AHK-Cu is a copper tripeptide built for hair. In cell work it stimulates dermal papilla cells, the signalling hub at the base of each follicle, and raises VEGF to grow the small vessels that feed it. It shifts the Bcl-2 to Bax balance toward survival, slowing follicle cell death. The copper also supports lysyl oxidase, which cross-links collagen in the surrounding skin.

## Who shouldn't take it
31. Wilson's disease or copper overload: Delivers copper that cannot be cleared. (inferred)
32. People with cholestatic liver disease: Copper leaves through bile; cholestasis raises body copper. (inferred)

## Where it comes from
33. GHK-Cu's lab cousin: a copper tripeptide (Ala-His-Lys + copper) with one amino acid changed, studied mostly for hair follicles.
34. First studied for: Hair growth, by stimulating follicle cells and vascularization.
35. Name story: One-letter code for Ala-His-Lys, holding a copper ion. It is GHK-Cu with the glycine swapped for alanine, a deliberate near-twin studied mainly on hair follicles. [S2] (verified)
36. 2000s: Copper tripeptide variant studied for hair follicles
37. Today: Cosmetic hair ingredient; not an approved drug
