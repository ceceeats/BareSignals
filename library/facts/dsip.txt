# DSIP
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/dsip
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: emideltide, delta-sleep-inducing peptide, Deltaran (DSIP product)
2. Origin: Made by the body
3. Sequence: WAGGDASGE (9 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Research chemical. The July 2026 FDA compounding advisory panel voted against it.

## Summary
5. DSIP, or Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide, is a small peptide linked to deep sleep. People inject it before bed for deeper sleep, and it is claimed to dampen stress hormones, though its mechanism is unknown. No gene or receptor for it has ever been found, and the old, small human studies gave inconsistent results.
6. Punch line: Named for deep delta sleep, injected before bed. No gene or receptor ever found; results vary widely.
7. Used for: Sleep

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Sleep & rhythms: Named for deep delta sleep
9. Pain & sensory: Pain relief in old studies
10. Neural protection: Stress protection in animals
11. Reward & urges: Withdrawal studied
12. Endocrine & growth (backed only by animal or user reports): Lowers stress hormones

## How it works
13. Unknown; no gene or receptor ever found · Unidentified · role Sleep & rhythms
   DSIP was isolated from the blood of sleeping rabbits in 1977, but no gene, precursor protein or receptor has ever been found. Its mechanism is unknown, and it may not even be a true mammalian hormone. Everything below rests on effects, not a target.
14. Claimed to promote deep sleep in early studies · Slow-wave EEG, claimed · role Sleep & rhythms
   Early human studies, all given into a vein, claimed more slow-wave, or delta, sleep. Results grew weaker as trials became better designed, and no study has tested it by injection under the skin, the way users take it.
15. Claimed to ease opioid and alcohol withdrawal · Opioid system, claimed · role Reward & urges
   Early studies claimed DSIP eased opioid and alcohol withdrawal, possibly through the body's opioid systems. These were small, older studies with inconsistent results, and the link to opioid receptors was never established.
16. Claimed to dampen stress hormones · ACTH, cortisol, claimed · role Endocrine & growth
   DSIP was claimed to blunt release of ACTH and cortisol, the main stress hormones. Findings were inconsistent across studies and remain unexplained, so this is a historical claim rather than an established effect.
17. Mechanism overview: DSIP, delta sleep-inducing peptide, is a nine amino acid peptide first isolated in 1977 from the blood of sleeping rabbits. No gene or specific receptor has ever been identified, so its mechanism is unknown. Early studies claimed it increases slow-wave delta sleep, dampens ACTH and cortisol stress responses, and eases opioid and alcohol withdrawal, possibly through opioid systems. These effects were inconsistent across studies and remain unexplained.

## What has been tested
34. Sleep & rhythms: Deep delta-wave sleep (human)
35. Pain & sensory: Chronic pain (human)
36. Endocrine & growth: Stress hormones (human)
37. Small, old human studies on sleep, chronic pain and stress hormones gave mixed results; interest faded for lack of a clear mechanism or gene.
38. What it helps: Small, old human studies gave some reports of deeper delta-wave sleep, effects on stress hormones and use in chronic pain, with inconsistent results. Users inject it before bed for sleep and describe highly variable outcomes. With no known mechanism, there is little to explain why it works for some people and not others.
39. Trial: none. Human sleep and withdrawal studies date to the 1980s and 1990s; no registered trial found, and the Russian product Deltaran has no registry trial on record. (checked 2026-10-23) [S5] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
40. People on opioids or sedatives: DSIP interacts with opioid systems in animal studies; stacking is untested. (inferred)
41. People with narcolepsy or sleep apnea: Unpredictable sedation on top of disordered breathing or sleep. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
42. Pinealon: DSIP is taken for sleep onset and Pinealon for claimed melatonin support and neuron protection, aiming at sleep from two angles. Both rest on weak evidence. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
43. Epitalon: Epitalon is used for claimed melatonin rhythm, DSIP for deeper sleep, so users pair them in sleep and ageing courses. Both have weak and inconsistent evidence. The pair has not been tested together.
44. Magnesium glycinate: Magnesium glycinate is a common sleep supplement, and magnesium plays a role in calming nerve signalling. Users add it to DSIP as a low-risk sleep base. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
45. Delta Sleep-Inducing Peptide, found in 1977 in the blood of rabbits put to sleep by brain stimulation. The oddity: no gene for it has ever been found.

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from, and the origin that never resolved [animals]
52. A Swiss group isolated the nonapeptide Trp-Ala-Gly-Gly-Asp-Ala-Ser-Gly-Glu from the cerebral venous blood of rabbits held in electrically induced sleep, with the defining papers dated 1977 [2] (verified). Its regulatory name is emideltide [1].
53. Half a century later, no gene, precursor protein or receptor has been found, and its site of synthesis is unknown [2] (reported). A database search lines the sequence up with a hypothetical protein from a soil bacterium, which raises the possibility, unproven, that what was isolated was not a mammalian hormone at all [2] (speculated). DSIP-like material has been measured in the hypothalamus, pituitary, gut, breast milk and blood, but immunoreactivity is not the same as proof of an endogenous peptide (inferred).

### What is actually in a vial [lab]
54. One certificate submitted to the FDA listed the formula C35H57N13O14S2, which the agency said corresponds to no form of emideltide; another tested for acetic acid but carried free-base identifiers [1] (verified). Neither form's certificate set impurity limits, and none reported endotoxin, bioburden or aggregates [1].
55. Solubility is a real constraint. The free base dissolves in water at about 0.5 mg/mL, so the proposed 1 mg/mL injection may not be achievable with it; the acetate dissolves at about 10 mg/mL in buffer [1] (verified). Supplier guidance recommends a carrier protein for long-term storage and warns against freeze-thaw [1].

### What the body does with it [mixed]
56. In vitro half-life is about 15 minutes, set by an aminopeptidase-like enzyme [2] (reported). It may circulate partly bound to carrier proteins, but that has not been confirmed. Human plasma levels of DSIP-like material are reported to fall at sleep onset, the opposite of what a sleep-inducing signal might predict, and levels in depression are reported both higher and lower by different studies [2] (reported).
57. No pharmacokinetic study by subcutaneous injection exists in people, and the FDA found no human trial by that route at all [1] (verified).

### The human record, which got weaker as it got better designed [people]
58. Every human trial gave DSIP intravenously, at 25 to 35 nanomoles per kilogram [1] (verified). The pattern across them is worth seeing whole.
59. The early, smaller or externally controlled studies looked positive. In 6 middle-aged insomniacs in a crossover, sleep improved from the second to the sixth hour after a slight arousal in the first. In 14 people given seven nightly injections, sleep latency fell from 58.4 to 27.6 minutes and total sleep rose from 313 to 385 minutes, but against an externally matched group, not a true placebo [1] (verified).
60. The properly placebo-controlled studies did not hold up. In 16 insomniacs over three nights, there was no significant objective difference against placebo, and the authors called the benefit little. In 6 severe insomniacs in a randomised crossover, there was no significant difference from placebo or baseline [1] (verified).
61. Narcolepsy rests on one 35-year-old man: sleep attacks fell from six to three, better with morning dosing [1]. In withdrawal, an uncontrolled study of 60 opioid users reported a good decrease in signs in 40 of those assessed, with anxiety and insomnia returning 24 to 72 hours later; a later open study of 7 saw withdrawal scores fall from 34.4 to 17.8 after the first injection, but only 2 finished and 2 needed another sleep drug [1] (verified). The authors of that later study called effectiveness an open question.
62. There is no established human dose by any route people use now.

### Safety and quality concerns [mixed]
63. What the trials recorded: transient arousal in the first hour, delayed effects on sleep 13 to 22 hours later, and rebound anxiety and insomnia after withdrawal treatment stopped [1] (verified). Long-term safety has never been studied [2].
64. The agency's main concern was not toxicity but identity and immunogenicity: an injectable peptide with unidentified impurities and no aggregate data, given repeatedly [1] (verified). A Russian preparation, Deltaran, is reported to have extended maximum lifespan in ageing mice by about 24% [2] (reported), but no human safety record follows from that.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
65. Not a registered drug in any country searched, and in no major pharmacopoeia [1] (verified). Listed in the US do-not-compound category in 2024 [6]. On 24 July 2026 the advisory committee voted 6 in favour, 7 against, with one abstention, for opioid withdrawal, chronic insomnia and narcolepsy, making it the only one of seven peptides that day not recommended [3,4] (verified). FDA staff had also recommended against [1].
66. Sport: not named, but with no government approval it falls within the catch-all non-approved class, prohibited at all times [5] (inferred application, verified wording).

### Open questions [mixed]
67. Is it a mammalian peptide? Without a gene or precursor, the question from 1977 is still open.
68. Does it do anything under the skin? The route in use today has never been tested in a human trial.
69. Why did effects shrink as controls improved? The honest reading is that the early signal was mostly expectation and night-to-night variation, but a larger modern trial would settle it (inferred).
