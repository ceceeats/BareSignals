# NAD+ + MOTS-c + 5-Amino-1MQ
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/mitoprime
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: MitoPrime, NAD+ / MOTS-c / 5-Amino-1MQ blend, NAD+ + MOTS-c + 5-Amino-1MQ, Mito blend, MOTS-c + NAD+ + 5-Amino 120 mg
2. Origin: Blend: 1 peptide + 1 small molecule + coenzyme
3. Legal and approval status: No approved product anywhere. US: NAD+ is compounded and sold as a supplement; MOTS-c and 5-Amino-1MQ are unapproved research chemicals (MOTS-c was recommended by the July 2026 FDA compounding panel, no rule yet). WADA: AMPK activators are banned under S4 metabolic modulators, which covers MOTS-c (inferred); the blend is not safe for tested sport.

## Summary
4. Mitoprime is a one-vial injectable mix of NAD+ (the cell's energy coenzyme), MOTS-c (a peptide coded by mitochondrial DNA) and 5-Amino-1MQ (a small molecule that blocks the fat-cell enzyme NNMT). People take it for energy and fat loss during a diet. Evidence is mostly in mice per component, the blend has no trial, and the vial's amounts fall below each part's usual dose.
5. Punch line: Mitochondria in one vial. NAD+ and MOTS-c fuel energy while 5-Amino-1MQ blocks fat storage; dosed through a diet.
6. Used for: Energy and fat loss support, often alongside a GLP-1 or cardio

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Cellular energy: NAD+ and MOTS-c both aim at mitochondria
8. Fat metabolism: 5-Amino-1MQ and MOTS-c cut fat gain in mice
9. Glucose & insulin: MOTS-c improves insulin response in mice
10. Drive & mental stamina: Users take it for energy during a diet
11. Muscle & performance (backed only by animal or user reports): MOTS-c studied for exercise capacity
12. Aging & senescence (backed only by animal or user reports): NAD+ falls with age
13. DNA & telomeres (backed only by animal or user reports): NAD+ feeds DNA-repair enzymes

## How it works
14. Refills the cell's energy coenzyme · NAD+ for sirtuins, PARPs, electron transport · role Cellular energy
   NAD+ is the coenzyme that carries electrons through glycolysis and the electron transport chain, and the fuel for sirtuins and PARPs. Supplying it aims to refill pools that fall with age and stress. Human studies mainly show raised blood levels, not proven fat loss or energy gains.
15. Feeds DNA-repair enzymes · PARP substrate · role DNA & telomeres
   PARPs are DNA repair enzymes that use up NAD+ every time they mark damaged DNA for repair. More NAD+ keeps them supplied without draining the energy pool. This is established cell biology; benefit from extra NAD+ in healthy people is not shown.
16. Mitochondrial signal that acts like exercise · MOTS-c, AMPK activation · role Cellular energy
   MOTS-c is a small peptide made from mitochondrial DNA that activates AMPK, the cell's energy sensor that also switches on during exercise. AMPK raises fat and sugar burning. In mice it resisted diet-induced fat gain; there are no human trials.
17. Better insulin response in muscle · MOTS-c, folate cycle, AICAR · role Glucose & insulin
   MOTS-c slows the folate cycle, which builds up AICAR, a natural AMPK activator. That increases glucose uptake into muscle and improves insulin response. This was shown in insulin-resistant mice; human data are lacking.
18. Stops fat cells wasting NAD+ precursors · NNMT inhibition · role Fat metabolism
   5-Amino-1MQ blocks NNMT, an enzyme in fat cells that uses up nicotinamide, an NAD+ precursor, and SAM methyl groups. With NNMT blocked, more of both stay available. In the blend, this is meant to slow NAD+ loss; it is shown in mouse and cell studies.
19. Shrank fat cells in obese mice · 5-Amino-1MQ, SAM sparing · role Fat metabolism
   By sparing NAD+ and SAM in fat cells, 5-Amino-1MQ raised their energy use and reduced fat cell size. Diet-induced obese mice lost fat mass without eating less. There are no human trials of this compound.
20. Mechanism overview: NAD+ is the hydride carrier for glycolysis and the electron transport chain and the substrate for sirtuins and PARPs. MOTS-c, a 16-residue peptide (MRWQEMGYIFYPRKLR) from the mitochondrial 12S rRNA region, inhibits the folate cycle, raises AICAR and activates AMPK, improving insulin sensitivity in mice. 5-Amino-1MQ is a quaternary methylquinolinium that inhibits nicotinamide N-methyltransferase (NNMT), which in adipose tissue drains nicotinamide and SAM; blocking it raised adipocyte NAD+ and reduced fat mass in obese mice. The blend pairs NAD+ supply with an NNMT block that should slow NAD+ precursor loss, plus a parallel AMPK signal. No study has tested whether the parts add up.

## What is inside the blend
21. NAD+: 100 mg · The energy coenzyme itself, the bulk of the vial
22. MOTS-c: 10 mg · Mitochondrial peptide signal, AMPK and insulin response
23. 5-Amino-1MQ: 10 mg · Blocks NNMT so fat cells keep NAD+ precursors
24. Most common ratio: NAD+ 100 · MOTS-c 10 · 5-Amino-1MQ 10 = 120 mg (12 of 12 listings (6 state the split, 6 sell 120 mg))
25. Version Common retail: NAD+ 100 · MOTS-c 10 · 5-Amino-1MQ 10 = 120 mg
26. Version Higher 5-Amino version: NAD+ 100 · 5-Amino-1MQ 50 · MOTS-c 10 = 160 mg
27. Why NAD+ is in it: Supplies the coenzyme directly
28. Why MOTS-c is in it: Adds a signalling peptide; the only part with an exercise-like effect
29. Why 5-Amino-1MQ is in it: Slows NAD+ precursor loss in fat cells
30. Mixing chemistry: NAD+ is 100 of the 120 mg, so it sets the vial's pH and saltiness.
31. Mixing chemistry: NAD+ breaks down faster at higher pH and in phosphate or HEPES buffers; peptides often prefer different buffers.
32. Mixing chemistry: MOTS-c carries 2 methionines and a tryptophan, the parts most prone to oxidation in liquid.
33. Mixing chemistry: 5-Amino-1MQ is a small charged molecule; no cross-reaction with NAD+ is known.
34. Mixing chemistry: No published stability data for the mixed vial after reconstitution.
35. Self-mixing: Mixing separate vials puts an unknown NAD+ pH onto MOTS-c for weeks; drawing each into one syringe just before injecting avoids most of that.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
36. Typical: 10–20 units of a 120 mg vial in 2 mL · 3–4× weekly, morning, fasted · cycle Not stated · route Sub-Q (reported)
37. Half-life: per component
38. Most people take: No standard dose. Sellers size it by the NAD+ share: 10 to 20 units of a 120 mg vial in 2 mL gives NAD+ 5 to 10 mg and MOTS-c and 5-Amino-1MQ 0.5 to 1 mg each. Much lower per part than people use when dosing each one alone.

## What people report
39. Sub-Q, morning, fasted, before cardio or training, 3 to 4 times a week (seller protocol). A second retail version with 50 mg 5-Amino-1MQ is dosed 25 to 50 units daily from a 5 mL mix. NAD+ injections are known for a burning sting and flushing, so expect that from the NAD+ share.
40. Liked / worked: Each part cut fat or improved glucose in mice
41. Liked / worked: Seller reports more energy while dieting
42. Liked / worked: Convenient one-vial dosing
43. What worked: A seller newsletter (Sep 2025) reports the author 'felt more energetic while losing fat' on 10 to 20 units 3 to 4 times a week. Component animal data: MOTS-c and 5-Amino-1MQ each cut fat gain in obese mice; NAD+ improved glucose handling in animal work.
44. Went wrong / did not work: Never tested together, in any species
45. Went wrong / did not work: Each part far below its usual solo dose
46. Went wrong / did not work: No stability data on the mixed vial
47. What went wrong: No human data on the blend and almost no independent user reports. At the published draw sizes each part lands far below its own usual dose: 20 units gives about 10 mg NAD+ (usual 50 to 100 mg), 1 mg MOTS-c (usual 5 to 10 mg) and 1 mg 5-Amino-1MQ (usually taken orally at 50 to 100 mg).
48. Most reported: 10 to 20 units, 3 to 4× a week
49. Most reported: Morning, fasted, before training
50. Most reported: NAD+ share can sting and flush
51. Most reported: Often stacked with a GLP-1

## What has been tested
52. Fat metabolism: 5-Amino-1MQ in diet-induced obese mice (mouse)
53. Glucose & insulin: MOTS-c in insulin-resistant mice (mouse)
54. Cellular energy: NAD+ infusion and oral precursors (human)
55. Cellular energy: The three combined (none)
56. Nothing tests the combination. Components: NAD+ in human infusion and supplement studies; MOTS-c in mouse obesity and exercise models; 5-Amino-1MQ in diet-induced obese mice (mostly oral or injected alone). No stability study of the mixed vial exists.
57. What it helps: Evidence is per component and mostly animal. MOTS-c and 5-Amino-1MQ each reduced fat gain in obese mice; NAD+ is studied in humans mainly for blood levels, not fat loss. The blend has no trial and only seller reports of more energy while dieting.

## Who shouldn't take it
58. People with active or recent cancer: Tumours depend on NAD+ for growth and DNA repair. (reported)
59. Insulin or sulfonylurea users: Raises insulin sensitivity via AMPK; adding it to glucose-lowering drugs can drop sugar too far. (inferred)
60. People on PARP inhibitors (olaparib etc.): PARP enzymes use NAD+; extra NAD+ may work against these drugs. (inferred)
61. Drug-tested athletes: WADA S4.4 bans AMPK activators as metabolic modulators; MOTS-c works through AMPK. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
62. SS-31: SS-31 sits in the inner mitochondrial membrane and steadies the energy machinery, an approved drug for Barth syndrome. The blend raises NAD+ and AMPK signalling. People pair them for mitochondrial energy; the pair has not been tested together.
63. SLU-PP-332: SLU-PP-332 switches on ERR, the receptors behind endurance genes, and builds mitochondria, while MOTS-c here activates AMPK. Both mimic exercise from different angles. Each has mouse data only, and the pair has not been tested.
64. BAM-15: BAM-15 makes mitochondria leak protons so fuel burns as heat, while the blend supplies NAD+ and an AMPK signal. People stack them for fat loss. Each has mouse data only; the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
65. L-Carnitine: L-carnitine carries fatty acids into mitochondria, the place where NAD+ and AMPK signalling push fat burning. People pair them for fat oxidation. Carnitine has small oral weight loss effects; the pair has not been tested.
66. Retatrutide: Retatrutide drives weight loss through three receptors, with large human trials. The blend is added for energy and fat metabolism while eating less. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
67. Tirzepatide: Tirzepatide does the main weight loss work through GIP and GLP-1 receptors, with large human trials. Users add this blend for energy during the deficit. The pair has not been tested together.
68. Semaglutide: Semaglutide cuts appetite through the GLP-1 receptor and has large human trials. The blend is added to offset low energy while dieting. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
69. A one-vial injectable mix of three compounds sold for mitochondria and fat loss: NAD+ (the cell's energy coenzyme), MOTS-c (a small peptide coded by mitochondrial DNA) and 5-Amino-1MQ (a small molecule that blocks the fat-cell enzyme NNMT). The common vial is 100 + 10 + 10 = 120 mg.
70. First studied for: Grey-market blend that appeared around 2025, marketed as a 'stable' all-in-one for energy during fat loss. Each part was studied alone; the mix never was.
71. Name story: A product name: 'Mito' for mitochondria, 'Prime' for priming them. The same name is used, unrelated, for an L-ergothioneine supplement ingredient and a mitochondrial vitamin formula, so searches mix them up. [S1] (reported)
72. 2013: 5-Amino-1MQ family identified as NNMT blockers; NNMT tied to obesity (inferred date)
73. 2015: MOTS-c discovered, coded in mitochondrial DNA
74. 2018: 5-Amino-1MQ shrinks fat in obese mice
75. 2025: Sold pre-mixed with NAD+ as a 120 mg vial
76. Today: Grey-market 'mito' blend for energy during fat loss
