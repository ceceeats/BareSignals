# Thymogen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/thymogen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, oglufanide, L-Glu-L-Trp, EW, IM862 (oglufanide disodium)
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: EW (2 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Registered as a medicine in Russia; research chemical elsewhere.

## Summary
5. Thymogen is a lab-made dipeptide (Glu-Trp) Khavinson immune bioregulator derived from thymic peptide research. It is registered as a medicine in Russia, including a nasal form, and is used for respiratory and immune conditions and faster recovery from infection. It is claimed to help T-cells mature, but independent data are thin and there is no Western validation.
6. Punch line: Khavinson's immune dipeptide, a registered Russian medicine. Injected or sprayed nasally to shorten infections.
7. Used for: Immune support during infections

11. Skin & aesthetics: Skin healing claims
12. Digestive & gut (backed only by animal or user reports): Gut lining claims

## How it works
13. Claimed to stimulate immune cells · Khavinson group · role Immunity
   Thymogen, the dipeptide Glu-Trp, is claimed by the Khavinson tradition to activate immune cells and rebalance their responses. That is the basis for its Russian clinical use, including a nasal form, in respiratory and immune conditions. Independent data are thin.
14. Claimed to help T-cells mature · Glu-Trp dipeptide, thymus · role Immunity
   Glu-Trp comes from research on the thymus, the organ where T-cells mature, and is claimed to help T-cell precursors finish that maturation. Better maturation would mean more working T-cells during infection. This rests on Russian studies with no Western validation.
15. Claimed to speed wound and tissue healing · Regenerative response · role Tissue repair
   The group also claims Thymogen speeds wound and tissue healing, framed as a knock-on effect of its immune action on the repair response. Russian clinical reports describe faster recovery from infection. Independent controlled trials of this are lacking.
16. Mechanism overview: Thymogen is the Khavinson dipeptide Glu-Trp, derived from thymic peptide research. The group claims it stimulates immune cells, helps T-cells mature and speeds wound and tissue healing. These claims rest largely on one research tradition with little outside data. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## Who shouldn't take it
36. Transplant recipients on anti-rejection drugs: Registered in Russia as a T-cell immunomodulator, which works against anti-rejection drugs. (inferred)
37. People with autoimmune disease: Turning up immune activity can flare an autoimmune condition. (inferred)

## Where it comes from
38. A dipeptide (Glu-Trp) Khavinson immune bioregulator, derived from thymic peptide research.
39. First studied for: Immune stimulation, developed in Russia, including a nasal form.
40. Name story: The dipeptide Glu-Trp, first found inside the Thymalin extract and then synthesised; registered in the USSR in 1990. Its INN is oglufanide, one of the few Khavinson-era peptides with an official international name. [S4] (verified)
41. 1990s to 2000s: Glu-Trp dipeptide from thymic peptide research in Russia
42. Today: Registered in Russia as an immune medicine, including a nasal form
