# Prostamax
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/prostamax
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, KEDP, Lys-Glu-Asp-Pro, prostate bioregulator
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: KEDP (4 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Prostamax is a lab-made tetrapeptide (Lys-Glu-Asp-Pro) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the prostate. It is used for prostate health in aging men, especially chronic prostate inflammation, and it is claimed to tune prostate tissue genes. Evidence is Russian animal work and reports with no Western validation, and first-hand reports are few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson prostate bioregulator for aging men, claimed to calm chronic prostate inflammation. Mostly Russian data.
7. Used for: Prostate health, BPH, prostatitis

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Organ function: Prostate support
9. Tissue repair: Prostate tissue claims
10. Reproductive: Men's reproductive health
11. Pain & sensory (backed only by animal or user reports): Less pelvic discomfort reported

## How it works
12. Claimed to tune prostate tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Organ function
   Prostamax is claimed to act on DNA in prostate cells and tune which genes are active, rather than binding a surface receptor. The aim is to keep prostate tissue working in aging men. This is one group's claim, from Russian animal work.
13. Claimed to calm chronic prostate inflammation · Prostate inflammation · role Pain & sensory
   The group claims it calms chronic inflammation in the prostate, the kind behind long-running prostatitis. Less inflammation would mean less swelling and pain. There is no Western validation and first-hand reports are few.
14. Mechanism overview: Prostamax is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide for the prostate. The group claims it tunes prostate tissue genes and calms chronic prostatic inflammation. These mechanisms are reported by one group without independent confirmation. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## What has been tested
31. Reproductive: Prostate function in aging men (animal)
32. Russian studies only.
33. What it helps: Russian animal work and reports describe support for prostate function in aging men, including chronic prostate inflammation. There is no Western validation. First-hand reports are few and come mainly from people with an interest in men's health and aging. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community. Interest centres on chronic prostate inflammation in older men.
34. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

35. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
36. Men under PSA monitoring: Effect on PSA unknown; can confuse surveillance. (inferred)

## Where it comes from
39. A tetrapeptide (Lys-Glu-Asp-Pro) Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the prostate.
40. First studied for: Prostate health and function in aging men.
41. Name story: Khavinson naming again: Prostamax = prostate. A tetrapeptide, Lys-Glu-Asp-Pro (KEDP), from the St. Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology. [S5] (inferred)
