# Petrelintide
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/petrelintide
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: ZP8396, petre, Zealand amylin, Roche amylin
2. Origin: Lab-modified natural chain: long-acting amylin analogue
3. Legal and approval status: Investigational, not approved anywhere as of Oct 2026. Phase 3 ZUPREME program started in 2026 with Roche. Not named on the WADA list (falls under S0, inferred).

## Summary
4. Petrelintide is Zealand Pharma's once-weekly copy of amylin, the fullness hormone released with insulin. It is developed for weight loss through earlier fullness and slower stomach emptying, as a gentler route and a partner to GLP-1 drugs. Phase 2 showed up to 10.7% weight loss at 42 weeks with good tolerance. That is well below tirzepatide or semaglutide, and some investors called the trial disappointing.
5. Punch line: Zealand's weekly amylin shot, the gentle fullness route. Up to 10.7% weight loss; built to pair with GLP-1s.
6. Used for: Obesity, type 2 diabetes, investigational

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Appetite & satiety: Makes you feel full sooner
8. Fat metabolism: Up to 10.7% weight loss at 42 weeks
9. Glucose & insulin: Lowers HbA1c in type 2 diabetes
10. Digestive & gut: Slows stomach emptying, milder nausea

## How it works
11. Activates amylin and calcitonin receptors in the brainstem · Amylin receptor agonist · role Appetite & satiety
   Petrelintide activates amylin and calcitonin receptors in the brainstem, the area that signals fullness after a meal. People feel satisfied with less food. In Phase 2 human trials this gave modest, steady weight loss with good stomach tolerance.
12. Slows stomach emptying · Gastric emptying · role Digestive & gut
   Amylin receptor signalling slows how fast the stomach empties into the intestine. Food stays longer, adding to fullness and flattening sugar rises after meals. This is textbook amylin pharmacology, shared with pramlintide.
13. Blunts glucagon after meals · Glucagon suppression · role Glucose & insulin
   After a meal it holds back glucagon, the hormone that tells the liver to release sugar. Less glucagon means a smaller post-meal glucose rise. This fits the improvements seen in the type 2 diabetes trial.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
14. Typical: Trial doses not disclosed in the sources checked (Phase 1b started at 0.6 and 1.2 mg) · Once weekly · cycle Trial only (28 to 42 weeks in Phase 2) · route Sub-Q (trial)
15. Most people take: No approved dose. Weekly injection with the dose raised every four weeks for up to 16 weeks, then held.

## What people report
16. Discussed online as the 'gentle' amylin shot and a possible partner for GLP-1 drugs; Roche plans a combination with its GLP-1/GIP drug CT-388. Little verified first-hand use.
17. Liked / worked: Up to 10.7% weight loss at 42 weeks
18. Liked / worked: Few stomach-related dropouts
19. What worked: Up to 10.7% weight loss at 42 weeks versus 1.7% on placebo in ZUPREME-1, with no vomiting and no stomach-related dropouts in the most effective arm. Up to 9.2% weight loss and HbA1c down 0.61 to 0.88 points in type 2 diabetes.
20. Went wrong / did not work: Less weight loss than GLP-1 drugs
21. Went wrong / did not work: Nausea during dose increases
22. What went wrong: Weight loss is well below tirzepatide, semaglutide and CagriSema; some investors called ZUPREME-1 disappointing. Nausea still occurs, mostly mild and during dose increases.
23. Most reported: Talked about as a gentler amylin option
24. Most reported: Possible GLP-1 partner

## What has been tested
25. Fat metabolism: Obesity, ZUPREME-1, 42 weeks (trial)
26. Glucose & insulin: Obesity with type 2 diabetes, ZUPREME-2 (trial)
27. Phase 1b over 16 weeks in 48 people; Phase 2 ZUPREME-1 in 493 people with obesity over 42 weeks; Phase 2 ZUPREME-2 in 220 people with type 2 diabetes over 28 weeks. Phase 3 (ZUPREME-3, 4 and 5, about 7,000 people) has started.
28. What it helps: Phase 2 shows modest, steady weight loss with good stomach tolerance: up to 10.7% at 42 weeks in obesity and up to 9.2% at 28 weeks in type 2 diabetes. Its main pitch is tolerability and use as a partner to GLP-1 drugs.
29. Trial: active · Phase 3. Zealand and Roche have started the ZUPREME-3, 4 and 5 Phase 3 trials with about 7,000 people. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1]

## Synergies (paired with)
30. Cagrilintide: Cagrilintide is another long-acting amylin analogue on the same receptors, so the two are alternatives. Stacking them duplicates the mechanism. It has not been tested together.
31. Amylin: Amylin is the natural hormone petrelintide copies, and pramlintide is its short-acting drug form. They overlap completely, so this is a reference point, not a stack. The pair has no trial data.
32. Eloralintide: Eloralintide is a competing weekly amylin drug on the same receptors. They are rivals in one class, not complementary partners. Combining them has not been tested.
33. Amycretin: Amycretin already combines GLP-1 and amylin activity in one molecule, so adding petrelintide doubles the amylin side. That overlap gives little reason to stack. The pair has not been tested together.
34. Semaglutide: Semaglutide cuts appetite through GLP-1 receptors while petrelintide adds amylin fullness signalling, two separate routes to eating less. Pairing with GLP-1 drugs is petrelintide's main pitch. The specific combination has not yet been tested in published trials.

## Where it comes from
35. Designed by Zealand Pharma in Copenhagen as a once-weekly copy of amylin, the fullness hormone released with insulin. Roche bought half the rights in March 2025 for 1.65 billion dollars upfront.
36. First studied for: Weight loss as a gentler alternative to GLP-1 drugs, aiming for fullness with less nausea.
37. Jun 2024: Phase 1b: 8.6% weight loss in 16 weeks
38. Dec 2024: ZUPREME-1 Phase 2 starts
39. Mar 2025: Roche deal worth up to 5.3 billion dollars
40. Mar 2026: ZUPREME-1: up to 10.7% at 42 weeks
41. Oct 2026: ZUPREME-2: up to 9.2% in type 2 diabetes
42. Today: Phase 3 ZUPREME program with about 7,000 people
