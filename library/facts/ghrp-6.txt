# GHRP-6
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ghrp-6
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: SK&F 110679, His-D-Trp-Ala-Trp-D-Phe-Lys-NH2, growth hormone releasing hexapeptide
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: HWAWFK (6 amino acids). D-Trp and D-Phe shown as W and F; amide end
4. Legal and approval status: Unapproved research chemical. WADA-banned.

## Summary
5. GHRP-6 is an early synthetic hexapeptide that acts like ghrelin to trigger a growth hormone pulse. People use it mainly for appetite stimulation and GH release, and it was studied in hunger and cachexia. The hunger is extreme for many users, it mildly raises cortisol and prolactin, and there are no outcome trials.
6. Punch line: The original hunger peptide. Ghrelin mimic with a reliable GH pulse and fierce appetite; a bulking favourite.
7. Used for: GH release and appetite for bulking

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Endocrine & growth: GH release
9. Appetite & satiety: Strong hunger
10. Sleep & rhythms: Better sleep reported

## How it works
11. Mimics ghrelin to trigger a GH pulse · Ghrelin receptor, GHSR-1a · role Endocrine & growth
   GHRP-6 binds the ghrelin receptor, GHSR-1a, on cells of the pituitary, the gland that stores growth hormone. Activation releases a pulse of GH into the blood. Human studies confirm a reliable GH rise.
12. Switches on strong hunger · Hypothalamic ghrelin signal · role Appetite & satiety
   In the hypothalamus, the brain's appetite centre, the same ghrelin receptor carries the body's main hunger signal. GHRP-6 switches it on hard, giving the strongest hunger effect of the GHRPs. This is shown in people and has been studied for cachexia, the wasting seen in serious illness.
13. Amplifies GHRH pulses, blunts somatostatin · GHRH synergy · role Endocrine & growth
   GHRP-6 boosts each pulse of GHRH, the hypothalamic hormone that tells the pituitary to release GH. It also blunts somatostatin, the hormone that holds GH back. The result is a bigger GH pulse than GHRH alone would give.
14. Mildly raises cortisol and prolactin · ACTH, prolactin release · role Endocrine & growth
   The ghrelin receptor also nudges the pituitary to release ACTH, which raises cortisol from the adrenal glands, and some prolactin. With GHRP-6 these rises are mild. They are part of why it is less selective than ipamorelin.
15. May deepen slow-wave sleep (ghrelin data) · Ghrelin, sleep EEG · role Sleep & rhythms
   Ghrelin signalling has been tied to deeper slow-wave sleep in sleep EEG research. That link comes from ghrelin data, not from sleep trials of GHRP-6. It is a possibility, not a shown effect.
16. Mechanism overview: GHRP-6 is a hexapeptide that activates the ghrelin receptor, GHSR-1a. In the pituitary that triggers a GH pulse, amplifying GHRH and blunting somatostatin. In the hypothalamus the same ghrelin signal switches on strong hunger. It mildly raises ACTH, so cortisol, and prolactin. Animal work shows cardioprotective effects, and ghrelin data suggest it may deepen slow-wave sleep. Its hunger effect is the strongest of the GHRPs.

## What has been tested
33. Endocrine & growth: GH release (human)
34. Appetite & satiety: Hunger and cachexia (human)
35. Heart & vessels: Cardioprotection (animal)
36. Human studies confirm GH release and strong appetite; studied for cachexia and as a cardioprotective agent.
37. What it helps: Human studies show a reliable GH pulse and powerful appetite stimulation, studied in hunger and cachexia. Animal work suggests heart protection. There are no outcome trials. Users take it mostly for appetite and GH, and many dislike how extreme the hunger gets.
38. Trial: none · heart and brain protection · CIGB Cuba. Only small academic human studies, mostly from Cuba in the 2000s and 2010s; no registered trial found since. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
39. People with Cushing’s or high cortisol: Raises cortisol and ACTH alongside GH. (reported)
40. People with prolactinoma or high prolactin: GHRPs push prolactin up alongside GH, worsening prolactin problems. (reported)
41. People with active or recent cancer: It raises GH and IGF-1, growth signals that tumour cells also respond to. (inferred)
42. People with binge eating: It mimics the hunger hormone ghrelin and drives intense appetite. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
43. CJC-1295 no DAC: CJC-1295 no DAC drives the GHRH receptor and GHRP-6 drives the ghrelin receptor, so the pituitary releases more GH than with either alone. The synergy between the two receptor types is established, but this exact pair has not been trialled, and GHRP-6 brings strong hunger.
44. CJC-1295 with DAC: The DAC version keeps GHRH tone raised for about a week while GHRP-6 adds pulses and appetite. People stack them for bulking, with more GH and more food intake, but the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
45. Sermorelin: Sermorelin starts a natural GHRH pulse and GHRP-6 amplifies it while blunting somatostatin. The mechanisms fit together, but the pair itself has not been tested together.
46. Tesamorelin: Tesamorelin is a stabilised GHRH with trial data for visceral fat, and GHRP-6 adds GH release through a second receptor. The strong hunger from GHRP-6 works against a fat-loss goal, and the pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
47. An earlier GH-releasing hexapeptide, strongly ghrelin-like, with marked appetite stimulation.
48. First studied for: A GH-releasing agent; its intense hunger effect became its signature.
49. Name story: Built by Cyril Bowers and Frank Momany in the early 1980s by reworking met-enkephalin, an opioid peptide. It hit a receptor nobody knew existed; ghrelin, the body's own key for that lock, wasn't found until 1999. [S5] (verified)
50. 1980s: One of the first GH-releasing hexapeptides, strongly ghrelin-like
51. 1990s to 2000s: Studied for cachexia and heart protection
52. Today: Known mainly for intense hunger; unapproved, WADA-banned
