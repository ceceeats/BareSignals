# Teriparatide
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/teriparatide
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Forteo, Forsteo, rhPTH(1-34), PTH(1-34), LY333334, Bonsity, Terrosa, Movymia
2. Origin: Fragment of a natural protein
3. Sequence: SVSEIQLMHNLGKHLNSMERVEWLRKKLQDVHNF (34 amino acids). PTH 1–34
4. Shown with an FDA approved badge
5. Legal and approval status: FDA-approved (Forteo, plus follow-on versions) for osteoporosis. Catalog vials are not the approved product.

## Summary
6. Teriparatide (Forteo) is the first 34 amino acids of parathyroid hormone. Given as a daily pulse it switches on bone building, and it is an FDA-approved treatment for severe osteoporosis that cuts spine fractures. It needs daily injection, the gains are lost unless another bone drug follows, and grey-market catalog vials are not the approved product.
7. Punch line: Forteo, the active front of parathyroid hormone. A daily pulse builds new bone and cuts spine fractures.
8. Used for: Osteoporosis with high fracture risk

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
9. Bone & cartilage: Builds new bone
10. Endocrine & growth: Parathyroid hormone piece
11. Pain & sensory: Less back pain after fractures

## How it works
12. Mimics parathyroid hormone's first 34 amino acids · PTH(1-34) · role Endocrine & growth
   Teriparatide is the first 34 amino acids of parathyroid hormone, the part that does the hormone's work. This piece carries full activity at the PTH1 receptor. It is a well-established approved drug.
13. Daily pulses switch bone building on · PTH1 receptor · role Bone & cartilage
   Given as a short daily pulse, PTH1 receptor activation switches on osteoblasts, the cells that lay down new bone. Constant PTH does the opposite and breaks bone down. Timing decides the outcome, and this is shown in large human trials.
14. Bone formation outpaces bone loss · Osteoblast activation · role Bone & cartilage
   Bone is constantly built by osteoblasts and removed by osteoclasts. Pulsed teriparatide tips the balance so formation outpaces removal, giving a net gain of new bone. Phase 3 trials show rising bone density.
15. Lowers the bone-building brake sclerostin · Sclerostin suppression · role Bone & cartilage
   Sclerostin is a protein from bone cells that acts as a brake on bone building. Teriparatide lowers it, freeing osteoblasts to work. This is part of how the pulse turns into new bone.
16. Briefly raises blood calcium · Renal Ca reabsorption, vit D · role Endocrine & growth
   Like natural PTH, it raises blood calcium for a few hours by helping the kidneys hold on to calcium and by activating vitamin D. The rise is brief because the drug is a short pulse. It is a known, monitored effect.
17. Cuts spine fractures and back pain · Fracture risk reduction · role Pain & sensory
   In large Phase 3 trials in severe osteoporosis, it cut new spine fractures and reduced back pain. It is one of the few drugs that builds bone rather than only slowing loss. The gains fade unless another bone drug follows.
18. Mechanism overview: Teriparatide is the first 34 amino acids of parathyroid hormone, the part that activates the PTH1 receptor. Constant PTH breaks bone down, but a daily pulse switches osteoblasts on and suppresses sclerostin, the brake on bone building. Formation then outpaces resorption, adding new bone. It also briefly raises blood calcium by increasing kidney reabsorption and vitamin D activation. The pulse matters: timing, not total dose, decides whether bone is built or lost.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
19. Typical: 20 mcg · 1× daily · cycle Up to 2 years (usual) · route Sub-Q (verified)
20. Half-life: ~1 h
21. Dose note: The 40 mcg trial arm had twice the side-effect dropouts and healed fractures no faster. 20 mcg is the real top.
22. Label: 20 mcg · 1× daily · up to 2 yr · Sub-Q · for osteoporosis; off-label fracture healing [S1] [S2] (verified)

24. High trial arm: 40 mcg · 1× daily · ~18 mo; 8 wk fracture trial · Sub-Q · for trial only [S1] [S4] (verified)

## What has been tested
35. Bone & cartilage: Bone density, Phase 3 (trial)

39. Trial: active · Phase 4 · postmenopausal osteoporosis · NCT06558188. Approved since 2002; still recruiting in osteoporosis and fracture studies, and an oral tablet version (EB613) is heading to Phase 3. (checked 2026-10-23) [S5] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
40. Paget's disease, unexplained high ALP, prior bone radiation: Higher baseline osteosarcoma risk; label warns. (verified)
41. People on digoxin: Transient high calcium can trigger digoxin toxicity. (verified)
42. People with kidney stones or high urine calcium: It raises urine and blood calcium, which can grow new stones. (verified)
43. People prone to dizziness on standing: Orthostatic hypotension at first doses. (verified)

## Where it comes from
44. The active 1-34 fragment of parathyroid hormone (Forteo), which, given in once-daily pulses, builds bone instead of breaking it down.
45. First studied for: Severe osteoporosis at high fracture risk.
46. Name story: The name hides a plain recipe: residues 1–34 of the 84-amino-acid parathyroid hormone, made recombinantly. Lilly's Forteo (Forsteo in Europe) carried a rat bone-cancer warning that human data never confirmed. [S6] (verified)
47. 2002: FDA approves PTH(1-34) as Forteo for severe osteoporosis
48. 2020: Old rat bone-cancer boxed warning removed
49. Today: An established bone-building drug, with follow-on versions
