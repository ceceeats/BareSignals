# NAD+
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/nadplus
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: nad coenzyme, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, DPN+, diphosphopyridine nucleotide, coenzyme I, cozymase, NADH (reduced form)
2. Origin: Not a peptide
3. Legal and approval status: Sold as a supplement/compounded infusion; not an approved drug for anti-aging.

## Summary
4. NAD+ is a coenzyme found in every cell, central to energy metabolism and to the sirtuin and PARP repair enzymes; it is not a peptide. People take it by infusion or injection to raise cellular NAD for energy, focus and recovery, since levels fall with age. Precursor trials raise NAD safely, but clear anti-aging outcomes in humans are unproven, and fast infusions often cause flushing and chest pressure.
5. Punch line: The energy coenzyme in every cell, falling with age. Infused or injected for focus; fast drips cause flushing.
6. Used for: Energy, recovery, healthy aging

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Cellular energy: Carries electrons that make cell energy
8. DNA & telomeres: Fuel for DNA repair enzymes
9. Cognition: Users report clearer thinking
10. Drive & mental stamina: Users report more drive
11. Reward & urges (backed only by animal or user reports): Studied in addiction clinics
12. Aging & senescence (backed only by animal or user reports): Levels fall with age

## How it works
13. Carries electrons to make cell energy · Redox coenzyme · role Cellular energy
   NAD+ is a redox coenzyme. In glycolysis and the Krebs cycle it accepts electrons to become NADH, which feeds complex I of the mitochondrial chain to make ATP. This role is settled biochemistry. Whether injected or infused NAD+ raises levels inside human cells has not been shown.
14. Fuels DNA repair enzymes · PARPs · role DNA & telomeres
   PARP enzymes burn NAD+ as fuel when they repair broken DNA, tagging damaged sites so repair proteins gather there. Heavy DNA damage can drain a cell's NAD+ supply this way. The rationale for supplementing is to keep this repair work fed, but no human trial has shown that outcome.
15. Feeds the sirtuin enzymes · Sirtuins · role Aging & senescence
   Sirtuins are enzymes that use NAD+ to strip acetyl groups from proteins, which changes how genes and metabolism are regulated. Their activity depends on NAD+ supply, which is the basis of the anti-aging case. Precursor trials raise blood NAD in people, but clear functional benefits have not followed.
16. Levels fall with age and stress · CD38 consumption · role Aging & senescence
   Tissue NAD+ falls with age, partly because CD38, an enzyme on immune and other cells, breaks it down more as inflammation rises. The one human infusion study found NAD+ cleared from blood almost as fast as it went in. Levels in blood do not show what happens inside tissues.
17. Supports neuron energy and resilience · Neuronal NAD+ pools · role Cognition
   Neurons have high energy needs and depend on steady NAD+ for both ATP production and stress repair. Animal and cell work links falling neuronal NAD+ to weaker resilience. Human users report better focus, but no human study has measured brain NAD+ after direct NAD+ dosing.
18. Mechanism overview: NAD+ is a coenzyme that carries electrons. In glycolysis and the Krebs cycle it accepts electrons to become NADH, which feeds complex I of the mitochondrial chain to make ATP. It is also consumed as a substrate: PARP enzymes burn it to repair DNA, sirtuins use it to strip acetyl groups from proteins, and CD38 breaks it down, increasingly with age and inflammation. Tissue levels decline with age, so the rationale is to restore supply for energy and repair. Whether intact NAD+ enters cells directly, or is first broken into precursors, is still debated.

## What has been tested
35. Aging & senescence: NR and NMN raise blood NAD (trial)

37. NAD itself is poorly cell-permeable, so most rigorous human trials use the precursors NR and NMN, which reliably raise blood NAD. Direct NAD infusion is widely used but thinly studied.
38. What it helps: Precursor trials with NR and NMN reliably raise blood NAD and look safe, but clear anti-aging outcomes in humans have not appeared. Direct NAD+ infusion is thinly studied. ## Who shouldn't take it
40. People with active or recent cancer: Tumours depend on NAD+ for growth and DNA repair. (reported)
41. People on PARP inhibitors (olaparib etc.): PARP enzymes use NAD+; extra NAD+ may work against these drugs. (inferred)
42. People with gout: Breaks down to nicotinamide and adenine; adenine feeds uric acid. (inferred)

47. GHK-Cu: GHK-Cu carries copper and turns up collagen making in skin and wounds, while NAD+ targets cell energy and repair enzymes linked to aging. People pair them in skin and healthy aging routines. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
48. Nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, a coenzyme in every cell, central to energy metabolism and to the sirtuin and PARP repair enzymes. Not a peptide. Levels fall with age.
49. First studied for: Given IV and by injection to raise cellular NAD for energy, recovery and anti-aging.
50. Name story: The "+" is not marketing: it marks the positive charge on a nitrogen in the oxidised form. NADH is the same molecule carrying electrons. Older textbooks call it DPN (diphosphopyridine nucleotide) or coenzyme I. [S5] (verified)

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Not a peptide, not a drug, and not a thing the body lets in [mixed]
54. NAD+ is a coenzyme every cell already makes and constantly recycles. That makes it unlike everything else in this library: there is no question of whether it is biologically important, and no question of whether a cell can use it. The whole question is whether putting it into the bloodstream, as a large charged dinucleotide, raises anything inside the cells that need it.
55. The honest framing is that NAD+ is not a deficiency state waiting to be corrected like iron. It is a flux: made from precursors, consumed by PARP enzymes repairing DNA, by sirtuins, and by CD38 on cell surfaces, which rises with age and inflammation (verified as the accepted biochemistry). Measuring a level tells you very little about the rate, and all the human product claims are about levels.
56. One structural point explains most of what follows. The molecule carries two phosphates and is large by cellular standards. There is no established plasma membrane transporter that brings intact NAD+ into cells in people, which is why the field works with precursors, nicotinamide riboside and nicotinamide mononucleotide, that have known entry routes (verified as the state of the field).

### The one human pharmacokinetic study, and what it found [people]
57. There is a single published human study tracking NAD+ in blood and urine during administration, and it is small: 11 healthy men aged 30 to 55, eight given 750 mg of NAD+ intravenously in saline over six hours at roughly 2 mg per minute, three given saline alone [1] (verified).
58. The central finding is the one most often skipped. Plasma NAD+ did not rise at all for the first hours; it was described as rapidly and completely removed from circulation for at least the first two hours, and only at the six-hour mark, the end of the infusion, was it about 398% above baseline [1] (verified). In other words, a slow infusion is almost entirely cleared as fast as it goes in, until the clearance routes saturate.
59. Where it went is partly visible in the metabolites: nicotinamide rose 409%, ADP-ribose 393% and methylnicotinamide 350% at six hours, with nicotinamide mononucleotide rising 472% only at eight hours [1]. Urinary NAD+ excretion rose 538% at six hours, while urinary nicotinamide did not change significantly, so intact NAD+ was appearing in urine [1] (verified). The authors attribute the disappearance to tissue uptake and to breakdown by the surface enzymes CD38, CD157 and CD203a, and state plainly that tissue uptake was not measured [1] (inferred by the authors, not demonstrated).
60. So the only human kinetic data show rapid destruction and urinary loss of a large fraction, with the rise in blood appearing only once the system is overwhelmed. Nothing in it shows that intracellular NAD+ in any tissue increased. The dose was chosen empirically from clinic practice, and the study was funded by a company running NAD+ infusion clinics, with one author a director of it and another receiving consulting fees [1] (verified).

### What raising blood NAD+ has and has not achieved in trials [people]
61. The precursors have been tested properly, which makes them the best available guide to whether the premise works. Nicotinamide riboside reliably raises measured NAD+ in blood or muscle, and the functional results have been consistently null.
62. Twenty-four adults aged 55 to 79 took 1,000 mg daily for six weeks with no significant group difference in vascular function after correction; 40 men took 2,000 mg daily for 12 weeks with no difference from placebo in metabolic outcomes, and muscle sampling from the same cohort found no mitochondrial change; 12 older men took 1,000 mg daily for 21 days with no change in how muscle mitochondria used oxygen; 30 adults took 2,000 mg daily for 12 weeks with no significant difference in walk distance or quality of life [2] (verified as reported, with trial sizes of 12 to 40 and durations of three weeks to three months).
63. That pattern, the biomarker moves and the outcome does not, is the most important fact in this entry. It is also the pattern that an injected or infused form inherits, since raising the biomarker is precisely what it is sold to do (inferred).
64. A separate and more basic gap: there is no published human study of subcutaneous or intramuscular NAD+ at all. Every human number above comes from intravenous infusion or oral precursors (verified as absent). The commonly quoted half-life figures for injected NAD+ have no human source found in this review.

### Tolerability, measured honestly for once [people]
65. The infusion experience is usually described as flushing or discomfort that passes. A 2026 retrospective review from a commercial clinic puts numbers on it, and they are not mild. Fourteen clients received 500 mg daily for four consecutive days in 500 mL of saline, six as NAD+ and eight as nicotinamide riboside [3] (verified).
66. All six NAD+ clients, six of six, reported moderate to severe abdominal cramping, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, raised heart rate, throat pain, congestion and chest pressure during the infusion, resolving when it stopped. Five of eight NR clients reported minor tingling in the tongue, jaw and arm with minor cramping [3] (verified).
67. The mechanism of the dose schedule is visible in the timing. Clients controlled their own flow rate by tolerance, and the NAD+ group slowed theirs so much that average infusion time was about 97 minutes against 37 for NR [3] (verified). The long slow drip is not a pharmacological choice; it is what the symptoms force.
68. The limits of that study are substantial and the authors state them: retrospective, 14 people, no control group, four days only, whole-blood NAD+ assays unable to detect changes, and all seven authors employed by the clinic that funded it, with the NR donated by its manufacturer [3] (verified). It is cited here not as efficacy evidence but because the symptom list is concrete and comes from the setting where this is actually used.

### Product reality [lab]
69. NAD+ is chemically unstable in ways that matter for a product. It hydrolyses in solution, is sensitive to pH and heat, and the oxidised and reduced forms are different molecules with different stability, which is why the plus sign in the name is chemistry rather than branding.
70. Because it is sold variously as a supplement ingredient, a compounded injectable and a research powder, there is no single quality framework behind a given vial. A compounded sterile injection from a licensed pharmacy carries beyond-use dating, sterility and endotoxin requirements; a research powder carries none of those, and the molecule's instability means that content at manufacture and content at use are different questions (inferred).
71. One practical consequence of the infusion data: because clearance saturates, the rate of administration changes exposure more than the nominal amount does [1]. Two vials of the same size given at different rates are not the same exposure, which makes nominal figures a poor description of what someone received (inferred).

### Where the law stands, and open questions [mixed]
72. This is a muddle rather than a position. NAD+ itself is sold as a supplement ingredient and as a compounded injectable in the US, and is not an approved drug for any anti-ageing or energy indication (verified). It has not been through the peptide compounding review process that covers the injectable peptides elsewhere in this library, because it is not a peptide.
73. The precursor side shows how fast the ground can move. In 2022 the FDA concluded that beta-nicotinamide mononucleotide cannot be marketed as a dietary supplement, on the basis that it had already been authorised for investigation as a new drug with substantial public clinical investigation, reversing its own earlier acceptance of another filing; enforcement has not followed, and the agency does not disclose the relevant authorisation date [4] (verified). Nicotinamide riboside was not affected by that decision [4].
74. In sport, NAD+ is not named on the 2026 Prohibited List. The constraint that does apply is the method prohibition on intravenous infusions totalling more than 100 mL per 12 hours outside hospital treatment, which a 500 mL drip plainly exceeds [5] (verified). Note also that the related mitochondrial peptide MOTS-c is named on the list as an AMPK activator, so metabolic agents in this neighbourhood are not uniformly outside the rules [5].
75. Does intracellular NAD+ in any human tissue rise after infusion? The one kinetic study measured plasma and urine and inferred tissue uptake without measuring it [1].
76. Why is the biomarker-to-outcome link so consistently empty? Every well-conducted precursor trial raised NAD+ and changed nothing measurable, which is either a dose, duration, population or premise problem, and nobody has distinguished which.
77. Is injecting the intact coenzyme better than giving a precursor the body has transporters for? No study has compared them on any outcome other than infusion tolerability, where the precursor was clearly easier [3].
78. What is the cost of flooding a saturable clearance system repeatedly? Methylnicotinamide rose 350% in six hours [1], which consumes methyl groups, and nothing has examined what weekly or more frequent loading does to methyl balance (speculated).
