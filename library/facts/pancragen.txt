# Pancragen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/pancragen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, KEDW, Lys-Glu-Asp-Trp, H-Lys-Glu-Asp-Trp-NH2
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: KEDW (4 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Pancragen is a lab-made tetrapeptide Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the pancreas. It is used mainly by the longevity community for pancreatic function and blood sugar, and it is claimed to steer cells toward an insulin-making type. The evidence is Russian animal work from one research tradition, and its effect on blood sugar in people is undocumented.
6. Punch line: Khavinson pancreas bioregulator, claimed to steer cells toward making insulin. Used for blood sugar; Russian data.
7. Used for: Pancreas and blood-sugar support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Organ function: Pancreas support
9. Tissue repair: Pancreas tissue claims
10. Glucose & insulin: Sugar handling claims

## How it works
11. Claimed to tune pancreas tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Organ function
   Pancragen is claimed to tune gene activity in pancreatic tissue, acting on DNA rather than a surface receptor. The aim is to keep the aging pancreas working as it should. This is the Khavinson group's claim, from animal work only.
12. Claimed to steer cells toward insulin-making type · Pdx1, Ngn3 expression · role Glucose & insulin
   The group claims it raises Pdx1 and Ngn3, transcription factors that steer pancreatic cells toward becoming insulin-making beta cells. More beta cells would mean better blood sugar control. This is from Russian animal studies, and its effect on blood sugar in people is undocumented.
13. Mechanism overview: Pancragen is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide aimed at the pancreas. The group claims it tunes pancreatic gene expression and raises Pdx1 and Ngn3, factors that steer cells toward the insulin-producing type. These claims come from one group and lack independent confirmation.

## What has been tested
29. Organ function: Pancreatic function (animal)

32. What it helps: Russian animal studies report support for pancreatic function and carbohydrate metabolism. The work comes from one research tradition with no Western validation. First-hand user reports are very few, so its effect on blood sugar in people is undocumented. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
33. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
34. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
35. Insulin or sulfonylurea users: Claimed to act on pancreatic cells; additive lows untested. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)

37. Thymalin: Thymalin rebalances immune cells, while Pancragen is aimed at pancreatic function. People add both to bioregulator courses to cover immune and metabolic aging at once. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
38. A tetrapeptide Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the pancreas.
39. First studied for: Pancreatic function and glucose-metabolism support.
40. Name story: Khavinson's organ-naming at work: Pancragen = pancreas. The tetrapeptide Lys-Glu-Asp-Trp was studied by his St. Petersburg group on aging pancreatic cells. [S5] (inferred)
41. Late 20th c.: Tetrapeptide Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the pancreas
42. Today: Sold as a bioregulator; not an approved drug anywhere
