# Bacteriostatic Water
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/bacteriostatic-water
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: BAC
2. Origin: Sterile mixing water, not a peptide
3. Class: water
4. Legal and approval status: Sold as a mixing liquid for freeze-dried peptides. Not taken on its own.

## Summary
5. Punch line: Sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol. The preservative slows bacteria, so one vial takes many draws over weeks.

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
6. Mixing water: Sterile water with 0.9% benzyl alcohol added as a preservative. The benzyl alcohol slows bacterial growth, so the vial can be punctured many times over several weeks.

## How it works

## Water
7. Made of: Water for injection, about 99.1%
8. Made of: Benzyl alcohol, 0.9% (9 mg per mL)
9. pH: About 5.7 (allowed range 4.5 to 7.0), per the US prescribing label for bacteriostatic water for injection.
10. When used: The default liquid for most lyophilized (freeze-dried) peptides that dissolve readily in water near neutral pH, such as BPC-157, TB-500, ipamorelin, CJC-1295 and the GLP-1 class.
11. When used: Used when one vial will be drawn from over days or weeks, because the preservative protects against contamination from repeated needle entries.
12. When used: Available in 3 mL (small vials, few draws) and 10 mL (larger or multiple vials).
13. not_for: Newborns and premature infants. The label reads 'Not for use in neonates' because benzyl alcohol has caused fatal toxicity in this group.
14. not_for: Anyone with a known allergy or hypersensitivity to benzyl alcohol, which is listed as a contraindication on regulatory product information.
15. not_for: Peptides that do not dissolve at its near neutral pH and need an acidic liquid (for example AOD-9604 or HGH fragment 176-191). Use acetic acid water for these.
16. not_for: Large volume use, IV fluid replacement, or spinal and epidural use. The label excludes these.
17. not_for: Pregnancy and breastfeeding call for caution: benzyl alcohol crosses the placenta, and product information advises counting total daily intake from all sources.
18. storage: Unopened: room temperature, 20 to 25°C, until the printed expiry date.
19. storage: After first puncture: the USP rule for multi-dose vials is to date the vial and discard it 28 days after first entry unless the manufacturer states otherwise.
20. storage: Once a peptide has been dissolved in it, the mixed vial follows the peptide's own storage rules, usually refrigerated at 2 to 8°C.
21. storage: Do not freeze the plain water. Keep away from heat and direct sunlight.
22. how: Check the expiry date, seal and clarity before first use. Write the date of first puncture on the vial.
23. how: Wipe the rubber stopper with an alcohol swab and let it dry before every entry.
24. how: Use a new sterile needle and syringe every time you draw from the vial.
25. how: Add the water slowly down the inside wall of the peptide vial instead of spraying it directly onto the powder.
26. how: Swirl gently or let the vial sit until the powder dissolves. Do not shake hard.
27. watch: Cloudiness, haze or color change in the plain water: discard.
28. watch: Floating particles, fibers or residue: discard.
29. watch: Cracked vial, missing or broken seal, or a stopper that has been punctured many times and leaks.
30. watch: A faint sweet, almond-like smell is normal (benzyl alcohol). A sour or unusual smell is not.
31. watch: Rash, itching or swelling after use may indicate benzyl alcohol sensitivity.
32. facts: In 1982 the US CDC and FDA reported 16 deaths in premature infants linked to benzyl alcohol in flush solutions and diluents, a condition later named 'gasping syndrome'. Since then the label carries a neonatal warning.
33. facts: Benzyl alcohol is a mild local anesthetic. A 1994 double-blind trial found lidocaine containing benzyl alcohol was 27% less painful to inject than plain lidocaine.
34. facts: Bacteriostatic means it stops bacteria from multiplying, not that it kills them. It is not a substitute for clean technique.

## Synergies (paired with)
42. BPC-157: BPC-157 comes as a dry powder and is usually dissolved in bacteriostatic water, whose preservative holds back bacteria so one vial serves many draws. The water plays no part in the peptide's repair actions. This is a handling pairing, not a therapeutic one.
43. Thymosin Beta-4: Thymosin Beta-4 is shipped freeze dried and mixed with bacteriostatic water before use. The preservative keeps a multi draw vial cleaner over the days it is in the fridge. The water adds nothing to Tβ4's repair effects.
44. Ipamorelin: Ipamorelin vials are reconstituted with bacteriostatic water so the same vial can be drawn from repeatedly. The preservative slows bacterial growth between draws. It has no effect on the GH pulse; the pairing is about handling.
45. CJC-1295 no DAC: CJC-1295 no DAC is short acting and often drawn several times from one vial, which is why bacteriostatic water is the usual mixing choice. The preservative limits contamination between draws and has no role in GHRH signalling.
46. Semaglutide: Powdered semaglutide is dissolved in bacteriostatic water so one vial lasts through weeks of doses. The preservative holds back bacteria across repeated draws. It does not change how semaglutide acts on GLP-1 receptors.
