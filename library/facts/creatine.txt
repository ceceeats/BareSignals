# Creatine
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/creatine
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: creatine monohydrate, N-methylguanidinoacetic acid
2. Origin: Not a peptide
3. Legal and approval status: Dietary supplement; safe and legal.

## Summary
4. Creatine is an amino-acid-derived compound stored in muscle as a rapid energy buffer; it is not a peptide. People take it orally for strength, power and muscle, because it refills cell energy during short bursts of effort. It is among the best-evidenced supplements here, with smaller newer benefits for the brain, though some people do not respond and early water weight is common.
5. Punch line: The best-proven supplement here. Refills muscle energy for short bursts; more strength, power, some water weight.
6. Used for: Strength, power, brain energy

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Cognition: Some help under sleep loss
8. Cellular energy: Quick energy buffer in muscle
9. Muscle & performance: More strength and power with training

## How it works
10. Refills cell energy in seconds · Phosphocreatine, ATP · role Cellular energy
   Creatine enters muscle and brain through a dedicated transporter and is loaded with phosphate to form phosphocreatine. During hard effort, phosphocreatine hands that phosphate back to ADP and rebuilds ATP within seconds, faster than any other pathway. Supplementing enlarges this reserve, shown repeatedly in human trials.
11. Backs short bursts of power · Creatine kinase · role Muscle & performance
   Creatine kinase is the enzyme that shuttles phosphate between phosphocreatine and ATP. A bigger phosphocreatine pool lets muscle sustain sprints and heavy sets a little longer before fatigue. More training volume then drives more strength and lean mass, one of the most consistent findings in human trials.
12. Draws water into muscle cells · Cell volume · role Muscle & performance
   Creatine pulls water into muscle cells as it is stored, raising cell volume. This explains the early weight gain and fuller look users report in the first weeks. The rise is water inside the muscle, not fat, and it is a known, harmless effect in healthy people.
13. Buffers brain energy under stress · Brain phosphocreatine · role Cognition
   The brain also stores phosphocreatine to buffer its energy supply. Under stress such as sleep loss or mental fatigue, a larger reserve may help keep ATP steady. Human trials show smaller benefits for memory and mental fatigue than for muscle, and brain protection work is still early.
14. Mechanism overview: Creatine is taken into muscle and brain by a dedicated transporter, then creatine kinase loads it with phosphate to make phosphocreatine. During hard, short efforts, phosphocreatine hands that phosphate back to ADP, regenerating ATP within seconds, faster than any other pathway. Supplementing enlarges the phosphocreatine pool, so more repetitions and sprints happen before fatigue. More training volume then drives more muscle growth. Creatine also pulls water into muscle cells, and in the brain it buffers energy supply under stress.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
15. Typical: 3–5 g · once daily · cycle ongoing · route Oral (verified)
16. Half-life: ~3 hours
17. Maintenance: 3–5 g · 1× daily · ongoing · Oral · for almost everyone [S1] (verified)
18. Brain baseline / load: 10 g daily; or 20–25 g (0.3 g/kg) load · daily; load split 4–5× · ongoing; load 5–7 days · Oral · for cognition and fast saturation [S2] [S3] (reported)
19. Acute high (stress): 20 g, up to 0.35 g/kg (~25 g) · single day · sleep loss or high-stress days · Oral · for sleep-deprived; tested in 15 adults [S4] [S2] (verified)

## What has been tested
32. Muscle & performance: Strength and power in training (trial)
33. Muscle & performance: Lean mass gain (trial)
34. Cognition: Memory and mental fatigue (trial)

36. One of the most studied supplements: reliably increases strength, power and muscle in training, with growing evidence for cognition and neuroprotection.
37. What it helps: Among the best-evidenced compounds here. Trials show repeatable gains in strength, power and lean mass alongside resistance training. Newer trials show smaller benefits for memory and mental fatigue, and early human work on brain protection. Users take oral monohydrate and report strength gains and fuller muscles. Some people are non-responders, and water weight rises early.
38. Trial: active · Not applicable · endurance performance with creatine and carbohydrate · NCT07712653 · California Polytechnic State University. One of dozens of open creatine trials, which range from dialysis patients to post concussion symptoms. (checked 2026-10-23) [S5] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
39. People monitoring kidney function by creatinine: Raises blood creatinine, making kidneys look worse on labs. (reported)
40. People with kidney disease or on nephrotoxic drugs: Case reports of kidney injury, mostly with pre-existing disease. (reported)
41. People with bipolar disorder: Case reports of mania during creatine use. (reported)

## Where it comes from
42. An amino-acid-derived compound stored in muscle as a rapid energy buffer, identified in 1832. Not a peptide.
43. First studied for: Athletic performance and muscle; later studied for the brain.
44. Name story: From Greek kreas, meat. French chemist Michel-Eugene Chevreul boiled down meat broth in 1832, found it, and named it after flesh. [S6] (verified)
45. 1832: Identified in muscle; an amino-acid-derived energy buffer
46. 1990s: Supplement boom in sport
47. 2000s to 2020s: Brain and neuroprotection studies grow
48. Today: Among the best-evidenced supplements; safe and legal
