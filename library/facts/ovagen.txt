# Ovagen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ovagen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, EDL, Glu-Asp-Leu
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: EDL (3 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Ovagen is a lab-made Khavinson bioregulator peptide aimed at the liver and digestive tract. It is used for liver and tissue aging support, and it is claimed to tune liver genes and shield liver cells from toxins. Evidence is Russian animal work on regeneration and aging, with no independent trial, and first-hand reports are very few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson liver and gut bioregulator, claimed to shield liver cells from toxins. Evidence mostly Russian.
7. Used for: Liver support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Organ function: Liver and gut support
9. Aging & senescence: Studied in aging
10. Cellular protection: Cell protection claims

## How it works
11. Claimed to tune liver tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Organ function
   Ovagen is claimed to enter liver cells and tune gene activity in liver tissue directly, without a surface receptor. The aim is to support liver regeneration and slow liver aging. This comes from one Russian research group and is not independently confirmed.
12. Claimed to shield liver cells from toxins · Hepatocyte protection · role Cellular protection
   The group claims it shields hepatocytes, the main working cells of the liver, from toxic damage. Protected hepatocytes keep up their work of detoxifying and making proteins. This is reported only in Russian animal work.
13. Mechanism overview: Ovagen is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide. The group claims it tunes gene expression in liver tissue and shields hepatocytes from toxic damage. These mechanisms are reported by one research group and have not been confirmed independently. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## What has been tested
28. Organ function: Liver regeneration and aging (animal)
29. Russian studies only.
30. What it helps: Evidence is Russian animal work on liver regeneration and aging, with reports of organ support. There is no Western validation and no independent trial. First-hand user reports are very few, so there is little to go on beyond the original group's claims.
31. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
32. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
33. People treating a diagnosed liver disease with it: No controlled trials show benefit; delays proven care. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
34. Epitalon: Epitalon is used as the base longevity bioregulator, with claims on telomerase and melatonin, while Ovagen covers the liver. They are combined in courses that add organ peptides on top of a general one. Neither the pairing nor the single claims have independent trial data.
35. Thymalin: Thymalin is aimed at immune balance and Ovagen at liver protection, two systems that both decline with age. Users rotate them in bioregulator courses to cover more than one organ. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
