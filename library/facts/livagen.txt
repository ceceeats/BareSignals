# Livagen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/livagen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, KEDA, Lys-Glu-Asp-Ala
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: KEDA (4 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Livagen is a lab-made peptide Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the liver, related to Vilon. It is used for liver function and tissue support, and it is claimed to loosen chromatin and wake silenced genes in old cells. Evidence is Russian animal work and cell studies with no independent trials, and user reports are very few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson liver bioregulator, Vilon's relative, claimed to loosen packed DNA and wake silenced genes in old cells.
7. Used for: Liver support, longevity stacks

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Organ function: Liver support
9. Tissue repair: Liver tissue claims
10. DNA & telomeres: Claimed to loosen packed DNA

## How it works
11. Claimed to loosen chromatin, shift genes · Khavinson group · role DNA & telomeres
   Livagen is claimed to loosen chromatin, the packed DNA and protein inside the nucleus, so more genes can be read. That would shift gene activity in the cells it reaches. This is from the Khavinson group's cell studies and is not independently replicated.
12. Claimed to wake silenced genes in old cells · Heterochromatin opening · role DNA & telomeres
   With age, stretches of DNA pack tightly into heterochromatin and the genes there go silent. Livagen is claimed to open these regions and switch those genes back on in old cells. This rests on cell work from one group.
13. Claimed to support liver and gut tissue · Tissue gene expression · role Organ function
   The group also claims it shifts gene expression in liver and gut tissue, supporting their function. Russian animal work reports liver support. There are no independent trials and very few user reports.
14. Mechanism overview: Livagen is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide. The group claims it decondenses heterochromatin, reopening genes silenced in aging cells, and shifts gene expression in liver and gut tissue. These chromatin effects come from one research group and are not independently replicated.

## What has been tested

32. Russian studies, including chromatin/gene-expression claims; no independent trials.
33. What it helps: Evidence is Russian animal work on liver function and cell studies on chromatin and gene expression, with reports of liver support. No independent trials exist. First-hand user reports are very few, so practical effects in people remain unclear. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
34. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
35. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
36. People treating a diagnosed liver disease with it: No controlled trials show benefit; delays proven care. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
37. Epitalon: Both peptides are claimed to reopen silenced genes in aged cells, with Epitalon adding telomerase and melatonin claims and Livagen focusing on liver and gut. Users stack them for gene-level anti-aging from overlapping angles. All of this is one group's work, and the pair has not been tested together.
38. Thymalin: Thymalin targets the immune system, while Livagen is claimed to act on chromatin and liver tissue. They are paired in longevity courses that combine immune support with gene-level claims. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
39. A peptide Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the liver (a Vilon relative), studied for gene-regulating effects.
40. First studied for: Liver function and tissue support.
41. Name story: Named in Khavinson house style for its target organ, the liver, with the "-gen" suffix shared by Cardiogen, Vesugen and Bronchogen. KEDA is simply its one-letter code: Lys-Glu-Asp-Ala. [S5] (inferred)
42. Late 20th c.: Khavinson bioregulator for the liver; a Vilon relative
43. Today: Sold as a bioregulator; not an approved drug anywhere
