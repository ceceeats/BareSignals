# Vesilute
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/vesilute
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, ED, Glu-Asp
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: ED (2 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Vesilute is a lab-made dipeptide Khavinson bioregulator aimed at the bladder and urinary tract. It is used for bladder and urinary tissue support, and it is claimed to tune bladder genes and support the urothelium and detrusor muscle. Evidence is Russian animal work with no Western validation, and user reports are very few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson's two-amino-acid bladder bioregulator. Used for urinary tissue support; claimed to tune bladder genes.
7. Used for: Bladder and urinary support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Organ function: Bladder and urinary support

## How it works
9. Claimed to tune bladder tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Organ function
   The Khavinson group proposes that this short peptide enters bladder cells and adjusts which genes are switched on, rather than acting on a surface receptor. The idea is that it nudges the tissue back toward a younger expression pattern. This rests on Russian animal work from that one group, with no outside confirmation.
10. Claimed to support bladder wall tissue · Urothelium, detrusor · role Organ function
   The second claim is that it supports the urothelium, the inner lining that seals urine off from the bladder wall, and the detrusor, the muscle that contracts to empty it. Russian animal studies report tissue support here, but its effect on bladder function in people is undocumented.
11. Mechanism overview: Vesilute is a short Khavinson bioregulator peptide aimed at the bladder. The group claims it tunes gene expression in bladder tissue and supports the urothelium and detrusor muscle. These mechanisms are claimed by one research group and not independently confirmed.

## What has been tested

25. What it helps: Evidence is Russian animal work and reports of bladder and urinary tissue support. There is no Western validation.
