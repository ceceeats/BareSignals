# Crystagen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/crystagen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, EDP, Glu-Asp-Pro, H-Glu-Asp-Pro-OH
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: EDP (3 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Crystagen is a short synthetic Khavinson immune bioregulator peptide (Glu-Asp-Pro). It is used mainly by the longevity community for immune balance in aging and chronic illness, and it is claimed to tune immune cell genes. Russian human studies report immune normalisation but have not been independently replicated, and user reports are very few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson's immune tripeptide. Longevity users take it to rebalance immunity in aging and chronic illness.
7. Used for: Immune support

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Immunity: Immune regulation claims

## How it works
9. Claimed to normalize immune function · Khavinson group · role Immunity
   The Khavinson group claims this tripeptide brings overactive or weakened immune function back toward normal, rather than simply boosting it. Russian human studies report this normalisation in aging and chronic illness. They have not been replicated outside the group.
10. Claimed to tune immune cell genes · Glu-Asp-Pro tripeptide · role Immunity
   Glu-Asp-Pro is said to act directly on DNA in immune cells, shifting which genes are expressed, rather than binding a surface receptor. That is the proposed route to its normalising effect. The mechanism comes from one research group only.
11. Mechanism overview: Crystagen is the Khavinson tripeptide Glu-Asp-Pro. The group claims it tunes gene expression in immune cells and normalises immune function. These mechanisms are reported by one research group and not replicated elsewhere. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## What has been tested
26. Immunity: Immune regulation in aging (human)
27. Russian studies only; no independent replication.
28. What it helps: Russian human studies report immune normalisation in aging and chronic illness. They have not been independently replicated and there is no Western validation. First-hand user reports are very few, so real-world effects are hard to assess. Its use outside Russia is driven mainly by the longevity community.
29. Trial: none. Khavinson bioregulator studied only in unregistered Russian clinical reports; no registry trial found in ClinicalTrials.gov, EU CTR, ISRCTN or ChiCTR, Russian registry unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S3] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
30. People with active or recent cancer: Marketed as gene-regulating; no human tumour safety data. (inferred)
31. People treating a diagnosed immune disease with it: No controlled trials show benefit; delays proven care. (inferred)
32. Transplant recipients on anti-rejection drugs: Sold as an immune stimulant, which works against anti-rejection drugs. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
33. Epitalon: Epitalon targets telomerase and the pineal gland, while Crystagen is aimed at immune cells, so people combine them in longevity courses to cover aging from two angles. Both rest on Khavinson group data, and the pair has not been tested together.
34. Thymalin: Thymalin has Russian human data on raising and rebalancing T and B cells, and Crystagen is claimed to normalise immune cell genes. People pair them for immune support in aging. The combination is user logic, not trial data.
