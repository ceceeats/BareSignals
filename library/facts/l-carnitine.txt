# L-Carnitine
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/l-carnitine
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: levocarnitine, Carnitor, LC120, LC216, LC526, Lipo-C, Lipo-B, vitamin BT, Carnitene, (R)-3-hydroxy-4-(trimethylazaniumyl)butanoate
2. Origin: Not a peptide: amino-acid-derived small molecule
3. Shown with an FDA approved badge
4. Legal and approval status: FDA-approved as levocarnitine injection (Carnitor and generics) for deficiency only; 'LC' blends are unapproved research mixes. WADA: not prohibited, but IV infusions over 100 mL per 12 h are (2026 M2.2).

## Summary
5. L-Carnitine is not a peptide but a small molecule the body makes from lysine and methionine to carry fatty acids into mitochondria. Injectable levocarnitine is approved only for deficiency, yet people inject it, alone or in lipotropic blends, for fat loss and training energy. Fat-loss results are mixed, muscle uptake needs insulin, and the injection stings.
6. Punch line: The fat shuttle. Carries fatty acids into mitochondria; injected alone or in lipo blends, mixed results, stings.
7. Used for: Fat loss, training energy, carnitine deficiency

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Fat metabolism: Ferries fat into mitochondria
9. Cellular energy: Fuels mitochondrial fat burning
10. Muscle & performance: Spares glycogen in training
11. Organ function: Replaces losses in dialysis
12. Reproductive (backed only by animal or user reports): Sperm motility in oral trials
13. Glucose & insulin (backed only by animal or user reports): Insulin sensitivity claims

## How it works
14. Shuttles fatty acids into mitochondria · CPT1/CPT2 carnitine shuttle · role Fat metabolism
   CPT1 attaches long-chain fatty acids to carnitine so they can cross the inner mitochondrial membrane, and CPT2 releases them inside for beta-oxidation. Without carnitine, long-chain fat cannot reach the place it is burned. This is textbook biochemistry, and replacement is approved for carnitine deficiency.
15. Clears excess acyl groups from cells · Acylcarnitine export · role Cellular energy
   Carnitine takes up excess acyl groups, such as acetyl groups, and exports them from the cell as acylcarnitines. That frees coenzyme A for other energy reactions. In inborn errors and dialysis, this clears build-up that can harm cells.
16. Spares muscle glycogen at easy pace · PDC flux, glycogen sparing · role Muscle & performance
   During exercise, carnitine buffers surplus acetyl groups, easing the pyruvate dehydrogenase bottleneck that limits carbohydrate use. Muscle then uses its glycogen stores more efficiently at easy pace. A 24-week human study with carbohydrate raised muscle carnitine and improved cycling output.
17. Muscle uptake needs insulin · OCTN2 transporter, insulin-stimulated · role Glucose & insulin
   Muscle takes carnitine in through the OCTN2 transporter, and insulin stimulates that uptake. This is why the human study that raised muscle carnitine paired it with carbohydrate. Without an insulin rise, little extra reaches muscle.
18. Mechanism overview: L-carnitine (levocarnitine) is a quaternary ammonium compound made from lysine and methionine. Carnitine palmitoyltransferase 1 (CPT1) attaches long-chain fatty acids to it so they can cross the inner mitochondrial membrane, where CPT2 releases them for beta-oxidation. It also buffers excess acetyl groups as acetylcarnitine, freeing coenzyme A and easing the pyruvate dehydrogenase bottleneck during exercise. Muscle takes it up through the OCTN2 transporter, which is stimulated by insulin.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
19. Typical: 50–200 mg/day (community); 10–20 mg/kg (label, dialysis) · daily or pre-workout; blends 1 mL 2–3×/week · cycle 8–16 weeks or ongoing · route Sub-Q or IM; IV clinically (reported)
20. Half-life: ~17 h (IV, terminal)
21. Most people take: Community SC/IM 50–200 mg daily or 100–200 mg pre-workout. Lipotropic blends (LC120, LC216, LC526) 1 mL 2–3×/week. Clinical IV 10–20 mg/kg after dialysis or 50 mg/kg in metabolic crisis. Oral 1–2 g/day with carbs is the studied athlete route.

## What people report
22. Injected SC or IM, often in lipotropic 'LC' blends or alongside GLP-1s, for fat loss and gym energy. People report injection sting, more energy in training, and a fishy smell at high doses; fat-loss reports are mixed.
23. Liked / worked: ~1.3 kg extra weight loss (oral meta-analysis)
24. Liked / worked: Muscle carnitine up 21% with carbs
25. Liked / worked: Treats deficiency in dialysis
26. What worked: Oral carnitine trials averaged about 1.3 kg extra weight loss. Six months of oral carnitine with carbs raised muscle carnitine ~21% and improved a 30-minute cycling test.
27. Went wrong / did not work: Small, inconsistent fat-loss effect
28. Went wrong / did not work: Muscle won't load without insulin
29. Went wrong / did not work: Seizures, nausea, odour (label)
30. Went wrong / did not work: Rare anaphylaxis
31. What went wrong: Muscle uptake needs insulin, so a shot alone may not load muscle. Label lists seizures, nausea, body odour and rare anaphylaxis.
32. Most reported: Injection sting, especially IM
33. Most reported: More energy in workouts
34. Most reported: Fishy body odour at high doses
35. Most reported: Mixed fat-loss results

## What has been tested
36. Fat metabolism: Weight loss, oral RCT meta-analysis (human)
37. Muscle & performance: Muscle fuel and cycling output, oral (human)
38. Organ function: Carnitine deficiency in dialysis (human)
39. Reproductive: Sperm motility, oral (human)
40. Approved for deficiency states; oral weight-loss RCTs pooled in meta-analyses; muscle-fuel studies in athletes.
41. What it helps: Its approved use is replacing carnitine in inborn errors and dialysis. Oral RCTs pooled in 2016 showed about 1.3 kg more weight loss than control. A 24-week study with carbohydrate raised muscle carnitine and improved cycling output. People inject it, alone or in lipotropic blends, for fat loss and training energy; fat-loss results are mixed and the injection stings.
42. Trial: active · Phase 4 · critically ill liver patients · NCT07642076 · Beni-Suef University. Approved for carnitine deficiency; still being tested as an add on in ICU liver patients in Egypt. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1] (verified)

## Who shouldn't take it
43. People on levothyroxine or with hypothyroidism: Carnitine blocks thyroid hormone entry into cells. (reported)
44. People with seizure disorders: The Carnitor label reports seizures in people with and without prior seizures. (verified)
45. People with heart disease or high TMAO: Gut bacteria turn carnitine into TMAO, linked to atherosclerosis. (reported)
46. People on warfarin: Case reports show carnitine raising INR and bleeding risk on warfarin. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
47. Semaglutide: Semaglutide cuts food intake, and L-carnitine supports moving fat into mitochondria to be burned. People pair them for fat loss and energy while eating less, but the pair has not been tested together.
48. Tirzepatide: Tirzepatide reduces intake and improves how fat tissue handles fat, and L-carnitine supports fat burning in muscle. The pairing is user logic for fat loss, not trial data.
49. 5-Amino-1MQ: 5-Amino-1MQ blocks the NNMT enzyme in fat cells, which shrank fat cells in obese mice, and L-carnitine shuttles fat into mitochondria. They are often stacked for fat loss, but the pair has not been tested together.
50. MOTS-c: MOTS-c mimics exercise through AMPK and improves fuel use in mice, and L-carnitine provides the shuttle that moves fat to be burned. Users pair them for training energy and fat loss, but the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
51. NAD+: NAD+ carries electrons for energy production, while L-carnitine delivers fatty acids into mitochondria to be burned. People pair them for energy and recovery as two steps of the same process, but the pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
52. A small molecule the body makes from lysine and methionine that ferries fatty acids into mitochondria to be burned. Injectable versions are sold alone or as 'LC' lipotropic blends with methionine, choline and B vitamins.
53. First studied for: Carnitine deficiency from inborn errors and in dialysis patients (Carnitor).
54. Name story: From Latin caro, carnis: flesh. It was first pulled from meat extract in 1905. Decades later it was briefly dubbed "vitamin BT" after mealworms (Tenebrio) turned out to need it in their diet. [S2] (verified)
55. 1950s: Carnitine's role in fatty-acid transport worked out (inferred date)
56. 1985–1992: Carnitor approved, oral then IV (dates inferred)
57. 2011: Muscle loading shown to need carbs and insulin
58. 2016: Meta-analysis: ~1.3 kg extra weight loss
59. Today: Sold as LC120, LC216, LC526 lipotropic blends
