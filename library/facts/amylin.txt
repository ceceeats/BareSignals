# Amylin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/amylin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: iapp reference, IAPP, islet amyloid polypeptide, diabetes-associated peptide (DAP), insulinoma amyloid peptide
2. Origin: Made by the body (reference)
3. Sequence: KCNTATCATQRLANFLVHSSNNFGAILSSTNVGSNTY (37 amino acids). Ring closed between the two C; amide end
4. Legal and approval status: Reference molecule (pramlintide is the approved analogue).

## Summary
5. Amylin is a natural hormone released with insulin that slows the stomach and curbs how much is eaten. It is the template behind cagrilintide and the approved add-on pramlintide, which carry its fullness pathway. The native hormone is too unstable to use as a drug, so this is a reference entry with no user reports.
6. Punch line: The pancreas's second fullness hormone, released with insulin; slows the stomach. Template for cagrilintide.
7. Used for: Pramlintide analog: mealtime glucose

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Appetite & satiety: Fullness signal from the pancreas
9. Glucose & insulin: Slows sugar after meals
10. Digestive & gut: Slows the stomach

## How it works
11. Released with insulin after a meal · Beta cells, co-secreted · role Glucose & insulin
   Amylin is made in pancreatic beta cells and released together with insulin after a meal. The two work as a pair: insulin moves glucose into tissue, while amylin slows how fast new glucose arrives. Human amylin clumps into amyloid fibrils, so drugs use stable analogues.
12. Slows the stomach, curbs intake · Amylin receptor, hindbrain · role Appetite & satiety
   Amylin receptors, built from the calcitonin receptor plus a RAMP partner protein, sit mostly in the hindbrain. Activating them slows stomach emptying and reduces how much is eaten. Analogues carry the human evidence: cagrilintide produces weight loss in obesity trials.
13. Holds back glucagon after meals · Amylin receptor · role Glucose & insulin
   Through amylin receptor signalling, it suppresses the glucagon surge that normally follows a meal. The liver releases less sugar while food is absorbed, so the post-meal glucose rise is gentler. Pramlintide, an analogue, is approved as a mealtime add-on in diabetes on this basis.
14. Signals fullness to the brainstem · Area postrema · role Digestive & gut
   The area postrema, a brainstem region outside the blood-brain barrier, is amylin's main site of action. Signalling there registers fullness, so meals end sooner and are smaller. This is the central mechanism behind the amylin analogue drugs.
15. Mechanism overview: Amylin is a 37 amino acid hormone co-secreted with insulin from pancreatic beta cells after meals. It binds amylin receptors, built from the calcitonin receptor plus a RAMP partner, mostly in the area postrema of the brainstem. There it signals fullness, slows gastric emptying and suppresses post-meal glucagon. The result is smaller meals and a gentler post-meal glucose rise. Human amylin clumps into amyloid fibrils, so it cannot be used directly; pramlintide and cagrilintide are stable analogues.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
16. Typical: 15–120 mcg · before major meals · cycle ongoing · route Sub-Q (verified)
17. Half-life: ~48 min
18. Type 1 (pramlintide): 15–60 mcg · before each major meal · ongoing; +15 mcg steps · Sub-Q · for type 1 diabetes with mealtime insulin cut 50% [S1] (verified)

20. Obesity trial: 120–360 mcg · 2–3× daily before meals · 16 wk · Sub-Q · for non-diabetic obesity (trial only) [S2] [S3] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
33. Insulin users with hypoglycaemia unawareness: Pramlintide has a boxed warning for severe insulin-induced lows. (verified)
34. People with gastroparesis or severe slow digestion: Pramlintide is contraindicated in gastroparesis. (verified)
35. People on drugs that slow the gut (anticholinergics): Label advises against combining with drugs that slow GI motility. (verified)

## Where it comes from
36. Found in 1987 in the amyloid clumps of a diabetic pancreas, released with insulin to slow the stomach and curb intake.
37. First studied for: The natural hormone behind cagrilintide and pramlintide.
38. Name story: First published in 1987 as 'diabetes-associated peptide', pulled from the amyloid plaques clogging pancreases of type 2 diabetics. It was renamed amylin, from amyloid, and is also called IAPP: islet amyloid polypeptide. [S5] (verified)
39. 1987: Found in the amyloid clumps of a diabetic pancreas
