# Bromantane
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/bromantane
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: ladasten, bromantan, bromontan, ADK-709, N-(4-bromophenyl)adamantan-2-amine
2. Origin: Not a peptide
3. Legal and approval status: Approved/used in Russia; research chemical elsewhere. WADA-banned (it caused a famous Olympic case).

## Summary
4. Bromantane is a Russian small molecule, not a peptide, classed as an actoprotector that is mildly stimulating and anti-anxiety, partly by boosting dopamine synthesis. It was developed in the Soviet Union for fatigue and anxiety, and users take it orally for energy, motivation and calm that builds over several days. Clinical data are almost only Russian trials, long-term safety is poorly mapped, and it is WADA-banned.
5. Punch line: Soviet oral actoprotector. Builds dopamine for drive and calm that grow over days, with no stimulant crash.
6. Used for: Drive, focus, fatigue

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Drive & mental stamina: Raises dopamine making
8. Cognition: Clearer focus reported
9. Mood: Less anxiety in Russian trials

## How it works
10. Raises the brain's dopamine output · Dopamine synthesis up · role Drive & mental stamina
   Bromantane raises dopamine output by increasing how much the brain makes, rather than forcing stored dopamine out the way amphetamine does. That gives a slower, steadier rise in dopamine tone. The mechanism work comes almost entirely from Russian groups, mostly in animals.
11. Switches on dopamine-making genes · Tyrosine hydroxylase, AADC · role Drive & mental stamina
   It raises expression of tyrosine hydroxylase and aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase (AADC), the two enzymes that build dopamine from tyrosine. More enzyme means more synthesis capacity over days, which fits its slow onset. This comes from Russian research, with little Western replication.
12. Lifts energy without a classic crash · Claimed, Russian studies · role Mood
   Because it builds dopamine supply instead of draining stores, there is no sudden drop when it wears off. Russian human trials report less fatigue and asthenia with this no-crash profile. Western trials are lacking and long-term safety is poorly mapped.
13. Eases anxiety · Mechanism unclear · role Mood
   Russian trials report lower anxiety alongside the energy lift, an unusual pairing for a stimulating compound. Why this happens has not been worked out. Treat the calming effect as reported in those trials, without a known target behind it.
14. Mechanism overview: Bromantane is an adamantane derivative developed in Russia as an actoprotector. Instead of releasing stored dopamine like amphetamine, it raises expression of tyrosine hydroxylase and aromatic L-amino acid decarboxylase, the enzymes that make dopamine. More synthesis capacity means a gradual, sustained lift in dopaminergic tone, which fits the slow onset and absence of a crash. Its anti-anxiety effect has no clear mechanism. Nearly all mechanistic work comes from Russian groups.

## What has been tested
31. Cellular energy: Fatigue and asthenia (trial)
32. Mood: Anxiety (trial)
33. Drive & mental stamina: Stimulant-without-crash profile (trial)
34. Russian human trials report reduced fatigue and anxiety with a stimulant-without-crash profile; it was a notable sports-doping case. Little Western study.
35. What it helps: Russian trials report less fatigue and asthenia, lower anxiety, and energy without the crash of classic stimulants. 36. Trial: none. Approved in Russia as Ladasten for fatigue; no registered trial found in Western registries, and Russian registry checks were not possible. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
37. Drug-tested athletes: Banned stimulant since the 1996 Olympics; on the WADA list. (verified)
38. People with bipolar disorder or psychosis history: Raises dopamine synthesis; may tip mood or perception. (inferred)

## Where it comes from
39. A Russian 'actoprotector' small molecule (ladasten) that is both mildly stimulating and anti-anxiety, acting partly by boosting dopamine synthesis. Not a peptide.
40. First studied for: Fatigue, asthenia and anxiety; developed in the Soviet Union.
41. Name story: A squashed version of its chemical name, adamantyl-bromphenyl-amine. Built in 1980s Moscow to help Soviet soldiers recover from exertion, it became famous when Russian athletes tested positive at the 1996 Olympics; it is sold in Russia as Ladasten. [S5] (verified)
42. Soviet era: Developed as an 'actoprotector' for fatigue and asthenia
43. 1996: Olympic doping cases put it on the map
44. Today: Used in Russia as ladasten; research chemical elsewhere; WADA-banned
