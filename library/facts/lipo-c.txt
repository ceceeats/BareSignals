# Lipo-C
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/lipo-c
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Lipo-C injection, lipotropic injection with carnitine, MIC + L-Carnitine, Lipo-C Fat Blaster, LC526
2. Origin: Blend: amino acids + carnitine + B vitamins (lipotropic injection)
3. Legal and approval status: US: not FDA-approved; made by 503A compounding pharmacies on prescription (levocarnitine alone is approved only for carnitine deficiency). Grey-market 'LC' vials are sold for research only. WADA: not prohibited, but IV infusions over 100 mL per 12 hours are.

## Summary
4. Lipo-C is a compounded injection of methionine, inositol and choline (the MIC trio) plus L-carnitine and B vitamins. It is used in weight-loss clinics to support fat metabolism, with carnitine carrying fat into mitochondria and choline helping the liver export fat. Only the individual parts have trial data, mainly modest weight loss from oral carnitine. Lipo-C itself has no trial, and any fat loss is usually credited to diet.
5. Punch line: The weight-loss clinic shot. MIC trio, carnitine and B vitamins push fat from the liver into mitochondria.
6. Used for: Fat loss support and training energy, usually with a diet or GLP-1

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Fat metabolism: Carnitine carries fat into mitochondria
8. Cellular energy: B vitamins and carnitine for fuel use
9. Organ function: Choline and methionine help the liver export fat
10. Muscle & performance: Used for training energy

## How it works
11. Carries fat into mitochondria to be burned · Carnitine shuttle, CPT1 · role Fat metabolism
   L-carnitine moves long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane through CPT1, the gate enzyme of the carnitine shuttle. Inside, they are burned by beta-oxidation. Oral carnitine trials in people show a small extra weight loss; injected Lipo-C has no trial.
12. Lets the liver package and export fat · Choline → phosphatidylcholine, VLDL · role Organ function
   Choline is turned into phosphatidylcholine, which the liver needs to wrap fat into VLDL particles and send it out to the blood. Choline deficiency causes fatty liver in animals and people. Extra choline helps when intake is low, not clearly when it is adequate.
13. Methyl donor for liver fat handling · Methionine → SAM · role Organ function
   Methionine is converted to SAM, the body's main methyl donor. SAM supplies methyl groups to make phosphatidylcholine by a second route and for other liver reactions. Lipotropic effects of methionine are shown mostly in rodents fed deficient diets.
14. Helps cells use fuel · Thiamine and pantothenate (B1, B5) as coenzymes · role Cellular energy
   Thiamine (B1) is a cofactor for pyruvate dehydrogenase, which feeds sugar into the energy cycle, and pantothenate (B5) builds coenzyme A, which carries fats and sugars into it. Both are essential, but extra amounts help mainly when someone is deficient.
15. Mechanism overview: Lipotropic agents promote fat export from the liver. Choline is the precursor of phosphatidylcholine, required for VLDL assembly; deficiency causes fatty liver. Methionine feeds S-adenosylmethionine, which supplies methyl groups including for phosphatidylethanolamine to phosphatidylcholine conversion. Inositol participates in phosphoinositide signalling and lipid handling. L-carnitine shuttles long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane via CPT1/CPT2 for beta-oxidation; muscle uptake depends on insulin-stimulated OCTN2 transport. Thiamine and pantothenate (dexpanthenol) are cofactors for pyruvate dehydrogenase and coenzyme A. The combination is rational on paper; no controlled trial has measured its effect on fat mass.

## What people report

46. Liked / worked: Carnitine: ~1 kg extra loss in oral trials
47. Liked / worked: Choline helps clear liver fat
48. Liked / worked: Clinics report more training energy
49. What worked: Carnitine is the best-supported part, with a modest average weight loss in oral trials. Clinics report more energy in training and support during a diet; most guides say it adds little without a calorie deficit.
50. Went wrong / did not work: No trial of the blend itself
51. Went wrong / did not work: Little effect without a calorie deficit
52. Went wrong / did not work: Nodules and sting, worse in the belly
53. What went wrong: No randomised trial of Lipo-C itself. Weight change in clinics is usually credited to diet and exercise. Reviews rate evidence as weak; carnitine uptake into muscle needs insulin, so an injection alone may not load muscle.
54. Most reported: 1 mL IM, 1 to 3× a week

## What has been tested
58. Fat metabolism: Oral carnitine, weight loss meta-analyses (human)
59. Organ function: Choline and liver fat (human)
60. Fat metabolism: Lipo-C as a whole (none)
61. The blend has never been tested as a whole. Component evidence: oral L-carnitine meta-analyses show about 1.1 to 1.3 kg extra weight loss; choline helps clear liver fat (rodents, one small human trial); methionine and inositol lipotropic effects are mostly rodent.
62. What it helps: Component evidence only. Oral carnitine averages about 1 kg extra weight loss across trials; choline supports liver fat clearance. Lipo-C itself has no trial, and guides agree any fat loss depends mostly on diet.

## Who shouldn't take it
63. People with homocystinuria or high homocysteine: Methionine is the direct precursor of homocysteine. (inferred)
64. People on levothyroxine or with hypothyroidism: Carnitine blocks thyroid hormone entry into cells. (reported)
65. People with Leber's hereditary optic neuropathy: Cyanocobalamin can speed optic nerve damage. (verified)
66. People with seizure disorders: The Carnitor label reports seizures in people with and without prior seizures. (verified)

## Synergies (paired with)
67. MIC + B12: MIC + B12 overlaps heavily with Lipo-C, sharing choline and methionine, and adds inositol and B12. People rotate or combine them during diets. Neither has a weight loss trial, and the pair has not been tested together.
68. L-Carnitine: Standalone L-carnitine raises the carnitine share above what Lipo-C contains. People add it for training energy and fat burning. Oral carnitine has small weight loss effects in trials; the injected pair has not been tested.
69. Vitamin B12: Vitamin B12 adds a cofactor for methionine recycling and nerve health, which Lipo-C lacks. Clinics add it for energy during a diet. B12 clearly helps people who are deficient; the pairing has not been tested.
70. Semaglutide: Semaglutide drives weight loss by cutting appetite through the GLP-1 receptor, with large human trials. Lipo-C is added for fat handling and energy while eating less. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
71. Tirzepatide: Tirzepatide does the heavy lifting on appetite and weight through GIP and GLP-1 receptors, with large human trials. Lipo-C is added for fat metabolism and energy during the deficit. The pair has not been tested together.
72. Retatrutide: Retatrutide cuts appetite and raises energy use through three receptors, with large human trials. Users add Lipo-C as a fuel and liver support layer during weight loss. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
73. AOD-9604: AOD-9604 is a growth hormone fragment aimed at fat breakdown, with rodent data only. Users stack it with Lipo-C for fat loss. The pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
74. A compounded 'lipotropic' injection: methionine, inositol and choline (the MIC trio) plus L-carnitine and B vitamins. The 'C' is for carnitine. A typical pharmacy formula per mL is methionine 15, inositol 50, choline chloride 50, L-carnitine 50, thiamine 15 and dexpanthenol 5 mg.
75. First studied for: Grew out of US weight-loss clinics and med spas in the 2000s and 2010s as a carnitine-boosted version of MIC shots; now also sold grey-market in stronger 'LC' versions.
76. Name story: 'Lipo' from lipotropic, Greek for fat-moving; 'C' for carnitine, the part that sets it apart from plain MIC or Lipo-B (B12) shots. [S1] (inferred)
77. 1950s: Carnitine's role in fat transport worked out (inferred date)
78. 2000s: Lipotropic MIC shots spread through US weight-loss clinics (inferred)
79. 2010s: Carnitine-boosted Lipo-C formulas added (inferred)
80. 2016: Meta-analysis: oral carnitine ~1.3 kg extra weight loss
81. Today: Compounded Lipo-C and grey-market LC120, LC216, LC526

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### What "never tested as a blend" means here, in the maker's own words [mixed]
82. Most blends in this library have to be assessed from outside. This one is unusual: a licensed compounding pharmacy publishes its own product page, and that page says the quiet part out loud. The evidence for the product is described as largely extrapolated from the individual components' pharmacology rather than from randomised trials of the combination; safety and efficacy for the formulation have not been evaluated by the FDA; and there is no single approved dosing schedule [1] (verified).
83. That is the honest baseline. Three separate things have not been studied: whether the components do anything together that they do not do apart, whether the mixture is stable and compatible as formulated beyond its dating, and what the combination's adverse-event profile is as opposed to each ingredient's.
84. The deeper problem specific to this product is that the name is not a formula. Published recipes under this label run from about 120 to 526 mg per mL, with different components and different ratios. A study of one would not describe another. There is no plausible reading in which a trial of this product could exist, because there is no single product to trial (inferred).

### The components do not have comparable evidence, and the biggest one has the least [people]
85. L-carnitine is the only component with weight-loss trials, and they are oral. A 2016 meta-analysis in a peer-reviewed obesity journal pooled nine randomised trials judged methodologically adequate, totalling 911 adults, and found a mean difference of minus 1.33 kg (95% CI minus 2.09 to minus 0.57) and minus 0.47 kg/m2 of BMI (95% CI minus 0.88 to minus 0.05) [2] (verified).
86. Two features of that result are usually dropped. The effect is about one and a third kilograms, which is small, and meta-regression found it diminished significantly with longer supplementation, p=0.002 [2] (verified). So the best-evidenced ingredient has a small effect that fades, measured by mouth at doses typically far above what a millilitre of this blend delivers.
87. Choline deficiency causes fatty liver, and choline is genuinely required to assemble the particles the liver uses to export fat; methionine genuinely supplies methyl groups through S-adenosylmethionine; thiamine and pantothenate are genuinely coenzymes for fuel use. All of that is real biochemistry and none of it is evidence of fat loss in people who are not deficient. No trial of injected choline, methionine, inositol or dexpanthenol for fat loss was found in this review (verified as absent).
88. Inositol's best human evidence sits in polycystic ovary syndrome, not obesity, and it is oral. It is also the component most often absent from the formula altogether, including from the four-part pharmacy version [1] (verified).

### The mechanism argument has a hole where the injection goes [mixed]
89. Carnitine's job is to carry long-chain fatty acids across the inner mitochondrial membrane. The limiting step for that in muscle is not how much carnitine is in the blood, it is how much gets into the muscle cell, which depends on the OCTN2 transporter and is stimulated by insulin. Raising plasma carnitine does not reliably raise muscle carnitine, which is why oral loading studies have generally needed carbohydrate co-ingestion to move muscle content at all (inferred from the transport biology).
90. The same logic applies to the liver components. Choline and methionine support fat export in someone short of them. In someone whose intake is adequate, adding more does not create a shortage to correct. The rationale is a deficiency-repletion argument being applied to people who have not been shown to be deficient (inferred).
91. Nothing here contradicts the biochemistry on the card. The gap is between "this molecule is required for fat oxidation" and "adding this molecule increases fat oxidation," and no human study of this product has crossed it.

### The TMAO question, and the one argument that favours injection [mixed]
92. The two largest components by mass in most versions, carnitine and choline, are also the two best-characterised dietary precursors of trimethylamine N-oxide, a metabolite associated with cardiovascular disease. This is worth laying out properly because it is the strongest safety-adjacent argument in the file and it cuts in an unexpected direction.
93. In a 2013 Nature Medicine study, gut bacteria converted dietary carnitine to trimethylamine and then TMAO: a week of oral antibiotics made labelled TMAO nearly undetectable in five omnivores, with production returning after at least three weeks off; vegans and vegetarians had much lower fasting TMAO and a markedly reduced capacity to produce it after a carnitine challenge; mice on 1.3% carnitine in drinking water had roughly double the aortic plaque area with plasma TMAO around 114 micromolar against 17, and antibiotics blocked the effect [3] (verified, in people and animals). In a clinical cohort of 2,595 patients undergoing cardiac evaluation, high carnitine predicted events after adjustment for traditional risk factors, but the association disappeared once TMAO was accounted for [3] (verified).
94. The pivotal detail for an injection is that all those challenges were oral, and the gut microbiota are the required step. The authors themselves note that parenteral administration would bypass the microbiota, and did not test it [3] (verified as their inference, not their result).
95. So the fair reading is: the TMAO pathway is a real and well-evidenced concern for swallowed carnitine and choline, and an injected route plausibly sidesteps the main generating step. That is an argument for the route, and it is untested. The product page's listing of transient fishy body odour attributed to trimethylamine [1] suggests the pathway is not entirely bypassed in practice (inferred).

### Product reality, and the parts of the label worth reading [lab]
96. This is a ready-made aqueous solution in a multi-dose vial, not a lyophilised powder, which changes the handling questions entirely. The published four-part pharmacy formula is methionine 15, choline chloride 50, L-carnitine 50 and dexpanthenol 5 mg per mL in water for injection, supplied in 10 and 30 mL multi-dose vials, for intramuscular use, with a beyond-use date of 28 days from compounding under the relevant sterile compounding standard [1] (verified).
97. Twenty-eight days is a formulation and sterility limit on a multi-dose vial, not a suggestion. Storage is specified as either room temperature or refrigerated depending on the exact formulation, protected from light in the original carton, not frozen, not shaken vigorously, and inspected for particulates or discolouration before use [1] (verified). The B vitamins in versions that contain them are light-sensitive, which is why the carton matters.
98. The contraindication list is more specific than the product's reputation suggests, and one entry is absolute: inborn errors of sulfur amino acid metabolism such as homocystinuria. Also listed are hypersensitivity to any component and severe hepatic failure. Cautions cover advanced kidney impairment or dialysis because of carnitine accumulation, active peptic ulcer disease, levodopa use, severe heart failure or arrhythmia, and pregnancy and breastfeeding [1] (verified).
99. The interaction list is the part most likely to be missed. Carnitine may potentiate warfarin, with reports of raised INR. Levodopa-carbidopa effect may be reduced. Cholinesterase inhibitors such as donepezil warrant monitoring for cramping or slow heart rate. Cysteine or N-acetylcysteine may alter paracetamol metabolism [1] (verified).
100. Documented adverse effects are mostly local: injection-site pain, redness and warmth, with nausea and loose stools in a minority resolving within a day or two, and the transient fishy odour. Uncommon but serious entries are immediate hypersensitivity including anaphylaxis, and delayed eosinophilic myositis, with systemic hypersensitivity cited below 0.1% in one retrospective series [1] (verified as reported by the pharmacy).
101. One honest note about the source: that page cites several commercial clinic blogs alongside its pharmacology, and two of its regulatory citations use placeholder identifiers [1] (verified). It is the best single document available for this product and it is not a peer-reviewed one.

### Where the law stands, and open questions [mixed]
102. In the US this is a 503A compounded preparation, made for an individual patient on prescription, explicitly not FDA-approved and not evaluated by the agency for safety or efficacy [1] (verified). That is a different legal position from the injectable peptides elsewhere in this library: the components are established substances with their own histories, and levocarnitine itself is approved, but only for carnitine deficiency. Research-labelled vials sold under the same name sit outside the pharmacy framework entirely.
103. In sport, no component is named on the 2026 Prohibited List. The applicable rule is the method prohibition on intravenous infusions totalling more than 100 mL per 12 hours outside hospital care, which does not bite on a one-millilitre intramuscular injection [4] (verified).
104. Does the combination do anything the components do not? The maker says this has not been tested, which makes the premise of the product unexamined rather than disputed [1].
105. Does injected carnitine reach muscle? Plasma carnitine and muscle carnitine are different things, and the transporter step is the one nobody has measured for this route.
106. Does the injected route genuinely avoid the TMAO pathway? The gut step is required for the oral effect and plausibly bypassed, but the fishy-odour reports suggest trimethylamine still appears, and nobody has measured TMAO after an injection [1,3].
107. What does a repeated methionine load do to homocysteine? Methionine is the direct precursor, and no study of this product has measured it.
108. Which formula is a given vial? Published versions range from 120 to 526 mg per mL with different components, so every number in circulation, including dose conventions, is formula-specific and usually unlabelled as such.
