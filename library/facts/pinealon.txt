# Pinealon
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/pinealon
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: EDR, EDR peptide, Glu-Asp-Arg
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: EDR (3 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Pinealon is a lab-made tripeptide (Glu-Asp-Arg) from the Khavinson bioregulator tradition, aimed at the brain. It is used for cognition, sleep and longevity, and is claimed to protect neurons by regulating genes and reducing oxidative stress. The evidence is Russian cell and rat work with no independent trials, and detailed first-hand reports are few.
6. Punch line: Khavinson bioregulator tripeptide for the brain. Claimed to shield neurons; evidence is Russian cell and rat work.
7. Used for: Cognition, sleep, brain aging

## How it works
15. Claimed to protect neurons via gene control · Khavinson group · role Neural protection
   The Khavinson group claims short peptides like Pinealon (Glu-Asp-Arg) enter the cell nucleus and bind DNA, adjusting gene activity in nerve tissue. No receptor is known, and the gene-control evidence is largely computer modelling. No independent lab has confirmed it.
16. Claimed to cut oxidative stress in neurons · ROS down, claimed · role Cellular protection
   In rat neuron cultures, Pinealon lowered reactive oxygen species, the damaging free radicals produced under stress, and reduced cell death after toxic insults. These are cell findings from the originating group only.
17. Claimed to aid memory under stress, in rats · Cognition, claimed · role Cognition
   Rat studies from the same group claim better memory and learning under stress after Pinealon. The work has not been repeated elsewhere, and there are no controlled human trials of cognition.
18. Claimed to support melatonin making · Pineal, claimed · role Sleep & rhythms
   Pinealon is linked by name and claim to the pineal gland, which makes melatonin. Supporting melatonin production is asserted in Russian reports, but no study cleanly shows it in people. Treat it as an unverified claim.
19. Mechanism overview: Pinealon is the tripeptide Glu-Asp-Arg from the Khavinson group in St Petersburg. The group claims short peptides like this enter the nucleus and bind DNA, adjusting gene expression in specific tissues. For Pinealon, the claimed effects are lower reactive oxygen species and less cell death in neurons under stress, better memory in stressed rats, and support for melatonin synthesis. No receptor is known, and no independent lab has confirmed the mechanism.

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from, and what kind of evidence that implies [lab]
54. Pinealon is the tripeptide Glu-Asp-Arg, also written EDR, one of a family of short "bioregulator" peptides from the St Petersburg Institute of Bioregulation and Gerontology and the Pavlov Institute group around Vladimir Khavinson [2,4] (verified). Unlike a fragment cut from a known hormone, it was designed to stand for a tissue: the logic is that tiny peptides carry tissue-specific signals into cells. That logic is the group's own hypothesis, not an established principle of biology (inferred).
55. The literature is almost entirely Russian-language or from the originating group, and the English record mostly consists of reviews that summarise that work rather than new primary data [2,4] (verified).

### Mechanism, one step at a time, with the model each step came from [mixed]
56. Free radicals, in cells. In rat cerebellar neurons, EDR reduced reactive oxygen species triggered by ouabain, homocysteine and hydrogen peroxide, delayed homocysteine-driven ERK1/2 activation from about 2.5 to about 20 minutes, and lowered lipid hydroperoxides; similar effects were reported in activated neutrophils [1,2] (reported).
57. Gene control, mostly on a computer. The headline claim that EDR binds DNA and adjusts genes rests on molecular modelling: two predicted binding motifs, a search that found matching motifs in the promoters of two metabolic genes (PPARA and PPARG) and in a serotonin-synthesis gene, and an assumption that the peptide reaches the nucleus [2] (verified). A separate older study reported short fluorescent peptides entering HeLa cell nuclei and binding DNA in a test tube [6] (reported), but the 2021 EDR review itself presents no uptake or nuclear-localisation data for EDR [2] (verified). Prediction, not demonstration.
58. Brain function, in rats. Reported findings include normalised CNS function in offspring of mothers with high homocysteine, lower brain caspase-3 and better maze learning after hypoxia in old and young rats, and raised antioxidant enzymes in hypoxia-sensitive animals [2,4] (reported). Most are cited secondhand in reviews.
59. Human neurons in a dish. The most modern study converted skin cells from three women aged 61 to 68 into neurons and gave EDR at 10 micrograms per millilitre for 10 days. Oxidative DNA damage fell 23% (p = 0.057, so borderline), primary processes rose 28% and total dendrite length 46%, while mitochondrial activity, lysosomes and two ageing markers did not change [3] (verified). Three donors, one concentration, a lab dish.

### What the body does with it [lab]
60. There is no published pharmacokinetic data for Pinealon in any species (verified as absent in the sources reviewed). Free tripeptides are generally broken down quickly by blood and gut peptidases, so how much of an injected or swallowed dose reaches a neuron intact is unknown (inferred). The product is sold both as oral capsules and as injectable powder, with no study comparing what either route delivers.

### The human record [people]
61. The 2021 review by the originating group describes oral EDR added to standard therapy in 72 patients with the after-effects of traumatic brain injury, reporting better memory, fewer and milder headaches and better work performance, and a second group with fewer errors on an attention test and a higher EEG alpha index [2] (reported). Neither design, control group nor blinding is described. Memory improvement in elderly patients is claimed in the abstract without detail.
62. No randomised, placebo-controlled trial of Pinealon has been located, no trial is registered, and no study outside the originating network exists. There is no established human dose (verified as absent in the sources reviewed).

### Safety, and the shape of what is missing [mixed]
63. No toxicology of any kind, acute, repeat-dose, reproductive or carcinogenicity, has been located, and no adverse-event series exists (verified as absent in the sources reviewed). The claimed mechanism, a peptide that enters the nucleus and changes gene expression, is precisely the kind of action that would normally demand long-term safety work; that it has none is the main gap (inferred).
64. A practical point: the human reports used oral capsules, while much current use is injection of lyophilised powder. Injected material raises sterility, endotoxin and impurity questions that have never been addressed for this peptide (inferred).

### Status and open questions [mixed]
65. Not an approved drug anywhere. Not on any US compounding list reviewed for this read, and not among the peptides at the July 2026 advisory committee. In sport it is not named, and as a substance with no government approval for therapeutic use it falls in the catch-all non-approved class [5] (inferred).
66. Open questions: does EDR reach the nucleus of a living neuron? Does any effect survive a placebo-controlled trial? Is any oral dose absorbed intact? And does an independent lab reproduce even the antioxidant cell findings? Each is answerable, and none has been answered.
