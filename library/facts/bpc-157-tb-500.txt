# BPC-157 + TB-500
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/bpc-157-tb-500
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Wolverine stack, Wolverine blend, BPC/TB, BPC-157 + Tβ4, BPC-157 + Thymosin Beta-4, TB-500 + BPC-157
2. Origin: Blend of 2 peptides
3. Legal and approval status: Not approved anywhere as a drug or as a combination. US: BPC-157 and TB-500 left FDA 503A Category 2 in April 2026 when nominations were withdrawn; the July 2026 advisory panel voted in favour of compounding, no rule yet. Australia: both Schedule 4, prescription only. WADA: both banned at all times.

## Summary
4. This blend combines BPC-157, a 15-residue peptide from a gastric juice protein, with TB-500, usually full thymosin beta-4, in one vial sold as the Wolverine stack. People pair them for tendon, joint and post-surgery recovery, since one is thought to grow blood vessels and the other to help cells move into wounds. The one combined rat study found no extra benefit from pairing.
5. Punch line: The Wolverine stack in one vial. BPC-157 grows blood vessels, TB-500 moves repair cells; the default injury stack.
6. Used for: Tendon, ligament, joint and post-surgery recovery

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Tissue repair: Two different routes to tendon and wound healing
8. Inflammatory signaling: Lowers inflammation around injury
9. Pain & sensory: Joint and tendon pain eases for many users
10. Digestive & gut: BPC-157 side protects gut lining in rats
11. Muscle & performance (backed only by animal or user reports): Used for muscle tears and training recovery
12. Heart & vessels (backed only by animal or user reports): Both studied for heart tissue in animals
13. Bone & cartilage (backed only by animal or user reports): Studied for bone and fracture healing in rats

## How it works
14. Grows new blood vessels into injured tissue · BPC-157: VEGFR2, eNOS · role Tissue repair
   BPC-157 switches on the VEGFR2 receptor and the eNOS enzyme, which together drive new blood vessel growth. More vessels bring oxygen and repair cells into poorly supplied tissue like tendon. This is shown in rat injury models, while TB-500 adds a separate cell-migration route.
15. Shields gut lining, in rats · BPC-157: cytoprotection · role Digestive & gut
   BPC-157 protects the gut lining, a property called cytoprotection, and shields it from damage such as NSAID injury in rats. This is where the peptide was first studied. Its gut role adds a second use to the blend, but human data for it are thin.
16. Helps cells move into the wound by managing actin · Tβ4: G-actin sequestering · role Tissue repair
   Thymosin beta-4 binds single actin units (G-actin) and keeps them ready, so repair cells can build their internal scaffold and crawl into the wound. In the 2026 rat Achilles study, TB-500 alone improved tendon strength. The combination added nothing measurable over either alone.
17. Calms inflammatory signalling · Tβ4: NF-κB down · role Inflammatory signaling
   Thymosin beta-4 lowers NF-κB, the main switch for inflammatory genes. Less inflammation lets repair proceed with less tissue damage. This is shown in animal and cell work, with BPC-157 adding its own anti-inflammatory effect in injury models.
18. Mechanism overview: BPC-157 drives angiogenesis through VEGFR2, Akt and eNOS, activates FAK and paxillin for fibroblast migration, and upregulates growth hormone receptor on tendon fibroblasts. Thymosin beta-4 sequesters G-actin, promoting cell migration, and lowers NF-κB driven inflammation; its 17 to 23 region carries the actin binding. The rationale for pairing is two partly separate repair routes. The 2026 rat Achilles study suggests they may converge downstream, since combining added nothing measurable. On paper the two prefer different pH in solution: BPC-157 about 6.5 to 8.5, thymosin beta-4 about 5.5 to 6.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
36. Typical: 0.5–1 mg blend · 1× daily · cycle 6–12 weeks · route Sub-Q (reported)
37. Half-life: per component
38. Most people take: 0.5 to 1 mg of a 1:1 blend daily, which is 250 to 500 mcg each of BPC-157 and TB-500. On a 20 mg vial in 2 mL that is 5 to 10 units. People size it to the BPC-157 dose; the TB-500 share ends up far below standalone TB-500 loading (2 to 5 mg twice a week).

## What people report
39. Sub-Q daily, usually 0.5 to 1 mg of the blend (250 to 500 mcg of each). Courses 6 to 12 weeks, 2 to 4 weeks off. Some inject near the injury. People who want the higher weekly TB-500 doses (2 to 5 mg twice a week) run separate vials instead, because a 1:1 daily blend gives far less TB-500 per week.
40. Liked / worked: Tendon stronger with TB-500 in the combo rat study
41. Liked / worked: Default injury stack; pain eases in about 2 weeks
42. Liked / worked: Some recorded improvement in US medical notes
43. What worked: Users describe it as the default stack for tendon, ligament, joint and post-surgery recovery, with pain easing in about 2 weeks and the clearest changes at 3 to 6 weeks. A September 2026 analysis of US medical records found BPC-157 users (often on this pair) mostly started for pain, sports injury or gut symptoms, with some notes recording improvement.
44. Went wrong / did not work: Combining gave no added benefit in rats

46. Went wrong / did not work: Some stopped for mood, skin or gut effects

In that records analysis responses were noted for only a third of patients, and some stopped for neuropsychiatric, skin or gut side effects. Independent testing is poor: of 8 classifiable Finnrick tests of the blend (May to Sep 2026), none passed its purity standard. Reported side effects: tiredness or head pressure in the first weeks, headache, nausea, dizziness, injection site redness.
48. Most reported: Tendon, joint and post-surgery recovery

## What has been tested
52. Tissue repair: Achilles transection, BPC-157 + TB-500 together (rat)
53. Tissue repair: Tendon, ligament and muscle injury, each alone (rat)
54. Pain & sensory: Knee pain, BPC-157 injected, 16 patients (human)
55. Tissue repair: Wounds and dry eye, full Tβ4 (trial)
56. Only one study has tested the pair together: a 2026 rat Achilles transection study (8 rats per group, 4 weeks). TB-500 alone improved tendon strength and histology scores; the combination improved one histology score but gave no added benefit over either peptide alone. Everything else is component research: BPC-157 in rats plus a 16-patient knee series, thymosin beta-4 in rats and in human wound and eye trials.
57. What it helps: Evidence is animal and user-reported. Each peptide alone speeds tendon and wound healing in rats; full thymosin beta-4 has human trials in wounds and dry eye. The one combined study, in rats, showed no extra benefit from pairing. Users rate it highly for tendon, joint and post-surgery recovery, usually within 2 to 6 weeks.

## Who shouldn't take it
58. People with active or recent cancer: Raises VEGFR2 signalling and new vessel growth in animal work; tumours use the same pathway. (inferred)
59. Drug-tested athletes: Banned since 2022 under S0 (non-approved substances); positives have led to bans. (verified)
60. People with proliferative retinopathy or vascular malformations: Pro-angiogenic peptides could feed abnormal vessel growth. (inferred)
61. People with known peptide or injection allergies: FDA reviewers flagged immunogenicity and impurity risk; no human safety dataset exists. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
62. GHK-Cu: GHK-Cu brings copper and collagen remodelling for skin and scar, while the BPC-157 and TB-500 pair covers deeper vessel growth and cell migration. Adding all three is sold as GLOW. No study has tested this stack, so the pairing is user logic, not trial data.
63. KPV: KPV blocks NF-κB inside gut and immune cells, while the blend works on vessel growth and cell migration. People add it for inflamed injuries or gut flares alongside repair. The combination has not been tested.
64. TB-500 frag 17–23: The 17 to 23 fragment carries the actin-binding region of thymosin beta-4, which the blend already contains in full. Adding it doubles up on the same mechanism. There is no data showing extra benefit, and the pair has not been tested together.
65. Thymosin Beta-4: This blend already contains thymosin beta-4 as TB-500, so adding more just raises that half's share. People do it when they want more cell migration and anti-inflammatory action. No study supports extra benefit.
66. BPC-157: This blend already contains BPC-157, so adding more raises the vessel growth and gut-protection half. In the only controlled tendon study, BPC-157 alone did not reach significance on strength. There is no data showing extra benefit from the added dose.

## Where it comes from
67. A two-peptide vial pairing BPC-157, a 15-residue peptide from a gastric juice protein, with TB-500. On the common retail version TB-500 means full 43-residue thymosin beta-4, not the 7-residue fragment. Sold 1:1 as 5+5, 10+10 or 30+30 mg.
68. First studied for: Assembled by the research-peptide market as an all-round injury stack. No company or trial designed it.
69. Name story: Sold as the Wolverine stack, after the X-Men character who heals from anything. A marketing name, not a scientific one. [S1] (verified)
70. 1981: Thymosin beta-4 isolated from calf thymus
71. Early 1990s: BPC-157 reported from gastric juice in Zagreb
72. 2010s to 2020s: Paired by users and sold premixed as the Wolverine stack
73. Apr 2026: Both removed from FDA Category 2 after nominations withdrawn
74. Jul 2026: FDA panel votes for compounding; same year a rat study finds no added benefit from combining
75. Sep 2026: US records study: new BPC-157 users up 33-fold since 2020

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### The one study that tested the pair, read properly [animals]
76. A 2026 study in a Turkish orthopaedic journal is the only published work to test these two together, and because it is the entire direct evidence base for the blend it is worth reading in full rather than by its conclusion [1].
77. Thirty-two male Sprague-Dawley rats, about 330 grams, were randomised into four groups of eight: vehicle, one peptide, the other peptide, and both. Each animal had a standardised full transection of the right Achilles tendon with a Kessler repair on day zero. Dosing began on day one and ran for 30 days, all by intraperitoneal injection: the gastric peptide at 10 micrograms per kilogram per day, the thymosin component at 60 micrograms per kilogram per day [1] (verified). Within each group of eight, four tendons went to mechanical testing and four to histology.
78. Maximum load to failure: vehicle 26.91 newtons, gastric peptide alone 37.16, thymosin alone 37.41, the combination 34.54 [1]. The overall test was significant, and the only surviving pairwise comparison after correction was the thymosin component against vehicle [1] (verified).
79. On histology, the Bonar total improved significantly only for the thymosin component against vehicle. The Movin total improved significantly for the thymosin component and for the combination, but not for the gastric peptide alone [1] (verified).
80. The authors state the result directly: combining the two conferred no additional benefit over either agent alone. They set out three candidate explanations, overlapping downstream pathways, a ceiling effect, or doses that were not optimal for a tendon model, and tested none of them [1] (verified).

### Three things in that study that cut against the usual framing [animals]
81. First, the gastric peptide alone did not reach significance on the mechanical measure, the one that speaks to whether a tendon can take load [1]. In a product whose reputation rests largely on that component, the single controlled tendon experiment did not confirm it, while the component usually treated as the junior partner did.
82. Second, the combination's mechanical result was numerically below both single agents, 34.54 against 37.16 and 37.41 [1]. With four tendons per group this is well within noise and should not be read as interference. It is, equally, the opposite of the additive pattern the pairing is sold on, and it is the only direct observation anyone has (verified, with the caveat stated).
83. Third, a collagen finding that reads as positive may not be. Type III collagen was higher with the thymosin component, and the authors explicitly cautioned that elevated type III should not be assumed favourable, because type III is characteristic of early repair rather than mature tendon [1] (verified). More early-repair collagen at four weeks could mean faster healing or could mean healing that has not matured.
84. The authors' own limitations are extensive and worth respecting: a single four-week timepoint so later maturation was not assessed, healthy young male rats only, no functional testing, no molecular or pharmacokinetic work, a thymosin dose not optimised for tendon, histology subsets of four with no a priori power calculation, no blinding of surgery or dosing, and mechanics limited to maximum load with no stiffness or cross-sectional area [1]. They describe the work as exploratory and hypothesis-generating. That is the correct reading, and it is also the strongest direct evidence this blend has.

### The route the study used is not the route people use [animals]
85. Dosing was intraperitoneal, into the abdominal cavity [1]. That is standard practice in rodent work and it is not comparable to subcutaneous injection in a person: absorption, first-pass exposure through the portal circulation and peak concentration all differ.
86. This matters for the blend specifically because the pairing's rationale is systemic repair signalling reaching an injured tendon. A study that delivers both peptides into the peritoneum of a rat tells you something about systemic effect in that species and nothing about what reaches a human tendon (inferred).
87. It also compounds a gap already present for both components separately: neither has human pharmacokinetic data by the routes in use. For the gastric peptide, regulators found no human pharmacokinetics by any proposed route, and in the human studies where plasma was measured it was undetectable or mostly below the limit of quantification [2]. For the thymosin fragment, there is no human pharmacokinetic data at all, and the only animal data is a single horse study [3] (verified). So there is no exposure figure for either component to connect a dose to a tissue.

### Which molecule is the second component [lab]
88. Regulators stated in 2026 that the seven-residue fragment and the 43-residue parent protein are not the same substance [3] (verified). On the common retail version of this blend the second component is the full protein, under the fragment's trade name.
89. That single fact breaks the chain of inference the product relies on in both directions. The animal study above used material called by the fragment's name; the wound and eye data people cite belongs to the full protein; and the formal regulatory assessment of effectiveness and safety was carried out on the fragment [1,3]. These are three different molecules' worth of evidence being pooled under one label.
90. Neither component has an approved generic name, and for each one regulators found nominators had been inconsistent about whether they meant a free base or an acetate salt, in at least one case submitting a certificate for one form under the name of the other [2,3] (verified). A two-component vial inherits both identity problems at once.
91. Independent batch testing of the blend is the weakest of the blends tracked: across eight classifiable results from May to September 2026, none passed the testing service's purity standard (reported, as logged in the card record and not re-verified in this pass).

### What combining adds, and what it costs [mixed]
92. In evidence terms, combining adds nothing measurable. The one study designed to detect an additive effect did not find one [1]. No study has tested any other ratio, any other route, or any other timepoint.
93. What combining costs is attribution, and there is a concrete illustration of that cost in the regulatory record. Both components' adverse-event reviews turned up cases involving blended products where causality could not be assigned to either ingredient: a report of injection-site redness and swelling in someone also injecting the thymosin component, and a report of hyperpigmentation with gum darkening that recurred on re-exposure to a two-peptide product [2,3] (verified). Each of those is an observation that a single-agent vial would have made interpretable and a blend made permanently ambiguous.
94. The same applies to benefit. A person who improves on a blend has learned that the blend did something, not which half, and therefore cannot narrow what to continue. Given that the one controlled experiment found the two components performing comparably alone and no better together, that lost information has a real cost (inferred).
95. There is one piece of human safety data touching either component, and it is very small: an uncontrolled pilot infusing the gastric peptide intravenously in two healthy adults at up to 20 mg, reporting no measurable change in cardiac, liver, kidney or thyroid markers and no adverse effects [4] (reported). Two people, no control, no efficacy endpoint.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
96. No approved product contains either component, and none contains the combination. Both left the US do-not-compound category in April 2026 because nominations were withdrawn, not because of a revised safety finding, which left them outside any recognised category rather than cleared [5] (verified).
97. On 23 July 2026 the advisory committee recommended both for the 503A bulks list, each by 8 to 6 with one abstention, against agency staff who had proposed adding neither [2,3,6]. Staff had assessed the gastric peptide for ulcerative colitis and the thymosin fragment for wound healing, and found the evidence weighed against both.
98. Those recommendations are advisory and change nothing by themselves: addition requires formal notice-and-comment rulemaking, which has not begun and has no deadline [7] (verified). An "Interim Access Policy for Compounding Using Certain Peptides" went to the White House budget office on 14 October 2026 and was unpublished as of mid-October, with its covered substances and conditions not public [7].
99. Nothing in any of this addresses combinations. A substance being eligible for compounding individually would not make a premixed two-peptide injectable an assessed product (inferred).
100. In sport, both components are prohibited at all times.

### Open questions [mixed]
101. Does any ratio or schedule produce an additive effect? The single experiment tested one ratio, one route, one duration, and found none. The question is open rather than answered.
102. Would the result hold at twelve weeks instead of four? Tendon maturation continues well past the single timepoint used, and the authors name this as their main limitation.
103. Why did the component with the larger reputation fail to reach significance on load to failure? Dose, route, or genuine absence of effect in this model are all live explanations.
104. Is elevated early-repair collagen good? The study's own authors declined to assume so, and nobody has followed the tissue out to maturity.
105. Which thymosin molecule is in a given vial, and does it matter? Regulators say the two are different substances; no routine identity test distinguishes them in grey-market supply.
106. What reaches an injured tendon in a person? No human pharmacokinetic data exists for either component by any route in use.
107. Why does independent purity testing of this blend fail more often than for the single agents? Testing could answer this; it needs no trial.
