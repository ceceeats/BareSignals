# VIP
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/vip
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: vasoactive intestinal polypeptide, aviptadil
2. Origin: Made by the body
3. Sequence: HSDAVFTDNYTRLRKQMAVKKYLNSILN (28 amino acids). Amide end
4. Legal and approval status: Aviptadil (synthetic VIP) with phentolamine is approved for erectile dysfunction in the UK and some European countries; not FDA-approved. Other uses are research or compounded.

## Summary
5. VIP (vasoactive intestinal polypeptide) is a 28-amino-acid hormone made in the gut, lung, nerves and immune cells that widens blood vessels and calms inflammation. It has been studied in pulmonary hypertension and sarcoidosis, and aviptadil is approved for erectile dysfunction in some countries. Nasal VIP is popular in mold-illness circles, but formal evidence is thin.
6. Punch line: Gut, lung and nerve hormone that widens vessels and calms inflammation. A nasal staple in mold illness protocols.
7. Used for: Mold illness (CIRS), inflammation

## How it works
15. Widens blood vessels · VPAC1, VPAC2 · role Organ function
   VIP binds VPAC1 and VPAC2, receptors that raise cAMP inside the smooth muscle around blood vessels. That relaxes the muscle and widens the vessels. Small human studies show vascular benefit in pulmonary hypertension.
16. Dampens immune inflammation, boosts Tregs · VPAC on immune cells · role Inflammatory signaling
   On immune cells, VPAC signalling lowers inflammatory cytokines and favours regulatory T cells, which calm immune responses. This shifts the immune system away from inflammation. Small human studies show anti-inflammatory signals in sarcoidosis.
17. Relaxes airway and gut smooth muscle · VPAC receptors · role Digestive & gut
   The same cAMP rise through VPAC receptors relaxes smooth muscle in the airways and gut. This is why VIP was studied in lung conditions. It is well-established receptor pharmacology, though human outcome trials are limited.
18. Relaxes erectile tissue, used for ED · Cavernosal VPAC, cAMP · role Connection & desire
   In erectile tissue, VPAC activation raises cAMP and relaxes the smooth muscle, letting blood fill the tissue. Aviptadil, a form of VIP, has been trialled with phentolamine for erectile dysfunction. Those trials used the combination, not VIP alone.
19. Shields neurons via glial protective factors · ADNP release · role Neural protection
   VIP prompts glial cells, the brain's support cells, to release protective factors such as ADNP. These factors shield neurons from damage. This is shown in cell and animal studies, not in people.
20. Syncs the body clock in the brain · SCN VPAC2 signalling · role Sleep & rhythms
   In the suprachiasmatic nucleus, the brain's master clock, VPAC2 signalling keeps clock neurons in step with each other. This keeps the body's daily rhythms synchronised. It is well established in animal research.
21. Mechanism overview: Vasoactive intestinal peptide is a 28-amino-acid neuropeptide acting on VPAC1 and VPAC2, Gs-coupled receptors that raise cAMP. In smooth muscle that relaxes blood vessels, airways, gut and erectile tissue. On immune cells it dampens inflammatory cytokines and favours regulatory T cells. It prompts glia to release protective factors such as ADNP that shield neurons, and VPAC2 signalling in the suprachiasmatic nucleus synchronises the body clock. Its half-life is very short.

## Who shouldn't take it
47. People with low blood pressure or on BP drugs: VIP dilates vessels; hypotension and flushing in trials. (reported)
48. People prone to dehydration or low potassium: VIP drives water and potassium into the gut; tumours making it cause severe diarrhoea. (reported)

## Where it comes from
49. Vasoactive Intestinal Peptide, a 28-amino-acid signaling hormone in gut, lung, nerves and immune cells, with broad anti-inflammatory and vasodilating action.
50. First studied for: Studied in pulmonary hypertension, sarcoidosis, and in the CIRS/mold-illness community (as a nasal spray) for immune and inflammatory dysregulation.
51. Name story: Vasoactive intestinal peptide: Said and Mutt pulled it from pig intestine in 1970 and named it for widening blood vessels. It later proved to be a brain and nerve messenger too. The drug version's INN is aviptadil. [S6] (verified)
52. 1970: Discovered in the gut as a vasoactive peptide
53. 2000s: Small human studies in pulmonary hypertension and sarcoidosis
54. 2010s: Nasal VIP adopted in CIRS and mold-illness protocols
55. Today: Aviptadil approved for ED in the UK and parts of Europe; not in the US
