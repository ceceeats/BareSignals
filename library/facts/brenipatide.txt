# Brenipatide
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/brenipatide
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: LY3537031, LY-3537031, 'brinipatide' (common misspelling), Lilly addiction GLP-1
2. Origin: Lab-designed: dual GIP and GLP-1 agonist peptide with a fatty acid tail
3. Legal and approval status: Investigational, not approved anywhere as of Oct 2026. Phase 3 for alcohol use disorder, recurrent depression and bipolar disorder; alcohol results expected by mid-2028. Not named on the WADA list (falls under S0, inferred).

## Summary
4. Brenipatide is Eli Lilly's long-acting GIP and GLP-1 dual agonist, a cousin of tirzepatide. It is aimed at alcohol use, depression relapse and inflammatory lung disease rather than weight, and is thought to quiet the brain's reward pathway. There is no efficacy data yet, and the addiction case rests on the wider GLP-1 class. Phase 1 showed it is tolerable, though abnormal skin sensations occurred in 15.2% on drug.
5. Punch line: Lilly's tirzepatide cousin aimed at cravings, not weight. In Phase 3 for heavy drinking; not on grey market.
6. Used for: Alcohol use disorder, depression, investigational

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Reward & urges: Aims to cut heavy drinking and cravings
8. Mood: Tested for preventing relapse of depression
9. Inflammatory signaling: Aimed at inflammatory pathways in asthma and COPD
10. Appetite & satiety: Shares tirzepatide's appetite pathway

## How it works
11. Turns on GLP-1 and GIP receptors together · GLP-1R and GIPR agonist · role Reward & urges
   Brenipatide activates GIP and GLP-1 receptors together, like tirzepatide, but Lilly designed it to reach these receptors in the central nervous system. The goal is brain and reward effects rather than weight. Phase 1 in people shows only that it is tolerated; no efficacy data exist yet.
12. Thought to quiet the brain's reward pathway · Mesolimbic signalling (inferred from GLP-1 class) · role Reward & urges
   GLP-1 drugs are thought to quiet the mesolimbic reward pathway, the dopamine circuit behind craving. Brenipatide is meant to do the same for alcohol and other addictions. This is inferred from the GLP-1 class, where semaglutide reduced heavy drinking in a small trial.
13. Stays in the body for over a week · C20 diacid, alpha-methyl residues · role Appetite & satiety
   A C20 fatty acid tail grips albumin, and alpha-methyl residues plus a D-tyrosine at the start resist enzyme breakdown. Together they give a half-life of over a week in Phase 1. Levels stay fairly flat between weekly doses.
14. Mechanism overview: Brenipatide is a synthetic GIP and GLP-1 receptor dual agonist from Eli Lilly. A single C20 diacid tail binds albumin, and alpha-methylated residues plus an N-terminal D-tyrosine resist enzyme breakdown, giving a half-life of about 9 to 12.5 days in Phase 1 with a low peak-to-trough ratio. Lilly describes it as designed to reach GIP and GLP-1 receptors in the central nervous system and in inflammatory pathways, including reward circuits.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
15. Typical: 0.3 to 4.5 mg tested in Phase 1; Phase 3 dose not public · Once weekly in Lilly's trials (some databases list monthly) · cycle Trial only (56 weeks in alcohol use disorder) · route Sub-Q (trial)
16. Half-life: 9 to 12.5 days (Phase 1)
17. Most people take: No approved dose. Phase 1 tested 0.3, 0.75, 1.5, 3 and 4.5 mg; Lilly's poster says the data support weekly maintenance doses across a broad range of body weights.

## What people report
18. Heavily discussed online after Lilly's Phase 3 alcohol trials opened in 2025 and 2026, often under the misspelling 'brinipatide'. No credible first-hand user reports; it is not on the grey market in any verified form.
19. Liked / worked: Well tolerated in Phase 1
20. Liked / worked: Long half-life suits weekly dosing
21. What worked: Phase 1: no deaths or serious adverse events, half-life of 9 to 12.5 days, and stomach side effects no more common than placebo (25% in both groups). No efficacy data for any addiction has been released yet.
22. Went wrong / did not work: No efficacy results released yet
23. Went wrong / did not work: Skin sensation side effect in Phase 1
24. What went wrong: Abnormal skin sensations (dysesthesia) in 15.2% on drug versus none on placebo in Phase 1; 2.2% stopped for side effects.
25. Most reported: Mostly news and forum speculation, no verified use

## What has been tested
26. Reward & urges: Alcohol use disorder, Phase 3 (trial)
27. Mood: Recurrent depression and bipolar disorder, Phase 3 (trial)
28. Reward & urges: Smoking and opioid use disorder, Phase 2 (trial)
29. Phase 1 in more than 200 people (NCT06606106) at 0.3 to 4.5 mg weekly. Phase 3 in alcohol use disorder (RENEW-ALC-1 and 2, about 2,200 people, 56 weeks), relapse prevention in depression (RENEW-MDD-1, about 1,000 people) and bipolar disorder; Phase 2 in smoking, opioid use disorder, schizophrenia, asthma, COPD and IBS.
30. What it helps: No efficacy data yet. Phase 1 in more than 200 people showed it is tolerable at up to 4.5 mg. The case for addiction rests on the wider GLP-1 class, where semaglutide reduced heavy drinking in a small 2025 trial.
31. Trial: active · Phase 3 · NCT07219966. Two 56-week Phase 3 trials in alcohol use disorder with about 2,200 people are running, with results expected by mid-2028. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1]

## Synergies (paired with)
32. Tirzepatide: Tirzepatide hits the same GIP and GLP-1 receptors but is built for weight and glucose, while brenipatide is aimed at the brain. They are related molecules, not a stack. Combining them has not been tested.
33. Semaglutide: Semaglutide's small alcohol trial is the main reason brenipatide exists, and both act on GLP-1 reward signalling. They overlap rather than complement. The pair has not been tested together.
34. GLP-1: Native GLP-1 is the hormone both GLP-1 arms of brenipatide copy, but it is broken down in about two minutes. It is a reference point, not a practical partner. The pairing has no trial data.

## Where it comes from
35. Made by Eli Lilly as a cousin of tirzepatide: the same GIP plus GLP-1 double action, but built to last longer and aimed at the brain and inflammation rather than at weight.
36. First studied for: Alcohol use disorder and recurrent depression, both taken straight into Phase 3 without a Phase 2.
37. 2024: Phase 1 dose-finding study (NCT06606106) in people with and without obesity
38. Late 2025: Two Phase 3 alcohol use disorder trials registered, RENEW-ALC-1 and 2
39. Dec 2025: Name brenipatide appears; listed in Phase 3 for alcohol use disorder and bipolar disorder
40. Jun 2026: UW Medicine and about 30 US sites enrolling for the alcohol trial
41. Sep 2026: Phase 1 data and Phase 3 designs shown at Psych Congress
42. Mid 2028: Alcohol use disorder results expected
