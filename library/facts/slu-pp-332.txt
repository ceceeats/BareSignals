# SLU-PP-332
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/slu-pp-332
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: slu pp 332 exercise mimetic, CAS 303760-60-3, 4-hydroxy-N'-(naphthalen-2-ylmethylidene)benzohydrazide
2. Origin: Not a peptide
3. Legal and approval status: Research chemical; no clinical program in humans.

## Summary
4. SLU-PP-332 is a small-molecule research chemical, not a peptide, called an exercise mimetic because it activates the ERR family of receptors that push cells toward an endurance state. It is studied for raising energy expenditure, endurance and fat loss without exercise. All data come from mice, there is no human safety or efficacy data, and first-hand grey-market reports are sparse.
5. Punch line: The exercise mimetic. Flips muscle into an endurance state through ERR receptors; mouse data only, no human safety.
6. Used for: Endurance, fat loss

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Cellular energy: Builds more mitochondria in mice
8. Muscle & performance: Longer running in mice
9. Fat metabolism: Fat loss in mice
10. Drive & mental stamina: Users report more stamina

## How it works
11. Pushes cells into an endurance state · ERR alpha, beta, gamma · role Muscle & performance
   SLU-PP-332 is a synthetic agonist of the estrogen-related receptors ERR alpha, beta and gamma, which are unrelated to estrogen itself. With the coactivator PGC-1 alpha, these receptors switch on endurance genes. In mice, the running effect required muscle ERR alpha.
12. Builds more mitochondria · ERR agonism · role Cellular energy
   Activating ERRs turns on genes that build new mitochondria, a process called mitochondrial biogenesis. Treated mouse muscle showed more mitochondria and more mitochondrial DNA and enzyme proteins. These results come only from mice given injections, with no human data.
13. Shifts muscle to burning fat · Fatty acid oxidation · role Fat metabolism
   The same gene programme shifts muscle toward burning fatty acids for fuel. In obese mice, treatment raised energy expenditure and cut fat gain without changing food intake or movement. No human safety or efficacy data exist.
14. Raised running endurance in mice · Oxidative fibre shift · role Drive & mental stamina
   Muscle fibres shifted toward the oxidative type built for endurance, and the fibres were smaller, the opposite of a muscle-building drug. Treated mice ran about 70% longer before exhaustion. All of this comes from one academic network and has not been replicated independently.
15. Mechanism overview: SLU-PP-332 is a synthetic small molecule agonist of the estrogen-related receptors ERR alpha, beta and gamma, strongest at ERR alpha. Despite the name, these orphan nuclear receptors are unrelated to estrogen signalling. With the coactivator PGC-1 alpha they control mitochondrial biogenesis and oxidative metabolism. Activating them switches on genes for mitochondria, fatty acid oxidation and oxidative muscle fibres, mimicking the transcriptional effect of endurance training. In mice that means more mitochondria, more fat burning and longer running capacity.

## What has been tested
31. Cellular energy: Running endurance (mouse)
32. Fat metabolism: Weight and fat loss (mouse)
33. Mouse studies: more mitochondria, better running endurance, fat loss. No human data.
34. What it helps: All data are from mice. Treated mice ran longer, built more muscle mitochondria and lost weight and fat. There is no human safety or efficacy data, and the BAM-15 pairing seen online is also unstudied in people. Grey-market use is very early, and first-hand reports are sparse.

## Who shouldn't take it
36. Drug-tested athletes: Sold as an "exercise mimetic"; metabolic modulators fall under WADA S4.4. (inferred)
37. People with heart disease: ERR agonism reshapes heart muscle metabolism; no human data at all. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
38. BAM-15: SLU-PP-332 switches on the genes that build mitochondria and burn fat, while BAM-15 lets protons leak across the mitochondrial membrane so cells burn more fuel to keep up. They are sold pre-mixed as SLU-PP-332 + BAM-15 oral. The pairing is unstudied in people.

## Where it comes from
39. A small-molecule 'exercise mimetic' from Saint Louis University that activates the ERR family of receptors, pushing cells toward the metabolic state of endurance exercise.
40. First studied for: Studied to raise energy expenditure and endurance without exercise.
41. Name story: 'SLU' almost certainly marks Saint Louis University, where the Burris and Walker labs built this series (a later sibling is SLU-PP-915). The papers never decode 'PP'. Despite the peptide shelf, it is a small hydrazone molecule. [S5] (inferred)
42. 2020s: Saint Louis University exercise mimetic shows endurance gains in mice
43. Today: Early grey-market oral, often paired with BAM-15; no human data

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### Where it came from: a tool compound from one academic network [lab]
44. It was designed by medicinal chemists working with Thomas Burris's group, across Saint Louis University, Washington University in St. Louis and later the University of Florida, as a probe for the estrogen-related receptors [1,2]. Those receptors had been studied almost entirely by switching them off genetically; a usable agonist let the group switch them on in a living animal (verified that this was the stated aim).
45. The published record is three papers from that network and its collaborators: an exercise paper in 2023, a metabolic syndrome paper in 2024, and a heart failure paper with a Baylor cardiology group in 2024 [1,2,3] (verified). No independent laboratory has published a replication, and no clinical programme has been announced. The group's own stated next steps were to refine the structure, ideally into something orally usable, and to do more animal safety work before any human trial [4] (reported).

### Mechanism, in the numbers the papers report [cells]
46. Potency depends on the assay. In a chimeric receptor assay the half-maximal concentrations were about 8 nanomolar for ERR alpha, 280 for beta and 770 for gamma; with the full-length receptors they were about 98, 230 and 430 [1] (verified). That is roughly four-fold preference for alpha in the more realistic assay, closer to a pan-agonist than the alpha label suggests.
47. The subtypes appear to carry different jobs. The endurance effect required muscle ERR alpha: in mice lacking it in muscle, treatment no longer improved running, and several target genes stopped responding [1] (verified, animals). In the heart, the protective effect ran mainly through ERR gamma [3] (verified, animals). One molecule is therefore doing two partly separate things in two organs.
48. The heart work also found that, alongside switching on fat-burning and mitochondrial genes, the agonists turned down cell-cycle and developmental gene programmes in heart muscle cells, partly through a regulator called E2F1 [3] (verified, animals and cells). In a failing heart that was presented as part of the benefit. What the same suppression does in tissues that divide normally has not been examined.

### What the animal studies measured [animals]
49. Endurance. Mice given the compound by intraperitoneal injection ran about 70% longer and about 45% further before exhaustion than vehicle-treated mice. Muscle showed stronger succinate dehydrogenase staining, more oxidative type IIa fibres, more mitochondria on electron microscopy, higher mitochondrial DNA relative to nuclear DNA, and more Complex I, Complex V and cytochrome c protein [1] (verified, animals). Myofibres were smaller in diameter, which is the opposite of a muscle-building drug and consistent with an endurance phenotype.
50. Obesity. In diet-induced obese mice, twice-daily treatment for a month produced about 12% weight loss, with roughly ten-fold less fat gained than untreated mice, improved insulin sensitivity and higher energy expenditure. Food intake did not change, and the mice did not move more; the investigator's summary was that they used more energy at rest [2,4] (reported for the numbers, which come from the university's release rather than the paper's abstract).
51. Heart failure. In a mouse pressure-overload model, this compound and a sibling improved ejection fraction, reduced fibrosis and improved survival without changing the degree of cardiac enlargement [3] (verified, animals). This is the strongest outcome in the whole record, and it is in a disease model rather than in healthy animals.

### What the body does with it, and why route matters [animals]
52. Exposure was measured in mice after a single 30 mg per kg intraperitoneal dose: about 0.8 micromolar in muscle and about 0.1 micromolar in plasma at two hours [1] (verified, animals). No plasma half-life, no oral bioavailability figure and no brain exposure were reported in the accessible version of that work, although brain was listed among tissues collected.
53. Every animal result above was produced by injection into the abdominal cavity, twice a day [1,2]. That is a laboratory route, not a human one, and the group's stated goal of making an orally usable version implies this molecule is not it (inferred from their own description [4]). Community oral and subcutaneous use is therefore extrapolating across both species and route from a compound whose oral behaviour has not been published.
54. No human pharmacokinetic data exist, and no established human dose exists.

### Safety: what has been looked at [animals]
55. The only safety assessment in the record is short. At 50 mg per kg twice daily for ten days in mice, the authors reported no overt toxicity, with normal blood counts and electrolytes and no significant change in creatine kinase [1] (verified, animals, ten days).
56. What that leaves untouched: no repeat-dose toxicology beyond those ten days, no genotoxicity, no reproductive or developmental studies, no carcinogenicity work, and no cardiac safety study in healthy animals. The heart results come from animals with induced heart failure, where raising oxidative metabolism helps; nothing tells you what sustained ERR activation does to a healthy heart (inferred).
57. One mechanistic question deserves naming rather than burying. The compound drives mitochondrial biogenesis and fat oxidation in several tissues at once, and in heart cells it suppressed cell-cycle programmes [1,3]. Both of those are broad transcriptional changes rather than narrow effects, and no study has looked for off-target consequences in a healthy animal over months.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
58. A research chemical with no approval anywhere, no investigational new drug programme announced, and no clinical trial registered for this molecule. Material sold for human use is unapproved by definition.
59. In sport it falls under WADA's S0 class, substances with no current approval for human therapeutic use, prohibited at all times (inferred from the class definition; the compound is not named individually on the list). Metabolic modulators are also covered as a class under S4, and anti-doping laboratories have added ERR agonists to screening interest as exercise mimetics (inferred).

### Open questions [mixed]
60. Does anything here happen in a person? Three animal papers from one network, no human data, no replication.
61. Does it work by mouth? The group's own plan to engineer an oral version suggests the published molecule is not well suited to it, and no oral exposure data have been published.
62. What does sustained ERR activation do to a healthy heart, given that the cardiac benefit was shown in failing hearts and came with suppression of cell-cycle genes?
63. Why do the mice burn more energy without moving more, and is that sustainable? The obesity paper reports the result; the thermogenic mechanism in intact animals is not pinned down.
64. Nothing beyond ten days has been examined in any species. That is the single largest gap, given that community use runs in multi-week cycles.
