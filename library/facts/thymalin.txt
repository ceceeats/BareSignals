# Thymalin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/thymalin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Timalin, Тималин, thymus polypeptide extract
2. Origin: Made by the body: calf thymus extract
3. Legal and approval status: Registered prescription immunostimulant in Russia (registration renewed June 2024) and sold in parts of the CIS; not FDA or EMA approved as of Oct 2026; not named on the WADA list.

## Summary
4. Thymalin is a 1974 extract of calf thymus peptides, the first of the Russian peptide bioregulators developed by Morozov and Khavinson. It is registered in Russia for immunodeficiency and severe infections, and is thought to rebalance T and B cells. Western users take it for immune aging, usually with Epitalon, but evidence comes almost entirely from the developers' own group.
5. Punch line: The first Russian bioregulator, from calf thymus. Short courses to rebuild T cells, often paired with Epitalon.
6. Used for: Immune recovery, infection, longevity courses

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Immunity: Restores T-cell numbers
8. Aging & senescence: Longevity courses, Russian data
9. Inflammatory signaling: Lower IL-6 and CRP reported
10. Tissue repair: Supports regeneration and blood making
11. Cancer biology (backed only by animal or user reports): Fewer tumours in old rodents

## How it works
12. Raises and rebalances T and B cells · T/B lymphocyte subsets · role Immunity
   Thymalin is a calf thymus extract of short peptides that acts on lymphocyte development. It raises and rebalances T and B cell subsets, the cells that run adaptive immunity. This is shown in Russian clinical use and in animals with the thymus removed, with no Western trials.
13. Boosts germ-eating cells · Phagocytosis · role Immunity
   It enhances phagocytosis, the process where white cells such as macrophages engulf and kill germs. Better phagocytosis means faster clearance of bacteria and debris. This comes from Russian animal and clinical studies of the extract.
14. Lowers inflammation markers in COVID-19 · IL-6, CRP, D-dimer · role Inflammatory signaling
   In a small single-blind Russian trial in severe COVID-19, patients on Thymalin showed falls in IL-6, CRP and D-dimer, markers of inflammation and clotting. Lymphocytes recovered faster and fewer died. It is one small trial and needs independent repeat.
15. Nudges blood stem cells toward T cells · Haematopoietic stem cell differentiation · role Tissue repair
   In culture, the extract pushes haematopoietic stem cells, the blood-forming stem cells, toward the thymocyte line that becomes T cells. That could explain T-cell recovery after thymectomy or radiation in animals. It is cell data, and the active peptides in the mix are not defined.
16. Claimed to slow immune aging · Thymic involution, claimed · role Aging & senescence
   With age the thymus shrinks and T-cell output falls, a process called thymic involution. Thymalin is claimed to slow this. The support is Khavinson's non-randomised elderly cohorts reporting lower mortality, which have major design flaws, so this remains a claim.
17. Mechanism overview: Thymalin is a lyophilised complex of low-molecular-weight peptides acid-extracted from calf thymus, standardised as 10 mg of thymus polypeptides per vial. Its best-studied pieces are the dipeptides Glu-Trp (EW, sold as Thymogen) and Lys-Glu (KE, Vilon). It raises and rebalances T and B lymphocyte subsets, enhances phagocytosis, restores T-cell counts in thymectomised animals, and in culture pushes haematopoietic stem cells toward the thymocyte line. The Khavinson group proposes its short peptides enter the nucleus and tune gene expression; this is not independently confirmed.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
18. Typical: 5–20 mg (adults) · once daily · cycle 3–10 days, 30–100 mg per course, repeat after 1–6 months · route IM (verified)
19. Half-life: Unknown
20. Most people take: Russian label: 5–20 mg IM daily for 3–10 days (prevention 5–10 mg for 3–5 days); children 1–5 mg by age. Community longevity course 10 mg IM daily for 10 days, 1–2×/yr, often with Epitalon. Sub-Q use reported.

## What has been tested
34. Immunity: Lymphocyte recovery in severe COVID-19 (human)
35. Aging & senescence: Mortality in elderly cohorts (human)
36. Immunity: T-cell restoration after thymectomy or radiation (animal)
37. Cancer biology: Spontaneous tumours and lifespan (animal)
38. Tissue repair: Blood stem cell differentiation (cell)
39. Decades of Russian clinical use and studies, including a small randomised single-blind trial in severe COVID-19 and non-randomised elderly cohorts; no Western trials.
40. What it helps: Russia registers it for immunodeficiency, severe infections, poor regeneration and immune suppression after chemotherapy or radiation. A small randomised single-blind Russian trial in severe COVID-19 reported faster lymphocyte recovery and fewer deaths, and Khavinson's elderly cohorts claim lower mortality with Thymalin and Epitalon courses. Western users take it in short courses for immune aging and longevity, usually with Epitalon. Reported side effects are injection-site reactions and rare allergy.
41. Trial: none · immune aging, COVID 19 · Khavinson group. Russian studies, including a 2020 COVID report, were not registered in any international registry; Russian registry status unverifiable. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
42. Transplant recipients on anti-rejection drugs: A thymus extract that stimulates T cells, which works against anti-rejection drugs. (inferred)
43. People with beef protein allergy: It is extracted from calf thymus, so it carries beef protein. (reported)

## Synergies (paired with)
44. Epitalon: Epitalon is claimed to act on telomerase and the pineal gland, while Thymalin targets the aging immune system. They are the classic Khavinson longevity course, and the 2003 elderly cohorts tested them alone and together. Those cohorts are small and flawed, so this is not solid trial data.
45. Thymosin Alpha-1: Thymosin Alpha-1 is a defined thymic peptide that matures T cells, primes dendritic cells and has human hepatitis B data. Thymalin is a crude thymus extract aimed at the same immune recovery. People pair them for infection and immune aging, but the pair has not been tested together.
46. Thymogen: Thymogen is the Glu-Trp dipeptide, one of the named candidate actives inside Thymalin. Using both doubles up on the same Khavinson immune signal from a defined piece and the whole extract. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.
47. Vilon: Vilon is the Lys-Glu dipeptide, another candidate active in Thymalin, claimed to boost thymus and spleen cell growth. People add it to the extract for a fuller immune and longevity course. Both rest on Khavinson group data, and the pair has not been tested together.
48. Thymulin: Thymulin is a zinc-dependent thymic hormone that matures T cells and lowers inflammatory cytokines in models. Thymalin covers broader lymphocyte rebalancing. People pair them to restore thymic signalling from two angles, but the pair has never been tested together.

## Where it comes from
49. Morozov and Khavinson's 1974 extract of calf thymus peptides, the first drug of the Russian 'peptide bioregulator' class.
50. First studied for: Restoring immunity in Soviet patients with immunodeficiency, infection, burns and radiation injury.
51. Name story: Calf-thymus extract isolated by Morozov and Khavinson in 1974 at the Leningrad Military Medical Academy, registered in the USSR in 1982, and the template for the bioregulator line. Not to be confused with thymulin, a single defined nonapeptide. [S2] (verified)
52. 1974: Morozov and Khavinson isolate small peptides from calf thymus
53. 1980s: Enters Soviet clinical use as an immunostimulant (reported)
54. 2002–2003: Khavinson elderly cohorts claim lower mortality with Thymalin and Epitalon
55. 2021: Small Russian COVID-19 trial reports halved hospital deaths
56. June 2024: Russian registration renewed, indefinite validity

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### What is actually in the vial, and why that is the whole problem [lab]
57. Everything else in this library is a defined molecule with a sequence. This is not. It is an acid extract of calf thymus, standardised only by total weight of low-molecular-weight peptides per vial, not by any identified active component (verified as the product's own basis of standardisation).
58. That has three consequences worth stating plainly. There is no sequence to test a batch against, so identity testing in the usual sense cannot be done; the content is a mixture whose composition can shift with the source tissue and the extraction run; and the named candidate actives, the dipeptides Glu-Trp and Lys-Glu, are sold as separate products in their own right, which means the extract's effects cannot be attributed to them without measuring them (inferred).
59. It is also a bovine tissue product given by injection. Animal-derived injectables carry a category of risk that synthetic peptides do not, around viral and transmissible agent clearance, and nothing in the accessible record describes what clearance steps the extraction includes (verified as absent from sources reachable for this review). This is the single biggest gap between how the product is discussed and how any regulator outside its home market would assess it.

### The longevity study, read line by line [people]
60. The claim that this extract extends human life rests on one 2003 paper reporting two cohorts, 266 people in total, run at institutes in St Petersburg and Kiev [1]. It is worth reading closely, because it is cited far more often than it is examined.
61. In the St Petersburg arm, 94 women aged 66 to 94 in a war veterans' home were allocated by what the paper calls stratification randomisation to placebo, Thymalin, Epithalamin, or both, described as double-blind, with 10 mg intramuscularly daily for 10 days, repeated in a second year. Six-year mortality was 81.8% in the control group of 22, against 41.7% on Thymalin, 45.8% on Epithalamin, 33.3% on both, and 20.0% in a separate 20-person group dosed annually for six years [1] (verified as reported).
62. In the Kiev arm, 152 people were allocated to placebo, Thymalin or Epithalamin, with eight-year mortality of 13.6%, 6.6% and 8.5% in groups of 48, 58 and 46 [1] (verified as reported).
63. Now the arithmetic that should stop a reader. The control group in one cohort died at 81.8% over six years; the control group in the other died at 13.6% over eight. Those are not the same kind of population, and no analysis reconciles them. The treatment effect in the first cohort rests on a control arm of 22 people in a care home, where an 82% six-year mortality means a handful of deaths either way moves the percentage by double digits (inferred).
64. What the paper does not report is as important as what it does: no causes of death, no dropouts or loss to follow-up, no confidence intervals, no mapping of the six listed statistical tests to individual results, no group sizes for the 94 women, no placebo composition, no allocation concealment, no blinding description for the Kiev arm, and no funding or conflict statement [1] (verified as absent). Its own abstract and tables disagree about whether the observation ran six or eight years and whether treatment ran two or three [1].
65. None of that makes the result false. It makes it uncheckable, which is a different thing, and it is why this paper has not moved practice outside the research tradition that produced it.

### The COVID-19 trial, which is the best-designed study in the file [people]
66. A 2021 trial at a hospital in Chita, Russia, is genuinely a controlled trial and should be given its due. Eighty adults with PCR-confirmed critical COVID-19 were randomised single-blind to standard care plus 10 mg of Thymalin intramuscularly daily for 10 days, or standard care plus 2 mL of saline on the same schedule [2] (verified).
67. In-hospital mortality was 18 of 44 (40.9%) in the control arm against 7 of 36 (19.4%) on drug, p=0.039, and time from randomisation to death differed, 7.22 against 11.2 days, p=0.005 [2] (verified).
68. That is a real randomised result with a placebo arm, and it is the strongest single piece of evidence this compound has. Its limits are structural rather than suspicious: a single centre, single-blind rather than double, 80 patients total, and a mortality difference that turns on 11 deaths. In critical COVID-19 in 2020 and 2021, single-centre trials of immunomodulators repeatedly produced large effects that larger trials did not reproduce, which is the base rate this result sits inside (inferred).
69. A second 2021 report in a Russian-language clinical journal compared 38 treated patients with 50 controls and described large fold-changes in interleukin-6, lymphocyte and CD4 counts and in antibody levels at day 200. It does not describe randomisation or blinding, does not state the dose, and reports no absolute values or statistical tests in the accessible text [3] (verified as reported, with those omissions).

### Who produced the evidence [mixed]
70. This is the part a reader cannot skip. The extract was isolated in 1974 by Morozov and Khavinson; the longevity paper is by Khavinson and Morozov; the COVID-19 trial lists Khavinson among its authors alongside the institute he heads; and the broader bioregulator theory the results are interpreted through is his [1,2] (verified). The COVID-19 trial declares no conflicts of interest [2], which is formally accurate for salary and equity but sits oddly with an author's five-decade authorship of the compound itself (inferred).
71. No independent group outside that network was found to have replicated either the mortality findings or the proposed mechanism in this review. The claim that these short peptides enter the nucleus and tune gene expression is the group's own proposal and has not been independently confirmed (verified as the state of the record found).
72. That is not an accusation of bad faith. It is the same structural problem BPC-157 has: a founding group dominating the literature on its own discovery, so that reading many papers is not the same as reading many independent assessments.

### What the body does with it, and safety [mixed]
73. There is no pharmacokinetic data: no half-life, no bioavailability, no distribution, in any species (verified as absent). For a mixture rather than a molecule, conventional pharmacokinetics is not even well defined, since there is no single analyte to measure (inferred).
74. On safety, the 2003 paper states that no allergic or other side effects were seen, but that is a general claim about decades of use rather than a reported adverse-event analysis from the trials, and the paper reports no trial-specific adverse events at all [1] (verified). The COVID-19 trial's published account focuses on efficacy and immune markers [2].
75. What is absent: no genotoxicity, no carcinogenicity, no reproductive toxicology, and no long-term controlled safety data accessible in this review. The direction of the intended effect, stimulating lymphocyte populations, raises the obvious questions about autoimmunity and about use in people with lymphoproliferative disease, and neither has been studied (inferred).
76. Decades of use in one health system is a form of evidence, and it is the weakest form, because spontaneous reporting from that era and that market is not a surveillance system anyone can audit (inferred).

### Where the law stands, and open questions [mixed]
77. It is a registered prescription immunostimulant in Russia, with registration renewed in June 2024, and is sold in parts of the former Soviet sphere (verified as stated in the card's label sourcing). It is not approved by the FDA or EMA, has not been nominated for or reviewed under the US compounding lists, and is not named on the 2026 WADA Prohibited List, which leaves it in the catch-all class for substances without approval for human therapeutic use in the relevant jurisdictions, prohibited at all times [4] (inferred from the class wording, complicated by the fact that it does hold approval in one country).
78. An approval in one jurisdiction based on a national dossier is not interchangeable with approval elsewhere, particularly for an animal-derived injectable, where tissue sourcing and viral clearance documentation are the first thing other regulators ask for (inferred).
79. What is the active component? Until that is identified and measured, every mechanistic claim is about a mixture, and no batch can be compared with another.
80. Does the COVID-19 mortality result replicate? It is the one finding here that a multicentre trial could confirm or refute, and nobody has tried.
81. Can the 2003 mortality claim be reconstructed? With no causes of death, no dropouts and two control arms that behaved six-fold differently, probably not from the published record.
82. What is in the extract that is not peptide? For a tissue extract given by injection, this is a manufacturing question with a safety answer, and the accessible record does not contain it.
83. Does stimulating T-cell populations in older people carry a cost? The theory says it restores; nothing tests whether it also drives autoimmunity or affects malignancy risk.
