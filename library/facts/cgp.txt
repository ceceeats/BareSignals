# cGP
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/cgp
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: cyclic glycine-proline, cyclo(Gly-Pro), cyclo-prolylglycine, cycloprolylglycine, CPG
2. Origin: Fragment of a natural protein: cyclised breakdown product of IGF-1
3. Sequence: GP (2 amino acids). Cyclic dipeptide (2,5-diketopiperazine) of glycine and proline; formed from GPE, IGF-1's first three residues
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug. Sold in New Zealand and online as a dietary supplement ingredient and blackcurrant extract. Not named on the WADA list.

## Summary
5. cGP (cyclo-prolylglycine) is a tiny ring of two amino acids the body makes when IGF-1 is broken down. It is also the main active metabolite of Noopept and appears in blackcurrants. It is studied for brain ageing, thought to compete with IGF-1 for its carrier protein and so steady IGF-1 activity. Higher levels link to better memory and stroke recovery in observational data. Whether taking it helps anything is untested.
6. Punch line: A tiny ring the body makes from IGF-1; also Noopept's active metabolite. Found in blackcurrants; tied to memory.
7. Used for: Brain ageing, memory

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Cognition: Higher levels track with better memory in older adults
9. Neural protection: Linked to better stroke recovery
10. Endocrine & growth: Steadies IGF-1 activity

## How it works
11. Competes with IGF-1 for its carrier protein · IGFBP-3 binding · role Endocrine & growth
   cGP binds IGFBP-3, the main carrier protein that holds IGF-1 in blood, at the same site IGF-1 uses. By taking that seat it changes how much IGF-1 is free to reach its receptor. This binding is shown in lab studies.
12. Raises IGF-1 activity when low, damps it when high · IGF-1 normalisation · role Endocrine & growth
   Because it competes for the carrier, cGP acts as a buffer: in vitro it raised IGF-1 activity when IGF-1 was low and inhibited it when IGF-1 was high. In older adults a higher cGP to IGF-1 ratio tracked with better memory, but that is observational, not proof.
13. Boosts fast excitatory signalling · AMPA positive modulation · role Cognition
   cGP positively modulates AMPA receptors, the glutamate receptors that carry fast excitatory signals and underpin learning. Stronger AMPA signalling fits its links to memory. Human memory data are observational, so whether taking cGP improves cognition is untested.
14. Raises BDNF · TrkB dependent neuroprotection · role Neural protection
   cGP raises BDNF, which acts through TrkB receptors to keep neurons alive and adaptable. This fits the observational finding that higher plasma cGP went with better stroke recovery in 34 patients. The neuroprotective mechanism comes from lab work, not supplement trials.
15. Mechanism overview: IGF-1 is cleaved to des-N-(1-3)-IGF-1 and the tripeptide glycine-proline-glutamate (GPE), which is further processed to cyclo(Gly-Pro). cGP binds IGFBP-3 at the site IGF-1 uses, so it shifts how much IGF-1 is free: in vitro it raises IGF-1 activity when IGF-1 is low and inhibits it when IGF-1 is high. It also positively modulates AMPA receptors and increases BDNF. The trofinetide and NNZ-2591 drug programmes grew out of the same GPE and cGP chemistry.

## What people report
20. A niche but growing brain health supplement, sold as standardised cGP or blackcurrant extract, mainly to people over 50 and to Noopept users who want the metabolite directly. Reports are sparse and mostly subtle: clearer thinking for some, nothing noticed for many. No consistent side effect pattern reported. No aggregator counts located.
21. Liked / worked: Blackcurrant raises cGP in spinal fluid
22. Liked / worked: Higher levels linked to better stroke recovery and memory
23. What worked: Blackcurrant supplementation raises cGP in blood and spinal fluid; cGP crosses into the brain. Animal and cell work shows it steadies IGF-1 activity, raising it when low and damping it when high.
24. Went wrong / did not work: No outcome trial of cGP itself
25. Went wrong / did not work: Human data mostly associations
26. What went wrong: No trial shows that taking cGP improves memory or any outcome in people. Most human data are associations.

## What has been tested
30. Neural protection: Stroke recovery, 34 patients, observational (human)
31. Cognition: Memory in older adults, observational (human)
32. Neural protection: Blackcurrant in Parkinson's, 11 patients (human)
33. Human data are observational plus one supplement study. In 34 stroke patients, higher plasma cGP went with better recovery at three months. In older adults, higher cGP and cGP to IGF-1 ratio tracked with better memory. In 11 Parkinson's patients, blackcurrant anthocyanin capsules (300 mg twice daily for 28 days) raised spinal fluid cGP by 74%.
34. What it helps: Observational human data link higher cGP to better stroke recovery and memory in older age, and blackcurrant anthocyanins raise cGP in blood and spinal fluid. Whether supplementing it improves any outcome is untested. Users report subtle effects at most.
35. Trial: none. No registered trial of cGP itself confirmed; past studies used blackcurrant extract to raise it. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1]

## Synergies (paired with)
36. IGF-1 LR3: IGF-1 LR3 drives growth directly and is built to escape carrier proteins, while cGP works on the carrier side, buffering how much natural IGF-1 is free. Since LR3 barely binds IGFBP-3, cGP likely does little to it, and the pair has not been tested together.
37. Somatropin (HGH): Somatropin raises liver IGF-1, and cGP is proposed to buffer IGF-1 availability by competing for IGFBP-3. People pair them to keep a raised IGF-1 axis in range, which is user logic, not trial data.
38. MK-677: MK-677 lifts GH and IGF-1 through the ghrelin receptor and deepens sleep, while cGP is proposed to normalise IGF-1 activity and supports AMPA and BDNF signalling. The pairing aims at the IGF-1 axis plus brain ageing, but has not been tested together.
39. Semax: Both raise BDNF, cGP through TrkB dependent signalling and Semax as reported in rodents, and both have links to stroke recovery. People stack them for brain ageing and memory, but the pair has not been tested together.
40. Dihexa: Dihexa boosts HGF and c-Met signalling that builds synapses in rats, while cGP modulates AMPA receptors, BDNF and IGF-1 availability. Users pair them for memory from separate growth factor routes, and the pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
41. A tiny ring of two amino acids that the body makes when IGF-1 is broken down: IGF-1 sheds the tripeptide GPE, which closes into cGP. It is also the main active metabolite of Noopept, and is found in blackcurrants and some cooked or fermented foods.
42. First studied for: Studied in New Zealand (Jian Guan's group) as a natural regulator of IGF-1 in the ageing brain, stroke and Parkinson's disease.
43. Name story: cGP is short for cyclic glycine-proline: two amino acids joined head to tail into a ring. [S2] (verified)
44. 1990s: Russian researchers describe cGP as a brain peptide; later shown to be Noopept's main metabolite
45. 2010s: Guan's group links cGP to IGF-1 regulation, ageing and stroke
46. 2020: Blackcurrant capsules raise spinal fluid cGP 74% in Parkinson's patients
47. 2023: Review: cGP normalises IGF-1 function
48. Today: Sold as a brain health supplement; no outcome trial
