# Amycretin
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/amycretin
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Zenagamtide, NN9487, NNC0487-0111, oral amycretin, GLP-1/amylin unimolecular
2. Origin: Lab-designed: GLP-1 and amylin analogues in one chain
3. Legal and approval status: Investigational, not approved anywhere as of Oct 2026. Phase 3: AMAZE 1 (weekly injection, obesity) recruiting since Feb 2026; AMAZE 2 (obesity with T2D) and HF-POLARIS (heart failure) cleared in Korea Feb 2026; T2D phase 3 planned 2H 2026. Not named on the WADA list.

## Summary
4. Amycretin is Novo Nordisk's single molecule that activates both GLP-1 and amylin receptors, built as one long acylated peptide rather than two drugs in one pen. It is being developed for obesity and type 2 diabetes by suppressing hunger and slowing stomach emptying. Early trials showed 22 to 24% weight loss at 36 weeks by weekly injection, with heavy nausea and vomiting. Community use is near zero.
5. Punch line: Novo's GLP-1 and amylin in one molecule, weekly shot or pill. Early trials reached 24% loss; heavy nausea.
6. Used for: Obesity, type 2 diabetes

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
7. Appetite & satiety: Deep hunger suppression, two pathways
8. Glucose & insulin: Lowers HbA1c in diabetes
9. Fat metabolism: Up to 24% weight loss
10. Digestive & gut: Slows stomach emptying, nausea
11. Heart & vessels (backed only by animal or user reports): Heart failure outcome trial running
12. Reward & urges (backed only by animal or user reports): Amylin may calm food reward

## How it works
13. Quiets hunger in the brain · GLP-1 receptor, hypothalamus/brainstem · role Appetite & satiety
   The GLP-1 segment of amycretin activates GLP-1 receptors in the hypothalamus and brainstem, the brain's hunger centres. That cuts appetite and food intake. Weight loss is shown in early human trials by injection and by mouth.
14. Adds a second fullness signal · Amylin (CTR/RAMP) receptor, area postrema · role Appetite & satiety
   The amylin segment activates calcitonin receptor and RAMP complexes, the amylin receptors, in the area postrema of the hindbrain. This adds a fullness signal separate from GLP-1, so the two together cut intake further. Shown in early human trials.
15. Slows stomach emptying · GLP-1R and amylin receptor · role Digestive & gut
   Both GLP-1 and amylin receptors slow how fast the stomach empties. Food stays longer, so people feel full and eat less. This also drives the nausea and vomiting seen early in human trials.
16. Raises insulin, cuts glucagon · GLP-1R beta/alpha cells; amylin glucagon suppression · role Glucose & insulin
   The GLP-1 part raises insulin and cuts glucagon when glucose is high, and the amylin part also holds glucagon back after meals. Together they lower blood sugar. A phase 2 human trial in type 2 diabetes showed HbA1c falling.
17. Fatty tail rides albumin · C18 diacid, albumin binding · role Fat metabolism
   A C18 fatty acid chain on amycretin binds albumin, the main blood protein. That slows clearance and protects it from breakdown, so a single injection lasts about a week. The oral form uses the SNAC absorption enhancer, as in oral semaglutide.
18. Mechanism overview: Amycretin is a single acylated peptide carrying a GLP-1 receptor agonist segment and an amylin receptor agonist segment joined by a short linker. The GLP-1 part acts in the hypothalamus and brainstem to cut hunger, slows gastric emptying and raises insulin while cutting glucagon when glucose is high. The amylin part acts on calcitonin receptor/RAMP complexes in the area postrema to add a separate satiety and glucagon-suppressing signal. A C18 fatty diacid binds albumin so it lasts a week by injection; the oral form uses the SNAC absorption enhancer, as in Rybelsus.

## What has been tested
39. Fat metabolism: Obesity, Sub-Q phase 1b/2a and oral phase 1 (human)
40. Glucose & insulin: Type 2 diabetes, phase 2 (human)
41. Heart & vessels: Heart failure outcomes, HF-POLARIS (ongoing) (human)
42. Phase 1b/2a injectable (125 people, Lancet 2025), phase 1 oral (144 people, Lancet 2025) and phase 2 in type 2 diabetes (448 people); phase 3 AMAZE began in 2026.
43. What it helps: Early trials put it near the top of the obesity field: 22–24% weight loss at 36 weeks by weekly injection and 13% in 12 weeks as a daily pill. In type 2 diabetes it cut HbA1c up to 1.8 points with up to 14.5% weight loss. The cost was heavy nausea and vomiting early on. Phase 3 is now testing obesity, diabetes and heart failure; community use is near zero.
44. Trial: active · Phase 3 · obesity and type 2 diabetes · Novo Nordisk. Novo moved both the shot and the pill into Phase 3 in 2026 under the new name zenagamtide. (checked 2026-10-23) [S4] (reported)

## Who shouldn't take it
45. Personal or family history of MTC or MEN2: Causes thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents; boxed warning and contraindication on the label. (inferred)
46. Anyone booked for surgery, endoscopy or sedation: Food can stay in the stomach for days and be inhaled into the lungs under anaesthesia. (inferred)
47. Insulin or sulfonylurea users: Amylin plus GLP-1 action stacks low sugar risk. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
48. Semaglutide: Semaglutide acts only on the GLP-1 receptor, which amycretin already covers. Stacking them doubles up GLP-1 action and side effects rather than adding a route. The pair has not been tested together.
49. Cagrilintide: Cagrilintide is a long-acting amylin drug, the same route as amycretin's amylin segment. Adding it doubles up amylin action rather than adding a new one. Amycretin already joins both signals in one molecule, and this stack has not been tested.

## Where it comes from
50. Novo Nordisk's first single molecule that hits both the GLP-1 and amylin receptors, built as one long acylated peptide instead of two drugs in one pen (as in CagriSema).
51. First studied for: Obesity, then type 2 diabetes; one weekly injection and one daily pill of the same molecule.
52. Name story: "Amycretin" blends amylin and incretin, the two hormone families it mimics. The formal INN zenagamtide carries the generic -tide peptide ending and appears in Novo's 2026 releases. [S5] (inferred)
53. Mar 2024: Oral phase 1 results announced
54. Jan 2025: Injectable phase 1b/2a topline: up to 22% loss
55. Jun 2025: Lancet papers; Novo commits both forms to phase 3
56. Nov 2025: Phase 2 in T2D: HbA1c down up to 1.8 points
57. Feb 2026: AMAZE 1 starts; INN zenagamtide in use by mid-2026
58. Today: Phase 3; high early GI rates and dropouts are the watch item

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### One molecule rather than two drugs, and why that is the engineering point [mixed]
59. The competing approach to combining a GLP-1 drug with an amylin drug is to put two separate peptides in one pen. This one instead covalently links a GLP-1 analogue and an amylin analogue into a single molecule, codenamed NN9487 and later given the name zenagamtide [1] (verified).
60. That has practical consequences, which rarely get spelled out. A single molecule has one pharmacokinetic profile instead of two, so the two signals cannot drift apart between doses, and the ratio between them is fixed by chemistry rather than by formulation. It also means the ratio cannot be adjusted if the balance turns out wrong, and that any immunogenicity or impurity question applies to one new chemical entity rather than to two known ones (inferred).
61. The oral form uses salcaprozate sodium, the same absorption enhancer as oral semaglutide, which is why the tablet dose runs in tens of milligrams while the weekly injection runs in single-digit to tens of milligrams [1] (verified). Oral peptide delivery of this kind is inefficient by design: most of the dose does not get absorbed, which is the reason for the gap.

### The human record, and how to read the big weight numbers [people]
62. Injection, phase 1b/2a: 125 people randomised, 101 to drug and 24 to placebo, aged 18 to 55 with BMI in the overweight to mild obesity range, in five parts with single then multiple ascending weekly doses up to 60 mg [2,3] (verified). Weight change against placebo was 24.3% versus 1.1% at 36 weeks on 60 mg, 22.0% versus a 1.9% gain at 36 weeks on 20 mg, 16.2% versus a 2.3% gain at 28 weeks on 5 mg, and 9.7% versus a 2.0% gain at 20 weeks on 1.25 mg [3] (verified).
63. Three things temper those figures. The arms were small and of unequal length, so the headline 24% comes from one part of a dose-finding study rather than a powered comparison. One third of participants withdrew, and the investigators noted that 59% of withdrawals were for reasons unrelated to adverse events, including withdrawn consent and recreational drug use [2] (verified). And the participants were young, with BMI largely in the 25 to 35 range, not the population a phase 3 obesity trial enrols (inferred).
64. Tablet, first-in-human phase 1: 144 people, up to 12 weeks, highest dose two 50 mg tablets daily. Weight fell 13% against 1% on placebo at the top dose, with no sign of plateau [2] (verified).
65. Type 2 diabetes, phase 2: 448 adults on metformin, about 40% also on an SGLT2 inhibitor, randomised to six weekly injected doses from 0.4 to 40 mg, three daily oral doses of 6, 25 or 50 mg, or placebo, for up to 36 weeks. Injected: HbA1c down up to 1.8 points from a baseline of 7.8%, with 89.1% reaching under 7% and 76.2% under 6.5%, and weight down up to 14.5% from 99.2 kg. Oral: HbA1c down up to 1.5 points from 8%, 77.6% under 7%, weight down up to 10.1%. Placebo moved HbA1c 0.2 to 0.4 points and weight about 2.5% [4] (reported, from the sponsor's topline release rather than a paper).
66. Across all of it, the recurring observation is the absence of a plateau at 36 weeks [2,3,4]. That is genuinely unusual for this class and is the strongest argument that the two mechanisms are doing different work rather than overlapping.

### Safety: what the early trials showed, and the honest caveat about rates [people]
67. Oral phase 1: treatment-emergent adverse events in 62% of participants, 364 events in total, of which 180 (49%) were gastrointestinal, mostly nausea, vomiting and reduced appetite. All were mild or moderate, frequency rose with dose, and there were no deaths [2] (verified).
68. Injected phase 1b/2a: in parts B to E, 80 of 83 people on drug reported at least one adverse event (496 events), as did 18 of 18 on placebo (72 events) [2] (verified). The near-universal rate in both arms is why those percentages say little; the investigators themselves attributed part of the gap in event counts to a nocebo effect in a small sample [2]. Gastrointestinal events dominated, were mostly mild to moderate and had resolved by study end.
69. The investigators raised a point worth keeping: rapid weight loss may not deliver the same benefits as gradual loss, and the optimal rate has not been established [2] (verified as their stated caveat). An accompanying editorial argued that the field's question is now cardiovascular and comorbidity risk, and called for head-to-head outcome trials against GLP-1 drugs with proven cardiovascular benefit before judging what these combinations add [2] (verified).
70. What has not been studied: no trial longer than 36 weeks has reported, no cardiovascular outcome trial has read out, and nothing has been published on lean mass, bone or longer-term amylin receptor effects for this molecule. With roughly 700 people treated across all published trials, rare events remain invisible (inferred).

### Who produced the evidence [people]
71. Every study is the developer's own. The two phase 1 trials were peer-reviewed in The Lancet in June 2025 [2]; the diabetes phase 2 result is, as of October 2026, a company release rather than a paper [4]. No independent trial exists.

### Where the law stands, October 2026 [people]
72. Investigational everywhere, not approved in any country. The developer announced in June 2025 that both the weekly injection and the daily tablet would go to phase 3 in weight management, after end-of-phase-2 regulatory feedback, with the programme due to start in the first quarter of 2026 [5] (verified). A phase 3 programme in type 2 diabetes followed the November 2025 phase 2 result [4] (reported).
73. In sport, GLP-1 and amylin receptor agonists are not named on the WADA Prohibited List, but an unapproved investigational substance falls under the S0 class, prohibited at all times (inferred from the class definition).

### Open questions [mixed]
74. Is a fused single molecule better than two peptides in one pen? Nothing has compared them head to head, and this is the central bet of the design.
75. Does the absence of a plateau hold past 36 weeks, and at what cost to lean mass? Phase 3 should answer the first; nobody has published on the second for this molecule.
76. What do the tolerability numbers mean when placebo groups report events at the same rate? Larger trials with proper titration schedules are the only way to separate drug effect from expectation.
77. Does it reduce cardiovascular events? The editorial accompanying the phase 1 papers made this the test that matters, and no outcome trial has reported.
78. How much of the oral dose is actually absorbed, and how variable is that between people? Standard for SNAC-based tablets, and not published for this one.
