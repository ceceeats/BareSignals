# Kisspeptin-10
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/kisspeptin-10
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: KP-10, metastin (45-54), kisspeptin-54 (45-54)
2. Origin: Fragment of a natural protein
3. Sequence: YNWNSFGLRF (10 amino acids). Amide end
4. Legal and approval status: Investigational; no approval. Research chemical on the grey market.

## Summary
5. Kisspeptin-10 is a 10-amino-acid fragment of kisspeptin, the upstream switch of the reproductive hormone axis that acts on the brain to trigger GnRH release. It is a research tool and potential treatment for infertility, and users take it for libido and a hormonal reset. Human studies show reliable LH and testosterone rises, but the libido data are early, the half-life is very short, and the user base is small.
6. Punch line: Fragment of the reproductive axis's master switch. Starts the chain at the brain, so LH and testosterone follow.
7. Used for: Testosterone, LH, libido

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Endocrine & growth: Master switch for reproductive hormones
9. Reproductive: Raises LH and testosterone
10. Connection & desire: More desire in brain studies

## How it works
11. Tells the brain to release GnRH · KISS1R, formerly GPR54 · role Endocrine & growth
   Kisspeptin-10 binds KISS1R, formerly called GPR54, on GnRH neurons in the hypothalamus. This releases GnRH, the master signal at the top of the reproductive axis. Human studies of this exact fragment show the downstream hormone response.
12. Raises LH and testosterone downstream · HPG axis · role Reproductive
   GnRH tells the pituitary to release LH and FSH, and LH drives testosterone in men and ovulation in women. In healthy men a single dose raised LH, but only sustained infusion raised testosterone. Larger doses gave a smaller LH response, not a bigger one.
13. Kisspeptin can trigger egg maturation in IVF · LH surge, oocyte maturation · role Reproductive
   Kisspeptin can set off the LH surge that matures eggs, so it has been used as an egg-maturation trigger in IVF trials. Those trials mostly used the longer kisspeptin-54, which reaches the target neurons more readily, so the result does not transfer directly to this fragment.
14. Kisspeptin boosts brain sexual arousal responses · Limbic activity, fMRI · role Connection & desire
   On brain imaging, kisspeptin increased activity in limbic regions that handle sexual and emotional processing when people viewed arousing images. These studies mostly used kisspeptin-54. For kisspeptin-10, the libido effect is untested in people.
15. Constant exposure shuts the axis down · KISS1R desensitisation · role Endocrine & growth
   Constant strong stimulation makes KISS1R internalise and stop responding, which can shut the reproductive axis down. In healthy men about a day of infusion did not cause this, while primates desensitised at higher exposure. Weeks of use have never been tested.
16. Mechanism overview: Kisspeptin-10 is the shortest active fragment of kisspeptin. It binds KISS1R, formerly GPR54, on hypothalamic GnRH neurons. This drives GnRH release, which triggers pituitary LH and FSH; LH raises testosterone in men and supports ovulation in women. Because it acts at the top of the reproductive axis, the response stays physiological. Constant exposure desensitises KISS1R and can shut the axis down. On brain imaging, kisspeptin also activates limbic regions involved in sexual and emotional processing.

## Who shouldn't take it
40. Hormone-sensitive cancer (breast, prostate): Triggers an LH and sex hormone surge. (inferred)
41. People tracking ovulation with LH test strips: It releases LH, which can produce false positive ovulation tests. (inferred)
42. Drug-tested athletes (men): Kisspeptin is named on the WADA list as an LH-releasing factor. (verified)

## Where it comes from
43. A 10-amino-acid fragment of kisspeptin, the master upstream switch of the reproductive hormone axis, acting on the brain to trigger GnRH release.

## Deep read (as of 2026-10)

### The human study of this exact fragment, which is better than the field usually gets [people]
49. Unlike most compounds in this catalog, the ten-residue fragment itself has been given to humans in a properly conducted study, with hormone measurements rather than self-report. A 2011 study in a major endocrine journal tested three protocols in healthy men: an intravenous bolus dose-response in 6 participants, a high-dose infusion in 4, and a low-dose infusion in 4 [1] (verified).
50. Bolus doses spanned 0.01 to 3.0 micrograms per kilogram. Luteinising hormone rose dose-dependently and peaked at 1 microgram per kilogram, from 4.1 to 12.4 international units per litre at 30 minutes [1]. Testosterone did not change significantly after any bolus dose [1] (verified).
51. The infusions did raise testosterone. The high-dose infusion, 4 micrograms per kilogram per hour for 22.5 hours, raised luteinising hormone from about 5.5 to about 21 units per litre and testosterone from 16.6 to 24.0 nanomoles per litre [1]. The lower infusion, 1.5 micrograms per kilogram per hour for 9 hours, raised luteinising hormone from 5.2 to 14.1 units per litre and increased pulse frequency from 0.7 to 1.0 pulses per hour, with pulse mass rising from 3.9 to 12.8 [1] (verified).
52. The pattern is informative. A single injection moves the pituitary hormone and does not move testosterone; sustained exposure moves both. Testosterone is downstream of accumulated pulses, so a brief spike in the signal above it does not translate (inferred, consistent with the measured data).
53. No adverse effects were reported, and blood pressure, heart rate, oxygen saturation and blood, kidney and liver tests remained stable [1] (verified).

### The dose-response is not monotonic, and that is the most useful finding [people]
54. At 3 micrograms per kilogram, the highest bolus tested, the luteinising hormone response was not significantly different from vehicle, and was lower than the responses at 0.3 and 1 micrograms per kilogram [1] (verified).
55. So more produced less. The peak response sat in the middle of the tested range and fell away above it. For a compound bought and used on the assumption that a larger amount gives a larger hormonal push, this is the single most practically relevant result in the human literature, and it points the opposite way.
56. Receptor-level explanations are available, chiefly that this receptor family internalises and desensitises on strong stimulation, but the study did not establish the cause (inferred).
57. It also complicates the common framing that this compound works upstream so the response stays physiological and self-limiting. The response is indeed self-limiting. One consequence of that is that overshooting the dose does not produce a bigger effect, it produces a smaller one, which is a different and less comfortable kind of self-limitation (inferred).

### Desensitisation: what the human data showed, and where the worry comes from [mixed]
58. The concern that continuous exposure shuts the axis down is reasonable from receptor biology and is widely repeated. The human data on it is more specific and more reassuring than the general claim.
59. Across the 22.5-hour high-dose infusion, no tachyphylaxis was observed. Luteinising hormone in the final 90 minutes, 23.9 units per litre, was not lower than in the first 90 minutes, 16.3 units per litre [1] (verified). The authors noted that primate studies had shown tachyphylaxis and suggested the difference was dose: the primate work used higher amounts [1].
60. So the accurate statement is that sustained exposure for roughly a day at these amounts did not desensitise the axis in healthy men, while higher exposures in primates did. Both the worry and the reassurance are dose-dependent, and nobody has tested exposures running weeks (verified as absent).
61. That bounds the question usefully. The risk is not that any continuous exposure collapses the axis; it is that enough exposure does, and the threshold in humans is unmapped.

### Why the longer form is used in research and the shorter one is sold [animals]
62. Most of the headline human work on this hormone family, the ovulation trigger in fertility treatment and the brain-imaging studies of sexual and emotional processing, used the longer 54-residue form rather than this fragment. There is a specific pharmacological reason, and a 2017 mouse study examined it directly [2].
63. Circulating half-life is about 32 minutes for the longer form against about 4 minutes for this fragment [2] (verified). But clearance was not the whole explanation: repeating fragment injections every 10 minutes for an hour did not reproduce the sustained hormone rise the longer form produced [2].
64. The proposed difference is access. After systemic injection in mice, only the longer form activated the immediate-early gene marker in the hormone-releasing neurons sitting behind the blood-brain barrier, and the authors suggest the longer form crosses while the fragment is less able to [2] (verified, in mice).
65. If that holds in humans, it has a sharp consequence. The target neurons sit behind a barrier, and the fragment may be reaching them poorly or reaching only the subset in regions where the barrier is incomplete. That would explain why the fragment needs sustained infusion to move testosterone while the longer form works more readily, and it means the fertility and desire findings from the longer form should not be transferred to this one (inferred).
66. It also means the very short half-life is not merely an inconvenience to be solved with more frequent dosing. The mouse data specifically tested that workaround and it did not work [2].

### Open questions [mixed]
67. Does this fragment reach the target neurons in humans? The mouse comparison suggests the longer form crosses into the relevant region and this one may not, which would undercut the whole rationale for using the shorter peptide.
68. What dose is optimal, given that the response peaked mid-range and fell away above it? Nobody has mapped the top of the curve in humans, and the one study that looked found more giving less.
69. Does intermittent subcutaneous use do anything to testosterone? The human study that moved testosterone used continuous intravenous infusion for hours. A single injection moved the pituitary hormone and not testosterone.
70. Where is the desensitisation threshold in humans? None appeared over 22.5 hours at the amounts tested; primates desensitised at higher amounts; nothing has been tested over weeks.
71. Do the desire and fertility findings transfer from the longer form? They were obtained with a different molecule that appears to have different access to the brain.
72. What happens with repeated courses? There is no human data beyond about a day of continuous exposure, for any endpoint.
73. Is a short-lived peptide requiring sustained exposure a sensible format at all? The pharmacology suggests the longer form or a stabilised analogue is the better tool, which is what research programmes have mostly used.
