# GHK (no Cu)
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ghk-no-cu
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: ghk copper free, glycyl-L-histidyl-L-lysine, Gly-His-Lys, tripeptide-1 (INCI)
2. Origin: Fragment of a natural protein
3. Sequence: GHK (3 amino acids). Same chain as GHK-Cu, without the copper
4. Legal and approval status: Cosmetic-ingredient status (as copper tripeptide's parent); not an approved drug.

## Summary
5. GHK (no Cu) is the glycyl-histidyl-lysine tripeptide of GHK-Cu without the copper, so it is clear instead of blue. It is used topically or by injection for skin repair and collagen, and it is thought to pick up copper from the body and boost collagen building. Most of its evidence is borrowed from GHK-Cu, and the copper-free form has few trials of its own.
6. Punch line: Copper-free GHK, clear instead of blue. Thought to borrow the body's copper for collagen; topical or injected.
7. Used for: Skin and general repair

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Skin & aesthetics: Collagen support

10. Tissue repair: Wound healing
11. Cognition: Studied for the brain

13. Inflammatory signaling (backed only by animal or user reports): Calms inflammation

## How it works
15. Picks up the body's own copper · Copper chelation · role Tissue repair
   GHK has a strong grip on copper ions, so the copper-free form binds copper already circulating in the body and carries it into cells. That copper feeds copper-dependent enzymes such as lysyl oxidase and superoxide dismutase. Most of this evidence comes from GHK-Cu research.
16. Boosts collagen building · Fibroblast signalling · role Skin & aesthetics
   GHK signals fibroblasts, the cells that make skin's structural proteins, to build more collagen. Cell and cosmetic studies of GHK-Cu show collagen and firmness gains. Data for the copper-free form on its own are sparse.
17. Claimed to reset repair genes · Gene-expression data · role Organ function
   Gene-expression studies claim GHK shifts large sets of genes toward repair and away from damage patterns. These come from gene-expression data in cells, not from outcome studies. Whether it resets genes in the body is unproven.
18. Calms inflammatory signals · IL-6, TNF-alpha down · role Inflammatory signaling
   GHK lowers IL-6 and TNF-alpha, two signalling proteins that drive inflammation. Less of these signals gives tissue a calmer setting to repair in. This is shown in cell studies, largely with GHK-Cu.
19. Curbs oxidative damage · Antioxidant genes · role Cellular protection
   By supplying copper to superoxide dismutase and turning up antioxidant genes, GHK is said to curb oxidative damage, the harm done by reactive oxygen molecules. This rests on cell and gene-expression work. There is no human proof for the copper-free form.
20. Claimed to protect nerve cells · Gene-expression data · role Neural protection
   The same gene-expression data claim GHK turns up genes linked to nerve cell survival and growth. That points to a protective role for nerve cells. It is a claim from cell data, not shown in people.
21. Mechanism overview: GHK is a tripeptide found in plasma, saliva and urine that declines with age. Its core trick is chelating copper and ferrying it into cells, so even the copper-free form picks up the body's own copper. That supply feeds copper-dependent enzymes like lysyl oxidase and superoxide dismutase, and GHK signals fibroblasts to build collagen. It lowers IL-6 and TNF-alpha. Gene-expression studies claim it resets large sets of repair and antioxidant genes and protects nerve cells.

## What has been tested
32. Skin & aesthetics: Collagen and repair, via GHK-Cu data (cell)
33. Skin & aesthetics: Topical cosmetic use (users)
34. Shares GHK-Cu's mechanism literature (collagen, gene-expression 'resetting'); copper-free topical use is common in cosmetics. Human trials specific to the copper-free form are sparse.
35. What it helps: Most evidence is borrowed from GHK-Cu, which has cell and cosmetic data on collagen, firmness and repair. The copper-free form has few trials of its own. Users apply it topically or inject it, reporting skin firmness and general repair similar to GHK-Cu.
36. Trial: none. No trial of copper free GHK found, though its copper bound form GHK-Cu is in a recruiting Phase 2 wound healing gel trial (NCT07437586). (checked 2026-10-23) [S1] (inferred)

## Who shouldn't take it
37. People with low copper (post-bariatric, high zinc): Copper-free GHK binds the body’s own copper. (inferred)

## Where it comes from
38. The same glycyl-histidyl-lysine tripeptide as GHK-Cu but without the copper, so clear instead of blue. In the body it picks up copper on its own.
39. First studied for: A skin-regenerating and wound-healing signal; the copper-free form for formulations where added copper is unwanted.
40. Name story: GHK is one-letter code for Gly-His-Lys. Loren Pickart pulled it from human plasma in 1973 after noticing young people's blood made old liver cells behave younger. The copper-bound form gets the fame; this is the bare peptide. [S2] (verified)
41. 1970s: GHK found in human plasma; copper complex studied
42. Today: Copper-free cosmetic ingredient; not an approved drug
