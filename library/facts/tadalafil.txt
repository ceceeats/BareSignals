# Tadalafil
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/tadalafil
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: Cialis, Adcirca, Alyq, Tadliq, Entadfi (with finasteride), IC351, LY450190, daily Cialis
2. Origin: Not a peptide: PDE5 inhibitor
3. Shown with an FDA approved badge
4. Legal and approval status: FDA approved (Cialis 2003; once daily ED 2008; BPH 2011; Adcirca for pulmonary arterial hypertension; Entadfi with finasteride 2021). Generic worldwide. Daily use for pumps, performance or longevity is off label. Not on the WADA Prohibited List.

## Summary
5. Tadalafil is a small-molecule drug from ICOS and Eli Lilly, not a peptide, that blocks the enzyme PDE5 and so keeps nitric oxide signalling on in blood vessel walls. It is approved for erectile dysfunction, enlarged prostate symptoms and pulmonary arterial hypertension, and lifters use it for fuller pumps. The gym claims rest on user reports, and the heart benefit is observational. It is dangerous with nitrates.
6. Punch line: Daily Cialis, not a peptide. The most stacked prescription in peptide circles, for blood flow, pumps and erections.
7. Used for: Blood flow, pump, BPH, erections

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Heart & vessels: Widens blood vessels via nitric oxide
9. Reproductive: Treats erectile dysfunction
10. Organ function: Eases urinary symptoms of an enlarged prostate
11. Muscle & performance: Fuller pumps reported by lifters

## How it works
12. Keeps the blood vessel relaxing signal switched on · PDE5 inhibition, cGMP up · role Heart & vessels
   Tadalafil blocks phosphodiesterase type 5, or PDE5, the enzyme that breaks down cGMP in smooth muscle. Nitric oxide from the vessel lining raises cGMP and relaxes the muscle, so with PDE5 blocked the relaxation lasts longer. Its long half-life keeps a steady effect on daily low dosing.
13. Lets penile blood vessels fill · Corpus cavernosum smooth muscle relaxation · role Reproductive
   In the erectile tissue, nitric oxide released during arousal relaxes the smooth muscle and lets the vessels fill. Tadalafil prolongs that signal, so it still needs arousal to work. This is proven in large human trials, on demand and daily.
14. Relaxes prostate and bladder neck muscle · PDE5 in lower urinary tract · role Organ function
   PDE5 is also present in the prostate and bladder neck, and blocking it relaxes that muscle. Urine flows more easily. Large human trials showed improved urinary symptoms in men with BPH.
15. Lowers lung artery pressure at high dose · Pulmonary vasodilation · role Heart & vessels
   At higher doses it relaxes the smooth muscle of the lung arteries, lowering their pressure. This is the basis of its approval for pulmonary arterial hypertension in human trials. It works the same way as in other vessels, through cGMP.
16. Mechanism overview: Tadalafil blocks phosphodiesterase type 5, the enzyme that breaks down cGMP in smooth muscle. Nitric oxide from the vessel lining raises cGMP and relaxes the muscle; with PDE5 blocked, that relaxation lasts longer wherever nitric oxide is being released. That is why it needs arousal to work for erections, why it relaxes prostate and bladder neck muscle, and why it lowers pressure in the lung arteries. Its half life of about 17.5 hours is far longer than sildenafil's, so a small daily dose keeps a steady level. Some PDE11 cross reactivity explains the back and muscle ache.

## Dosage (community reported unless tagged)
17. Typical: 2.5–5 mg · once daily · cycle Continuous · route Oral (reported)
18. Half-life: ~17.5 hours (label)
19. Most people take: Off label users take 2.5 to 5 mg once a day, every day. Label doses: 5 to 20 mg before sex for ED, 2.5 to 5 mg daily for ED, 5 mg daily for BPH, 40 mg daily for pulmonary hypertension.
20. Taken continuously rather than cycled; many start at 2.5 mg for two to four weeks to let headaches settle. Never combined with nitrates or other PDE5 inhibitors.

## What people report
21. The most stacked prescription drug in peptide and enhanced lifting circles: 2.5 to 5 mg every day for blood flow, fuller pumps, morning erections and easier urination, often next to TRT, PT-141 or citrulline. Users describe pumps and vascularity as noticeable but modest. Headache and flushing in the first one to two weeks, nasal congestion and lower back or muscle ache are the usual complaints, and some drop to 2.5 mg for that reason. No aggregator counts located.
22. Liked / worked: Approved for ED, BPH and pulmonary hypertension
23. Liked / worked: Daily 5 mg improves vessel lining markers
24. Liked / worked: Observational link to fewer heart events
25. What worked: Reliable for erections and for BPH urinary symptoms. Daily dosing improves vessel lining markers in men with ED or cardiovascular risk. Observational studies tie PDE5 inhibitor use to fewer cardiovascular events and deaths, with a meta analysis estimating about 22% fewer major events.
26. Went wrong / did not work: Heart and longevity data are observational
27. Went wrong / did not work: No trials of pumps or strength in healthy lifters
28. Went wrong / did not work: Headache, flushing, back ache common
29. What went wrong: The heart and longevity signal is observational and may reflect healthier men getting prescriptions. Gym claims of bigger pumps and better performance rest on user reports, not trials in healthy lifters. Headache, flushing, back pain and indigestion are common; it can drop blood pressure dangerously with nitrates.
30. Most reported: Daily 2.5 to 5 mg for blood flow and pumps

## What has been tested
34. Reproductive: Erectile dysfunction, on demand and daily (trial)
35. Organ function: BPH urinary symptoms (trial)
36. Heart & vessels: Pulmonary arterial hypertension (trial)
37. Heart & vessels: Endothelial markers, daily 5 mg vs 20 mg on demand (human)
38. Heart & vessels: Cardiovascular events in men with ED, observational (human)
39. Large randomized trials in ED, BPH and pulmonary hypertension, including once daily 2.5 to 5 mg against placebo. Endothelial work is small: a 60 man study (Aversa 2021) found 5 mg daily beat 20 mg on demand for nitric oxide, endothelin 1 and flow mediated dilation. Heart and survival links come from observational data, such as a US insurance study of over 29,000 men with ED (Kloner 2024).
40. What it helps: Proven for erectile dysfunction, BPH urinary symptoms and pulmonary arterial hypertension. Daily low dosing improves endothelial markers in men with ED or cardiovascular risk, and large observational studies link PDE5 inhibitor use to fewer cardiovascular events and deaths. The gym uses, bigger pumps and vascularity, rest on user reports.
41. Trial: active · Phase 2. A Dutch trial (TAD-PD, EU CT 2025-521819-39-00) is testing daily tadalafil for motor symptoms in early Parkinson's disease, started March 2026. (checked 2026-10-23) [S1]

## Synergies (paired with)
42. PT-141: PT-141 raises desire through melanocortin receptors in the brain, while tadalafil handles blood flow. People pair them to cover desire and erection from two ends. Both can affect blood pressure, and the pair has not been tested together.
43. Kisspeptin-10: Kisspeptin-10 works upstream on the hormone axis, raising LH and testosterone, while tadalafil works on blood flow. The pairing targets libido and function from different angles. It is user logic, not trial data.
44. Creatine: Creatine refills muscle energy and draws water into muscle, while tadalafil widens blood vessels. Gym users pair them for pump and fullness during training. The pump benefit rests on user reports, and the pair has not been tested together.
45. L-Carnitine: L-carnitine moves fatty acids into mitochondria, and tadalafil improves blood flow to muscle. Users pair them for training energy and pump. The pairing is user logic, not trial data.

## Where it comes from
46. A small molecule from ICOS and Eli Lilly that blocks PDE5, the enzyme that switches off nitric oxide signalling in blood vessel walls. Its long half life (about 17.5 hours) is why it became the daily option among erection drugs.
47. First studied for: Erectile dysfunction, approved in the US in 2003; later a once daily low dose for ED (2008), urinary symptoms of an enlarged prostate (BPH, 2011) and, at higher doses as Adcirca, pulmonary arterial hypertension.
48. Name story: Tadalafil is the generic name; Cialis was the Lilly brand, and the bathtub ads and 36 hour window made it 'the weekend pill'. [S2] (reported)
49. 1990s: ICOS and Glaxo, then ICOS with Lilly, develop IC351 as a PDE5 inhibitor
50. 2003: FDA approves Cialis for erectile dysfunction
51. 2008: Once daily low dose approved for ED
52. 2011: Approved for BPH urinary symptoms
53. 2018: US generics arrive
54. 2024: Large observational studies link PDE5 inhibitors to fewer heart events
55. Today: Daily 2.5 to 5 mg is a staple off label stack for blood flow and pumps
