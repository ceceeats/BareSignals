# Testagen
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/testagen
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: bioregulator, KEDG, Lys-Glu-Asp-Gly, Testoluten
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: KEDG (4 amino acids)
4. Legal and approval status: Not an approved drug anywhere; sold as a Khavinson bioregulator.

## Summary
5. Testagen is a short synthetic peptide (Lys-Glu-Asp-Gly) sold as a Khavinson bioregulator for the reproductive and endocrine system. It was developed in Russia to support testosterone and reproductive function in aging men, and is claimed to tune reproductive tissue genes. It is not an approved drug, and the evidence is Russian reports with no independent Western trials.
6. Punch line: Khavinson's testis bioregulator, swallowed with meals. Aimed at testosterone and libido in aging men.
7. Used for: Male hormonal support

## How it works
12. Claimed to tune reproductive tissue genes · Khavinson group · role Reproductive
   The Khavinson group claims this tetrapeptide acts directly on DNA in reproductive tissue to retune gene expression, not through a surface receptor. The aim is to restore function that declines with age. Support comes from one group with no independent trials.
13. Claimed to support testis hormone output · Lys-Glu-Asp-Gly, testis · role Endocrine & growth
   Lys-Glu-Asp-Gly is aimed at the testis, where Leydig cells make testosterone. The group claims it supports that hormone output in aging men. Russian human reports describe this, but no independent Western trial has tested it.
14. Mechanism overview: Testagen is the Khavinson tetrapeptide Lys-Glu-Asp-Gly, aimed at reproductive tissue. The group claims it tunes gene expression in the testis and supports hormone output. These claims rest on one research group with no independent trials. Like other bioregulators, it is a short peptide said to act directly on DNA rather than through a surface receptor.

## Where it comes from
39. A short synthetic Khavinson bioregulator peptide aimed at the reproductive/endocrine system.
40. First studied for: Developed in Russia to support testosterone and reproductive function in aging men.
41. Name story: Khavinson's organ naming: Testa- for testis. The sequence is Vesugen's KED plus one glycine, and Vilon's KE sits at the front of both. Related products also circulate as Testoluten. [S5] (inferred)
42. 2000s: Made in Russia as a bioregulator for aging men's hormones
