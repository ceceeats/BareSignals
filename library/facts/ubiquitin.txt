## Summary
5. Ubiquitin is a small protein made by nearly every cell, almost unchanged across species, that tags other proteins for recycling, work that won the 2004 Nobel Prize. It is a fundamental cell signalling molecule rather than a therapeutic and is not used as an injectable. Its clinical relevance is indirect, through drug targets such as proteasome blockers. This is a reference entry with no user reports.
6. Punch line: The cell's recycling tag, nearly identical in every species. Marks damaged proteins for disposal; Nobel Prize 2004.

## How it works
11. Tags worn proteins for shredding · Ubiquitin-proteasome system · role Cellular protection
   Ubiquitin is attached to worn or unneeded proteins, and K48-linked chains of it send them to the proteasome, the cell's protein shredder. This keeps damaged and misfolded proteins from building up. It is one of the best-mapped systems in cell biology, though not a drug itself.
12. Chains of tags route proteins to fates · E1, E2, E3 ligases · role Cellular protection
   An enzyme cascade attaches ubiquitin: E1 activates it, E2 carries it, and one of hundreds of E3 ligases picks the target protein. The linkage type of the chain sets the fate, destruction or signalling. This fine control is shown across a vast cell and biochemistry literature.
13. Marks DNA damage sites for repair · Histone ubiquitination · role DNA & telomeres
   Ubiquitin added to histones, the proteins DNA wraps around, flags sites of DNA damage. The mark recruits repair proteins to the break. This is established in cell studies and is part of how cells keep their genome intact.
14. Switches on immune alarm signalling · K63 chains, NF-kB · role Immunity
   K63-linked ubiquitin chains do not trigger destruction; they act as scaffolds that assemble signalling complexes. One key output is activation of NF-kB, the master switch for immune and inflammatory genes. This is mapped in cell studies.
15. Clears damaged organelles · Mitophagy, PINK1/Parkin · role Organ function
   When mitochondria are damaged, PINK1 builds up on their surface and recruits Parkin, which coats them with ubiquitin. The tag marks the organelle for mitophagy, its removal and recycling. This quality-control pathway is well shown in cell studies.
16. Mechanism overview: Ubiquitin is a 76 amino acid protein found in every eukaryotic cell. An enzyme cascade of E1 activators, E2 conjugators and E3 ligases attaches it to lysines on target proteins. The chain type sets the fate: K48-linked chains send proteins to the proteasome for destruction, while K63-linked chains act as scaffolds for NF-kB immune signalling and DNA repair. Ubiquitin on histones marks DNA damage sites. PINK1 and Parkin use it to tag damaged mitochondria for removal. Hundreds of E3 ligases give fine control over which proteins survive.
