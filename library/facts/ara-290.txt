# ARA-290
Page: https://baresignals.co/library/#/p/ara-290
Checked against Library v68, exported 2026-10-25

## Identity
1. Also called: cibinetide (INN/USAN), ARA290, pHBSP, pyroglutamate helix B surface peptide
2. Origin: Lab-designed
3. Sequence: EQLERALNSS (10 amino acids). Starts with pyroglutamate, shown as E
4. Legal and approval status: Investigational; no approval. Research chemical on the grey market.

## Summary
5. ARA-290 (cibinetide) is an 11-amino-acid peptide that copies the tissue-protective part of erythropoietin (EPO) without its blood-making effect. It was studied for neuropathic pain and nerve damage, where it is thought to calm inflammation and protect small nerve fibres. Phase 2 trials showed modest benefit, no Phase 3 followed, and it remains unapproved.
6. Punch line: EPO's tissue-protective piece, minus the blood-making. Phase 2 showed small nerve fibre regrowth and less pain.
7. Used for: Small fibre nerve pain

## Roles (what it is used for, strongest first)
8. Neural protection: Repairs small nerve fibres
9. Tissue repair: Tissue protection from EPO
10. Inflammatory signaling: Calms inflammation
11. Pain & sensory: Less nerve pain in trials

## How it works
12. Protects tissue without making blood · Innate repair receptor · role Tissue repair
   ARA-290 binds the innate repair receptor, the tissue-protective receptor that erythropoietin also uses, but not the EPO receptor pair that drives red cell production. So it carries EPO's protective signal without making blood. Phase 2 trials found no rise in red cells.
13. Calms inflammation around nerves · EPOR/CD131 complex · role Inflammatory signaling
   The innate repair receptor is a complex of the EPO receptor with CD131, and activating it dampens inflammatory signalling around injured nerves. Calmer surroundings give damaged nerve fibres a better chance to recover. This is the proposed route to its effects in neuropathy.
14. Regrows small nerve fibres in cornea (trials) · Corneal nerve fibre density · role Neural protection
   Small nerve fibres in the cornea can be counted under a microscope and serve as a window on small-fibre nerve health elsewhere. In Phase 2 trials in sarcoidosis and diabetic neuropathy, ARA-290 increased corneal nerve fibre density, a sign of nerve regrowth.
15. Eases neuropathic pain in small trials · IRR-mediated analgesia · role Pain & sensory
   Through the innate repair receptor, ARA-290 reduces neuropathic pain signalling from damaged small fibres. In Phase 2 trials in sarcoidosis and diabetic neuropathy, people reported less pain. Benefits were modest, and no confirmatory Phase 3 followed.
16. Mechanism overview: ARA-290, or cibinetide, is an 11-amino-acid peptide copying the tissue-protective face of erythropoietin. It binds the innate repair receptor, a complex of the EPO receptor with CD131, rather than the EPO receptor dimer that drives red cell production. So it protects tissue without making blood. Activation dampens inflammation around injured nerves, supports regrowth of small nerve fibres, and reduces neuropathic pain signalling.

## What has been tested

35. Pain & sensory: Diabetic neuropathy (trial)

## Who shouldn't take it
40. People with active or recent cancer: EPO-derived; EPO raises tumour progression risk, though ARA-290 does not raise red cells. (inferred)

## Synergies (paired with)
41. BPC-157: ARA-290 works on nerve inflammation and small-fibre regrowth through the innate repair receptor, while BPC-157 grows blood vessels and draws repair cells into injured tissue. People pair them for nerve and tissue injury recovery, but the pair has not been tested together.

## Where it comes from
42. Also called cibinetide, an 11-amino-acid peptide copying the tissue-protective part of erythropoietin (EPO) without the blood-making part, developed by Araim.
43. First studied for: Neuropathic pain and tissue protection, especially diabetic neuropathy and sarcoidosis-related small-fibre neuropathy.
44. Name story: ARA-290 is a code from its developer, Araim Pharmaceuticals; the official name is cibinetide. It copies the outward-facing 'helix B' of erythropoietin, which protects tissue without raising red blood cells, hence its lab name pHBSP: pyroglutamate helix B surface peptide. [S5] (verified)
45. 2000s: EPO's tissue-protective piece isolated as cibinetide
46. 2010s: Phase 2 trials in sarcoidosis and diabetic neuropathy
47. Today: Stalled nerve-pain peptide; grey-market only
