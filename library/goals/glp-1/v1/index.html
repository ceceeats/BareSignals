1Cravings & habits8

About 58 forum users say the urge to drink faded, often from dose one.LikelyDrug

People describe "alcohol noise" going quiet, stopping after one glass, or a bottle a night shrinking to half a glass within a week. Trials show a real but smaller effect. In the best one (Lancet 2026, 108 people with alcohol use disorder and obesity, all getting therapy), heavy drinking days fell 41 percentage points on semaglutide versus 26 on placebo. A smaller US trial (48 people, 9 weeks, low doses) cut drinks per drinking day and craving, but not the number of drinking days. The pattern across trials: these drugs cut how much people drink once they start more than whether they drink at all. The likely reason is GLP-1 receptors in the brain's "wanting" hub; in rodents the drugs blunt the dopamine surge alcohol triggers. Not approved for alcohol anywhere; phase 3 trials report from 2028.

Alcohol hits later and softer: breath alcohol rises more slowly on GLP-1s.PlausibleDrug

Some users don't just want less, they feel less: "two drinks and I barely get a buzz", or the opposite, drunk fast and a brutal hangover. In a small study of 20 people with obesity, GLP-1 users had a delayed rise in breath alcohol and a delayed "felt" effect after a drink, and nausea didn't explain it. In rats trained to recognise how alcohol feels, semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide all blunted that internal signal. A UK survey of 207 users found 22% said they feel drunk faster, while the top reason for drinking less was simply wanting it less (34%). The likely mechanism is slowed stomach emptying, which changes how fast alcohol reaches the blood, stacked on the brain's reward brake. Small studies, so Plausible.

Forums make it sound universal. Wider samples say roughly 1 in 3. A JAMA Psychiatry 2026 survey of people newly started on a GLP-1 found 31.1% drank less and 6.0% drank more. Honest read: a real nudge for many, a big change for some, nothing for most of the rest.

"Food noise", the nonstop chatter about food, often goes quiet within days.LikelyDrug

About 18% of US adults say they have frequent food noise. GLP-1 users describe it switching off in the first days, before meaningful weight loss, which points to the drug. In a study of 79 people, those six months into treatment scored lower on a five-question Food Noise Questionnaire than people about to start, with less craving and less pull from food cues, while their sense of taste barely changed. In a 30-person interview study it was the first of eight themes. Liraglutide also cut binge-eating episodes versus placebo. A 2026 mouse study in Nature found a group of GLP-1 receptor neurons that cut dopamine in the reward hub and selectively reduced eating of tasty food. Caveats: it returns after stopping, and eating-disorder clinicians warn it can reinforce restriction.

Practical downside: a narrower diet raises iron deficiency risk.

Veterans with an addiction had half the substance-related deaths on GLP-1s.PlausibleUnknown

A 2026 BMJ study of US veterans with type 2 diabetes compared GLP-1 drugs with SGLT-2 drugs, a fair rival because both are modern diabetes medicines. GLP-1 users had lower risk of new alcohol (HR 0.82), opioid (0.75), cocaine (0.80) and cannabis (0.86) use disorders. 2Body & longevity11

Heart protection showed up within weeks, before people had lost even 5%.ProvenBoth

SELECT gave semaglutide or placebo to 17,604 adults with heart disease and overweight but no diabetes. Over about 40 months, heart attack, stroke or heart death fell from 8.0% to 6.5%, a 20% drop. The curves split from about day 20: at three months there were 36 events versus 58, when weight loss was still under 5% and nobody had reached full dose, and by six months 67 versus 113. A Lancet 2025 analysis found the benefit was the same whatever people weighed at the start, and how much they lost by week 20 didn't predict their risk. About a third of the benefit tracked waist shrinkage; roughly two thirds didn't track weight at all. That is the strongest human evidence these drugs do something beyond weight.

In the same trial, about 1 in 5 fewer people died, many fewer from infection.LikelyBoth

SELECT recorded 833 deaths. Death from any cause was 19% lower on semaglutide (HR 0.81), and the gap came mostly from non-heart deaths (HR 0.77). Inflammation (CRP) fell 12% by week 4, when weight was down just 1.4%.ProvenDrug

Proven (as a blood marker)

CRP is a standard blood marker of body-wide inflammation. In SELECT it fell 38% more than placebo by two years. In 6,011 people with early bloods, it had already dropped 12% at week 4 and 19% at week 8, when weight loss was only 1.4% and 2.8% (summaries don't say whether these early drops are placebo-corrected). Among people who lost under 2% of their weight, CRP still fell on semaglutide and not on placebo. Adjusting for that fall explained part of the heart benefit. In heart failure patients, CRP dropped 43.5% versus 7.3%. GLP-1 receptors sit on immune cells, and switching them on calms inflammatory signalling. The limit: in Alzheimer's trials, CRP fell strongly and the disease didn't change.

Old female mice on semaglutide lived about 12% longer: nearly 100 extra days.PlausibleUnknown

Plausible (for humans)

This is why people call it a "longevity drug". In a 2026 Nature study, 39 versus 40 female mice started treatment at 20 months, roughly late middle age, and median lifespan rose from 742 to 834 days. Fatty liver inflammation cleared in 63% on semaglutide versus 34% on placebo.ProvenWeight

Drug or weight: Weight (mostly)

"My fatty liver is gone" is one of the most common claims, and biopsies back it. In ESSENCE, 800 people with liver inflammation and scarring had biopsies before and after 72 weeks: inflammation resolved in 62.9% versus 34.3%, and scarring improved in 36.8% versus 22.4%. Tirzepatide cleared it in up to 62% versus 10% in a phase 2 trial. Retatrutide cut liver fat by 81 to 82% at higher doses, and 79 to 86% of people reached normal liver fat by 24 weeks. Serious kidney events fell 24% in people with diabetes and kidney disease.ProvenBoth

FLOW gave semaglutide or placebo to 3,533 people with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease and stopped early because the benefit was clear. Major kidney events fell 24% (HR 0.76), kidney function declined more slowly each year, heart deaths fell 29% and deaths overall 20%. The likely reasons: lower pressure inside the kidney's filters, less inflammation and scarring, and better sugar and blood pressure. Among people with diabetes, "my kidney numbers improved" is a common report. Two gaps: no outcome trial yet in kidney disease without diabetes, and no study has cleanly separated the drug from the weight here. Tirzepatide and retatrutide kidney trials are running.

Tirzepatide cut heart failure emergencies by 38% in stiff-heart failure.ProvenBoth

HFpEF is the "stiff heart" kind of heart failure, closely tied to obesity. In SUMMIT (731 people, about two years), heart death or worsening heart failure fell from 15.3% to 9.9% on tirzepatide (HR 0.62). Roughly 1 kg in every 4 lost is not fat: the same split as dieting.LikelyWeight

"It eats your muscle" is a common fear. In a body-scan substudy of tirzepatide (160 people), about 75% of weight lost was fat and 25% lean mass, the same split as the placebo group, who lost less. A 2026 review of 35 trials put the median non-fat share at 28%; about two thirds of GLP-1 studies passed the usual 25% benchmark, but so did nearly half of diet-only studies. A 22-trial network analysis also landed near 25%. "Lean mass" on these scans includes water and organs, not just muscle, and no study measured real strength. In an MRI substudy of 55 people on the 7.2 mg dose, thigh muscle shrank slightly (not significant) while chair-stand performance held. In old mice, repeated on-off cycles left lean mass that never came back. Bottom line: like any big diet, more relevant for older people, unknown for strength.

Injured and on a GLP-1? Muscle is being lost from two directions.PlausibleWeight

Plausible (the combined effect is inferred)

Each half is measured; the sum isn't. Disuse is fast: in a knee brace, thigh volume fell 1.7% in 2 days and 5.5% in 7 days. After a hamstring or calf strain, the injured muscle was still about 9% smaller at 3 and at 12 months, and extra whey protein in a 50-person trial didn't change that. Separately, about 25 to 28% of weight lost on GLP-1s is lean tissue (fact 18). No study has looked at people who are injured and on these drugs at once, so the size of the combined hit is unknown. The sensible read: rehab loading matters more, not less, during GLP-1 treatment. GLP-1s are not repair drugs and are not on the anti-doping banned list.

Unproven (in humans)

Every label carries a boxed warning because these drugs caused thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents, and they're off-limits for anyone with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2. In humans, a Scandinavian study of 145,410 GLP-1 users followed for about four years found no increase (HR 0.93), though the medullary estimate was too imprecise to rule anything in or out. Rodent thyroid C-cells carry GLP-1 receptors while human and monkey ones carry few, and 20 months of liraglutide at over 60 times human exposure caused no C-cell growth in monkeys (a maker-affiliated study). One French study reported a raised risk; we haven't re-checked it yet.

BQuick answers

How do they work?
They copy a gut hormone that boosts insulin when sugar is high, slows the stomach and tells the brain "full".
Which is strongest?
In big trials: retatrutide ~24 to 25%, tirzepatide ~20 to 21%, CagriSema ~20%, semaglutide ~14 to 15%, orforglipron ~11%, liraglutide ~8%.
How much will I lose?
On top approved doses, 15 to 21% on average without diabetes; 9 to 14% lose under 5%.
What happens when I stop?
Most regain about two thirds within a year (fact 51).
Can I taper to avoid regain?
No trial shows tapering works; only exercise has randomised support.
How much of the loss is muscle?
About 25 to 28% is lean mass, the same share as dieting (fact 18).
Most common side effects?
Nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting, constipation: nausea 44% on Wegovy versus 16% on placebo.
Will my hair fall out?
Labels say 3 to 5% versus 1 to 2% on placebo, mostly temporary stress shedding (fact 42).
Thyroid cancer?
Rodent tumours drove the boxed warning; a 145,410-person human study found no rise (fact 58).
Suicidal thoughts?
The FDA checked 91 trials and 2.24 million users and found no increased risk, comparing GLP-1 users with users of another diabetes drug class (fact 52).
Does it affect the pill?
Tirzepatide and Foundayo yes, for the first weeks and after dose steps; semaglutide no meaningful effect (fact 21).
When will retatrutide be available?
Lilly plans to file in the first quarter of 2027, so approval is unlikely before late 2027 (fact 59).

ProvenRandomised trials or label rates against placebo.
LikelyA consistent controlled signal, not settled yet.
PlausibleA believable mechanism plus small trials, cases or records.
UnprovenReports, animal work, or never tested.
MythGood evidence says no.

How we graded. Each fact gets one tag. Proven means randomised trials or drug-label rates against placebo show it on a real outcome (or, where stated, a measured marker). Likely means a consistent controlled signal or strong, consistent data that isn't settled yet. Plausible means a believable mechanism plus small trials, case reports, surveys or records data. Unproven means anecdote, animal work or untested. Myth means good evidence says no, or the claim misreads the evidence. "Drug or weight?" asks whether the effect shows up before weight loss, tracks dose rather than kilos, or appears in lean people (Drug); tracks weight lost (Weight); shows both (Both); or hasn't been tested (Unknown). Community counts are first-hand forum and press reports, mostly UK women on Mounjaro, and never upgrade a tag on their own. Sources are PubMed IDs, trial registry numbers, labels and named news pieces, current to 3 Nov 2026.

DWarnings and fine print

Who shouldn't use them. Anyone with a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2 (boxed warning), or a serious allergy to the drug. Trials and guidance urge caution with past pancreatitis (excluded from retatrutide trials), severe gut disease or gastroparesis, diabetic eye disease when blood sugar drops fast, and severe liver impairment (Foundayo). People with or at risk of eating disorders: a 21-year-old given tirzepatide by an online service dropped from 47 to 41 kg and was later diagnosed with anorexia.

Pregnancy and contraception. Not recommended in pregnancy. The Wegovy label says stop at least two months before a planned pregnancy, because of its roughly one-week half-life; Zepbound and Foundayo labels give no fixed washout. Fertility can return fast. Tirzepatide lowers oral pill absorption: the label advises a non-oral method or a barrier method for four weeks after starting and after each dose increase, and the Foundayo label has a 30-day version of the same rule. Vomiting or diarrhoea on any of these drugs can also make the pill fail. Breastfeeding is not advised by most guidelines.

Flatness at high doses. A minority report losing joy, drive or libido, mostly at top doses, usually easing within weeks of a lower dose. One case report describes mood and psychotic symptoms after stopping abruptly. Tell your prescriber about mood changes.

Gallbladder and pancreas. Across 76 trials, gallbladder and bile duct problems were 37% more likely, and about 2.3 times more likely in weight-loss trials, higher with bigger doses and longer use. On the labels, gallstones run 1.6% versus 0.7% on Wegovy and 1.1% versus 1% on Zepbound. Fast weight loss itself causes gallstones. Pancreatitis is a label warning, but trial rates are low: 0.2 versus under 0.1 cases per 100 patient-years on Wegovy, 0.14 versus 0.15 on Zepbound. One small claims study found a raised risk with very wide uncertainty, resting on only 2 semaglutide cases.

Eyes (NAION). Europe's regulator calls this sudden optic-nerve stroke a "very rare" side effect of semaglutide, about one extra case per 10,000 person-years in diabetes studies, roughly a doubled risk. Stop and seek care for sudden vision loss.

Tested sport. Semaglutide and tirzepatide are on WADA's 2027 Monitoring Program: watched, not banned. Many other peptides people pair with them are banned at all times (reported via USADA, 6 Oct 2026; WADA's own list not yet read).

Gut and surgery. Nausea, diarrhoea, constipation and vomiting are common, mostly during dose increases. Food can stay in the stomach despite fasting, so tell your anaesthetist before sedation or surgery. Wound problems after body-contouring surgery were more common in one Danish study (infection odds 3.7 times higher). One user's blood-thinner (warfarin) level rose sharply on tirzepatide.

Regain after stopping. Most people regain about two thirds of lost weight within a year of stopping, and most stop: in one insurer's data, 8% were still on treatment at three years.

Lawsuits. As of 1 Sep 2026, about 4,022 US cases were pending over gastroparesis, ileus and bowel obstruction (MDL 3094) and 216 over vision loss (MDL 3163). No trial date or settlement has been set. Filings are claims, not proven injuries, and a 2025 court order required proper stomach-emptying tests.

Unapproved research versions. Retatrutide, eloralintide and cagrilintide are not approved anywhere as of 3 Nov 2026 (CagriSema's FDA decision is expected in Q4 2026; retatrutide filing is planned for the first quarter of 2027), and almost nothing is known about them in pregnancy, fertility or mental health. Compounded and "research use" products are not FDA-reviewed for identity, purity or dose. By Jul 2025 the FDA had 605 adverse-event reports for compounded semaglutide and 545 for compounded tirzepatide, many from dosing errors with multi-dose vials. Nothing on this page is a dosing guide.
