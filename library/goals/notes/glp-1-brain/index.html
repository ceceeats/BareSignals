GLP1 Claims Brain v1

Hub research for baresignals.co: "GLP-1 beyond weight loss: the brain claims" Compiled 3 Nov 2026. Version 1 (first pass). Companion to GLP1 Research v1 (mechanism, trials, FAQ) and Addiction Research v1 (alcohol, nicotine, opioids; not repeated here).

Evidence tags: [verified] = primary abstract, trial registry or regulator page read this pass. [reported] = secondary source (review, news, company release). [inferred] = my reasoning from verified facts. [speculated] = plausible, unsupported. Verdict scale: Proven (replicated RCT benefit on a clinical outcome) / Likely (RCT signal or strong consistent data, not yet settled) / Plausible (mechanism plus observational or small-trial hints) / Unproven (anecdote, animal, or untested) / Contradicted (good trials show no effect, or harm). Rules kept: no dosing instructions; trial doses appear only as what a trial tested. No supplier names. Paraphrased throughout. Source list: PubMed via DOI links, ClinicalTrials.gov IDs.

0. The one question that decides every card: does the drug reach the brain, and is the effect more than weight and sugar?

Brain access. - Semaglutide does not cross the blood-brain barrier in rodents. It reaches the brain through the "leaky" circumventricular organs (area postrema, median eminence) and a few sites next to the ventricles, then switches on deeper areas (e.g. lateral parabrachial nucleus) through nerve relays. [verified, rodent brain-mapping study 2020, JCI Insight, PMID 32213703, DOI] - Plain meaning: the drug "knocks on the door" of the brain and the message is passed inward. Direct exposure of the hippocampus or cortex (memory, mood areas) is limited. That matters for dementia and depression claims. [inferred] - In the EVOKE trials, oral semaglutide shifted some spinal-fluid markers of brain inflammation (YKL-40, GFAP down about 7 to 10%), so some central signal exists, but it was too small to change disease course. [verified, evoke(+) lessons review 2026, J Prev Alzheimers Dis, PMID 42785112, DOI] - A large EHR study found only low, regionally restricted GLP1R transcript in human nervous tissue. [verified, Murugadoss 2026, npj Metab Health Dis, PMID 42680805, DOI] - Exenatide and lixisenatide are smaller and not albumin-bound; they are generally described as entering the brain more readily than semaglutide. [reported, review literature; no human CSF data pulled this pass, see Gaps] - Tirzepatide: GIP receptor effects on appetite run through the area postrema (also a circumventricular organ) in mice. [verified, Lewis 2026, Nat Metab, PMID 42498824, DOI] Human brain-penetration data: none found. - Amylin drugs (cagrilintide, eloralintide) act mainly at the area postrema and hindbrain, linking fullness with reward pathways. [verified, Le Foll & Lutz 2026 review, PMID 42557048, DOI]

Separating drug from weight loss. Only a handful of studies try. They are flagged as "Separation test" in each card. The strongest are: dose-response at matched weight loss (Murugadoss 2026), mediation analysis in Mendelian randomisation (Zheng 2025), and early or weight-independent effects (exenatide lowers brain pressure in 2.5 hours; liraglutide migraine pilot with no BMI change).

Big-picture caution for the hub. Observational studies keep showing large benefits (dementia, depression, suicide); every completed brain RCT so far (Alzheimer's, Parkinson's phase 3, depression cognition) was negative or marginal on its primary endpoint. That gap is the core story of this page. [inferred]

Card 1. Mental clarity, "brain fog lifts"

The claim as people say it: "My head feels clearer", "I can think straight for the first time in years", "afternoon fog is gone." Common in Reddit and media accounts, usually alongside food-noise talk. The opposite ("brain fog", "slow") is also reported, especially early, with low intake or dehydration. [reported, community and media; no prevalence study found]

What studies show - RCT, depression with cognitive impairment, overweight: oral semaglutide 14 mg vs placebo, 16 weeks, n=72. Primary executive-function score: no difference (Z diff 0.32, 95% CI -0.92 to 1.58, p=0.60). Secondary global cognition: small benefit (2.39, CI 0.19 to 4.60, p=0.03). [verified, Badulescu 2025, Med, PMID 41218611, DOI; NCT04466345] - Meta-analysis of RCTs in people at risk of Alzheimer's (5 RCTs, n=13,711): global cognition effect g=0.014 (essentially zero). [verified, Abbatecola 2026, Nutrients, PMID 42942883, DOI] - Observational, psychiatric patients (n=13,007; 1,261 on semaglutide): fewer clinician-recorded cognitive signs at 12 months vs no treatment (mean ratio 0.75), glipizide (0.83), empagliflozin (0.81); not significant vs sitagliptin. [verified, De Giorgi 2026, BMJ Ment Health, PMID 42624612, DOI] - Observational, T2D with mild cognitive impairment: semaglutide n=72 vs 60 controls, MoCA +4 vs -1.5 at 24 months; 0 vs 14 progressed to dementia. Small, unblinded, not randomised. [verified, Cassataro 2026, Intern Emerg Med, PMID 42493728, DOI] - Small RCT in idiopathic intracranial hypertension (n=15): exenatide group improved fluid intelligence, processing speed and memory over 12 weeks, but no difference vs placebo between groups. [verified, IIH:Pressure cognition substudy 2024, Eye, PMID 38212401, DOI]

Separation test: In the 63,215-patient EHR study, new "cognitive symptoms" tracked weight loss more than dose (p<0.001), unlike mood and anxiety outcomes, which tracked dose. [verified, Murugadoss 2026, PMID 42680805] So the clarity signal looks weight and metabolism linked. [inferred]

Mechanism in plain language: Steadier blood sugar (fewer spikes and crashes), less inflammation, better sleep apnea, and less time spent thinking about food can all free up attention. None needs the drug to act on thinking areas directly. [inferred]

Class differences: No head-to-head cognition data across GLP-1, GIP/GLP-1, triple or amylin. Tirzepatide and retatrutide produce more weight loss and bigger sleep-apnea gains, so indirect clarity gains may be larger. [speculated]

Verdict: Plausible (as an indirect effect). Unproven as a direct brain effect. Trials to watch: none designed for "brain fog" in healthy users. Indirect: NCT06072963 (semaglutide plus intranasal insulin, cognition and cerebral blood flow).

Card 2. "Food noise" goes quiet

The claim: Constant, intrusive thoughts about food stop within days of starting. One of the most repeated GLP-1 phrases on TikTok and Reddit. [verified that the term grew on social media, Wylie 2026 review, Appetite, PMID 42772688, DOI]

How common it is - US nationally representative survey (May 2026, n=1,000): 50.9% report food noise at least sometimes, 18.0% frequently. Frequent food noise was higher in current GLP-1 users (25.8%) than non-users (16.3%), which likely reflects who seeks the drug. [verified, Hayashi & Masterson 2026, Obes Sci Pract, PMID 42801078, DOI] - Stricter questionnaire, n=3,646: 4 to 9.5% of US adults meet criteria; 6 to 14% of adults with obesity. [verified, Jovanovic 2026, preprint (Research Square), PMID 42926667, DOI] Not peer reviewed.

What studies show - Cross-sectional, n=79: Food Noise Questionnaire scores lower in 6-month GLP-1 users than people about to start (p=0.026), with lower craving, cue responsiveness, food reward and intake. Taste changed little (only sweet detection threshold rose). [verified, Rutigliani & Mattes 2026, Physiol Behav, PMID 42641845, DOI] - Qualitative, n=30: "reduction in food noise" was the first of eight themes. [verified, de Vere Hunt 2026, JAMA Netw Open, PMID 42247231, DOI] - Binge eating: liraglutide cut binge frequency vs placebo (mean difference -1.28 episodes) in a meta-analysis. [verified, Choudhury 2026, Clin Ther, PMID 41862354, DOI] - No randomised trial yet uses food noise as its primary endpoint. [inferred from searches]

Separation test: The drop is reported within days, before meaningful weight loss. [reported, community] Bariatric surgery patients also report food noise, and it rises with weight self-stigma during weight stability (n=60), so it is not only a drug phenomenon. [verified, Bond 2026, Obesity, PMID 42782188, DOI]

Mechanism: The drug signals "fed" to the hindbrain and hypothalamus and lowers the "wanting" response in reward circuits, so food cues grab less attention. Five competing theories exist (hunger signals, cue reactivity, food addiction, thought suppression, memory inhibition). [verified, Wylie 2026]

Class differences: Amylin adds a separate fullness signal through the area postrema; combinations (CagriSema) target this. No food-noise comparisons by class found. [inferred]

Negative mirror: The construct is new, overlaps older measures (craving, cue reactivity), and may label normal thoughts as pathology. [verified, Brewis 2026, Appetite, PMID 42392316, DOI] Eating-disorder clinicians warn the same effect can reinforce restriction in vulnerable people. [verified, Škudar 2026, Int J Eat Disord, PMID 42223191, DOI] Food noise returns after stopping. [reported]

Verdict: Likely (the experience is real and consistent; the quantified trial base is thin). Trials to watch: NCT06847399 (tirzepatide vs lisdexamfetamine vs placebo for binge eating); NCT07870538 (semaglutide across binge spectrum, KU Leuven).

Card 3. ADHD: better focus, less impulsivity

The claim: "Ozempic works like my ADHD meds", "I stopped impulse buying", "I finish tasks now." Widely repeated on wellness blogs and telehealth sites. [reported; checked one 2026 explainer: it cites no primary GLP-1 and ADHD study, fetched this pass]

What studies show - No randomised trial of any GLP-1 drug for ADHD symptoms was found in PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov (searched 3 Nov 2026). [verified absence, this pass] - Mendelian randomisation (genetic proxy for GLP-1 receptor activation) across 10 mental illnesses: no causal signal for ADHD. [verified, Xiang & Peng 2025, Int J Mol Sci, PMID 40141382, DOI] - Negative mirror: in a chart review of 226 GLP-1 users, new-onset ADHD diagnoses appeared in a subset, with the shortest latency of any condition (7.8 months). Small, uncontrolled. [verified, Sa 2026, J Psychiatr Res, PMID 41616750, DOI] - Adolescents treated for ADHD had higher liraglutide adherence (an adherence finding, not a symptom finding). [verified, Yaron 2025, Child Obes, PMID 41047941, DOI] - Higher semaglutide dose was linked to fewer later "personality and impulse-control disorder" diagnoses (p=0.028). [verified, Murugadoss 2026, PMID 42680805]

Separation test: None for ADHD.

Mechanism: GLP-1 signalling dampens dopamine-driven "wanting" in rodents. That could reduce food or shopping impulses, but ADHD stimulants work the other way (raising dopamine and noradrenaline in prefrontal areas). A dampener is not a focus drug. [inferred] Overlap with binge eating (common in ADHD) may explain much of the anecdote. [inferred, see ADHD Research v1]

Class differences: No data. Practical note for page: GLP-1 drugs slow stomach emptying, which could change how oral ADHD medication is absorbed. [reported, uncited on secondary sites; needs a primary pharmacology check]

Verdict: Unproven. Trials to watch: none registered for ADHD symptoms. NCT06847399 (tirzepatide vs an ADHD drug, lisdexamfetamine, in binge eating) is the closest comparison.

Card 4. Depression and anxiety: better, worse, or "flat" (anhedonia)

The claim, both directions: "My mood lifted", "my anxiety dropped" vs "I feel nothing", "Ozempic personality", "I lost joy in hobbies, sex, music." The "Ozempic personality" story went mainstream in April 2026 (Washington Post, syndicated). Media pieces cite no prevalence numbers and describe it as not widespread. [reported, KTLA/WaPo syndication 16 Apr 2026]

Trials (best evidence on safety) - Semaglutide STEP 1, 2, 3 pooled (n=3,377, people without major psychiatric illness): depression score PHQ-9 slightly better than placebo at 68 weeks (difference -0.56); fewer shifted to worse depression category (OR 0.63). Suicidal ideation 1% or less, same as placebo. [verified, STEP post hoc 2024, JAMA Intern Med, PMID 39226070, DOI] - Tirzepatide SURMOUNT 1 to 3 pooled (n=4,056): PHQ-9 difference -0.6; fewer worsened (18.2% vs 24.3%). Suicidal ideation 0.6% both arms; nonfatal suicidal behaviour 0.1% vs 0. [verified, Wadden 2026, Obesity, PMID 41537305, DOI] - 25 RCTs: suicidal behaviour RR 0.84 (0.54 to 1.32), no signal. [verified, Chen 2025, J Diabetes, PMID 40887719, DOI] - Depression as a treatment target: the one completed RCT (semaglutide in depression, n=72) did not change depressive symptoms. [verified, Badulescu 2025, PMID 41218611] - Caveat: these trials excluded people with major psychiatric illness, so they say little about the vulnerable group. [verified, stated in both pooled papers]

Observational (mixed) - Lower risk: VA cohort (n=215,970 GLP-1 users) found lower substance use and psychotic disorders, no broad depression signal. [verified, Xie 2025, Nat Med, PMID 39833406, DOI] Semaglutide vs other diabetes drugs: no increase in depression, anxiety or suicidality at 12 months. [verified, De Giorgi 2024, eClinicalMedicine, PMID 39764175, DOI] - Swedish registry study (Lancet Psychiatry 2026, ~100,000 people, >20,000 GLP-1 users): 44% lower depression, 38% lower anxiety. [reported via secondary site; primary not located in PubMed this pass; flag] - Meta-analysis (82 studies): no association with depression or anxiety either way. [verified, Choudhury 2026, PMID 41862354] - Semaglutide-only meta-analysis: depression RR 1.25 (0.95 to 1.65), anxiety 1.22, suicidal ideation 1.20, all non-significant, very high heterogeneity. [verified, Bhaduri 2026, Clin Obes, PMID 42584177, DOI] - Small prospective cohort (n=78, 3 months): PHQ-9 ≥10 fell from 55% to 12%; not related to % weight lost. Uncontrolled. [verified, Kır 2026, J Endocrinol Invest, PMID 42611111, DOI]

Negative mirror (worsening) - Pharmacovigilance: FAERS signals for semaglutide depression (ROR 1.87) and suicide/self-injury (1.73), not for tirzepatide or liraglutide. [verified, FAERS study 2025, J Affect Disord, PMID 40523410, DOI] WHO VigiBase: signals for depressed mood and suicidal thoughts across semaglutide, liraglutide, tirzepatide, concentrated in people already on antidepressants; no signal for attempts or completed suicide. [verified, Aboukaoud 2026, J Affect Disord, PMID 42002107, DOI] Spontaneous reports cannot show cause. [inferred] - Regulators: EMA PRAC (April 2024) found the evidence does not support a causal link to suicidal thoughts; no label change. [verified, EMA PRAC highlights 8 to 11 Apr 2024, link] FDA (Jan 2024) reported no evidence so far, monitoring continues. [reported, FDA drug safety communication, not opened this pass] - Anhedonia case series: 3 women on high-dose tirzepatide reported flatness and loss of motivation; improved with dose reduction, returned on re-escalation in one, while weight held. [verified, Nadolsky 2026, Obes Pillars, PMID 42518361, DOI] This is the clearest published version of the community "flat" report. [inferred] - Stopping abruptly: case of acute mood and psychotic symptoms after rapid GLP-1 discontinuation. [verified title only, Horien 2026, Prim Care Companion CNS Disord, PMID 42945438, DOI; abstract not available] - Chart review: new-onset depression in 9.0%, anxiety in 8.7% over ~20 months, no control group. [verified, Sa 2026, PMID 41616750]

Separation tests - Mendelian randomisation: genetic GLP-1 receptor activation linked to lower anxiety, depression and emotional lability; weight loss explained only 18% of the depression effect and 8% of emotional lability. [verified, Zheng 2025, Eur Psychiatry, PMID 41331950, DOI] - Dose-response: higher attained semaglutide dose linked to fewer later mood (p<0.001) and anxiety (p<0.001) diagnoses, with these outcomes tracking dose more than weight. [verified, Murugadoss 2026, PMID 42680805] - Heart-failure cohort (n=122): mood gains strongly correlated with weight loss. [verified, Balata 2025, Clin Res Cardiol, PMID 40493069, DOI]

Mechanism: Two pathways pull opposite ways. Improvement: less inflammation, better sleep, body image, glucose stability. Flatness: the same reward-dampening that quiets food and alcohol cravings may, at high doses in some people, dull other rewards (dopamine "wanting"). [inferred, consistent with Nadolsky 2026 and ED spotlight]

Class differences: FAERS signal limited to semaglutide in one analysis; another showed tirzepatide signals too. Tirzepatide anhedonia cases were at the top dose. Retatrutide and amylin: no psychiatric data found beyond trial safety tables. [verified/inferred as noted]

Verdict: Mood improvement: Plausible. Increased depression or suicidality for most users: Contradicted by RCTs and regulators. Dose-related anhedonia in a subset: Plausible (case series plus mechanism; unquantified). Trials to watch: NCT07136714 (semaglutide vs placebo for mood in major depression with overweight, n=116, completion Sep 2027); NCT07213466 (obesity treatments in bipolar, Mayo); NCT07586150 (semaglutide in severe mental illness, Munich, starts 2028).

Card 5. Compulsive behaviours (nail biting, shopping, gambling)

Brief by design. Full substance data is in Addiction Research v1.

The claim: "I stopped biting my nails", "no more Amazon carts", "lost interest in betting." Common anecdote in media pieces on cravings. [reported] What studies show: - Case report: dopamine-agonist-induced impulse control disorder (Parkinson's) resolved after a GLP-1 drug was started. [verified title, Kelly & Davis 2026, Mov Disord Clin Pract, PMID 42260955, DOI; abstract not available] - Gambling: GLP-1 drugs listed as an investigational option; no completed trials. [verified, Bissig & Mutschler 2026 review, Front Pharmacol, PMID 42147309, DOI] Hypothesis paper only. [verified, Ballerio 2026, PMID 42645133] - Dose-response EHR signal for "personality and impulse-control disorders" (p=0.028). [verified, Murugadoss 2026] - Negative mirror: rare new-onset trichotillomania (hair pulling) in the 226-person chart review. [verified, Sa 2026, PMID 41616750] - Nail biting, shopping: no studies found. [verified absence] Mechanism: Same reward dampening as food and alcohol. [inferred] Verdict: Unproven (behavioural addictions); see Addiction file for substances (alcohol is Likely there). Trials: none for gambling, shopping or body-focused repetitive behaviours found.

Card 6. Alzheimer's and dementia

The claim: "Ozempic prevents dementia." Driven by 2024 to 2025 headlines from database studies, then the Nov to Dec 2025 EVOKE failure. [reported]

RCTs (the decisive evidence) - EVOKE and EVOKE+ (oral semaglutide 14 mg vs placebo, amyloid-confirmed early Alzheimer's, n=3,808, 40 countries). CDR-SB change at 104 weeks: 2.3 vs 2.3 (EVOKE, diff -0.08, p=0.57) and 2.2 vs 2.1 (EVOKE+, diff 0.10, p=0.46). Both stopped for negative outcome; extension cancelled. [verified, Cummings 2026, Lancet, PMID 41865758, DOI; NCT04777396, NCT04777409] Topline presented at CTAD, Dec 2025. [verified, Alzheimer Europe] - Biomarkers: CRP fell strongly (body inflammation), CSF YKL-40 and GFAP fell 7 to 10%, no effect on decline. Authors conclude lowering body inflammation is not enough once Alzheimer's is symptomatic. [verified, evoke(+) lessons review 2026, PMID 42785112] - ELAD (liraglutide, n=204, mild to moderate AD, 52 weeks): primary brain glucose metabolism not different (p=0.14); executive score slightly better (unadjusted p=0.01); daily function and CDR-SB no difference. [verified, ELAD 2025, Nat Med, PMID 41326666, DOI; NCT01843075]

Observational (prevention, mostly diabetes) - Semaglutide vs other diabetes drugs (n=1,094,761): 40 to 70% lower first AD diagnosis (HR 0.33 vs insulin; 0.59 vs other GLP-1s). [verified, TTE study 2024, Alzheimers Dement, PMID 39445596, DOI] - Semaglutide and related dementias (n=1,710,995): HR 0.54 to 0.80, vascular dementia reduced, no effect on frontotemporal or Lewy body. [verified, TTE study 2025, J Alzheimers Dis, PMID 40552638, DOI] - Tirzepatide vs semaglutide: dementia HR 0.69 (0.48 to 0.99), 2-year follow-up. [verified, TTE study 2026, Diabetes Res Clin Pract, PMID 41544899, DOI] - MCI plus T2D, GLP-1 vs DPP4 (n=1,320): dementia HR 0.74; 15.5% vs 20.4% at 3.9 years. [verified, PMID 42769274, DOI] - Key counterweight: in an 82-study meta-analysis, no dementia benefit vs SGLT2 inhibitors (RR 1.07); benefit appears mainly vs DPP4 or no treatment. [verified, Choudhury 2026, PMID 41862354] Plain meaning: much of the "protection" may be any good metabolic drug vs a weaker one, or healthier people getting newer drugs. [inferred]

Separation test: EVOKE is the separation test: in mostly non-diabetic people (86% without T2D), the drug lowered weight and inflammation but did not slow disease. [verified, PMID 42769274 background; Cummings 2026] The observational signal may still reflect prevention of vascular damage in diabetes, a different question. [inferred]

Mechanism: In animals, GLP-1 drugs lower brain inflammation (microglia), improve insulin signalling and reduce plaques. Human direct evidence is limited or absent. [verified, Metabolism 2026 review, PMID 42962966, DOI]

Class differences: Tirzepatide looked better than semaglutide in one database study (above). No RCT of tirzepatide, retatrutide or amylin drugs in dementia found. Amylin and Alzheimer's: older animal and small human literature exists (pramlintide), not reviewed this pass. [flag]

Verdict: Treating established Alzheimer's: Contradicted. Preventing dementia in people with diabetes: Plausible (strong observational, no RCT; benefit vanishes vs SGLT2). Trials to watch: NCT07200622 (oral semaglutide, Imperial, n=60); NCT07135245 (semaglutide combinations, n=180, to 2030); NCT06072963; NCT07651319 (semaglutide plus IL-2).

Card 7. Parkinson's disease

The claim: "GLP-1 drugs slow Parkinson's." Built on exenatide phase 2 (2017) and lixisenatide (2024).

RCTs - LIXIPARK (lixisenatide vs placebo, early PD, n=156, 12 months): motor score change -0.04 vs +3.04 (diff 3.08, p=0.007). Nausea 46%, vomiting 13%. Phase 2. [verified, Meissner 2024, NEJM, PMID 38598572, DOI; NCT03439943] - Exenatide-PD3 (exenatide weekly 2 mg vs placebo, n=194, 96 weeks): motor score worsened 5.7 vs 4.5 points (coefficient 0.92, p=0.47). No effect on any secondary outcome. Published Feb 2025. [verified, Vijiaratnam 2025, Lancet, PMID 39919773, DOI; NCT04232969] - NLY01 (pegylated exenatide, n=255, 36 weeks): primary missed; post hoc benefit under age 60 only. [reported, company release Mar 2023, Business Wire; NCT04154072] - Meta-analysis of 8 RCTs (n=850): small motor benefit (SMD -0.21) with high heterogeneity; mood (SMD -0.36) and cognition signals exploratory. [verified, Chen 2026, Front Neurol, PMID 42490867, DOI] Another meta found no significant off-medication motor benefit (MD -3.29, CI -7.84 to 1.26). [verified, Choudhury 2026, PMID 41862354]

Observational: lower risk of developing PD in GLP-1 users (pooled HR 0.70). [verified, Choudhury 2026]

Separation test: Parkinson's patients are mostly not obese or diabetic; trials enrolled non-diabetics. So a positive result would be weight-independent by design. The phase 3 was negative. [inferred]

Mechanism: Protect dopamine neurons from inflammation and mitochondrial stress (animal data). The PD3 authors call for "agents that show better target engagement", meaning brain exposure was a suspected weak point. [verified, Lancet 2025 conclusion]

Class differences: Lixisenatide (short-acting, smaller) was positive in phase 2; exenatide weekly was negative in phase 3; semaglutide PD trial (Oslo, NCT03659682) has unknown status. [verified registry]

Verdict: Contradicted for disease modification (best evidence), with one positive phase 2 unconfirmed. Risk reduction: Plausible. Trials to watch: no active phase 3 found. NCT03659682 (semaglutide, results not posted). Next-generation brain-penetrant agonists (not searched this pass).

Card 8. Migraine and idiopathic intracranial hypertension (IIH)

The claim: "My migraines dropped by half on Mounjaro." Growing in headache forums; IIH patients are heavy GLP-1 users. [reported]

IIH (raised brain pressure, mostly in young women with obesity) - RCT, IIH:Pressure (exenatide vs placebo, n=16, telemetric pressure monitors): brain pressure fell 5.7 cmCSF at 2.5 hours (p=0.048), 6.4 at 24 hours (p=0.03), 5.6 at 12 weeks (p=0.058). [verified, Mitchell 2023, Brain, PMID 36907221, DOI] - Meta-analysis (n=1,550): papilloedema RR 0.25, visual loss RR 0.41; monthly headache days -3.6 at 3 months, -4.8 at end; no BMI association with outcome. [verified, Eur J Neurol 2025, PMID 40937960, DOI] - TriNetX (6,188 per arm): shunt surgery RR 0.42 at 1 year, stenting RR 0.53. [verified, PMID 41832796, DOI] - Systematic reviews call certainty low; database cohorts positive, prospective data mixed. [verified, Headache 2025, PMID 41246926, DOI; J Headache Pain 2025, PMID 41057780, DOI] - Negative mirror: the industry phase 3 of an exenatide formulation (Presendin) was terminated after 14 enrolled. [verified, NCT05347147]

Migraine without IIH - Open-label pilot (liraglutide add-on, n=31, obese, treatment-resistant migraine, IIH signs excluded): headache days 19.8 to 10.7 per month; BMI 34.0 to 33.9 (no change); BMI change did not predict response. [verified, Headache 2025, PMID 40525593, DOI] - Tirzepatide case series (2 patients): fewer migraines, but weight loss confounds. [verified, Headache 2026, PMID 42403198, DOI] - Mouse chronic-migraine model: liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide reduced pain hypersensitivity, as well as propranolol, partly through opioid pathways. [verified, Br J Pharmacol 2026, PMID 42950887, DOI] - Negative mirror: headache is a listed common side effect of GLP-1 drugs [reported, labels; rates not pulled]. A Danish study infused native GLP-1 to see if it triggers migraine (NCT04232605, results not posted). [verified registry]

Separation test: Strongest of any card. Pressure drops within 2.5 hours (no weight change possible) and the migraine pilot showed no BMI change. [verified] Mechanism: GLP-1 receptors on the choroid plexus (the brain's fluid factory) reduce spinal-fluid secretion, lowering pressure. The choroid plexus sits outside the tight barrier, so even semaglutide-size drugs can reach it. [reported mechanism from IIH literature; access point inferred] Class differences: Meta-analysis pooled GLP-1 and tirzepatide; first tirzepatide RCT now recruiting. [verified]

Verdict: IIH: Likely (small RCT plus consistent cohorts). Migraine: Plausible. Trials to watch: NCT07191873 (tirzepatide vs placebo in IIH, Duke, n=60, Aug 2027); NCT06027567 (semaglutide rapid weight loss in IIH, completed, results pending); NCT06361823 (semaglutide IIH, status unknown).

Card 9. Sleep quality

The claim: "I sleep through the night", "stopped snoring". Opposite: "insomnia on tirzepatide", "vivid dreams." [reported]

What studies show - Sleep apnea (proven, but weight-driven): tirzepatide cut apnea events by 20 to 24 more per hour than placebo (baseline ~50), with better patient-reported sleep. FDA-approved for OSA Dec 2024. [verified, Malhotra 2024, NEJM, PMID 38912654, DOI; NCT05412004] - Retatrutide (TRIUMPH-1-OSA, n=243): -24.4 events per hour vs placebo at 9 mg, 80 weeks. [verified, Grunstein 2026, NEJM, PMID 42904878, DOI] - Negative mirror: in diabetes, GLP-1 drugs did not prevent new OSA diagnoses vs DPP4 (HR 1.07, n=206,381). [verified, Khouri 2026, AJRCCM, PMID 42745463, DOI] - General sleep quality: prospective cohort (n=78), poor sleep (PSQI >5) fell 55% to 17% in 3 months, linked to mood improvement, not weight. Uncontrolled. [verified, Kır 2026, PMID 42611111] - Insomnia mirror: semaglutide vs metformin in PCOS (n=41,070) showed a small but significant rise in insomnia. [verified, Raj 2026, Cureus, PMID 42741750, DOI]

Separation test: OSA benefit tracks fat loss around the airway. [verified, Arslan 2026 review, PMID 42897716] No study isolates a direct sleep-centre effect. Class differences: Tirzepatide and retatrutide have OSA phase 3 data; semaglutide does not have an OSA-specific phase 3 found this pass. Verdict: OSA: Proven (tirzepatide), driven by weight loss. General sleep quality: Plausible, indirect. Insomnia as a side effect: Plausible, small signal. Trials to watch: NCT05929066, NCT05929079 (retatrutide TRIUMPH, OSA subsets reported).

Card 10. Chronic pain, fibromyalgia, back and knee pain

The claim: "My back pain disappeared", "fibro flares stopped", "joint pain gone before weight loss." [reported]

What studies show - Knee osteoarthritis RCT (retatrutide, TRIUMPH-1, n=574 with knee OA): WOMAC pain fell 3.5 to 3.6 vs 1.9 placebo (diff -1.6 to -1.8 on a 10-point scale). [verified, Jastreboff 2026, NEJM, PMID 42814954, DOI] Semaglutide STEP 9 knee OA RCT (2024) also positive. [reported, not re-pulled this pass] - Fibromyalgia (TriNetX, 48,025 per arm): GLP-1 users had fewer opioid prescriptions (OR 0.65), fewer pain codes (0.79), fewer fatigue codes (0.68); no difference in disability codes. Uses billing codes, not symptom scales. BMI and HbA1c stayed higher in GLP-1 users. [verified, Eshak 2026, Rheumatology, PMID 41578948, DOI] Published comment raised methods concerns. [verified title, PMID 41923358] - Chronic low back pain pilot (n=35, tirzepatide 24, semaglutide 11, single arm): pain severity 4.8 to 2.0 over 12 months; BMI -12.5%; CRP and leptin down; experimental pain sensitivity unchanged. [verified, medRxiv 2026, PMID 42238413, DOI] - Knee OA real-world (semaglutide, n=88 completers): VAS pain -2.47; weight loss independently linked to function gains; dose linked to pain. No comparator. [verified, J Clin Med 2026, PMID 42589980, DOI] - Diabetic neuropathy: only small, heterogeneous phase 2 RCTs, no clear benefit. [verified, Ziegler 2026, PMID 42533340, DOI] - Animal: intrathecal semaglutide reduced burn pain via spinal enkephalin pathway in mice. [verified, PMID 42669356]

Negative mirror: Retatrutide caused dysaesthesia (abnormal skin sensations) in 4 to 7% vs 1% placebo (TRIUMPH-2). [verified, Bellido 2026, Lancet, PMID 42810372, DOI] Xie 2025 found more arthritic disorders with GLP-1 vs usual care. [verified, PMID 39833406]

Separation test: Knee OA pain partly mechanical (less load). Fibromyalgia study is the most interesting: benefit despite higher BMI in the GLP-1 group, but billing codes are weak. The back-pain pilot found no change in central pain sensitivity. [verified/inferred] Mechanism: Less load on joints, lower inflammatory markers (CRP), possible spinal GLP-1 receptors on astrocytes damping pain signals (animal). [inferred] Class differences: Retatrutide has the largest knee-pain RCT and a nerve-sensation side effect. No amylin pain data.

Verdict: Weight-bearing joint pain: Likely (RCTs, mostly weight-mediated). Fibromyalgia: Plausible. Neuropathic pain: Unproven. Trials to watch: NCT07035093 (retatrutide in chronic low back pain, phase 3, n=586, Sep 2027); NCT07731256 (semaglutide RCT in back pain and obesity, 2027 to 2031).

Card 11. Neuroinflammation (the umbrella mechanism)

The claim: "GLP-1s calm brain inflammation", used to explain all the above. Common in wellness and biohacking content. [reported]

What studies show - Animal: consistent. Semaglutide rescued inflammation-induced memory loss in mice (LPS model). [verified, Liu 2026, Neuromolecular Med, PMID 42622904, DOI] Tirzepatide had antidepressant-like effects and raised BDNF in stressed mice; IL-6 change not significant. [verified, Maradia 2026, PMID 42131414, DOI] - Human: EVOKE showed strong drops in blood CRP and only 7 to 10% drops in CSF glial markers, with no clinical effect. [verified, PMID 42785112] Reviews state direct human evidence for these brain mechanisms "remains limited or absent". [verified, Metabolism 2026, PMID 42962966] - No human PET (TSPO) study of GLP-1 drugs and brain inflammation found this pass. [verified absence in searches; flag]

Separation test: EVOKE again: body inflammation fell a lot, brain markers a little, disease not at all. [verified] Verdict: Plausible as a mechanism; Unproven as a clinically meaningful human brain effect. Trials to watch: AD trials above (biomarker endpoints).

Negative mirror, pulled together

  1. Every completed brain-disease RCT missed its primary endpoint except LIXIPARK (phase 2) and IIH:Pressure (n=16): EVOKE, EVOKE+, ELAD, Exenatide-PD3, NLY01, semaglutide in depression cognition.
  2. Observational "protection" shrinks or vanishes against strong active comparators (SGLT2), pointing to healthy-user and comparator bias.
  3. Pharmacovigilance mood and suicidality signals exist, concentrated in people already on antidepressants; regulators found no causal link.
  4. Published anhedonia (high-dose tirzepatide), abrupt-stop psychosis case, new-onset ADHD/trichotillomania in a chart review, retatrutide dysaesthesia, small insomnia signal.
  5. Food noise as a construct is contested; eating-disorder risk flagged.
