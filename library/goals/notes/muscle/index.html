0. Headline findings (for the page top)

  1. Muscle heals far better than tendon. It regenerates from satellite cells; most strains heal without surgery. Typical elite hamstring lay-off 18 to 24 days for MRI grade 1 to 2 (Ekstrand, Lee, Healy 2016, DOI). [verified v2, primary abstract]
  2. Re-injury is the real problem, not first healing. 16% of all muscle injuries in pro football are re-injuries, and they keep players out longer (Ekstrand 2011). [verified]
  3. Loading early beats resting. Lengthening-focused rehab (Askling L-protocol) cut return to play from 51 to 28 days in footballers and from 86 to 49 days in sprinters. [verified]
  4. Nordic curls prevent hamstring injuries, but how much is disputed: "halves the rate" (van Dyk 2019, RR 0.49) vs "inconclusive" on stricter methods (Impellizzeri 2021, RR 0.59, CI 0.27 to 1.29). Most amateur players never do them (69%). [verified]
  5. PRP does nothing for hamstring strains in the best trial (Reurink 2014 NEJM, n = 80, double blind). [verified]
  6. No peptide has any human muscle-injury result yet, and the only registered BPC-157 muscle trial is not credible. The grade 2 hamstring "trial" (NCT07437547) comes from a sponsor whose sister TB-500 record calls itself "fictional" and whose portfolio includes a copy of a finished Lilly trial. Do not tell readers "results in 2027". [verified registry, 4 Nov 2026; credibility judgement inferred]
  7. BPC-157 muscle-injury data = one lab. Every rat strain, crush, cut or detachment study found is from the Zagreb group that discovered it. The single independent study (Ankara, 2026) is a 2-hour ischaemia model, not an injury that heals. [verified authorship, v2 corrected]
  8. TB-500's parent protein failed to speed muscle regeneration in one independent mouse study (Gonzalez-Franquesa 2021). [verified]
  9. Injured muscle stays smaller. After hamstring or calf strains the injured muscle was 9.4% smaller at 3 months and still smaller at 12 months; extra protein did not help (Mertz 2025 RCT). [verified]
  10. Community: 17 muscle injury reports from 15 people found outside Reddit; 10 "worked", 5 early or unclear, 1 no change, 1 worse. Zero clinician-confirmed effects. [verified per page; tally inferred] (v1: 14 from 13.)
  11. Pec and distal biceps tears are tendon problems with good surgical numbers: about 90% return to sport after repair, at about 6 months. Repair beats no repair on strength (distal biceps supination 89% vs 63%). [verified]
  12. IGF-1 has a modest, probably causal link to breast, prostate and colorectal cancer in genetic studies (about 5 to 10% higher odds per standard deviation). IGF-1 LR3 is a modified long-acting analogue, and nobody has studied what injecting it does to cancer risk over years. [verified MR data; any extrapolation to LR3 speculated]

1. What each injury is

1.1 Grading (applies to all muscle strains)

Grade Classic definition Feels like Typical time out
1 (mild) Under 10% of fibres disrupted Tightness, can keep moving, little strength loss Hamstring ~18 days (elite football, MRI grade) [verified, Ekstrand, Lee, Healy 2016]
2 (moderate) 10 to 50% disrupted Sudden pain, limp, weakness, sometimes bruising Hamstring ~24 days [verified, Ekstrand, Lee, Healy 2016]

Grade percentages from the calf literature (Hauer 2026 JAAOS, DOI). [verified] v2 caution: the 18 and 24 day figures use MRI (radiological) grades, not the fibre-percentage definition in column 2, and the spread is wide (SD 13 to 15 days). In the same 255 injuries, 84% hit biceps femoris, and oedema size explained only 6 to 12% of lay-off time. [verified, DOI]

Modern grading (BAMIC, British Athletics): grades 0 to 4 plus a letter for site: a = muscle edge (myofascial), b = muscle-tendon junction, c = into the tendon. Elite sport uses it because the letter predicts time out more than the number. [reported] - Elite Australian cricket, 141 MRI-confirmed hamstring strains over 13.5 seasons: median 22 days out (IQR 15 to 38); grade 2 or 3 and tendon involvement meant longer (Cooney 2026, DOI). [verified] - Hungarian pro football, 57 hamstring injuries: low/moderate patterns returned in 9 to 27 days (median); high-grade patterns took 46.5 and 94.5 days (Zsidai 2026, DOI). [verified] - US college football, 96 hamstring injuries: oedema fully wrapping the sciatic nerve on MRI added ~10 days (33.7 vs 21.5) (Lee 2026 BJSM, DOI). [verified] - Tendon involvement is the big multiplier: sprinters 73 vs 31 days; footballers' "stretching type" 43 vs 23 days (Askling 2013/2014). [verified] - A caution for the page: radiologists barely agreed on BAMIC grades for one lesion type (Fleiss κ near 0; Corsini 2026, DOI). [verified] Strength and flexibility tests could not tell which injuries involved the tendon (Crema 2017, DOI). [verified]

1.2 Each injury

Hamstring strain (most common). 37% of all muscle injuries in pro football; 22% of players get one each season; match rate 9 times training rate; training injuries rising 4% a year (Ekstrand 2011, DOI; Ekstrand 2016, DOI). [verified] Biceps femoris is hit most (63% in cricket, Cooney 2026). [verified] Two types: sprinting (high speed, usually biceps femoris, quicker) and stretching (kicks, splits, dance; often semimembranosus near the tendon, slower) (Askling 2013). [verified]

Calf strain / "tennis leg". 13% of football muscle injuries; the only muscle group whose rate rises with age (Ekstrand 2011). [verified] Gastrocnemius: explosive push-off with knee straight (classic "tennis leg", medial head). Soleus: repetitive running with knee bent (Hauer 2026). [verified] In AFL, soleus was 84.6% of calf strains; return 25.4 days (soleus) vs 19.1 (gastrocnemius); running-related injuries took ~12 days longer (Green 2019, DOI). [verified] Recreational tennis leg: 6 to 12 weeks (Schwach 2026, DOI). [verified] Runners' forum consensus: 4 weeks off for grade 1, ~6 for grade 2, early return often relapses [reported, Fetch Everyone forum].

Quadriceps. 19% of football muscle injuries; rectus femoris most often (73% in one series) (Ekstrand 2011; Kruse 2026, DOI). [verified] After ACL reconstruction, thigh muscle injuries doubled in the 2 years after return; patellar tendon grafts tripled quad injuries (RR 3.5) (Della Villa 2025, DOI). [verified]

Groin / adductor. 23% of football muscle injuries (Ekstrand 2011). [verified] Complete adductor longus ruptures can be managed without surgery: returns at 96 and 146 days in two case reports; conservative if the gap is under ~1 cm (Quintana-Cepedal 2025, DOI; Abate 2023, DOI). [verified, case reports only]

Pec tear. Usually the tendon at the arm bone during heavy bench press. Repair gives full return at about 6 months; non-surgical care only for low-grade partial tears (Liem 2017 review, DOI 10.1007/s40141-017-0154-9). [reported, abstract summary via university page; v2 replaced by primary reviews in 1.2a] Structurally closer to a tendon injury: link to Tendon Base. [inferred]

Bicep tear. Distal biceps rupture left without repair loses supination and flexion strength and endurance (Tzeuton 2023, DOI). [verified] Also a tendon injury in most cases. [inferred]

Contusion (dead leg, cork). Direct blow crushes fibres and connective tissue; healing is regeneration plus scar (Mackey 2012, DOI). [verified] In elite football, direct thigh injuries were 12% of thigh muscle injuries and players were back in a mean 7 days, vs 18.5 for strains; 42% involved foul play (Ueblacker 2015, 2,287 thigh injuries, DOI). [verified] Mouse contusion work shows scar can be reduced by blocking TGF-β1 (losartan) (Kobayashi 2016, DOI). [verified, mice]

DOMS and training recovery. Not an injury: temporary soreness peaking 24 to 72 h after unaccustomed or eccentric work and settling without treatment within about a week [reported, standard teaching]. Most supplements only move soreness, not strength or blood markers (see 3.5). [verified]

Muscle loss during injury or immobilisation. Fast: thigh volume down 1.7% after 2 days and 5.5% after 7 days in a knee brace (quads 6.7%, hamstrings 3.5%), about 0.8% a day, in 13 young men (Kilroe 2020, DOI). [verified v2] Muscle protein synthesis fell 36% over the week in the same men (Kilroe 2019, DOI). [verified]

2. How muscle heals (plain language)

2.1 The biology

  • Satellite cells are stem cells parked on every fibre. Injury wakes them; they multiply and fuse to rebuild fibres (Karalaki 2009, see Tendon Base). [verified]
  • Three overlapping phases: destruction (days 1 to 3: bleeding, inflammation, clean-up by immune cells); repair (days 3 to ~21: new fibres plus a connective tissue scar compete); remodelling (weeks to months). [reported, Järvinen 2005, see Tendon Base] These phases run in parallel, not one after another (Ferré 2007 pig injection model, DOI). [verified]
  • Scar vs regeneration. The scar is driven by TGF-β1; myostatin slows regeneration. Blocking either (follistatin in transgenic mice; losartan in mice) gave more new fibres and less scar (Zhu 2011, DOI; Kobayashi 2016). [verified, mice] No human test of these anti-scar strategies after strain. [gap]
  • Thymosin beta-4 is made naturally at the injury site and attracts muscle precursor cells (Tokura 2010, DOI). [verified, mice] This is the logic behind TB-500. [inferred]

2.2 Why hamstrings re-injure [verified where cited; mechanism inferred]

  • Re-injuries = 16% of all muscle injuries and cause longer absence (Ekstrand 2011). [verified]
  • In Petersen 2011 controls, the recurrent-injury rate was 45.8 per 100 player seasons vs 8.1 for new injuries: about 5.7 times the rate [rates verified v2; ratio inferred; the abstract does not say whether the two rates share a denominator, so treat 5.7 as approximate].
  • Biceps femoris injuries recurred in 18% vs 2% for semimembranosus and semitendinosus combined (Hallén 2014; Ekstrand, Lee, Healy 2016). [verified]
  • The repair site changes for good: after hamstring or calf strains the aponeurosis (inner tendon sheet) was 6 times larger at 3 months and still 5 times larger at 12 months, while muscle volume was 9.4% lower at 3 months and the loss "persisted" at 12 months (Mertz 2025, DOI). [verified v2; the 12-month percentage is not in the abstract] A stiffer scar next to weaker muscle is a plausible re-tear point. [inferred]
  • Returning on a timeline instead of criteria raises risk; consensus RTP criteria list no pain on palpation, strength and flexibility testing, functional tests, similar flexibility, field testing and psychological readiness (van der Horst 2017 Delphi, 58 experts, DOI). [verified]
  • Team factors matter: a new head coach bringing his own fitness coach was linked to ~3 times the hamstring burden (Ekstrand 2023, DOI); better medical and performance staff communication = 4.1 fewer injury days per 1000 h per step (Ekstrand 2025, DOI). [verified, observational]

3.3 PRP and other injections

  • Reurink 2014 NEJM (DOI): 80 athletes, double blind, PRP vs saline. No faster return; 1-year re-injury HR 0.89 (CI 0.38 to 2.13); no MRI or score difference (Reurink 2015, DOI). [verified]
  • Pas 2015 meta of 3 PRP trials: HR 1.03, no effect. Afonso 2023: does not support PRP. [verified]
  • Contrary signal: Liu 2025 meta (8 studies, 330 patients, includes unblinded) found return ~2.3 units faster (unit not stated in abstract; CI -4.11 to -0.47) and re-injury not significantly lower (RR 0.79, CI 0.34 to 1.83) (Liu 2025, DOI). [verified v2; unit still a gap] Blinded trials are null; open trials positive. [inferred]
  • Calf: "limited evidence" of quicker return with PRP (Hauer 2026). [verified, review statement]
  • Quad: platelet-poor plasma, 37 patients, return 30 ± 15 days, 1 recurrence; no control group (Kruse 2026). [verified]

3.4 Anti-inflammatories (NSAIDs): the debate

  • Review: early benefit of NSAIDs after muscle injury "not maintained", possibly a long-term repair deficit; evidence of harm to satellite cells (Mackey 2012). [verified]
  • But a human RCT pointing the other way: ibuprofen 1200 mg/day for 2 weeks before and 4 after an electrically induced thigh injury increased satellite cell activity and sped repair at 30 days vs placebo (Mackey 2016 FASEB J, DOI). [verified] Lab injury model, young men, not a sports strain. [inferred]
  • Topical NSAIDs for strains and sprains: NNT 3.9 for 50% pain relief at 1 week, side effects same as placebo (Moore 1998 BMJ, 86 trials, DOI); updated in Cochrane (Derry 2015, DOI). [verified]
  • Steroids: systemic methylprednisolone "markedly aggravated" crushed-muscle healing in rats (Pevec 2010, Zagreb). [verified, rats] Page line: "Short-term pain relief: yes. Healing: the data disagree. No good reason to take them for weeks." [inferred]

3.6 Return-to-play criteria (page box)

4.3 TB-500 / thymosin beta-4

  • Muscle injury raises Tβ4 locally; Tβ4 pulls muscle precursor cells in (Tokura 2010). [verified, mice]
  • Dystrophic mice, 150 µg twice weekly for 6 months: more regenerating fibres, no strength, heart or fibrosis improvement (Spurney 2010, DOI). [verified]
  • Exercise raises Tβ4 in human blood (it is an "exerkine"), but giving it to mice did not enhance muscle regeneration (Gonzalez-Franquesa 2021, DOI). [verified]
  • TB-500 is a 7-amino-acid fragment; human trials used the full protein for eye, heart and skin, not muscle (Achilles v2). [verified]
  • Best TB-500 animal result is tendon, not muscle (Bicer 2026, rat Achilles, see Achilles v2). [verified] Grade X for muscle: one positive mechanism, two weak or null outcome studies. [inferred]

4.4 IGF-1, IGF-1 LR3, MGF

  • IGF-1 (and bFGF) injected into lacerated mouse calf improved regeneration and twitch and tetanus strength at 1 month (Menetrey 2000, DOI). [verified] Same group later found growth factors also drive scar; hence the anti-scar work. [reported]
  • IGF-1 LR3 (the long-acting analogue people buy): no muscle injury study found. [verified absence, 2 searches]
  • MGF: the synthetic 24-amino-acid peptide sold as "MGF" corresponds to no product ever found in tissues or blood (Matheny 2010 review, DOI). [verified] Overexpressing MGF in injured mouse muscle delayed clearing of inflammatory macrophages with no obvious regeneration benefit (Sun 2018, DOI). [verified]

4.5 Follistatin

  • Follistatin-overexpressing mice regenerated more fibres with less fibrosis after injury (Zhu 2011). [verified] Decorin gene transfer raised follistatin and sped mouse muscle healing (Li 2007, DOI). [verified]
  • These are genetic models, not injected peptide. No injected-follistatin muscle injury study and no human data. [verified absence, 2 searches]
  • WADA: follistatin added to the List in 2019 under myostatin/activin inhibitors [reported, BSCG and UKAD headlines]; 2026 List: S4.3, myostatin-binding proteins [verified, 2026 List text via News Check v1]; 2027 wording not read [gap].

4.6 GH secretagogues (MK-677, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, tesamorelin)

  • MK-677, hip fracture, 161 patients, 6 months: IGF-1 +84% vs +17%; no significant function gain (Bach 2004, DOI). [verified]
  • MK-677, hip fracture, 123 patients, 25 mg/day: gait speed better; stair climbing and most measures not; stopped early for a congestive heart failure signal (Adunsky 2011, DOI). [verified]
  • Real GH in ACL reconstruction (pilot, 19 men, 6 weeks): +29% knee extension strength at the pilot threshold (p = 0.05, significance set at 0.10); no muscle volume difference (Mendias 2020, DOI; NCT02420353). [verified] A Danish trial is testing GH on connective tissue after muscle injury (NCT05746650, recruiting, completion 2027). [verified registry]
  • CJC-1295, ipamorelin, tesamorelin: no muscle injury or immobilisation study found. [verified absence for this pass] 2026 AJSM review: MK-677 linked to heart failure and insulin resistance; do not recommend (Tewari 2026, see Achilles v2). [verified]

4.7 MOTS-c and SS-31 (mitochondrial peptides)

  • MOTS-c, cancer cachexia mice: quad weight preserved +12% vs vehicle; did not stop body weight or fat loss (Jamnick 2026, DOI). [verified] Blood MOTS-c tracks fitness in young men (cross-sectional; Zhang 2026, DOI). [verified] No strain or immobilisation study. [gap] WADA: MOTS-c is prohibited at all times, S4.4.1, named as an AMPK activator alongside AICAR, on the 2026 List [verified via News Check v1]. HHS's draft policy lists MOTS-c acetate SC or IM among its 6 peptides [verified, News Check 2].
  • SS-31 (elamipretide), 14-day hindlimb immobilisation in mice: prevented fibre atrophy (Min 2011, DOI). [verified] After spinal cord injury in mice, no protection at 7 or 14 days (Graham 2021, DOI; Graham 2022, DOI). [verified] Heart failure rats: better muscle force and less atrophy (Vahle 2026, DOI). 4.8 GLP-1 drugs and lean mass (the opposite direction)

  • Network meta-analysis, 22 RCTs: lean mass ~25% of weight lost; semaglutide 2.4 mg and tirzepatide 15 mg least lean-sparing (Karakasis 2024, DOI). [verified]
  • Annals 2026 systematic review, 35 primary RCT-based studies (median 26 weeks, median 78 people): median 28.3% (IQR 15.9 to 39.9%) of weight lost was muscle-related tissue; about two thirds exceeded benchmarks; no study measured physical function; too varied for meta-analysis (Batsis 2026, DOI). 7. Options (page section, in evidence order) [inferred from sections 3 and 4]

  1. Get it graded if there was a pop, big bruise, gap or major weakness (avulsion, pec, distal biceps need a surgeon early).
  2. Start loading within days, lengthening-focused for hamstrings (Askling protocol); progress by pain and function, not calendar.
  3. Add agility and trunk work before return; it is what cut re-injury.
  4. Return on criteria (van der Horst 2017 list).
  5. Prevention: Nordic curls 2 to 3 sets of 8 to 12, 2 to 3x/week in season, or FIFA 11+; the hard part is actually doing them.
  6. During a cast or brace: move what you can; expect ~5% thigh muscle loss in the first week (measured over 7 days only); neither protein nor creatine reliably stopped it; plan the rebuild.
  7. Pain: topical NSAID short term is well supported; long oral courses are debated.
  8. Skip PRP for hamstring strains (best trial null).
  9. Peptides: no human muscle result yet, and the only registered muscle trial is not credible (4.2a). If someone still chooses, the honest framing is "experimental, banned in sport, unknown product quality; FDA found no muscle study, no cancer testing and no immune testing".
  10. Pec or distal biceps deformity: see a surgeon within days; about 90% get back to sport after repair at about 6 months, and delay costs strength (1.2a). [verified]

8. Usual questions (evidence answers; 15 from v1, all updated where needed, plus Q16 in v2)

  1. How long will my hamstring take? MRI grade 1 ~18 days, grade 2 ~24 days in elite football (wide spread, SD about 2 weeks); median 22 days in cricket; much longer if the tendon is involved (31 vs 73 days in sprinters). Calf ~13 to 25 days in pros, 6 to 12 weeks for recreational tennis leg; a dead leg ~7 days. [verified]
  2. Why does it keep tearing? Repeat injuries are 16% of all muscle injuries; recurrent-injury rate ~5.7 times the new-injury rate in one trial's controls; biceps femoris recurs in 18% vs 2% for the other hamstrings; the scar sheet is still 5 times larger a year later. [verified; ratio inferred]
  3. Rest or move? Move early. Lengthening rehab got footballers back in 28 vs 51 days. [verified]
  4. Do Nordic curls work? Probably, especially against repeat tears (NNT 3 for recurrent injury in Petersen 2011); stricter reanalyses call the overall effect inconclusive. [verified]
  5. Is PRP worth it? Not for hamstring strains: double-blind NEJM trial null at 6 months and 1 year. [verified]
  6. Should I take ibuprofen? Short-term pain relief yes; long-term healing effect disputed (one review suggests harm, one human RCT suggests faster regeneration). [verified]
  7. Does BPC-157 heal muscle? In rats, yes, in one lab's studies (plus one independent 2-hour ischaemia study). In humans, unknown. FDA's 2026 review found no human muscle study, and the only registered hamstring trial comes from a sponsor whose sister record calls itself fictional. [verified]
  8. Does TB-500 heal muscle? Weakest case of the popular peptides for muscle: an independent mouse study found no faster regeneration. [verified]
  9. Do I inject near the injury? No human data. Rat BPC-157 worked by injection into the belly cavity, cream and drinking water, which argues against "must be local". [verified animal routes; inferred]
  10. What dose? None established in humans. Rat studies used 10 µg/kg/day (and far lower); no registered human musculoskeletal trial states its dose; FDA's toxicology was in rats and dogs only. [verified]
  11. Will I lose muscle while injured? Yes: ~5.5% of thigh volume in 7 days of bracing; injured hamstring/calf 9.4% smaller at 3 months and still smaller at 12. [verified]
  12. Can creatine, protein or HMB stop it? Protein: no (RCT). Creatine: size not protected on imaging, maybe better regain. HMB: small effect in older adults on bed rest, untested after strains. [verified]
  13. Do GH peptides or IGF-1 speed recovery? MK-677 raised IGF-1 but did not improve most function after hip fracture and raised a heart failure signal; IGF-1 injections helped mice in 2000. Genetically higher IGF-1 is linked to slightly more breast, prostate and colorectal cancer. [verified]
  14. I'm on a GLP-1; does it matter? Yes: ~25 to 28% of GLP-1 weight loss is muscle-related tissue, on top of disuse loss. GLP-1s are not banned in sport, only monitored. [verified; WADA status reported]
  15. Will it get me banned? Yes, at all times. 2026 List: BPC-157 S0; TB-500/Tβ4 and IGF-1 S2.3; GHRH analogues and GH secretagogues S2.2.4; follistatin S4.3; MOTS-c S4.4.1 [verified]. 2027: "peptides" in general are now named in S0 [reported, USADA]. A NZ rugby player got 2 years for importing BPC-157 for an injury. GLP-1s and collagen are allowed. [verified case; GLP-1 reported]
  16. Should my torn pec or biceps be operated on? Usually yes, and early: about 90% return to sport after repair; non-operative distal biceps keep only ~63% supination strength vs ~89% after repair. [verified]
