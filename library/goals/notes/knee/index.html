0a. [v2] Natural-history baseline (what happens without peptides; put this near the top of the page)

Every peptide story has to beat these numbers, because most knees in this group improve anyway. - Torn ACL, rehab first (KANON, 121 young adults, mean 26): at 11 years, rehab-first and early surgery scored the same (difference 1.6 points on a 100-point scale; the smallest change patients notice is about 9); 52% of the rehab-first group had eventually been reconstructed, so about half never needed surgery (Lohmander 2023, PubMed abstract). [verified] In COMPARE, 50% of the rehab arm had surgery by 2 years (Reijman 2021). [verified] The ligament itself: in KANON, 16 of 54 (30%) of everyone offered rehab first, and 16 of 30 (53%) of those who never had surgery, showed a continuous ACL on MRI at 2 years (Filbay 2022). [verified] - Meniscus tear without locking, physio vs surgery: in ESCAPE (321 people aged 45 to 70), knee function rose 20.4 points with 16 physio sessions and 26.2 with keyhole surgery over 2 years; the gap (3.6) was inside the non-inferiority margin; 29% of the physio group later chose surgery (van de Graaf 2018). [verified] Keyhole trimming equalled sham surgery in FIDELITY (Sihvonen 2013). [verified] In young adults (DREAM, 18 to 40) the gap was not clearly different from zero (Damsted 2024). [verified] - Knee osteoarthritis, the placebo response: across 73 placebo-controlled injection trials (5,895 people), saline injections into the knee produced clinically meaningful pain and function gains at 1, 3 and 6 months, peaking at 4 to 8 months, with responder rates above 50%; the effect was bigger in trials with more women and in newer trials (Previtali 2025 EFORT Open Rev). [verified] In STEP 9, the placebo group's pain fell 27.5 points on a 100-point scale over 68 weeks (Bliddal 2024); in TRIUMPH-4, placebo pain fell 40.3% [reported]. [verified/reported] - MCL sprain: exercise therapy "success" 70 to 98% across 26 reports; return to sport 3 to 12 weeks (Svantesson 2024). [verified, very low-quality evidence per the authors] - Jumper's knee: 12 weeks of decline squats matched open surgery at 12 months (Bahr 2006); but over 15 years half of affected athletes quit their sport (Kettunen 2002). [verified] - What this means for anecdotes [inferred]: a knee that "felt better within weeks" on a peptide is the expected course for MCL sprains, meniscus flares and OA flares. Lee 2021's 14 of 16 "better" sits inside the >50% responder rate saline gets in OA injection trials. Only a controlled trial can separate the two.

0. Page summary (the lines a reader should leave with; v2 adds lines 9 and 10)

  1. Most knee problems in this group get better with structured rehab: half of young adults with a torn ACL never needed reconstruction in two randomised trials, with the same results at 2, 5 and 11 years. [verified]
  2. For meniscus tears without locking, physio equals surgery in people 45 to 70 and in young adults 18 to 40, and keyhole meniscus trimming equals sham surgery. [verified]
  3. Ligament inside the joint (ACL) heals poorly because joint fluid dissolves the healing clot; ligament outside the joint (MCL) usually heals on its own in weeks. [verified/reported]
  4. After ACL reconstruction 81% return to some sport but only 55% to competitive level; roughly 1 in 4 young athletes who go back to pivoting sport injure an ACL again. [verified]
  5. Knee osteoarthritis: semaglutide (STEP 9) cut pain more than placebo, mostly via weight loss; PRP and hyaluronic acid did not beat placebo in the best trials; repeated cortisone thinned cartilage. [verified]
  6. BPC-157's only human knee data is one uncontrolled phone survey: 17 people injected, 16 reached, 14 said pain improved, 9 said relief lasted 6 months or more. Every other BPC-157, TB-500 and GHK-Cu knee finding is in rats. [verified, v2 full text]
  7. Collagen peptides have the strongest human knee data of anything called a "peptide" here: small but real pain reductions in randomised trials. [verified]
  8. [v2] Community reports outside Reddit (v1 + v2): 12 people on BPC-157/TB-500 for a knee (10 better, 2 no change, 0 worse); 10 people on collagen (10 better). Both clear the 5-person CONSISTENT bar for "knee pain felt better", but no single diagnosis does, and 0 of the knee reports had a doctor or scan confirm a peptide effect. In the same threads, non-knee reports run about 38 better, 11 no change, 4 mixed, 2 worse. [verified count of what I read]
  9. [v2] The new weight-loss drugs are the biggest knee-pain movers in trials: retatrutide cut knee pain by about three quarters in TRIUMPH-4 (company data), semaglutide by 41.7 vs 27.5 points in STEP 9. Placebo also helps a lot in knee OA. [verified/reported]
  10. [v2] Bracing a torn ACL to make it heal (Cross Bracing Protocol) is unproven: one controlled cohort found 70% recurrent instability vs 2.5% after surgery; the randomised trial reports around 2029–2030. [verified]

1. The conditions (so readers can match their symptoms)

1.1 ACL tear (anterior cruciate ligament)

  • What it is: the ligament in the middle of the knee that stops the shin sliding forward and twisting under the thigh. Usually torn without contact: landing, cutting, pivoting, or a sudden stop. [reported]
  • Symptoms: a pop, fast swelling within hours (blood in the joint), a knee that "gives way" when turning. Many can walk within days, which fools people into thinking it is minor. [reported]
  • Who: KANON trial participants were young active adults, mean age 26, 74% male (Lohmander 2023 NEJM Evidence). [verified] Female athletes are commonly cited at 2 to 8 times the ACL rate of males in jumping and cutting sports [reported; primary not retrieved this pass].
  • In her circle's sports: basketball (jumping, cutting) and BJJ. In IBJJF competition, athletes exposed to the heel hook had 26.5 knee injuries per 1,000 matches vs 2.2 unexposed, about 12 times higher (Piekarski 2026 Sports Health). [verified] Knee was the single most common injured location in US emergency visits for martial arts, 9.7% of 8,400 cases (Bickley 2023 Injury). [verified] Padel: no ACL figure found; the main padel review does not discuss ACL. [verified absence in that review]
  • Imaging: MRI confirms the tear and shows bone bruises, meniscus and cartilage damage. Clinical tests (Lachman, pivot shift) are standard. [reported]
  • Natural history (no surgery): In KANON, 16 of 30 people managed with rehab alone (53%) showed a continuous ACL on MRI at 2 years, and they had better 2-year scores than both non-healed and reconstructed knees (Filbay 2022 BJSM). [verified] But at 11 years, ACL continuity on the 5-year MRI was linked to worse scores than reconstruction (adjusted difference about 15 to 20 points) (Filbay 2025 AJSM). [verified] So "my ACL healed on the scan" is not the end of the story.
  • Cross Bracing Protocol: an Australian method that braces the knee at 90 degrees for weeks so the torn ends can touch and heal. One 2026 case: an elite female Australian rules footballer, complete rupture, normal-looking ACL on MRI by 36 months, back to elite play at 1 year (Mead 2026 Clin Case Rep); MRI criteria to pick who may heal were published in 2025 (Filbay 2025 JSAMS). [verified] One case, no control; the RCT is not yet reported. [gap]
  • [v2] Cross Bracing Protocol, the wider evidence (4 Nov 2026):
  • Controlled cohort against it: Porter & Shadbolt 2026, Clin J Sport Med: 80 patients aged 16 to 40 with an acute isolated ACL rupture who play pivoting sport, at one private Australian practice; 40 chose surgery, 40 chose the bracing protocol. At 2 years the braced group had recurrent instability in 70% vs 2.5%, new medial meniscus tears in 62% vs 2.5%, and worse scores on every patient questionnaire (all p < 0.001). [verified abstract] Not randomised: patients picked their own treatment. [verified]
  • Rebuttal: the protocol's developers wrote a letter titled "Critical methodological flaws" in reply (Filbay, Mead, Cross 2026, Sep 2026). [verified title; letter body not read, gap]
  • More case reports: a US college soccer player (Frazier 2026, Curr Sports Med Rep). [verified title; no abstract]
  • The RCT: EMBRACE, NCT06956339, University of Melbourne: 180 people aged 16 to 40, MRI-confirmed complete ACL rupture, baseline questionnaire within 16 days of injury; 12 weeks in a brace, 23 physio visits and MRI at 3 and 18 months vs surgery within 8 weeks. Primary outcome KOOS4 at 18 months. Recruiting since Aug 2025; primary completion Nov 2029. It excludes torn ends gapped 8 mm or more with displaced tissue, and requires an anticoagulant (rivaroxaban) during bracing, which signals the clot risk of weeks in a fixed brace. [verified registry]
  • Page wording [inferred]: "Some torn ACLs can reconnect, but whether a brace makes that happen reliably is being tested now. Early comparative data were poor."

1.2 Meniscus tear

  • What it is: two C-shaped cartilage shock absorbers between thigh and shin bone. Young people tear them by twisting (traumatic); older people get gradual fraying (degenerative). [reported]
  • Symptoms: pain on the inside or outside joint line, swelling the next day, catching. True locking (the knee gets stuck and cannot straighten) is the one symptom that changes the plan toward surgery; the big trials excluded it. [verified exclusion criterion in ESCAPE]
  • Imaging trap: in a random sample of 991 people aged 50 to 90, MRI showed a meniscus tear in 19% of women 50 to 59 up to 56% of men 70 to 90, and 61% of people with a tear had no knee pain in the past month (Englund 2008 NEJM). [verified] A tear on MRI after 40 is often not the cause of the pain.
  • Padel: medial meniscus problems and MCL injury are listed among the common padel knee injuries, without percentages (Aspetar Journal review). [verified]
  • Natural history: both trial arms improve a lot (see 2.2). Forum anecdote: one 47-year-old reported a meniscus tear took two years to settle without treatment, now about 95% (Rokslide meniscus thread). [reported, anecdotal]
  • Why it heals poorly: only the outer rim has a blood supply; the inner part relies on joint fluid. [reported; primary vascular study not retrieved this pass, gap] Removing meniscus (meniscectomy) is the most consistent risk factor for arthritis 10+ years after an ACL injury (Lie 2019 BJSM; Shah 2026 AJSM). [verified]

1.3 Patellar tendinopathy (jumper's knee)

See Tendon Base v1, section 2 (patellar column) for loading plan, cortisone, PRP and shockwave results. - Symptoms: pain just below the kneecap on jumping, landing, squatting, stairs; often "warms up" then hurts later. [reported] - How common: 613 Norwegian elite athletes: 14.2% had it currently; volleyball 44.6%, basketball 31.9%, none in cycling; mean symptom duration 32 months (Lian 2005 AJSM). [verified] Padel lists it as a common cause of knee pain, males 2 to 4 times more often (Aspetar review). [reported within that review] - Natural history: 15-year follow-up: 53% of athletes with jumper's knee had quit their sport because of it, vs 7% of controls; symptoms were mild but lasting (Kettunen 2002 AJSM). [verified, small: 20 vs 16 responders] - Surgery vs exercise: open tenotomy equalled 12 weeks of decline-board eccentric squats at 12 months (VISA from 30 to 70 in both); 5 of 20 exercise knees went on to surgery (Bahr 2006 JBJS). [verified] This fills Tendon Base gap 3.

1.4 MCL sprain (medial collateral ligament)

  • What it is: ligament on the inside of the knee, injured by a blow or force pushing the knee inward (valgus): tackles, BJJ guard passes, padel lunges. [reported]
  • Grades: I (stretched), II (partial), III (complete). Grade III often comes with an ACL tear. [reported]
  • Natural history: a 2024 systematic review (26 reports, 1,912 patients) found exercise therapy "success" in 70 to 98%; return to sport 3 to 12 weeks; about 10 days missed for grade I and 35 days for grade II in football data (Svantesson 2024 BMJ OSEM, PMC full text). [verified via full text; evidence rated very low quality by the authors] Femoral-side tears tend to do well; tibial-side can be harder. [verified in same review]
  • With ACL: nonoperative grade I to II MCL plus ACL reconstruction gave the same 2-year scores as isolated ACL reconstruction (34 vs 119 patients) (Bae 2025 J ISAKOS). [verified] Elite soccer and rugby (238 knees): 95.4% returned, mean 12.0 months, whether the MCL was treated or not (Abdul 2026 AJSM). [verified]

1.5 Knee osteoarthritis

  • What it is: wear and remodelling of joint cartilage, bone and lining; pain on loading, morning stiffness under 30 minutes, swelling. [reported]
  • Link to sport injury: 44% of KANON participants (injured at mean 26) had X-ray osteoarthritis by 11 years (Lohmander 2023). [verified] In ACL-deficient knees at mean 11 years, 37.8% had X-ray arthritis vs 35.2% after reconstruction (5.0% in the other knee); moderate to severe 18.1% vs 12.8% (Shah 2026). [verified] So reconstruction does not clearly prevent arthritis.
  • Imaging: X-ray grade (Kellgren-Lawrence) for diagnosis; MRI shows bone marrow lesions and synovitis. Pain and X-ray severity correlate poorly. [reported]

1.6 Post-surgery recovery (ACL reconstruction, meniscus surgery, knee replacement)

  • ACL graft: a reconstructed graft "ligamentises" over many months; return to sport clearance usually 9 to 12 months (see 2.1). [reported]
  • Second trauma: reconstruction keeps inflammatory signals (IL-6, IL-8, TNF) in joint fluid higher at 4 and 8 months than rehab alone (KANON synovial fluid analysis 2017, OAC). [verified] Rehab-alone knees also showed less synovitis on MRI at 2 and 5 years (Roemer 2021 Radiology). [verified]
  • Quad weakness is the main limiter after ACL surgery; quad symmetry before return cuts re-injury (Grindem, 2.1). [verified]

1.7 Why ligament and cartilage heal poorly (one paragraph for the page)

  • ACL (inside the joint): bleeding happens, but the fibrin-platelet clot that normally bridges a wound never forms or is dissolved: joint fluid carries plasmin, and injury increases its activator. Cells can still multiply and migrate; the missing piece is the scaffold (Boston Children's Sports Medicine Research Lab summary). [reported; primary Murray papers not retrieved, gap] This is why bracing protocols and scaffold surgery try to keep torn ends together. [inferred]
  • [v2] Primary sources now read (retag to [verified]):
  • No bridge forms: 23 ruptured and 10 intact human ACLs examined under the microscope: the torn ACL goes through inflammation (to 3 weeks), surface repair (3 to 8 weeks), and new vessels and cells (8 to 20 weeks), like other dense connective tissue, but at no time was there tissue bridging the two torn ends; a synovial layer grows over each stump and may block repair (Murray 2000, JBJS Am). [verified]
  • Plasmin in the joint fluid: in 55 people with a swollen knee at least 3 weeks after injury, joint fluid had more plasmin-antiplasmin complexes and urokinase than their blood, i.e. clot-dissolving activity is made locally inside the joint (Rość 2002, Med Sci Monit). [verified abstract; no DOI listed] The lab's own review names "premature loss of provisional scaffold" as the mechanism (Vavken & Murray 2010). [verified]
  • The early joint reaction is destructive: in pigs, enzymes that break down cartilage (MMP-1, MMP-13, ADAMTS-4) rose in synovium and ligament within days of ACL transection, and cartilage breakdown fragments in joint fluid nearly doubled by day 5 (Haslauer 2013); inflammation peaked at day 1, the repair phase began around day 9 (Haslauer 2014). [verified, animal] Timing of any treatment may matter. [inferred by the authors]
  • Proof the scaffold idea works in people: BEAR (a protein sponge placed between stitched ends) vs hamstring reconstruction, 100 patients median age 17, 2:1 randomised: same patient scores and laxity at 2 years, much better hamstring strength (98% vs 63%), but 14% vs 6% needed a second ACL operation (p = 0.32) (Murray 2020, AJSM). [verified] Faster early symptom recovery (Barnett 2021). [verified] A 200-patient multicentre trial vs patellar-tendon reconstruction (BEAR-MOON) is under way (design paper 2022). [verified]
  • MCL (outside the joint): surrounded by soft tissue with blood supply, it forms a normal clot and heals, which is why rats are a good MCL model and why most MCL sprains need no surgery. [inferred from the above plus Svantesson 2024]
  • Cartilage: no blood vessels, few cells; adult damage is mostly not replaced. [reported] Tendon renewal: see Tendon Base 1.1.

2.3 Patellar tendinopathy

Exercise loading first line; surgery = eccentrics (Bahr 2006, above); progressive tendon loading beat eccentrics (Breda 2021); PRP null; shockwave null; cortisone short gain then relapse. All in Tendon Base v1, section 2. [verified there]

2.4 MCL

Brace for grade II to III as needed, early weight bearing, range of motion and strength; surgery mostly for grade III with valgus instability in full extension or a tibial "Stener-like" lesion (Abdul 2026 indications; Wright 2023 review). [verified] Grade III combined with ACL reconstructed: 87.5 to 90.6% returned to previous activity. [verified, Wright 2023]

[verified] No cartilage, ACL or meniscus animal study found. [verified absence]

  • A TB-500 phase 1/2 trial in heart disease is recruiting (NCT07487363), same sponsor as the hamstring trial; no knee trial. [verified registry]

3.4 GHK-Cu

  • Rat ACL reconstruction, 2015 (J Orthop Res): 72 rats; weekly knee injections of 0.3 or 3 mg/ml from week 2 for 4 weeks. At 6 weeks: less knee laxity (p = 0.009) and a stiffer graft at the low dose. At 12 weeks: no difference. No difference in pull-out strength, gait or microscopy. The effect stopped when the injections stopped. [verified]
  • No human knee data. A topical GHK-Cu skin-wound phase 2 trial is recruiting (NCT07437586). [verified registry]

3.5 Collagen peptides (strongest human "peptide" data for knee pain)

  • Liang 2024 (Osteoarthritis Cartilage): 35 RCTs, 3,165 people with OA; pain SMD -0.35 (small to moderate, moderate certainty), function SMD -0.31 (high certainty); no more side effects than control. [verified]
  • Simental-Mendía 2024 (Clin Exp Rheumatol): 11 RCTs, 870 knee OA patients; pain -13.6 points, function -6.5, high heterogeneity. [verified]
  • Active young adults, no disease: 5 g/day specific collagen peptides for 12 weeks: 139 athletes, activity pain fell 19.5 vs 13.9 mm (p = 0.046) (Zdzieblik 2017); replication in 180 people aged 18 to 30: -21.9 vs -15.6 mm (p = 0.024), physician scores agreed (Zdzieblik 2021 Nutrients). [verified] Differences of about 6 mm on a 100 mm scale are small. [inferred] One co-author is from an industry-linked collagen institute. [verified affiliation]
  • Collagen synthesis mechanism: 15 g vitamin C-enriched gelatin 1 hour before 6 minutes of skipping doubled a blood marker of collagen production (8 men, crossover) (Shaw 2017 AJCN). [verified]
  • UC-II (undenatured type II collagen), 40 mg daily: 8 small RCTs suggest pain and function gains (Kumar 2023; Gupta 2025, industry-affiliated author). [verified]
  • A completed 150-person RCT of collagen peptide vs glucosamine/chondroitin (NCT07746466) has no results read. [gap]
  • [v2] Registry checked 4 Nov 2026: single centre in Karachi, open-label (no blinding), adults 40+ with Kellgren-Lawrence grade 1 to 3 OA, 12 weeks, primary = WOMAC total and EQ-5D; completed Aug 2025; no results posted, no paper found. Even when published it compares two supplements without placebo, so it cannot show collagen beats nothing. [verified registry]

3.6 Growth hormone axis

  • Real rhGH after ACL surgery (Mendias 2020 AJSM): pilot RCT, 19 men 18 to 35, twice daily from 1 week before to 5 weeks after surgery. IGF-1 doubled; knee extension strength +29% vs placebo (p = 0.05, with the pilot's looser 0.10 threshold); MMP3 (cartilage breakdown marker) 36% lower; no change in muscle size or patient scores. [verified]
  • GH secretagogues (CJC-1295, ipamorelin, MK-677): no knee data; MK-677 linked to congestive heart failure and insulin resistance in the AJSM 2026 review. [verified] WADA S2. See Tendon Base 1.5/1.6.
  • IGF-1 injected into knees: no human trial found this pass. [gap]

3.7 KPV and LL-37

  • KPV is a 3-amino-acid end piece of α-MSH. α-MSH analogue reduced senescence markers, oxidative stress and IL-6 in human OA chondrocytes in a dish, with sex differences; mice lacking its receptor (MC1R) aged worse (Schäfer 2026 Aging Dis). [verified] α-MSH did not harm fracture healing in mice (Graue 2025). [verified] No KPV knee study exists; extrapolating from α-MSH is [speculated].
  • LL-37: mice missing their version (Cramp) got worse OA (Choi 2025 IJMS); in rheumatoid arthritis, antimicrobial peptides can drive synovitis and act as autoantigens (Anania 2026 review). [verified] Direction for injection is unclear; a pharmacy news piece says LL-37 was expected to stay on the FDA restricted compounding list for safety reasons (Pharmacy Times, Jun 2026). [reported]

3.8 Pentosan polysulfate (not a peptide; included because people group it with peptides)

  • Phase 2 RCT (Skerrett 2026 Arthritis Res Ther): 61 people with moderate to severe knee OA pain; injections under the skin once or twice weekly for 39 days vs placebo. Primary outcome was joint-fluid markers: ARGS (cartilage breakdown) fell 57% at day 56 and 74% at day 168 vs placebo; most other markers not significant. Run by the drug's developer. [verified] Clinical pain results not in abstract. [gap]
  • Oral pilot (Liu 2023): 38 people, open-label, 10 mg/kg every 4 days in two 5-week cycles; pain 6.4 to 3.6 out of 10 at week 16; most common side effect was positive stool blood test. [verified] No control group.
  • Case report: bone marrow oedema resolved on MRI after IM injections (Sampson 2017). [verified, n = 1, developer co-author]
  • Long-term oral use (bladder indication) has a reported eye (maculopathy) warning. [reported; primary not read this pass, verify before publishing]
  • [v2] Primary sources read (retag [verified]): the first case series described 6 adults (median age 60) on oral pentosan for interstitial cystitis with a new pigment-layer damage pattern at the back of the eye; median cumulative dose 2,263 g over a median 186 months (15.5 years); main symptom difficulty reading and slow dark adaptation (Pearce 2018, Ophthalmology). [verified] A US claims cohort (3,012 users vs 15,060 matched controls) found no link at 5 years but higher odds of macular disease at 7 years (OR 1.41, 95% CI 1.09 to 1.83) (Jain 2019, Br J Ophthalmol). [verified] Reviews put required exposure at as little as 0.325 kg over 2.25 years, averaging 1 to 2 kg over 10 to 15 years, and prevalence in long-term users around 20%; the damage can progress after stopping (Abou-Jaoude 2021; Lindeke-Myers 2021). [verified] One urology review argues the link may not be causal (Proctor 2023). [verified, minority view]
  • Knee relevance [inferred]: the knee trials used short courses (39 days injected; two 5-week oral cycles), far below the doses linked to eye damage. The US label added a retinal warning in 2020 [reported; label text not read this pass]. For the page: "linked to eye damage after years of daily use for bladder pain; short knee courses have not been studied for this."

6. Options (ordered by evidence)

  1. Supervised, progressive rehab for every condition here; 12 weeks before judging. ACL: rehab-first is a trial-backed option. Meniscus without locking: physio first. Patellar: progressive loading (Tendon Base). MCL: brace as needed, early loading. [verified]
  2. Return-to-sport tests after ACL: wait 9+ months, pass strength and hop symmetry (>90%) before pivoting sports. [verified, Grindem]
  3. Weight loss for knee OA, including GLP-1 drugs where prescribed (STEP 9). [verified]
  4. Collagen peptides (5 to 10 g/day in trials; 15 g gelatin plus vitamin C in the mechanism study): low risk, small benefit. [verified dosing as used in trials]
  5. Surgery when indicated: locked knee, repeated giving way despite rehab, grade III MCL with instability, failed 3 to 6 months of loading. [verified from trial exclusions and failure analyses]
  6. Experimental: BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu: animal data only for ligament and cartilage; one uncontrolled human knee survey. Watch the 2027 hamstring and cuff trial results. [verified]
  7. Not supported by best trials: HA injections, PRP for knee OA, repeated cortisone, keyhole trimming of degenerative meniscus tears. [verified]

7. Fifteen usual questions (evidence answers)

  1. Do I need surgery for a torn ACL? Not necessarily. In two trials about half of people who started with rehab never needed surgery, and results matched early surgery at 2, 5 and 11 years. Younger, very active people with instability more often end up reconstructed. [verified]
  2. Can a torn ACL heal by itself? Sometimes on MRI: about half of rehab-only KANON knees looked continuous at 2 years. But at 11 years that "healed" look was linked to worse scores than reconstruction, so a healed scan is not a guarantee. [verified] [v2] That 11-year finding rests on 14 healed vs 10 non-healed knees (level 3 evidence), and the healed group had the least tibiofemoral arthritis (14%). Bracing to make it heal (Cross Bracing Protocol) is being tested in a 180-person trial; one comparison found 70% of braced knees stayed unstable vs 2.5% after surgery. [verified]
  3. How long until I can play padel/basketball/BJJ after ACL surgery? Most clearance is 9 to 12 months. Each month of waiting up to 9 months cut re-injury risk about 51%. [verified]
  4. What are my chances of getting back to my level? 81% return to some sport, 65% to the same level, 55% to competitive play. [verified]
  5. Will I tear it again? About 15% overall get a second ACL injury; about 23% of under-25s who return to sport. [v2 corrected] People who passed strength and hop tests before returning tended to re-injure less (6% vs 38%), but in that 106-person study the gap was not statistically certain (p = 0.075); waiting until 9 months and getting the thigh muscles symmetrical were the clearer protectors. [verified]
  6. Should I get my torn meniscus "cleaned up"? For tears without locking, physio matched surgery in 45 to 70 year olds and in 18 to 40 year olds, and trimming equalled sham surgery. [verified]
  7. My MRI shows a meniscus tear. Is that why it hurts? Maybe not. In people over 50, tears are common on MRI and 61% of people with one had no pain that month. [verified]
  8. How long does an MCL sprain take? Usually 3 to 12 weeks; about 10 days missed for grade I, about 5 weeks for grade II. Most need no surgery. [verified]
  9. Does BPC-157 heal ACL or meniscus tears? Unknown in humans. Rat studies show faster MCL healing; no rat or human ACL or meniscus study exists. The one human knee study was a phone survey (17 injected, 16 reached, 14 said pain improved, 9 said it lasted 6+ months; one 4 mg injection into the knee in most; no control, no scans). In OA injection trials, more than half of people respond to salt-water placebo. [v2] Forum reports: 10 of 12 people said their knee felt better on BPC-157 or TB-500, none confirmed by a scan or doctor. [verified]
  10. Does it help after knee surgery? No controlled human data. The first controlled post-surgery BPC-157 trial (shoulder) starts January 2027. One forum report had a surgeon note faster-than-usual recovery after knee replacement, alongside other treatments. [verified] [v2] One post-meniscus-surgery user noticed no change. Peptides around surgery have no bleeding, healing or infection safety data; tell the surgeon. [verified + inferred]
  11. Is collagen worth it for knees? It has the best human data among "peptides" here: small to moderate pain relief in 35 trials; in young active people the gain over placebo was about 6 mm on a 100 mm scale. Low risk. [verified]
  12. Does semaglutide help knee arthritis? In people with obesity, yes for pain (STEP 9: -41.7 vs -27.5 points), mostly through weight loss. It is not proven to rebuild cartilage. [verified] [v2] Retatrutide went further in its knee trial (pain down about 75% vs 40% on placebo, with 26 to 29% weight loss; company data, not yet peer reviewed) but more people stopped for side effects (12 to 18% vs 4%). Tirzepatide's knee trial (does it prevent knee replacement?) reports in 2027. [reported/verified]
  13. Are PRP or hyaluronic acid injections better than peptides? For knee OA, both failed to beat placebo by a meaningful amount in the best trials; HA had more serious side effects. [verified]
  14. Is cortisone bad for my knee? Injections every 3 months for 2 years caused more cartilage thinning and no extra pain relief vs saline. One injection for a flare is a different question not tested there. [verified + inferred]
  15. Can I use these if I compete? BPC-157, TB-500 and GH secretagogues are banned at all times by WADA; see the Tendon Base dated block. Trial participants who are drug tested were excluded from the hamstring trial for this reason. [verified] [v2] From 1 Jan 2027 WADA names "peptides" as a group in S0; TB-500, Tβ4, ipamorelin, GHRPs and IGF-1 sit under S2 (per USADA). Semaglutide and tirzepatide stay on the Monitoring Program, not banned; collagen is food. [reported, USADA 2027 advisory]
