0. One-screen summary

  • Hot flashes are a brain problem first: when estrogen falls, KNDy neurons in the hypothalamus overfire and release too much neurokinin B, which tells the body to dump heat. Infusing neurokinin B into women triggered flushes in 8 of 10; blocking its receptor cut flushes. [verified]
  • The proven fixes have big, clean numbers: oral HRT cuts hot flash frequency about 75% versus placebo (24 RCTs, 3,329 women). The two NK3 blockers cut about 2 to 3 more flashes a day than placebo. Testosterone helps low desire in postmenopausal women. Teriparatide and abaloparatide (both peptides, both approved) cut new spine fractures by about two thirds. [verified]
  • Placebo is huge here: women on placebo in HRT trials had 58% fewer flashes by the end. Any "it worked for me" story needs that in mind. [verified]
  • Peptides sold online (BPC-157, CJC/ipamorelin, GHK-Cu, epitalon, MOTS-c, kisspeptin, DSIP, Selank) have zero human menopause trials. MOTS-c has one mouse study after ovary removal. Epitalon has rat and monkey data. [verified absence on PubMed]
  • The approved or trial-grade peptides that touch menopause: teriparatide/abaloparatide (bone, strong), bremelanotide/PT-141 (desire, approved only before menopause), tesamorelin (belly fat, proven only in HIV), GLP-1s (weight and knee pain proven; hot flashes unproven, a tirzepatide RCT reports 2027), oxytocin vaginal gel (mixed; the best positive RCT is now marked retracted). [verified]
  • Community signal (v2 totals, v1 plus v2): on GLP-1s, 9 women said hot flushes eased or went (often within days to weeks), 4 said they got worse or came back (usually after big weight loss); 6 said joint pain eased; 6 said brain fog, sleep or anxiety eased. About 18 reported bleeding after menopause, the single most common "hormone" report; scans that were done came back normal. Zero clinicians confirmed a hot flush change. No first-hand non-vendor reports for BPC-157, GH peptides, epitalon, MOTS-c, kisspeptin, DSIP, Selank, NAD+ or oxytocin in menopause; one coach-reported client on CJC/ipamorelin/BPC-157. [verified counts, small self-selected sample]
  • FDA, 10 Nov 2025: boxed warnings on breast cancer, heart disease and dementia removed from HRT labels; the endometrial cancer box stays only on systemic estrogen-alone. 1. What peri and menopause are (stages, symptoms)

1.1 Stages in plain words

Doctors use the STRAW+10 staging system (Harlow et al., Menopause 2012), here as tabled by the Merck Manual [reported; primary PMC page blocked by a captcha]:

Stage What you notice Hormones How long
Early perimenopause (early transition) Cycles still come but length swings: a persistent difference of 7+ days between consecutive cycles FSH high but jumpy Variable
Late perimenopause (late transition) Gaps of 60+ days with no period FSH above 25 IU/L 1 to 3 years; hot flashes likely
Menopause The last period, confirmed after 12 months without one One day, seen in hindsight
Early postmenopause No periods FSH rises for about 2 years then plateaus About 2 years when flashes are most likely, then 3 to 6 more years
Late postmenopause Dryness and urinary symptoms grow Stable high FSH, low estradiol Rest of life

[reported via Merck; standard definitions, high confidence]

1.2 What is happening hormonally

  • Progesterone drops first. Cycles stop releasing an egg reliably, so the second-half progesterone rise goes missing. A 2026 perspective argues perimenopause is "a loss of cyclical predictability rather than a gradual hormone deficiency", with progesterone's calming brain effect going before estradiol becomes erratic (Radytė 2026, PMID 42519421, DOI) [verified as the authors' argument; authors work for a neurostimulation company].
  • Estradiol swings, then falls. In perimenopause estrogen can spike higher than normal, then crash; after menopause it stays low. [reported, standard physiology]
  • FSH climbs because the brain shouts louder at ovaries that answer less. In a 2017 study, healthy postmenopausal women averaged LH 30.8 and FSH 78.7 IU/L with estradiol under 50 pmol/L (George 2017, PMID 28186349, DOI) [verified].

1.3 Why hot flashes happen (KNDy neurons)

  • In the arcuate nucleus of the hypothalamus sit KNDy neurons, named for the three signals they carry: Kisspeptin, Neurokinin B, Dynorphin. Estrogen normally keeps them calm. When estrogen falls they overfire, release too much neurokinin B onto NK3 receptors in the brain's temperature centre, and the body reacts as if it is overheating: skin vessels open, sweat starts, heart speeds up (Torres, Nat Rev Endocrinol 2026, PMID 41981275, DOI) [verified]. The same review puts hot flashes at about 70% of people going through menopause [verified].
  • Proof in humans: 10 healthy women got 30-minute IV infusions of neurokinin B or salt water, blinded. 8 of 10 flushed on neurokinin B, 0 on placebo (P = 0.0007), with heart rate and skin temperature rising like a natural flush (Jayasena 2015, PMID 25683060, DOI) [verified].
  • Blocking it works: 11 postmenopausal women took an NK3 blocker (MLE4901) 40 mg twice daily for 7 days. In the 8 with flashes, flashes fell from 3.4 to 1.0 a day (P = 0.008) (Skorupskaite 2017, PMID 28380486, DOI) [verified]. This is the logic behind fezolinetant and elinzanetant.
  • How long: in SWAN (1,449 women with frequent flashes), median total duration was 7.4 years, and flashes lasted a median 4.5 years after the last period. Women whose flashes started early in perimenopause had them longest (median over 11.8 years) (Avis 2015, PMID 25686030, DOI) [verified].

1.5 Bone, muscle, fat and insulin: the rates

  • Bone: rapid bone loss starts about a year before the last period (Karlamangla 2022, SWAN, PMID 35373854, DOI) [verified]. Bone quality (trabecular bone score) was flat until 1.5 years before the last period, then fell 1.16% a year, easing to 0.89% a year from 2 years after; total loss over the 10 years around menopause was 6.3% in white women and 4.9% in Black women (Greendale 2020, PMID 31613958, DOI) [verified]. The common "about 2% bone density a year in the fast phase" figure was not re-read in a primary abstract this pass [reported, gap].
  • Fat and muscle: in SWAN, at the start of the transition the rate of fat gain doubled and lean mass started falling, until 2 years after the last period. Total weight did not speed up; the body swapped muscle for fat at a steady scale weight (Greendale 2019, PMID 30843880, DOI) [verified]. This is the cleanest answer to "I eat the same and my shape changed."
  • Insulin resistance: in 1,315 SWAN women, HOMA-IR rose 4.3% a year in the 4-year window from 1.5 years before to 2.5 years after the last period, versus 1.2% before and 0.9% after, adjusted for BMI change (Karlamangla, JCI Insight Oct 2026, PMID 42837175, DOI) [verified]. So menopause itself, not only age or weight, pushes insulin resistance.

2. Standard treatments with numbers

2.1 Hormone therapy (HRT / MHT)

  • Hot flashes: Cochrane review, 24 double-blind RCTs, 3,329 women: oral HRT cut weekly flashes by 17.9 more than placebo, a 75% reduction (95% CI 64 to 82%); severity odds ratio 0.13. Placebo groups improved 58% on their own (MacLennan 2004, PMID 15495039, DOI) [verified].
  • Breast cancer, the WHI numbers that matter: 20+ years follow-up of 27,347 women. Estrogen alone (women without a uterus) lowered breast cancer: annual rate 0.30% vs 0.37% (HR 0.78), and breast cancer deaths (HR 0.60). Estrogen plus the progestin MPA raised breast cancer: 0.45% vs 0.36% a year (HR 1.28), with no significant change in breast cancer deaths (Chlebowski 2020, PMID 32721007, DOI) [verified]. In plain numbers the combined-HRT excess is about 9 extra cases per 10,000 women per year [inferred from the two annual rates].
  • When to start: WHI review supports starting before 60 for bothersome symptoms without contraindications; not for preventing heart disease or dementia (Manson 2024, PMID 38691368, DOI) [verified].
  • Clots depend on the route: UK nested case-control, 80,396 clot cases: oral HRT raised clot odds 1.58x (combined 1.73x; equine estrogen plus MPA 2.10x); transdermal patches and gels showed no raised risk (OR 0.93) (Vinogradova 2019, PMID 30626577, DOI) [verified].
  • FDA label change, 10 Nov 2025: HHS and FDA removed boxed-warning language on heart disease, breast cancer and probable dementia from all menopausal hormone products (systemic and vaginal), dropped "lowest dose for shortest time", and kept the boxed warning for endometrial cancer only on systemic estrogen-alone products. Heart and breast information stays in the label, outside the box. Labels now suggest considering starting under 60 or within 10 years of menopause (Contemporary OB/GYN, 10 Nov 2025) [verified from trade press summary of the FDA release; FDA bulletin not opened]. The same page links later labeling actions dated 12 Feb 2026 and 30 Oct 2026 that I did not open [gap].
  • (v2) The release itself and what followed:
  • 10 Nov 2025: announced at a press conference with HHS Secretary Kennedy and FDA Commissioner Makary, after a July 2025 expert panel. FDA requested labeling changes from companies (it did not rewrite labels that day). Removed or recommended for removal from the box: heart disease, breast cancer, probable dementia, and the endometrial cancer line except on systemic estrogen-alone; the "lowest dose, shortest time" line dropped. For systemic HRT, labels are to support starting for moderate to severe hot flashes and night sweats under 60 or within 10 years of menopause; vaginal estrogen labels are to be condensed to the risks relevant to local use. Same day: first generic of conjugated estrogens in over 30 years approved, plus a non-hormonal hot flash drug (RAPS Regulatory Focus, 10 Nov 2025) [verified from RAPS; RAPS also says FDA is "retaining warnings" on heart and breast, which fits those warnings moving out of the box into the main label, inferred]. The non-hormonal approval is likely elinzanetant, approved two weeks earlier [inferred; dates do not match exactly, gap].
  • 12 Feb 2026: FDA release (White Oak dateline, reposted via GlobeNewswire): first 6 products approved with revised labels, covering four categories (combined estrogen plus progestogen, systemic estrogen-alone, progestogen-alone for women with a uterus on estrogen, vaginal estrogen). 29 companies had submitted proposed changes. Brand names not given (BioSpace repost of FDA release; ASCO Post) [verified].
  • 30 Oct 2026: 3 more products, all estradiol transdermal patches from two companies, relabelled; 9 products in total. FDA said it was not aware of an imminent nationwide shortage and that "patches are only one option" (Contemporary OB/GYN, 30 Oct 2026) [verified via trade press].
  • Plain read: as of 3 Nov 2026 most HRT products in the US still carry the old box on the physical label; only 9 have the new one. The exact new box wording was not found in any source read [gap].

2.2 NK3 blockers (non-hormonal, aimed at KNDy neurons)

  • Fezolinetant (approved US May 2023, not 2025 [reported; the brief's "2025" fits elinzanetant only]). SKYLIGHT 2: women with 7+ moderate to severe flashes a day; at 12 weeks, 30 mg cut 1.86 and 45 mg cut 2.53 more flashes a day than placebo; benefit seen from week 1 and held to week 52 (Johnson 2023, PMID 36734148, DOI) [verified]. A meaningful change for one woman is about 6 fewer flashes a day from baseline (pooled SKYLIGHT, 1,022 women; Morga 2024, PMID 38775925, DOI) [verified]. Sleep: 51% on 45 mg felt much or moderately better vs 34% on placebo (Shapiro 2024, PMID 38749864, DOI) [verified]. FDA added a boxed warning for rare serious liver injury in Dec 2024 [reported, multiple news headlines, e.g. HCPLive; not opened].
  • (v2) The FDA communication itself (FDA Drug Safety Communication, first posted 12 Sep 2024, boxed warning added 16 Dec 2024) [verified]:
  • Trigger: one post-marketing report of raised liver tests with signs of liver injury after about 40 days on the drug. Age, lab values and outcome not published on the page; no total case count given.
  • Testing: liver blood tests before starting, then monthly for the first 3 months, then at months 6 and 9.
  • Stop at once and call the prescriber for: unusual tiredness, nausea or vomiting, itching, pale stools or dark urine, yellow eyes or skin, belly swelling, pain under the right ribs. FDA says stopping may let liver function return to normal.
  • Elinzanetant (blocks NK1 and NK3; FDA approved 24 Oct 2025 (Contemporary OB/GYN) [verified via trade press]). OASIS 1 and 2 (about 800 women, baseline 13 to 16 flashes a day): 120 mg once daily cut about 3.2 more flashes a day than placebo at week 12, and improved sleep and quality of life (Pinkerton 2024, PMID 39172446, DOI) [verified]. OASIS 3, 52 weeks, 628 women: -5.4 vs -3.5 flashes a day at week 12 (difference 1.6); no liver toxicity, no endometrial thickening, no bone loss; treatment-related side effects 30% vs 15%, mostly sleepiness, fatigue, headache (Panay 2025, PMID 40920404, DOI) [verified]. Trade press says frequency fell 73.8% vs 47% placebo [reported]. Approved capsule strength and daily dose need checking against the label: trade press says 60 mg capsules, trials used 120 mg [gap].
  • (v2) Elinzanetant label (Drugs.com professional label) [verified]: dose is 120 mg (two 60 mg capsules) once daily at bedtime; 60 mg once daily with moderate CYP3A4 inhibitors; avoid strong CYP3A4 inhibitors, grapefruit juice and CYP3A4 inducers. No boxed warning. Liver: baseline ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase and bilirubin; do not start if ALT/AST or bilirubin is 2x the upper limit or more; recheck at 3 months; stop if transaminases go over 5x, or over 3x with bilirubin over 2x. Sleepiness and other nervous system effects 11.9% vs 3.5% on placebo (no driving until it settles). Seizures reported. Contraindicated in pregnancy. Most common in OASIS 3: headache 9.6% vs 7.0%, fatigue 7.3% vs 2.9%, dizziness 6.1% vs 1.9%, sleepiness 5.1% vs 1.3%. So the "no liver toxicity" line from OASIS 3 still comes with liver testing on the label [inferred].
  • Why this matters for the page: NK3 blockers are the first drugs built straight from the KNDy story, and they are the option for women who cannot take estrogen (e.g. after breast cancer). An OASIS 4 trial in women on breast cancer endocrine therapy is reported positive but is not on the label [reported].

2.3 SSRIs and similar

  • Paroxetine 7.5 mg (the only FDA-approved non-hormonal pill before NK3 blockers): two RCTs, 1,184 women; beat placebo on frequency at weeks 4 and 12, benefit held to 24 weeks (Simon 2013, PMID 24045678, DOI) [verified]. Abstract gives P values, not counts. SSRIs/SNRIs typically cut flashes less than HRT [reported, common knowledge, not re-verified].

2.4 Testosterone for low desire

  • Meta-analysis of 36 RCTs, 8,480 women: in postmenopausal women testosterone raised satisfying sexual events by about 0.85 a month, desire (SMD 0.36), pleasure, arousal, orgasm, and cut distress. Non-oral (patch, cream) is preferred because oral raised LDL. More acne and hair growth; no serious harms seen (Islam 2019, PMID 31353194, DOI) [verified]. Plain version: a real but modest gain, a bit under one extra good sexual experience a month.

2.5 Vaginal estrogen and other local options

  • First line for mild dryness is lubricants and moisturisers; prescription options are low-dose vaginal estrogen, vaginal DHEA, oral ospemifene (Menopause Society 2020, via News-Medical) [reported].
  • Breast cancer survivors: SEER-Medicare, 25,874 survivors 65+: vaginal estrogen users did not have worse overall or breast-cancer survival; early "lower death" numbers (HR 0.49) disappeared in a time-dependent model, so they were bias (Mitchel 2026, PMID 42361283, DOI) [verified].

2.6 Bone drugs (including two approved peptides)

  • Teriparatide (PTH 1-34, a 34 amino acid peptide): 1,637 women with prior spine fractures, 20 or 40 micrograms daily injection, about 21 months. New spine fractures 14% placebo vs 5% and 4% (relative risk 0.35 and 0.31); non-spine fractures 6% vs 3%; spine bone density +9 and +13 points (Neer 2001, PMID 11346808, DOI) [verified].
  • Abaloparatide (PTHrP analogue peptide): ACTIVE trial, 2,463 women, 80 micrograms daily injection for 18 months vs placebo vs open-label teriparatide 20 micrograms. Fewer new spine and non-spine fractures than placebo; hypercalcaemia 3.4% vs 6.4% with teriparatide (Miller 2016, PMID 27533157, DOI) [verified]. Exact fracture percentages sit in a table not in the abstract [gap].
  • Bisphosphonates, denosumab and romosozumab not re-searched this pass [gap].

3.1 Teriparatide and abaloparatide (bone) [A]

See 2.6. These are the strongest peptide story on the page: real peptides, injected, large fracture trials, approved. Worth leading the peptide section with them because readers rarely know bone drugs are peptides. [inferred]

3.2 Bremelanotide / PT-141 (desire) [A premenopausal, C after]

  • What it is: a melanocortin receptor agonist (MC4R), acts in the brain on desire, not on blood flow. [verified, MeSH terms]
  • RECONNECT: two phase 3 RCTs, 1,267 premenopausal women (mean age 39), 1.75 mg under the skin as needed. Desire score rose 0.35 more than placebo; distress fell 0.33 more (Kingsberg 2019, PMID 31599840, DOI) [verified]. The desire scale runs about 1.2 to 6, so the gain is small [inferred].
  • 52-week extension: 684 enrolled, 272 completed; nausea 40%, flushing 21%, headache 12% (Simon 2019, PMID 31599847, DOI) [verified].
  • Safety programme (3,500 people): small, transient but significant blood pressure rises on ambulatory monitoring; focal skin darkening in over a third of those dosed 16 days in a row, rare at label dosing (Clayton 2022, PMID 35147466, DOI) [verified].
  • After menopause: reviews state flibanserin and bremelanotide are approved for premenopausal HSDD, while transdermal testosterone is the evidence-based option for postmenopausal HSDD (Nappi 2022, PMID 35428435, DOI) [verified]. No published postmenopausal bremelanotide RCT found this pass [verified absence in PubMed search; earlier phase 2 work in postmenopausal women is rumoured but not traced, gap].

3.3 Oxytocin (vaginal atrophy, desire) [C]

  • Meta-analysis, 7 RCTs, 631 women: clinically assessed atrophy improved (RR 0.32) but no difference in painful sex, vaginal pH, maturation index or biopsy; the authors conclude their findings "show the opposite" of oxytocin being a viable estrogen alternative (Farahat 2023, PMID 37716966, DOI) [verified].
  • Swedish double-blind RCT, 157 women, 12 weeks: 400 IU oxytocin in a hypromellose gel vs the same gel without oxytocin. Both improved symptoms equally; oxytocin added nothing (Fianu Jonasson 2020, PMID 33116956, DOI) [verified]. Plain version: the moisturising gel did the work.
  • Retraction flag: the Egyptian RCT showing 47 of 70 improved on oxytocin gel vs 0 of 70 on placebo (Torky 2018, PMID 29347848, DOI) is tagged "Retracted Publication" in PubMed [verified]. It may be inside the meta-analysis above [speculated; check]. Do not cite it as evidence.
  • Registry: a 244-woman phase 3 vaginal oxytocin RCT (NCT06514586) is listed with unknown status, primary completion June 2025 [verified registry]. A 50-woman Egyptian trial (NCT05275270) completed 2021 [verified registry].
  • Nasal oxytocin for desire: 30 pre- and postmenopausal women with HSDD, 32 IU nasal spray before sex vs placebo: partner outcomes improved equally on oxytocin and placebo (Muin 2017, PMID 28189292, DOI) [verified]. The women's own results paper was not pulled [gap].

3.4 Kisspeptin [C desire, D hot flashes]

  • Desire: 32 premenopausal women with HSDD, 75-minute IV kisspeptin-54 (1 nmol/kg/h) vs placebo, crossover: changed brain activity to erotic videos and faces, linked to lower sexual aversion, no side effects (Thurston 2022, PMID 36287566, DOI) [verified]. Brain scans, not behaviour; not postmenopausal.
  • Hormones after menopause: postmenopausal women were resistant to a 24-hour continuous kisspeptin infusion (12.5 micrograms/kg/h); those on estradiol responded more over time (Lippincott 2017, PMID 28368443, DOI). A single bolus of kisspeptin-10 did raise LH and FSH more in postmenopausal women (George 2012, PMID 22956346, DOI) [verified].
  • Hot flashes: no human trial of kisspeptin for flashes exists (PubMed search returned 0) [verified absence]. Kisspeptin sits in the same KNDy neurons that drive flashes; the drugs that work block this system rather than add to it, so taking kisspeptin for hot flashes has no logic behind it [inferred]. Whether extra kisspeptin could worsen flashes is untested [speculated].

3.5 GLP-1s (weight, hot flashes, joint pain) [A/B/C]

From GLP1 Claims Hormones v1 section 7 [verified there], plus new: - Hot flashes: unproven. Scoping review says "limited studies" (PMID 41704988). Case report: a 51-year-old on oral estradiol had worse flashes and mood during semaglutide dose steps, fixed by slowing titration (Parker 2025, PMID 40729307) [verified]. Mayo tirzepatide vs placebo RCT, primary outcome hot flash frequency, 40 women, due Sep 2027 (NCT07218445) [verified registry this pass]. - HRT boosts GLP-1 weight loss? 16 women on HRT vs 90 not: 16% vs 12% weight loss at 12 months (Hurtado 2024, PMID 38446869) [verified in prior file]. Hypothesis only. - Joint pain (new): STEP 9, 407 people with obesity and knee osteoarthritis, 81.6% women, mean age 56: semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly for 68 weeks cut weight 13.7% vs 3.2%, and WOMAC pain 41.7 vs 27.5 points on a 0 to 100 scale (Bliddal 2024, PMID 39476339, DOI) [verified]. Not a menopause trial, but the population is largely menopause-age women [inferred]. - Mumsnet-flagged risks (section 4): post-menopausal bleeding and HRT absorption. The British Menopause Society advises raising oral progestogen for four weeks or switching to non-oral progestogen when using incretin drugs [reported, quoted by posters; BMS document not opened]. - (v2) BMS statement, April 2025 ("Use of incretin-based therapies in women using HRT", via GPnotebook summary) [verified secondary; BMS PDF not opened]: slowed stomach emptying may cut absorption of oral progestogen, extrapolated from pill data, not HRT data. Non-oral progestogen preferred throughout, at minimum for 4 weeks after starting and after each dose step; the 52 mg levonorgestrel coil is likely the most complete option (expert opinion). If staying oral, raise the dose temporarily for 4 weeks after starting and keep it higher through each step until the dose is stable. Persistent unscheduled bleeding after adjusting, or worrying features, needs assessment; consider earlier checks in high-risk women. No change needed with a combined patch, coil or vaginal progesterone (HealthEd, 24 Apr 2025) [verified]. The source data: one 5 mg tirzepatide dose cut the pill's peak level 55 to 66% and total exposure 16 to 23% [verified as reported there, from the manufacturer]. An Australian gynaecologist suggests 100 to 200 mg micronised progesterone for 4 weeks after starting and after each step [reported, his own suggestion, not BMS]. - (v2) HRT plus GLP-1, bigger data: a Mayo Clinic abstract at The Menopause Society meeting, Oct 2026, about 3,900 women aged 45+ (mean 57): at 1 year, semaglutide plus HRT lost about 11% vs 9.5% on semaglutide alone; 22% vs 18% lost 20% or more. Tirzepatide at 3 months 6.6% vs 5.9%. No hot flush data. Not peer reviewed (Powers Health / HealthDay, 21 Oct 2026) [reported]. A 2026 clinical review says the HRT plus obesity drug synergy data are small, retrospective and confounded, and HRT should not be prescribed for weight loss (Younglove 2026, PMID 41883510, DOI) [verified]. - Trials running: NCT06715514 (HRT plus GLP-1, glucose and energy, 96 women, Dec 2026), NCT07057310 (Mayo, postmenopausal physical and vascular health), NCT06975111 (Colorado, menopause transition, semaglutide arm, to 2029) [verified registry].

3.6 Tesamorelin and GH peptides (belly fat) [B in HIV, D menopause, E!]

  • Tesamorelin (a GHRH analogue, approved only for HIV-related belly fat): two phase 3 RCTs, 806 patients, 2 mg daily: visceral fat -15.4% vs placebo at 26 weeks, maintained to 52 weeks; under-skin belly fat unchanged; triglycerides -12%; IGF-1 up 108 ng/ml (Falutz 2010, PMID 20554713, DOI) [verified].
  • Healthy older adults: 152 adults aged 55 to 87 (MCI and healthy), tesamorelin 1 mg nightly for 20 weeks: better executive function (P = 0.005), body fat -7.4%, IGF-1 +117% (within normal range), fasting insulin +35% in the MCI group, mild side effects 68% vs 36% (Baker 2012, PMID 22869065, DOI) [verified]. Sexes mixed; no menopause subgroup in the abstract [gap].
  • CJC-1295, ipamorelin, sermorelin: no menopause RCT found. Blogs claim "modest" sleep and recovery benefits with no data (Yellow Bird newsletter, author a trainer and coach, affiliate links) [reported]. A 2026 AJSM review of these peptides found 67% of papers were animal and human data "modest improvements at best" (Tewari 2026, PMID 42578445, in Achilles Research v2) [verified there].
  • Why E!: these raise IGF-1, a growth signal; insulin rose in one trial group. Postmenopausal women are already gaining insulin resistance (1.5). Cancer risk from raised IGF-1 is a theoretical concern with no menopause data [speculated; see warnings].

3.7 MOTS-c [D]

  • Mice with ovaries removed (a menopause model): MOTS-c prevented weight gain and insulin resistance, reduced fat build-up and inflammation, kept brown fat active, via the AMPK pathway (Lu 2019, PMID 30725119, DOI) [verified]. No human menopause study. FDA's compounding advisory panel voted 7-5 in July 2026 to add MOTS-c to the compounding list (for obesity and osteoporosis), not binding (American Med Spa Association) [reported].

3.8 Epitalon (melatonin, cycle claims) [D]

  • Cycles: in female rats given a toxin that ages the reproductive system early, epitalon and epithalamin restored daily rhythms of brain chemicals that drive the ovulation surge (Arutjunyan 2012, PMID 23237594, DOI) [verified]. Rats, toxin model, one lab group.
  • Melatonin: in old female rhesus monkeys, epitalon raised night melatonin and improved glucose handling; no effect in young monkeys (Goncharova 2005, PMID 15664732, DOI) [verified]. Co-author is the peptide's developer [verified].
  • Humans: a 2026 narrative review: "biologically active but clinically unproven"; human evidence limited to small biomarker studies; no adequately powered RCT (Jain 2026, PMID 42884164, DOI) [verified]. A Russian double-blind study of epithalamin (the older pineal extract, not synthetic epitalon) in older women with heart disease reported lower blood pressure and better lipids (Merkur'eva 2008, PMID 18546838) [verified, abstract only, no numbers].
  • FDA advisory panel voted 7-4 in July 2026 to add epitalon to the compounding list, for insomnia [reported].
  • No study of epitalon on hot flashes, periods, or menopause timing in women found [verified absence].

3.9 DSIP and Selank (sleep, anxiety) [C-]

  • DSIP: 16 chronic insomniacs, 25 nmol/kg IV: slightly better sleep efficiency but "weak" effects partly due to a chance change in the placebo group; "not likely to be of major therapeutic benefit" (Bes 1992, PMID 1299794, DOI) [verified]. A 1987 crossover reached the same "little clinical significance" (Monti 1987, PMID 3583493) [verified]. FDA panel rejected DSIP (emideltide) for compounding in July 2026, 6-7 [reported].
  • Selank: 62 patients with generalised anxiety or neurasthenia, Selank vs the benzodiazepine medazepam: similar anxiety relief, no placebo arm (Zozulia 2008, PMID 18454096) [verified, Russian abstract]. No menopause data.

3.10 GHK-Cu and collagen (skin, hair, bone) [C]

  • Collagen and bone: 131 postmenopausal women with low bone density, 5 g specific collagen peptides daily for 12 months vs placebo: spine T-score +0.10 vs -0.03, hip +0.09 vs -0.01; bone formation marker up (König 2018, PMID 29337906, DOI) [verified]. A co-author is from a collagen research institute [verified affiliation]. Gains are small next to teriparatide's +9 points [inferred].
  • Peptides and skin: meta-analysis of 19 RCTs, 1,341 people, oral and topical peptides: better hydration and brightness, small wrinkle effect mostly from oral polypeptides, inconsistent on elasticity (Nukaly 2026, PMID 41924746, DOI) [verified]. Not menopause specific.
  • GHK-Cu: topical cosmetic data only; nothing injected, nothing menopause specific (Acne Research v1 section 3.1) [verified there]. Pro-blood-vessel growth effects (Endometriosis Research v1) matter for women with endometriosis history [inferred].

3.11 BPC-157 (joints) [D]

  • PubMed search for BPC-157 with ovariectomy, osteoporosis, estrogen or menopause returned 2 old papers, not about menopause [verified count; titles not opened]. No human menopause or joint-pain trial. A 2025 review cited by a clinician found 36 preclinical studies and one human trial overall (KevinMD, Apr 2026) [reported]. FDA panel voted to add BPC-157 to compounding list July 2026, final FDA sign-off pending [reported].

3.12 NAD+ [C]

  • 10-week double-blind RCT in postmenopausal women with prediabetes and overweight: NMN raised muscle insulin sensitivity (clamp-measured) and insulin signalling; placebo no change (Yoshino 2021, PMID 33888596, DOI) [verified]. Dose not in the abstract [gap]. No hot flash, mood or skin data. This is NMN by mouth; injected NAD+ has no menopause trial [verified absence not run; inferred].

5. Pairings and related conditions

5.1 What people pair

  • GLP-1 plus HRT is the dominant real-world pairing in the Mumsnet threads: 3 of 5 Mounjaro users with improvements were on HRT [verified count]. The only data on the pair is the small Mayo cohort (16 vs 90) [verified prior file].
  • HRT route switch with GLP-1: oral to transdermal estrogen, oral to vaginal or coil progestogen, per BMS guidance quoted on Mumsnet [reported].
  • Blogs pair HRT with "peptide stacks" (GH secretagogues, BPC-157, GHK-Cu), always with sales links [reported]. No data.

5.2 Endometriosis after menopause

  • European Menopause Society guide (2025): endometriosis can persist or appear after menopause; surgery preferred for symptomatic disease; estrogen-only HRT should be avoided even after hysterectomy; continuous combined HRT may lower recurrence and cancer change; NK3 blockers suggested for flashes when HRT is not used; avoid herbal products that may contain estrogen-like compounds (Erel 2025, PMID 40907339, DOI) [verified]. Link to the Endometriosis page.
  • Pro-angiogenic peptides (BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu) carry the same theoretical lesion concern after menopause as before [inferred, Endometriosis Research v1].

5.3 PCOS after menopause

  • Higher risk of heart attack in women with PCOS persists after menopause, and stroke risk is likely raised (Lee 2025, PMID 40907648, DOI) [verified]. Combined with the menopause rise in insulin resistance (1.5), this is a double hit for metabolic risk [inferred]. Link to the PCOS page.

5.4 Frozen shoulder

  • Peaks in women 40 to 60. Duke pilot, 1,952 menopausal women: frozen shoulder in 7.65% without HRT vs 3.95% with HRT (OR 1.99), not statistically significant (Reinke 2026, PMID 41614260, DOI) [verified]. Media reported this as HRT "may help" (Healthline) [reported]. Case report links low estrogen plus thyroid disease (Wise 2026, PMID 41773146) [verified]. Link to the Shoulder page.

5.5 Knee osteoarthritis and early menopause

  • Women with menopause before 40 had more knee osteoarthritis, knee pain and nerve-type pain than women with menopause at 45 to 50 (172 Japanese women, Tanaka 2026, PMID 42024254, DOI) [verified]. Link to the Knee page.

7. Fifteen usual questions

  1. Am I in perimenopause if my periods are still regular? Possibly early: cycle lengths that keep varying by a week or more mark the early transition; symptoms like night sweats can start before cycles change. [reported, STRAW+10]
  2. Can a blood test confirm perimenopause? Not reliably; FSH swings day to day in perimenopause. Diagnosis over 45 is usually by symptoms and cycle pattern. [reported]
  3. How long will hot flashes last? Median 7.4 years in total and 4.5 years after the last period; longer if they start early. [verified, SWAN]
  4. Is HRT safe now the FDA removed the warning? For women under 60 or within 10 years of menopause without contraindications, benefits usually outweigh risks. Combined HRT still adds about 9 breast cancers per 10,000 women a year; estrogen alone lowered breast cancer in WHI. [verified numbers; inferred framing]
  5. Patch or pill? Patches and gels did not raise clot risk; pills raised it 1.4 to 2.1 times. [verified]
  6. What if I can't take estrogen? NK3 blockers cut 2 to 3 more flashes a day than placebo; paroxetine 7.5 mg is another option. [verified]
  7. Do peptides help hot flashes? No peptide has a positive human hot flash trial. Kisspeptin works in the same brain circuit but the working drugs block it. [verified absence; inferred logic]
  8. Does Ozempic or Mounjaro help hot flashes? Unproven. Some women report flushes vanishing; others report them returning, and oral HRT may absorb less during dose steps. A placebo-controlled trial reports in 2027. [verified] (v2) In forum reports read so far, about 9 women said flushes eased (often within days to weeks) and 4 said they got worse, mostly after large weight loss; no doctor confirmed either. Bleeding after menopause was reported more often than either. [verified counts]
  9. Why is my belly growing when my weight is the same? Fat gain doubles and muscle starts falling at the transition while scale weight stays on trend. [verified, SWAN]
  10. Is PT-141 approved for me after menopause? No, only for premenopausal women with low desire. Expect nausea in about 4 in 10, and small blood pressure rises. [verified]
  11. Does testosterone work for women? Yes for postmenopausal low desire, modestly: under one extra satisfying sexual event a month on average. [verified]
  12. Is vaginal estrogen safe after breast cancer? A 25,874-woman cohort found no worse survival; decide with the oncologist. [verified]
  13. Do GH peptides (CJC/ipamorelin, sermorelin) reverse menopause body changes? No menopause trial. Tesamorelin cut belly fat 15% in HIV and body fat 7% in older adults, while raising IGF-1 and in one group insulin. [verified]
  14. Can collagen or GHK-Cu fix my skin? Small gains in hydration and some wrinkle measures; nothing restores what estrogen loss takes. [verified, inferred]
  15. What actually protects my bones? Weight-bearing exercise and, if needed, approved drugs. Two of the best are peptides: teriparatide cut new spine fractures from 14% to 5%. [verified]

8. Warnings (bottom of page)

  1. Breast cancer history: systemic HRT is generally avoided; NK3 blockers are the non-hormonal hot flash option; vaginal estrogen showed no survival harm in a large cohort but needs oncologist sign-off. Peptides raising IGF-1 (GH secretagogues, tesamorelin) have no safety data here. [verified/inferred]
  2. Clotting risk: oral estrogen raises clot odds 1.4 to 2.1x; transdermal did not. History of clots, stroke or clotting disorders needs specialist advice. [verified]
  3. Unopposed estrogen: with a uterus, estrogen must be paired with a progestogen to protect the womb lining; the FDA kept the endometrial cancer boxed warning on systemic estrogen-alone products. With GLP-1 drugs, oral progestogen may absorb less; BMS suggests a higher dose or a non-oral route. Any bleeding after menopause needs a scan. [verified/reported]
  4. Endometriosis history: avoid estrogen-only HRT even after hysterectomy (EMAS 2025). [verified]
  5. PT-141 / bremelanotide: transient blood pressure rise; caution with heart disease or uncontrolled blood pressure; skin darkening with frequent dosing; approved only before menopause. [verified]
  6. GH peptides and cancer: they raise IGF-1, a growth signal; one tesamorelin trial raised insulin 35% in one group; no long-term cancer data in women; avoid with active or past cancer [inferred/speculated]. MK-677 has been linked to heart failure risk (AJSM 2026 review) [verified in Achilles v2].
  7. Fezolinetant liver warning: boxed warning for rare serious liver injury added Dec 2024; liver tests are part of use [reported]. (v2) Liver tests before starting, monthly for 3 months, then at months 6 and 9; stop at once for tiredness, nausea, itching, dark urine, pale stools, yellowing or right upper belly pain [verified, FDA]. Elinzanetant has no boxed warning but still needs baseline and 3-month liver tests, and can cause sleepiness [verified, label].
  8. (v2) GLP-1s in midlife women: about 18 community reports of bleeding after menopause, some in women never on HRT; any such bleed needs a scan regardless of the drug. Fast weight loss reports include a knee insufficiency fracture with osteopenia and a worsened prolapse; strength training and protein matter more, not less, on these drugs [verified posts; inferred advice]. One woman's estrogen and testosterone levels rose on labs while on HRT, so HRT doses may need review as weight falls [reported].
  9. Unregulated products: BPC-157, MOTS-c and epitalon passed an FDA advisory vote for compounding in July 2026 but final FDA sign-off is pending; DSIP was rejected [reported]. Research-market vials have no quality guarantee, and the "peptides for menopause" web is dominated by sellers [verified by inspection].
  10. Retracted evidence: the most positive oxytocin vaginal gel RCT (Torky 2018) is retracted; claims built on it are unsupported. [verified]
  11. Placebo: women on placebo in hot flash trials improved 47 to 58%; a single good experience proves little. [verified]
