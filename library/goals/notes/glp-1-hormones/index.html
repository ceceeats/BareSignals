GLP1 Claims Hormones v1

Research pass for the "GLP-1 beyond weight loss" hub on baresignals.co. Topic: hormones, fertility, sex, reproduction. Run date: 3 Nov 2026. Sources: PubMed (via PubMed tool), ClinicalTrials.gov (via Clinical Trials tool), WebSearch/WebFetch. About 40 searches and fetches.

Tag key: [verified] = read in the primary source (abstract or registry record) this pass. [reported] = stated by a secondary source (news, press release, label summary) I could not trace to full primary data. [inferred] = my reasoning from verified data. [speculated] = hypothesis with no direct data. Verdict key: Proven / Likely / Plausible / Unproven / Contradicted. Drug classes: GLP-1 only (semaglutide = Ozempic/Wegovy, liraglutide, dulaglutide, exenatide). GIP/GLP-1 (tirzepatide = Mounjaro/Zepbound). Triple GIP/GLP-1/glucagon (retatrutide). Amylin (cagrilintide, eloralintide; CagriSema = cagrilintide plus semaglutide).

Main cross-cutting finding: almost every positive hormone claim below was studied in people with obesity, PCOS or type 2 diabetes, and almost none of the studies separate the drug from the weight loss. The one placebo trial in healthy lean men (dulaglutide) found no effect on sex hormones, desire or semen. That is the strongest hint so far that most "beyond weight loss" hormone effects are really weight and insulin effects. [inferred] Second cross-cutting finding: retatrutide, cagrilintide and eloralintide have no published human reproductive, sexual or pregnancy data that I could find. Anything said about them on this topic is extrapolation. [inferred, negative search result]

1. Fertility boost and "Ozempic babies"

The claim as people say it: "I got pregnant on Ozempic/Mounjaro, even on the pill." "GLP-1s make you more fertile." How common: The term started on TikTok; news coverage peaked in 2024 (Fox News, 16 Apr 2024, citing user videos; Healthline; The Week; ABC/6abc). [reported] No outlet gave a count of cases; it is anecdote-driven. [verified that no numbers were given, Fox News article] Fertility clinics now market "Ozempic babies" blog content (RBA, Advanced Fertility). [reported] Population signal: Australian GP data, 1.6 million women aged 18–49 (2011–2022): of 18,010 women newly prescribed a GLP-1, only about 21% had recorded contraception, and 2.2% were pregnant within 6 months; 90.5% of 2022 starters had no type 2 diabetes. (Grzeskowiak et al., likely Med J Aust 2025, DOI 10.5694/mja2.70026, via newsGP 10 Sep 2025) [reported; journal name not confirmed on page]

What studies show: - Human RCT, PCOS: semaglutide plus metformin vs metformin alone, 100 women with overweight/obesity and PCOS, 16 weeks, then metformin only to week 40. Natural pregnancy 35% vs 15% (P<0.05); weight loss 6.1 kg vs 2.3 kg. Open-label. Chen et al. 2025, PMID 40713699, DOI [verified] - Meta-analysis of RCTs, PCOS: 3 trials, 252 women, GLP-1 based therapy vs metformin. Overall pregnancy OR 1.41 (0.81–2.45, not significant); natural pregnancy OR 3.10 (1.74–5.49, moderate certainty); IVF pregnancy OR 1.01 (no effect). Kar et al. Oct 2026, PMID 42893679, DOI [verified] - Older RCT cited in review: liraglutide plus metformin 69.2% vs metformin 35.4% pregnancy rate. Becker et al. 2026 review, PMID 42441883, DOI [reported; original trial not traced this pass] - Male side: GLP-1s raise total testosterone and sometimes semen quality in men with obesity, but no adequately powered study has measured conception or live birth. Kaltsas et al. Sep 2026, PMID 42796084, DOI [verified]

Pill interaction (tirzepatide): One single-dose study, 40 women, tirzepatide 5 mg with an ethinyl estradiol/norgestimate pill: EE Cmax down 59%, norgestimate Cmax down 66%, AUC (total exposure) down about 20–21%, peak delayed 2.5–4.5 h. Source: UK faculty guidance summarising Skelley et al. 2024 (CoSRH PDF) [reported; primary PK paper not opened]. Label advice: switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier method for 4 weeks after starting and 4 weeks after each dose increase; non-oral hormonal methods are not expected to be affected (Lilly Medical) [verified]. Semaglutide contrast: subcutaneous semaglutide (39 women) and oral semaglutide (25 postmenopausal women) showed no clinically relevant change in pill exposure (same CoSRH summary) [reported]. Registry: Novo's PK trial of semaglutide plus NNC0480-0389 with a levonorgestrel pill, NCT05153564, completed 2022 [verified registry entry]. Plain point: the tirzepatide effect is about the pill being absorbed late and with a lower peak, mostly early in treatment and after dose steps. Vomiting and diarrhoea on any GLP-1 can also cause pill failure. [inferred]

Weight-independent or not: Mostly explained by weight loss and insulin improvement. Reviews say so directly ("primarily indirectly through weight reduction", Varra et al. 2026, PMID 42654342, DOI) [verified]. No trial in lean women. No study matched weight loss by diet against a GLP-1 for fertility. [inferred from negative search] The "Ozempic baby" story likely has three separate causes stacked: (1) restored ovulation from weight loss in women with obesity/PCOS, (2) reduced pill absorption on tirzepatide, (3) about 79% of starters not using contraception at all. Cause 3 is probably the largest. [inferred]

Mechanism in plain language: Excess fat and high insulin push the ovary to make more androgen and lower SHBG, which blocks ovulation. Losing weight and lowering insulin lets ovulation return. GLP-1 receptors exist in ovary and hypothalamus in animals, but a direct ovarian effect in humans is unproven. [verified in reviews PMID 42278283, DOI; direct effect speculated]

Which drug: Most fertility RCTs used liraglutide, exenatide or semaglutide. Tirzepatide: no fertility RCT result yet. The pill interaction is a tirzepatide label issue, not a semaglutide one. Retatrutide/amylin: no data. [verified/inferred]

Verdict: Fertility improves in women with obesity/PCOS: Likely (mainly via weight). Pill interaction with tirzepatide: Proven as a PK effect; actual pregnancy rate from it: Unproven. "GLP-1s raise fertility in normal-weight women": Unproven. Trials coming: NCT07326111 PERIODS (tirzepatide vs placebo, PCOS, 198 women, Germany; clinical pregnancy rate after stopping is a tracked outcome; completion Dec 2029) [verified]; NCT07242534 (semaglutide before IVF, embryo quality, 62 women, Dexeus, primary completion Feb 2027) [verified]; NCT06775093 (GLP-1 plus metformin before fertility treatment, 392 women, Peking) [verified]; NCT07179120 (semaglutide/tirzepatide for obesity-related male infertility, 180 men, not yet recruiting) [verified]. Negative mirror: Unplanned pregnancies while on a drug that is not recommended in pregnancy; animal data show fetal harm at exposure levels relevant to labels [reported in reviews]. Weight regain after stopping (STEP 4: +6.9% over 48 weeks; SURMOUNT-4: +14.0% over 52 weeks; Marchetti et al. 2026, PMID 42941232, DOI) [verified] can reverse cycle gains, which matters because labels advise stopping about 2 months before trying (semaglutide) [reported].

2. Endometriosis pain relief

The claim as people say it: "My endo pain dropped within weeks of starting GLP-1." "It's anti-inflammatory, so it calms endometriosis." How common: News pieces in May 2026 (Local10, WFSB) quote clinicians saying it "works" with no data cited [verified, Local10 cites no study]. Many telehealth blog pages push it [reported]. One structured survey exists (below).

What studies show: - Survey (preprint, not peer reviewed): GLEAM, 227 people with endometriosis/adenomyosis using any GLP-1. 63% met the improvement endpoint (95% CI 57–69%); 84% improved in at least one symptom domain. Weight loss did not predict improvement (adjusted OR 1.02 per % lost, p=0.22); higher starting BMI predicted less improvement (OR 0.94 per BMI unit). Normal-BMI subgroup with <5% weight loss: 12 of 14 improved. Baudoin and van Bakel, posted 14 Jul 2026, Research Square [verified on preprint page]. Big caveats: self-selected online respondents, no control group, recall bias; people who felt better are more likely to answer. [inferred] - Case report: 20-year-old, BMI 21, severe primary dysmenorrhea (not confirmed endometriosis), low-dose compounded semaglutide. Pain 12/10 to 3/10 within about a month with no weight change; symptoms returned during a 3-month break and improved again on restart. Tran et al., Cureus Jan 2025 (PDF) [verified]. One person, but the stop/restart pattern is a useful within-person signal. [inferred] - Human tissue (case-control): peritoneal fluid GLP-1 lower in 54 women with endometriosis vs 30 controls (p=0.009), with more of the enzymes that break incretins down on macrophages. Krasnyi et al. 2022, PMID 36142272, DOI [verified]. Not replicated. - Hypothesis paper: tirzepatide as add-on for refractory endometriosis; authors state the "peritoneal incretin deficiency" idea rests on that one study and direct validation is absent. Jacobsen et al. Jun 2026, PMID 42449952, DOI [verified] - No RCT. No published cohort with pain outcomes found. [verified by negative PubMed title search: only 4 papers with "endometriosis" in title plus incretin terms]

Weight-independent or not: The best weak evidence here actually points toward weight independence (lean case report, survey showing no weight link and better response at lower BMI). This is the one hormone-area claim where the weight explanation looks least likely, though the evidence is low quality. Note too that endometriosis is more common in lower-BMI women, so weight loss is a poor explanation by default. (Desai et al. 2026, PMID 41798441, DOI) [verified/inferred]

Mechanism in plain language: GLP-1 receptors sit on immune cells (macrophages) and in spinal pain-processing cells. Activating them lowers inflammatory signals (NF-kB, NLRP3, TNF, IL-6) in animal and cell models. GLP-1 drugs also slow the gut and may dampen visceral pain signals (an IBS cohort of 6,665 per arm showed lower abdominal pain codes, 31.6% vs 35.8%; Khan et al. 2026, PMID 42570075, DOI) [verified]. Applying this to endometriosis is [speculated].

Which drug: Reports mostly semaglutide. Tirzepatide is the subject of the hypothesis paper only. No data for retatrutide or amylin drugs. [verified/inferred]

Verdict: Plausible (upgrade from Unproven because of a within-person rechallenge case and a weight-independent survey pattern; still no controlled data). Trials coming: None found on ClinicalTrials.gov for GLP-1 drugs and endometriosis (searched 3 Nov 2026). [verified negative] This is a real gap. Negative mirror: GLP-1s can cause abdominal pain, nausea and constipation that may be confused with or mask endo flares [inferred]. Endometriosis medical care relies on hormones; delayed pill absorption on tirzepatide could lower effect of an oral hormonal endo treatment early on [inferred].

3. PCOS: periods return, ovulation, androgens

The claim as people say it: "My cycle came back for the first time in years." "My PCOS acne/hair got better." How common: Very common in PCOS online groups and fertility blogs [reported]. Not quantified this pass.

What studies show: - Meta-analysis, liraglutide, 7 RCTs, 330 women with overweight/obesity and PCOS: menstrual frequency up (Hedges g 1.76, 95% CI 0.28–3.24, high heterogeneity); LH and free androgen index down; SHBG up modestly; ovulation and pregnancy could not be pooled. Lu et al. Jan 2026, PMID 41508932, DOI [verified] - RCT semaglutide plus metformin (Chen 2025, above): more menstrual recovery, lower testosterone and CRP. [verified] - Large cohort (TriNetX target trial emulation): 48,126 women with PCOS per arm, pre-conception GLP-1/tirzepatide vs metformin. Composite adverse pregnancy outcomes HR 0.38 (0.34–0.41); type 2 diabetes HR 0.31. Henney et al. Oct 2026, PMID 42857939, DOI [verified]. Observational; an effect this large invites suspicion of residual confounding. [inferred] - Tirzepatide review: clear weight and waist reductions in PCOS; no proven metabolic superiority among agents; most trials short and at low doses, often with metformin. Koceva et al. Oct 2026, PMID 42948675, DOI [verified]

Weight-independent or not: Not separated. Every trial is in overweight/obese women. The review by La Vignera (PMID 42278283) says evidence is "restricted to overweight/obese populations" [verified]. No lean-PCOS trial found. Some reviews propose GLP-1 receptors on ovarian/granulosa cells (FOXO1, CXCL10 pathways) but mostly from animal and cell work [verified as stated in reviews; human relevance speculated].

Mechanism in plain language: Insulin acts like a second LH signal on the ovary, pushing it to make androgens. GLP-1 drugs lower insulin and body fat, SHBG rises, free androgen falls, and the follicle can mature and release. Possibly also a direct hypothalamic effect on LH pulses [speculated; no human pulsatility study found].

Which drug: Liraglutide has the most RCTs, semaglutide some, tirzepatide only metabolic data so far. No retatrutide or amylin PCOS data. [verified/inferred]

Verdict: Cycles and androgens improve in overweight/obese PCOS: Likely to Proven for menstrual frequency (meta-analysis of RCTs), Likely for ovulation. Effect in lean PCOS or beyond weight loss: Unproven. Trials coming: NCT07326111 PERIODS (tirzepatide vs placebo, 72 weeks, primary = menstrual bleeding ratio and weekly progesterone-confirmed ovulation, plus a 52-week off-drug follow-up to see if cycles last) [verified]; NCT05819853 (semaglutide restoring ovulation, youth and adults, 80, primary completion Feb 2028) [verified]; NCT07169136 (GLP-1 and neuro-reproductive function, adolescent PCOS, observational, 20) [verified]; NCT05646199 Hull and NCT06896981 Dhaka (semaglutide vs metformin, status unknown/completed, results not found) [verified registry]. Negative mirror: Off-drug durability unknown; PERIODS is the first to test it. Weight regain after stopping is well documented (see card 1). [verified/inferred]

4. Period changes (heavier, irregular, spotting)

The claim as people say it: "Mounjaro made my period heavier/early/weird." "Spotting after every dose increase." How common: Largest structured data: Reddit analysis of 410,198 posts (May 2019–Jun 2025), 67,008 users self-reporting semaglutide or tirzepatide use. Of all users: intermenstrual bleeding 266 (0.9%), heavy menstrual bleeding 261 (0.9%), irregular periods 211 (0.7%), menstrual disorder 178 (0.6%), painful periods 168 (0.6%). Authors note these would be higher in a women-only denominator and call it a possibly unrecognised effect. Sehgal, Tronieri, Ungar, Guntuku, arXiv 2603.12341 [verified; preprint]. Many consumer health pages discuss it (Ro, SingleCare, Medino) [reported].

What studies show: No RCT or cohort with menstrual pattern as a safety outcome found. PubMed search for GLP-1 plus abnormal uterine bleeding returned 0 hits [verified negative]. Lilly states no studies have measured tirzepatide's effect on estrogen, progesterone or gonadotropins (Lilly Medical) [verified].

Weight-independent or not: Unknown. Three non-exclusive explanations: (1) rapid weight loss changes estrogen made in fat tissue and can shift cycles (seen with any fast weight loss) [inferred]; (2) in women on the pill, tirzepatide's reduced pill peak can cause breakthrough bleeding, as with any missed pill [inferred]; (3) return of ovulation in previously anovulatory women means periods come back, sometimes heavy at first [inferred]. Explanation 2 would be drug-specific (tirzepatide > semaglutide) and timed to dose steps; nobody has tested this. [speculated]

Which drug: Reddit data pooled semaglutide and tirzepatide. No split found. [verified]

Verdict: That some users get period changes: Likely (consistent self-report signal). That the drug directly causes them, independent of weight loss or pill effects: Unproven. Trials coming: None with menstrual safety as an outcome. PERIODS (NCT07326111) tracks bleeding but in PCOS and without the pill. [verified] Negative mirror: This card is the negative mirror of card 3: the same mechanism that restores cycles in PCOS may disrupt them in women with normal cycles. [inferred]

5. Libido and erectile function

The claim as people say it: Two opposite camps. "My sex drive came back with the weight loss / better confidence." And "Ozempic killed my libido" / "erection problems since starting." How common: Reddit analysis: decreased libido in 161 of 67,008 users (0.6%) [verified, arXiv 2603.12341]. A netnographic study of social posts found libido changes in both directions, up (body image, weight) and down (Merhi 2026 review, PMID 41870138, DOI) [verified as summarised].

What studies show: - Placebo RCT, healthy lean men: dulaglutide crossover, no effect on sexual desire, HPG hormones or semen (as summarised in Merhi 2026, PMID 41870138) [reported; original trial not traced this pass]. - Animal: exendin-4 in specific reward brain areas (VTA, nucleus accumbens shell) reduced sexual behaviour in rodents (Merhi 2026) [verified as summarised]. - FAERS: reports of ED, low libido and orgasm problems exist, but disproportionality was not significant (Merhi 2026) [verified as summarised]. - Harm signal, cohort: non-diabetic men with obesity aged 18–50 on semaglutide vs matched non-users, n=3,094 per arm (TriNetX). New ED or PDE5 prescription 1.47% vs 0.32% (RR 4.5, 2.3–9.0); testosterone deficiency 1.53% vs 0.80%. Able et al. 2024, PMID 38778151, DOI [verified]. Big caveat: controls were men who never got semaglutide, so men on semaglutide had more doctor contact and more chance to be diagnosed or prescribed (surveillance bias). [inferred] - Harm signal, better design: men with type 2 diabetes, GLP-1 vs DPP-4 inhibitor (active comparator), 4,910 vs 5,524. ED 35.2 vs 28.0 per 1,000 person-years, HR 1.26 (1.08–1.46); association lost significance after negative-control calibration. Tang et al. Apr 2026, PMID 42005929, DOI [verified] - Benefit signal, cohort: men with type 2 diabetes, tirzepatide vs sitagliptin, semaglutide, dulaglutide. ED or PDE5 RR 0.70, 0.67 and 0.55 respectively, all favouring tirzepatide. Cowart et al. 2025, PMID 40614622, DOI [verified] - Benefit, men with obesity and low T: improvements in erectile function reported alongside testosterone rises (reviews PMID 42587729, 42748969) [verified as summarised]. A network meta-analysis of 23 RCTs found only testosterone plus lifestyle significantly improved IIEF scores; GLP-1 therapy did not reach significance. Yang et al. Aug 2026, PMID 42704281, DOI [verified] - Women: "near-complete absence of data" (Klotz 2026, PMID 42748969, DOI) [verified]; case reports of female sexual side effects exist (Merhi 2026) [reported; not traced].

Weight-independent or not: The healthy-lean-men placebo trial says no direct effect in men without metabolic disease. Benefits seen in men with obesity are best explained by weight, insulin and testosterone. A possible direct negative effect on desire through brain reward circuits is real in rodents and fits the "less wanting overall" experience people describe with food and alcohol. [verified/speculated]

Mechanism in plain language: GLP-1 drugs turn down the brain's "wanting" system, which is why food and alcohol cravings drop. Sex drive uses overlapping reward circuits, so some people may feel it dim. On the other side, weight loss raises testosterone and improves blood vessel function, which helps erections. Which one wins likely depends on the person's starting point. [inferred from verified pieces]

Which drug: Harm signals mostly semaglutide; benefit signal strongest for tirzepatide (one cohort). Whether GIP adds anything is [speculated]. No retatrutide or amylin data; amylin also acts on brain reward areas, so a similar libido question is open [speculated].

Verdict: ED harm: Unproven (signals weaken in better-designed studies). ED/libido benefit in men with obesity: Plausible, mostly via weight and testosterone. Libido drop through brain reward: Plausible (animal evidence, low human report rate). Women: Unproven (almost no data). Trials coming: None with sexual function as primary outcome found. NCT07179120 (male infertility) may report IIEF [inferred, not confirmed]. Negative mirror: Covered above (RR 4.5 cohort, HR 1.26 cohort).

6. Male testosterone

The claim as people say it: "My T went up on Ozempic/Mounjaro." Sometimes "GLP-1 is a natural TRT alternative." How common: ENDO 2025 press coverage (NBC News, Healthline, News-Medical) [reported].

What studies show: - ENDO 2025 conference abstract: 110 men with obesity or type 2 diabetes on semaglutide, dulaglutide or tirzepatide, 18 months; share with normal total and free testosterone rose from 53% to 77%, alongside about 10% weight loss. Not peer reviewed; no control group stated. (Endocrine Society release) [verified on release] - Systematic review, 10 studies, 639 men: total testosterone consistently up in obese/diabetic/hypogonadal men; free testosterone inconsistent (SHBG rises too); LH and FSH preserved or increased (unlike TRT, which suppresses them); no change in healthy men. Deameh et al. Jan 2026, PMID 41498523, DOI [verified] - Systematic review, 29 studies: mean total T increase 2.5–5.2 nmol/L in obesity-related functional hypogonadism, "largely mediated by weight loss and improvement of insulin resistance rather than direct androgenic action." RCTs small (n=12–42), 12–24 weeks. La Vignera and Condorelli Aug 2026, PMID 42653816, DOI [verified] - Systematic review, 8 studies, including one placebo crossover RCT in healthy eugonadal men with no endocrine effect. Zafrani, Khan, Zitzmann Jul 2026, PMID 42587729, DOI [verified] - Registry: NCT06489457 semaglutide vs testosterone in men with T2D, obesity and functional hypogonadism, 25 men, completed 2023 (Ljubljana); NCT03619330 liraglutide vs TRT, 30 men, completed 2015 [verified registry]. Results not traced. - Pilot: in 10 hypogonadal men who lost <5% on tirzepatide, adding testosterone improved lean mass and IIEF vs tirzepatide alone. Seminara et al. 2026, PMID 41801155, DOI [verified]. n=5 per arm.

Weight-independent or not: Answered as well as this field gets: no effect in healthy lean men (placebo-controlled), effect scales with metabolic dysfunction. Explained by weight and insulin. [verified across 3 reviews]

Mechanism in plain language: Belly fat converts testosterone to estrogen and inflames the brain's hormone thermostat, which then sends a weaker signal (LH) to the testes. Lose the fat and that brake comes off, so the body makes more of its own testosterone. Because the body makes it, the testes keep working, which is why sperm production is not shut down the way it is on TRT. [verified in reviews, simplified]

Which drug: Data mostly liraglutide and semaglutide; tirzepatide included in reviews with similar direction; no head-to-head. No retatrutide or amylin data. [verified/inferred]

Verdict: T rises in men with obesity/T2D: Proven (consistent across reviews). Independent of weight: Contradicted for healthy men (no effect), and unshown for others. "Fertility-sparing alternative to TRT": Plausible. Trials coming: NCT05586802 (Klinefelter, semaglutide arm, primary completion Apr 2027) [verified]; NCT07179120 (male infertility, semaglutide/tirzepatide, 180 men) [verified]. Negative mirror: TriNetX semaglutide cohort showed more testosterone deficiency diagnoses (1.53% vs 0.80%, PMID 38778151), likely surveillance bias [verified/inferred]. Lean mass loss on GLP-1 is a concern in hypogonadal men (Antonič and Zitzmann 2025, PMID 41399135, DOI) [verified]. Isolated reversible drops in sperm quality reported (Pereira et al. 2025, PMID 41024420, DOI) [verified as summarised].

7. Menopause symptoms and hot flashes

The claim as people say it: "My hot flashes got better on Wegovy." Also the reverse: "hot flashes started with Mounjaro." And "HRT makes GLP-1 work better." How common: Reddit analysis: "hot flush" in 151 of 67,008 users (0.5%), chills 1.1%, feeling cold 0.6% [verified, arXiv 2603.12341]; these are side-effect reports, so the reverse direction. Positive hot flash claims are mostly telehealth marketing [reported].

What studies show: - Scoping review: "limited studies" report improved vasomotor symptoms; no numbers in abstract. Graczyk and Bisschops Jan 2026, PMID 41704988, DOI [verified; underlying studies not traced] - Case report (negative mirror): 51-year-old on oral estradiol had worse hot flashes and mood during semaglutide dose increases, resolved when titration slowed. Hypothesis: delayed gastric emptying lowers oral estrogen absorption. Parker Dec 2025, PMID 40729307, DOI [verified] - HRT plus semaglutide cohort: 16 women on HRT vs 90 not; weight loss at 12 months 16% vs 12% (adjusted, significant). Hurtado et al. 2024 (Mayo), PMID 38446869, DOI [verified]. Tiny HRT group. Spanish Menopause Society 2026 calls it "hypothesis-generating" and says HRT should not be started to boost weight loss (PMID 42664617, DOI) [verified] - ECO 2026 real-world data, more than 34,000 menopausal women: semaglutide vs HRT users, migraine risk 42–45% lower, depression 25% lower; hot flashes not reported. (Khaleej Times, 12 May 2026) [reported; conference data, not peer reviewed] - Lilly: no data on tirzepatide with oral HRT [verified].

Weight-independent or not: Unknown. Higher body fat is linked to more hot flashes in some cohorts, so weight loss could help indirectly [inferred; not verified this pass]. The only randomized test, the Mayo tirzepatide pilot, is not finished.

Mechanism in plain language: Hot flashes come from the brain's temperature centre (KNDy neurons in the hypothalamus) misfiring when estrogen falls. GLP-1 receptors sit in the hypothalamus too, so a direct effect is possible but unstudied [speculated]. A practical opposite effect is known: slower stomach emptying may reduce oral estrogen absorption during dose steps [verified case; mechanism plausible].

Which drug: Semaglutide has most menopause data (observational). Tirzepatide trial ongoing. Nothing for retatrutide or amylin. [verified]

Verdict: Hot flash relief: Unproven. Worse symptoms on oral HRT during titration: Plausible (one case, consistent mechanism). HRT boosting weight loss: Plausible (small cohort). Trials coming: NCT07218445 (tirzepatide vs placebo, primary = hot flash frequency and severity at 24 weeks, 40 women, Mayo, primary completion Sep 2027) [verified]; NCT06975111 (menopausal transition, semaglutide arm among others, 200, Colorado, to 2029) [verified]; NCT06715514 (HRT plus GLP-1 on glucose and energy, 96, completion Dec 2026) [verified]; NCT07057310 (semaglutide/tirzepatide physical and vascular health, postmenopausal) [verified]. Negative mirror: Hot flush listed as a self-reported side effect on Reddit (0.5%) [verified]; muscle and bone loss concern in postmenopausal women (Pereira et al. 2026, PMID 42772087, DOI) [verified].

8. Breastfeeding questions

The claim as people say it: "Can I use Ozempic while breastfeeding to lose baby weight?" "It's a big molecule so it doesn't get into milk." Also "my supply dropped." How common: Frequent question on postpartum forums and in telehealth FAQs [reported, not quantified].

What studies show: - Milk study: 8 women on subcutaneous semaglutide, milk sampled at 0, 12, 24 h. Semaglutide not detected in any sample (detection limit 1.7 ng/mL). Worst-case relative infant dose 1.26%, under the usual 10% safety line. Diab et al. 2024, PMID 39275201, DOI [verified] - Systematic review of diabetes drugs in milk: only 5 of 20 drugs have human milk data; semaglutide is one; 78% of guidelines advise against antidiabetic drugs while breastfeeding, and only 4.4% of recommendations rest on clinical evidence. Richardson et al. Feb 2026, PMID 41670332, DOI [verified] - 36-study safety review: lactation data "sparse"; one PK study with no transfer. Ozbek et al. Mar 2026, PMID 41885132, DOI [verified] - Milk supply and composition: no published data. [verified negative] - Tirzepatide in milk: not found this pass. [gap]

Weight-independent or not: Not applicable as a benefit claim. The supply question is about weight loss itself (rapid calorie restriction can lower supply) and reduced food intake, so any effect would likely be via weight and intake [inferred].

Mechanism in plain language: Semaglutide is a large peptide bound tightly to blood albumin, so very little can pass into milk; any that reached the baby's gut would mostly be digested. Oral semaglutide carries an absorption enhancer (SNAC) and may behave differently, which the Colorado trial explicitly excludes for that reason. [verified for trial rationale; rest inferred]

Which drug: Only subcutaneous semaglutide measured. Nothing for tirzepatide, retatrutide, or amylin drugs. [verified/inferred]

Verdict: "Semaglutide barely enters milk": Likely (n=8). "Safe for the infant": Unproven (no infant outcome data). Effect on supply: Unproven. Trials coming: NCT07720414 (GLP-1 and dual agonists, milk production rate and macronutrients, 18 women, Colorado, primary completion Jun 2028) [verified]; NCT07797036 (postpartum semaglutide after gestational diabetes, 226, starts Sep 2027) [verified]. Negative mirror: Labels and most guidelines still advise against use while breastfeeding [verified via Richardson review]. Reduced maternal intake could affect milk nutrients [speculated].

9. Miscarriage and pregnancy exposure

The claim as people say it: Two fears: "Ozempic causes miscarriage" and "I got pregnant on it, will my baby be harmed?" How common: FAERS (US adverse event reports) 2004–2023: 354 pregnancy cases, 1,671 reports, steep rise since 2012; spontaneous abortion and pre-eclampsia were the strongest disproportionality signals. Zhou et al. Dec 2024, PMID 39658840, DOI [verified]. Spontaneous reports cannot give rates and over-capture bad outcomes. [inferred]

What studies show: - Prospective, 6 teratology services: 168 GLP-1 exposed first-trimester pregnancies vs 156 with diabetes and 163 overweight/obese. Major birth defects 2.6% vs 2.3% and 3.9% (no increase); pregnancy loss 23% vs 26% and 29% (no increased risk). Dao et al. 2024, PMID 38663923, DOI [verified] - Multinational, women with type 2 diabetes: 938 GLP-1 exposed infants; major malformation adjusted RR 0.95 (0.72–1.26) vs insulin. Cesta et al. JAMA Intern Med 2024, PMID 38079178, DOI [verified] - Meta-analysis, 286,599 women (43,577 exposed): malformations RR 1.02 (0.96–1.08); preterm, SGA, stillbirth not raised; miscarriage comparable in the one study that reported it. Liu et al. Jul 2026, PMID 42447044, DOI [verified] - Meta-analysis, 7 cohorts, >40,000 exposed: no significant increase in malformations (first trimester OR 1.39, 0.73–2.65), stillbirth, spontaneous abortion, SGA, preterm. Urinary tract malformation OR 1.24 (1.05–1.47) from unadjusted data only. Uysal et al. Jul 2026, PMID 42420519, DOI [verified] - Largest meta-analysis: 10 studies, 2,118,215 pregnancies, 8,325 exposed around conception: no clearly detectable increase in miscarriage, stillbirth, anomalies, preterm birth and other outcomes. Med, 29 Sep 2026 (City St George's) via Pharmaceutical Journal [reported; university page failed to load, primary paper not opened] - Semaglutide-only review, 1,128 exposed pregnancies: mixed; one study 23% miscarriage comparable to controls; no consistent birth defect signal. Mandal et al. 2025, PMID 41313865, DOI [verified] - Expert guidance: existing human data do not support recommending termination after inadvertent exposure; stop about 2 months before planned pregnancy. Amiri et al. Jun 2026, PMID 42262435, DOI [verified]

Weight-independent or not: Not a benefit claim. Key confounder: obesity and diabetes themselves raise miscarriage and malformation risk, which is why studies compare with diabetic or obese controls. [verified design]

Mechanism in plain language (for the fear): Animal studies at high exposures show fetal loss and growth problems, partly from mothers eating much less. Human data so far show no matching signal for early exposure. Continued use through pregnancy is barely studied. [verified in reviews/inferred]

Which drug: Most data are liraglutide, semaglutide, dulaglutide, mostly in diabetic women. Tirzepatide pregnancy data are thin (included in a few pooled reviews only). No retatrutide or amylin human pregnancy data. [verified/inferred]

Verdict: "GLP-1 exposure around conception causes miscarriage or birth defects": Contradicted for early, inadvertent exposure to GLP-1-only drugs (large, consistent, observational). Safety of use through pregnancy: Unproven. Tirzepatide/retatrutide/amylin: Unproven. Trials coming: No RCTs in pregnancy (ethically unlikely). Pregnancy registries are the source; I did not confirm specific registry IDs this pass [gap]. Negative mirror: FAERS miscarriage signal (above); unadjusted urinary malformation signal (Uysal); semaglutide stopping in pregnancy linked to macrosomia and neonatal low sugar in one study, a reminder that stopping also has effects (Mandal review) [verified].
