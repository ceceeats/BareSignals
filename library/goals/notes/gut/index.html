0. Quick verdict (for the page summary)

  • The best peptide evidence in the gut is for approved peptide drugs: linaclotide and plecanatide (IBS with constipation), teduglutide (short bowel). These are real peptides with phase 3 data. [verified]
  • BPC-157 was literally born in the gut (isolated from a gastric juice protein) and has a huge rat literature on ulcers, colitis, fistulas and anastomoses, almost all from one Zagreb group. Its only patient trial (ulcerative colitis enema, 53 people, 2 weeks) did not beat placebo by a clear margin. [verified, FDA briefing]
  • KPV works well in mouse colitis when swallowed (it rides a gut transporter, PepT1). Zero human gut trials. [verified]
  • Larazotide (a "leaky gut" tight junction drug) is the cautionary tale: one positive phase 2, then the phase 3 was stopped. [verified]
  • GLP-1 drugs have a surprising and growing observational signal in IBD and IBS, plus a real but small risk of gastroparesis and bowel obstruction. [verified] v2: a 2026 claims study found no drop in hard IBD relapse (PMID 42309434), while patient reports for IBS-D are overwhelmingly positive (20 of 21 on Mumsnet), usually at the cost of constipation. [verified study; reported community]
  • Glutamine (an amino acid, not a peptide) has one striking RCT in post-infection IBS-D with proven leaky gut: 80% vs 6% responders. Needs replication; the placebo was whey protein, which may have made placebo look worse (3.10). Forum users split, 3 of 8 worse. [verified]
  • "Leaky gut" is real as a measurable thing in subsets (post-infection IBS, IBS-D, IBD, celiac). The zonulin blood test used by wellness clinics is not reliable: most commercial kits don't detect zonulin at all. [verified]

1. The conditions (what each is, plain language)

1.1 Biology in plain words

  • Gut lining: one cell thick. Cells are stitched together at the top by tight junctions (proteins like occludin, claudins, ZO-1). These seams decide what slips between cells. Under them sits a mucus layer (MUC2) that keeps bacteria at arm's length, and the gut's own antimicrobial peptides, like LL-37 / cathelicidin, live in that mucus. [verified, Cobo 2017, PMID 28069814; Mirzadzare 2025, PMID 40735968]
  • Zonulin: proposed as the body's "seam opener" (thought to be pre-haptoglobin-2). Larazotide was designed to block it. [verified, Hoilat 2021, PMID 34339872]
  • Intestinal permeability ("leaky gut"): measured properly by drinking two sugars (lactulose, a big one; mannitol, a small one) and checking urine. More lactulose getting through = leakier seams. [verified, Zhou 2019, PMID 30108163]
  • Microbiome: the bacteria living in the gut. They ferment fibre into butyrate, which feeds the lining and even switches on LL-37 production. [verified, PMID 28069814]
  • Visceral hypersensitivity: gut nerves turned up too loud, so normal gas or stretch feels like pain. A core IBS feature. [reported, general literature; GLP-1 reviews describe "visceral sensitivity" effects, PMID 42570075]
  • Gut-brain axis: two-way wiring (vagus nerve, hormones, immune signals). This is why hypnotherapy and CBT work for IBS (section 2.6). [verified via outcome, Black 2020, PMID 32276950]

1.2 Real vs wellness myth: leaky gut

Real [verified, Hanning 2021 systematic review of 66 studies, PMID 33717210]: - Barrier problems show up in 17 to 50% of post-infection IBS, 37 to 62% of IBS-D, and only 4 to 25% of IBS-C. - At group level permeability was higher than healthy people in 9 of 13 IBS-D studies and 4 of 4 post-infection IBS studies, but only 2 of 7 IBS-C studies. - Leakier gut tracked with more pain in 9 of 13 studies and with bowel changes in 6 of 9. - Also proven in celiac (gluten raised permeability by 70% in the placebo group of a larazotide trial, PMID 17697209) and in IBD.

Myth or unproven: - The zonulin blood test. A 2026 systematic review (8 studies, 421 IBS vs 428 controls) found 2 studies higher, 4 no different, 1 contradicted itself depending on kit. Independent checks show the most used commercial kits detect haptoglobin, complement C3 or properdin, not zonulin. The one study using a truly specific antibody found zonulin lower in IBS. [verified, Marginean 2026, PMID 42940160] - "Leaky gut causes everything" (autism, fatigue, every skin problem): associations exist (e.g. fibromyalgia and ME/CFS show higher barrier markers, PMID 37744357), but cause and effect isn't shown, and those studies used the same ELISA kits under question. [verified for study; causation inferred as unproven] - Page line: leaky gut is a measurable feature in some gut conditions, not a diagnosis on its own. [inferred]

1.3 IBS (all types)

  • Pain linked to bowel habit, no visible damage on scope. Subtypes: IBS-C (constipation), IBS-D (diarrhoea), IBS-M (mixed). [reported, standard Rome criteria]
  • Post-infection IBS: after a bad bacterial gut infection, about 1 in 8 people (pooled 11 to 14% depending on bug) develop IBS. Odds about 5 times higher after any infectious gastroenteritis (OR 4.9). [verified, Svendsen 2019, PMID 31112663]
  • Global prevalence figure not pulled this pass. [gap]

1.4 IBD (Crohn's, ulcerative colitis)

  • Real inflammation and ulcers seen on scope and biopsy. UC: colon only, continuous from rectum. Crohn's: anywhere, patchy, full thickness, can form fistulas. [reported, standard]
  • Measured by doctors with faecal calprotectin (stool inflammation marker), colonoscopy (Mayo endoscopic score), and activity scores (Mayo for UC, CDAI for Crohn's). [verified as endpoints in PMID 42627215, PMID 19821509]

1.5 Gastritis and ulcers

  • Stomach lining inflamed or broken through. Main causes: H. pylori, NSAIDs (ibuprofen, aspirin), alcohol. [reported, standard]
  • This is where BPC-157 started: most early rat work is alcohol and NSAID stomach injury. [verified, Sikiric 2024 review, PMID 38980576]

1.6 Reflux (GERD)

  • Acid backs up into the oesophagus because the valve (lower oesophageal sphincter) relaxes at the wrong times. [reported, standard]
  • Up to 40% of people still have symptoms despite PPIs. [verified, Hillman 2017, PMID 28859358]

1.7 SIBO

  • Too many bacteria in the small intestine, diagnosed with breath tests. Can follow long-term PPI use and gut surgery. [verified, Maslennikov 2026, PMID 42346839]

1.8 Post-antibiotic gut

  • Antibiotics thin out the microbiome; butyrate drops; the lining loses a fuel source. Direct peptide data: none found this pass. [inferred from mechanism; gap]

1.9 Gut-skin and gut-brain links

  • Gut-brain: strongest proof is that brain-targeted therapy (hypnotherapy, CBT) reduces gut symptoms (2.6). [verified]
  • Gut-skin: rosacea is listed among SIBO-linked conditions in a 2026 review (PMID 42346839). Deeper gut-skin data not pulled this pass. [verified mention only; gap]

1.10 Endometriosis, PCOS and the gut

  • Endometriosis: IBS is about 3 times more likely (OR 2.97, 11 studies, 96,119 people); about 1 in 4 women with endo also meet IBS criteria (pooled 23.4%). [verified, Nabi 2022, PMID 35957857] A 2026 meta-analysis found the same: IBS RR 2.51, OR 3.49; celiac OR 4.50; IBD OR 2.82 but not significant. Evidence quality low. [verified, Zheng 2026, PMID 42901254]
  • Why it matters: endo on the bowel causes painful bowel movements and gets mislabelled as IBS. Shared mechanisms: inflammation and central pain sensitisation. [verified for proposed mechanism, PMID 42901254]
  • PCOS: IBS in 10 to 52% of women with PCOS vs 5 to 50% of controls across 6 small case-control studies; association not firmly established. [verified, Saei Ghare Naz 2024, PMID 38494583] A genetic (Mendelian randomisation) study suggests PCOS slightly raises IBS risk (OR 1.034), not the reverse. [verified, Gao 2026, PMID 40247811]
  • In 2,579 people assigned female at birth with chronic pelvic pain data, those with pelvic pain had IBS in 37% and PCOS in 20%. [verified, Tordoff 2024, PMID 39011578]
  • Interlink with Endometriosis Research v2 and PCOS Research v2 (endo file already lists OR 3.26 from a 2020 paper).

2. Standard treatments, with numbers

2.1 Low FODMAP diet

  • 2025 network meta-analysis (28 RCTs, 2,338 people): low FODMAP cut the risk of global IBS symptoms not improving to 0.51 of usual diet (24 trials). It was the only diet that beat usual diet for bloating (RR 0.55). Moderate confidence vs habitual diet. A starch and sucrose reduced diet ranked higher (RR 0.41) but only 2 trials. [verified, Cuffe 2025, PMID 40258374]

2.2 Peppermint oil

  • 10 RCTs, 1,030 people: number needed to treat (NNT) 4 for global symptoms, 7 for pain. More side effects (RR 1.57, mostly reflux). Evidence quality very low. [verified, Ingrosso 2022, PMID 35942669]
  • 2026 plant meta-analysis (60 studies): peppermint RR 1.63 for improvement. [verified, Meyer 2026, PMID 42572206]

2.3 Antispasmodics

  • Numbers not pulled this pass. [gap] (Peppermint is itself an antispasmodic; the Ingrosso review frames antispasmodics as first-line.) [verified]

2.4 Linaclotide (an approved peptide drug)

  • A 14 amino acid peptide that acts on guanylate cyclase-C receptors on the gut lining, pulling water into the bowel and calming pain nerves. Swallowed, barely absorbed, works locally. [verified mechanism class, Chey 2012]
  • Phase 3, 804 IBS-C patients, 290 μg once daily, 26 weeks: 33.7% vs 13.9% met the FDA responder endpoint (NNT 5.1). Pain responders 48.9% vs 34.5%; bowel movement responders 47.6% vs 22.6%. Diarrhoea made 4.5% stop vs 0.2% on placebo. [verified, Chey 2012, PMID 22986437]
  • Why it matters for the page: proof that a swallowed peptide can work in the gut because it doesn't need to be absorbed. [inferred]

2.5 Plecanatide (approved peptide)

  • Same receptor, modelled on the body's own uroguanylin. Pooled phase 3 (4 trials, 4,815 people, 3 or 6 mg): better stool consistency and bowel movement frequency vs placebo at 12 weeks, in under 65s and over 65s. [verified, Menees 2020, PMID 32660770] Responder % not in abstract. [gap]

2.6 Rifaximin (IBS-D, SIBO)

  • IBS without constipation, TARGET 1 and 2 (550 mg three times daily, 2 weeks): adequate relief 40.7% vs 31.7%; bloating 40.2% vs 30.3%. About 1 extra responder per 11 treated. [verified, Pimentel 2011, PMID 21208106; NNT inferred]
  • SIBO: 32 studies, 1,331 patients, eradication 70.8%; symptom improvement in eradicated patients 67.7%. Study quality poor. [verified, Gatta 2017, PMID 28078798] A 2026 review says controlled RCTs conflict and one meta-analysis showed no effect. [verified, PMID 42346839]

2.7 Gut-directed hypnotherapy and CBT

  • 41 RCTs, 4,072 people. Risk of staying symptomatic: self-guided CBT 0.61, face-to-face CBT 0.62, hypnotherapy 0.67. Roughly 1 in 3 fewer people still symptomatic. Bias risk high, so likely overestimated. [verified, Black 2020, PMID 32276950]

2.8 IBD biologics and small molecules

  • Upadacitinib vs vedolizumab, ustekinumab, tofacitinib (indirect comparison of phase 3 trials): upadacitinib 30 mg beat all three over a year, NNT 3.2 to 8.7. [verified, Reinisch 2024, PMID 39126596]
  • Trial-level remission rates per biologic (GEMINI, UNIFI etc.) not re-read this pass. [gap]
  • Teduglutide in Crohn's (see 3.6).

2.9 PPIs (reflux, ulcers)

  • Up to 40% still symptomatic on PPI; slow metabolisers vs fast (CYP2C19 genes) changes healing. [verified, PMID 28859358]
  • PPI healing rates vs H2 blockers not pulled. [gap]
  • Long-term PPIs can lead to SIBO. [verified, PMID 42346839]

3. Peptides, from the endpoint (evidence grades)

Grades: A = phase 3 human; B = randomised human, smaller; C = human pilot or strong observational; D = animal or cell only.

3.1 BPC-157. Grade C-minus for UC (one failed-ish trial), D for everything else in the gut

Origin. 15 amino acids (GEPPPGKPADDAGLV), a fragment of a protein first isolated from human gastric juice. Developed as PL 14736 (also PL-10, PLD-116) for IBD. [verified, Sikiric 2012, PMID 22300085; Veljaca 2002 abstract, bib.irb.hr/192824]

The human UC trial (the most important fact on the page) [verified, FDA PCAC briefing July 2026, fda.gov/media/193343]: - Ruenzi et al. 2005, meeting abstract only, never fully published. - 53 people with mild to moderate UC, randomised 1:1 to an 80 mg rectal enema or placebo, once daily for 2 weeks; 46 finished. - Disease Activity Index dropped 3.2 points on BPC-157 vs 1.6 on placebo. The difference (1.6) had a confidence interval crossing zero, so not statistically clear. - Dropouts for adverse events: 3 vs 2, mostly "UC getting worse". Commonest side effects: headache, flatulence. - FDA called the data inadequate for efficacy or safety. - Sikiric's own reviews describe the programme as "phase II, no side effects" without these numbers. [verified, PMID 38980576] Page should show both framings. - Earlier phase 1: 32 healthy volunteers, single and 7-day enemas, no significant side effects. [v1 tag verified; v2 note: the CROSBI record of Veljaca 2002 (UEGW poster, Eur J Pharm Sci 17:74-75) confirms rectal dosing in healthy men, safe and well tolerated, but the record shows no n or dose, so the 32 comes from the FDA briefing. [verified, bib.irb.hr/120878]] - Plasma BPC-157 after enema was mostly undetectable. [verified, FDA briefing]

Animal gut data (almost all one lab, Zagreb) [verified unless noted]: - Alcohol and NSAID stomach lesions, oesophagitis, sphincter function, colitis, short bowel, many anastomoses (oesophagus to stomach, colon to colon, small bowel) and fistulas (colon to skin, stomach to skin, rectum to vagina and others), healed in rats. [Bajramagic 2024, PMID 39204186] - Ischaemic colitis in rats: 10 μg/kg applied to the colon restored blood flow within 15 minutes; mucosa nearly spared at day 10. [Duzel 2017, PMID 29358856] - Independent 2026 critical review (non-Zagreb author): heterogeneous, short, single-dose studies with unclear bias; "hypothesis-generating", not established therapy. [Demirtaş 2026, PMID 42794771] - A 2025 commentary criticised the 2024 Sikiric gut review. [verified exists, Whitehouse 2025, PMID 40759852; content only summarised as a discussion]

Oral vs injected (the stability claim): - Claim: stable in human gastric juice for more than 24 hours, so it works orally. Source: the developers. [reported, Sikiric 2024, PMID 38980576] - FDA: no human PK data for oral, SC, nasal or transdermal use. A phase 1 oral study (NCT02637284, 42 volunteers, 2015/16) has no posted or published results. IV half-life in animals: about 5 minutes (dog), 15 minutes (rat). [verified, FDA briefing; NCT02637284] - Most rat gut studies used oral or stomach-tube dosing (e.g. 10 μg/kg and 10 ng/kg ig). [verified, PMID 42356491] - Inferred: for the gut, oral may make more sense than for tendons because the target is the lining itself, as with linaclotide. Unproven in humans. [inferred]

Safety signals in FDA's review [verified, FDA briefing]: 3 injection-related FAERS reports (confounded); 28-day animal IM studies showed clotting marker shifts (aPTT shorter in rats, longer in dogs) and liver-related changes; no carcinogenicity studies; immunogenicity untested; FDA says oral immunogenic risk "likely low".

Regulatory, as of 3 Nov 2026: - 23 July 2026: FDA's compounding advisory committee voted 8 to 6 (1 abstention) to add BPC-157 to the 503A bulks list; the nomination was specifically for ulcerative colitis. FDA staff recommended against. [reported, RAPS, link; American Med Spa Assoc, link] - 28 Oct 2026 HHS interim enforcement policy covers BPC-157 (SC, IM, oral, topical) and KPV (oral, topical) at US 503A pharmacies under 10 conditions, including documented human bioavailability for oral forms. [reported, carried over from Achilles Research v2 section 6.1; not re-read] - Still not FDA approved. [verified]

Trials now: hamstring phase 2 (n 120), rotator cuff phase 1. No gut trial registered. [verified, ClinicalTrials.gov search]

3.2 KPV. Grade D

  • Lys-Pro-Val, the tail end of alpha-MSH. Enters gut and immune cells via PepT1, a transporter that is switched on in the colon during IBD. Nanomolar amounts shut down NF-κB inflammation signalling. [verified, Dalmasso 2008, PMID 18061177]
  • Given in drinking water, it reduced DSS and TNBS colitis in mice. [verified, same]
  • Packed in hyaluronic acid nanoparticles and swallowed, it healed mouse colitis better than plain carrier. [verified, Xiao 2017, PMID 28143741]
  • In a rectal gel with EGF, it raised tight junction proteins in rat colitis. [verified, Li 2024, PMID 38289234]
  • Human gut trials: none found. [verified negative search]
  • Advisory committee voted 8 to 6 to add KPV (for wounds and inflammatory conditions). [reported, RAPS]
  • Why oral is plausible: tripeptides are exactly what PepT1 carries. [inferred]

3.3 Larazotide. Grade A-negative (phase 3 stopped)

  • 8 amino acid zonulin blocker, oral. [verified, PMID 34339872]
  • Proof of concept: gluten raised permeability 70% on placebo, not at all on larazotide 12 mg. [verified, Paterson 2007, PMID 17697209]
  • Phase 2b, 342 celiac patients on gluten-free diet: only the lowest dose (0.5 mg three times daily) beat placebo; 1 and 2 mg did not. [verified, Leffler 2015, PMID 25683116]
  • Meta-analysis (4 RCTs, 626 people): no change in lactulose/mannitol permeability vs placebo; symptom help only during gluten challenge. [verified, PMID 34339872]
  • Phase 3 (NCT03569007, 307 enrolled) terminated July 2022, no results posted. [verified, ClinicalTrials.gov] Reason for stopping (v2): on 21 Jun 2022 the sponsor said an independent interim re-estimate, on about the first half of the 525-patient target after the 12-week blinded period, showed the number of extra patients needed to separate drug from placebo was too large to continue. [verified, sponsor press release via BioSpace]
  • Lesson for the page: "seals the leaky gut" in early data did not survive a big trial. [inferred]

3.4 LL-37 (cathelicidin). Grade D

  • The gut makes its own; vitamin D switches it on. In UC patients, higher vitamin D tracked with higher LL-37; higher LL-37 linked to less histologic inflammation and relapse, but not independently of vitamin D. Rectal cathelicidin reduced mouse DSS colitis. [verified, Gubatan 2020, PMID 31955203]
  • LL-37 and its fragment KR-12 (1 to 5 mg/kg by injection) reduced colitis in 4 mouse models. [verified, Fabisiak 2020, PMID 33219923]
  • Mouse version (Cramp, 4 mg/kg) reduced DSS colitis and protected the barrier. [verified, Jiang 2024, PMID 38380090]
  • Human gut trials: none found.

3.5 Thymosin alpha-1. Grade D for gut

  • Approved in some countries for hepatitis B and vaccine response. A review mentions a protective role against immune checkpoint inhibitor colitis (cancer drug side effect), preclinical. Author works for the maker. [verified, Mao 2023, PMID 36871535]
  • An engineered LL-37 plus Tα1 hybrid peptide protected mouse jejunum from LPS injury and raised ZO-1 and occludin. [verified, Zhang 2019, PMID 31447849] Not the same as taking Tα1.

3.6 TB-500 / thymosin beta-4. No gut evidence found

  • PubMed title search returned gastric cancer biology (Tβ4 promoting metastasis in mice), not healing. [verified, An 2021, PMID 34081824] Flag as [speculated] for gut use; the cancer link is cell/mouse biology, not shown in humans.

3.7 GLP-2 analogues (teduglutide). Grade A for short bowel

  • Short bowel phase 3 style RCT (83 patients, 24 weeks): 0.05 mg/kg/day gave 16 of 35 responders (at least 20% less IV nutrition) vs 1 of 16 on placebo; 3 patients came fully off IV feeding. Villus height and citrulline rose. [verified, Jeppesen 2011, PMID 21317170]
  • Crohn's pilot (100 patients, 8 weeks, SC): highest dose 44% response and 32% remission vs 32% and 20% on placebo; numerically better, not a proven win. [verified, Buchman 2010, PMID 19821509]
  • It grows gut lining, which is why it is watched for polyps (label detail not re-read). [reported]

3.8 GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide). Grade C (observational), trials running

IBD - UC, 150 GLP-1 users vs 150 matched: symptomatic remission at 12 weeks 66.7% vs 25.3%; adjusted OR 5.9. Weight loss did not explain it. In a subset with scopes, endoscopic remission 58% vs 38%. Single centre, retrospective. [verified, Alqinai 2026, PMID 42627215] This is the clinician-observed (scope) signal on the page. - Tirzepatide vs GLP-1s (3,042 per group): 19% lower risk of IV steroid use. [verified, Patel 2026, PMID 42717572] - J-pouch after UC surgery (n 20): median bowel movements fell from 9 to 6 a day. [verified, Chaudhry 2026, PMID 42384913] The placebo-controlled liraglutide pouch trial stopped at 8 patients. [verified, NCT04763564] - v2 counter-evidence: in stable IBD with obesity or diabetes, starting semaglutide or tirzepatide did not change 1-year relapse (7.9% vs 7.9%, 2,028 pairs; 12.4% vs 15.6% on advanced therapy, 346 pairs, not significant). [verified, Yeh 2026, PMID 42309434] - Running RCTs: tirzepatide in Crohn's (n 60; n 24), and two phase 3b trials of a UC/Crohn's biologic with or without tirzepatide (n 350, n 290), completing 2028. [verified, ClinicalTrials.gov] IBS - 6,665 per group, TriNetX: GLP-1 users had less new diarrhoea (8.9% vs 10.6%), constipation (19.8% vs 22.0%), abdominal pain (31.6% vs 35.8%), bloating (8.3% vs 10.9%). Small absolute differences, coded data. [verified, Khan 2026, PMID 42570075] - ROSE-010 (a GLP-1 analogue): in 166 people having IBS pain attacks, injections were about twice as effective as placebo for total pain relief. [verified as reported in Hellström 2011 review, PMID 20518750] In 46 IBS-C women it slowed stomach emptying and caused nausea and vomiting. [verified, Camilleri 2012, PMID 22517769] - Bile acid diarrhoea (often misdiagnosed as IBS-D): early RCTs comparing GLP-1 with standard binders; not established. [verified, PMID 42037908] Risks (see warnings): gastroparesis, obstruction, pancreatitis.

3.9 Linaclotide and plecanatide. Grade A (see 2.4, 2.5)

3.10 Glutamine (amino acid, not peptide). Grade B (one trial, needs replication)

  • Post-infection IBS-D with proven leaky gut, 5 g three times daily, 8 weeks, 106 completers: 79.6% vs 5.8% responders; stools per day 5.4 to 2.9; permeability normalised. [verified, Zhou 2019, PMID 30108163] The placebo rate of 5.8% is unusually low for IBS (usually 30 to 40%), a reason for caution. [inferred, compare TARGET placebo 31.7%]
  • Added to low FODMAP (15 g/day, 6 weeks, 50 patients): 88% vs 60% had at least 45% symptom improvement. [verified, Rastgoo 2021, PMID 34977110]

v2 adversarial check of the 79.6% vs 5.8% against the full paper and registry [verified, full text PMC9549483, DOI; registry NCT01414244] - The numbers are real as published: 43 of 54 vs 3 of 52 hit the primary endpoint (IBS-SS drop of at least 50 points). Double-blind, computer randomisation, independent analyst. - Placebo was whey protein, 15 g a day. Whey is a dairy protein; the authors themselves note it could trigger IBS symptoms. A placebo that slightly irritates the gut would push placebo response down and widen the gap. Lactose intolerance was a registry exclusion, which softens but does not remove this. [verified that authors flag it; effect on result inferred] - Completers only: 115 randomised (59 vs 56), only the 106 finishers analysed, not intention-to-treat. Small effect here since dropouts were few. [verified] - Internal inconsistencies: dropouts stated as 5 vs 2 in one place, but flow numbers imply 5 vs 4 (59 to 54, 56 to 52); 52 of 56 is 92.9%, printed as 92.5%. The paper says 2 of 54 (3.7%) did not respond "so 96% responded", mixing the strict endpoint with any improvement. Registry lists one site (Tulane); paper says Ohio State and UTMB Galveston. Registry start Nov 2010, paper Aug 2011. Minor, but they lower confidence in reporting care. [verified] - Narrow population: only 115 of 287 screened were eligible; SIBO (by breath test), celiac and IBD excluded; all had a raised lactulose/mannitol ratio. Results do not apply to IBS in general, IBS-C, or IBS without a leaky gut test. [verified] - No replication found in PubMed through Nov 2026 (8 hits for glutamine + IBS + randomised; only Rastgoo 2021 is a new RCT, and it is a different design). [verified search] - Crohn's: a Cochrane review (2 RCTs, 42 people) found no benefit for inducing remission (4/9 vs 5/9) and no permeability change; low certainty. [verified, Akobeng 2016, Cochrane CD007348] - Verdict: strong signal in one carefully selected group, weakened by whey placebo and no replication. Page line: "one trial in post-infection IBS-D with a proven leaky gut; not tested well elsewhere". [inferred] - Community check (4.5): 3 of 8 users got worse (cramps, loose stools), none had the permeability test. [reported]

3.11 Collagen peptides. Grade: no gut evidence

  • Only finding: 15 g/day for 7 days in 15 women raised GLP-1 and cut a later meal by about 10%, with no change in gut symptoms. [verified, Reynolds 2025, PMID 40685650] Nothing on gut lining repair found. [gap]

3.12 Vasoactive intestinal peptide (VIP). Grade D for gut

  • 21 PubMed titles on VIP and colitis; reviews propose it rebalances T cell subsets in UC. [verified, Yang 2024, PMID 38790102] Human gut trials not found. VIP is a natural gut nerve messenger; excess VIP (VIPoma tumours) causes severe watery diarrhoea. [reported, standard physiology] Worth a warning line.

4.2 GLP-1s and IBS: the biggest signal in this pass [reported unless noted]

  • 20 of 21 Mumsnet posters with IBS who took a GLP-1 say their IBS got better; 0 worse. Most describe IBS-D or loose stools. [verified count, pages read]
  • What improved: fewer flares (weekly down to 2 in 3 months for one 20-year sufferer), food triggers gone (gluten, high FODMAP, grapes, porridge, bread), urgency gone, two stopped long-term loperamide (one had a 90-capsule monthly prescription). (thread E, thread F, thread G)
  • How fast: from hours (1) and about 4 days (1) to about 3 months; one says no flare in a year.
  • Price paid: constipation is the common trade (at least 5 posters; "every 5 to 6 days", straining). Several posters themselves link the benefit to slower gut transit. [verified as stated by posters]
  • One mixed: two severe flares in the first 2 months, otherwise better (thread H).
  • One clinician-touch: a semaglutide user says her 6 to 8 weekly inflammatory markers also improved (no values). (thread A)
  • No IBS-C posters found. The mechanism (slower transit) predicts IBS-C could get worse. [inferred]
  • Fits the study data: the 6,665-pair TriNetX cohort showed less new diarrhoea and bloating on GLP-1s (PMID 42570075). [verified study; link inferred]
  • Bias: threads titled around success attract success stories; weight loss, eating less and diet changes run alongside; "IBS" is self-labelled. Two near-identical posters ("PearlsPearl", "PearlsTeapot", both "every 5 to 6 days") were treated as one person. [inferred]

4.3 GLP-1s and IBD (UC, Crohn's) [reported unless noted]

  • 10 better, 1 mixed, 3 no change, 2 worse out of 16 people with a clear outcome. (A, B, C, D)
  • Best quality self-reports: 2 dechallenge/rechallenge cases. A UC poster flared within about 4 weeks of stopping and went back into remission on restart; a left-sided colitis poster's pain returned in a 2-month break and eased on restarting. This on/off pattern is the closest a forum gets to a test. [verified as posted]
  • What improved: no blood, mucus or urgency; one formed stool a day; one cut mesalazine suppositories from 3 a week to 1 a fortnight; less stoma output (Crohn's, drug not named).
  • How fast: about 4 days, 1 week, 4 weeks, 3 months.
  • No change: 3, all already in remission (stayed in remission) or only 5 days in.
  • Worse, 2 UC: one full flare with blood at week 4, stopped, settled on more mesalazine; she wondered if slow stomach emptying hurt absorption of her oral mesalazine. One on 15 mg for about a year had diarrhoea alternating with constipation and some blood; stool test showed inflammation, urgent gastro referral. This is the only clinician-measured result in the IBD threads, and it was a worsening. [verified as posted; absorption idea speculated]
  • Mixed, 1 Crohn's: firmer stools early, nausea and stomach pain after a dose rise.
  • 0 posters reported calprotectin numbers or a scope on a GLP-1.
  • Counterweight study (new in v2): a 2026 target trial emulation in stable IBD with obesity or diabetes (OptumLabs claims, 2018 to 2023) found no change in 1-year relapse: 7.9% vs 7.9% (2,028 matched pairs, RR 1.01) and 12.4% vs 15.6% on advanced therapy (346 pairs, RR 0.80, CI 0.55 to 1.15). Safety no different. A third stopped the drug within a year. [verified, Yeh 2026, PMID 42309434, DOI; coverage AGA news, 17 Jul 2026] Outcome was hard relapse (steroids, admission, surgery) in already-stable patients; the positive Alqinai cohort measured symptomatic remission. 5. Pairings and related conditions

  • BPC-157 + KPV is the most marketed gut "stack" (seen across vendor and clinic pages). No study tested them together. [verified absence in PubMed; marketing reported]
  • Glutamine + low FODMAP: the one tested pairing with an RCT (3.10). [verified]
  • GLP-1 + biologic: being tested in phase 3b in UC and Crohn's (3.8). [verified]
  • LL-37 + vitamin D: vitamin D drives the gut's own LL-37 (3.4). [verified]
  • Same problem elsewhere: interstitial cystitis (BPC-157 bladder pilot exists, per FDA list of 5 human studies), endometriosis, PCOS, celiac, fibromyalgia (barrier markers raised). Link the case pages.

6. Options (ordered by evidence)

  1. Get the diagnosis right: IBS vs IBD vs celiac vs bile acid diarrhoea vs endometriosis on the bowel. Calprotectin separates IBS from IBD. [inferred from 1.4, 3.8]
  2. IBS: low FODMAP (with a dietitian), peppermint oil, gut-directed hypnotherapy or CBT, then drugs by subtype (linaclotide/plecanatide for C, rifaximin for D). [verified numbers above]
  3. Post-infection IBS-D: glutamine has the best single trial; ask about it. [verified]
  4. IBD: specialist care, biologics or small molecules; GLP-1s as an add-on when weight or diabetes is also an issue is being studied. [verified]
  5. Reflux/ulcers: PPI, H. pylori testing, stop NSAIDs. [reported]
  6. Peptides from the research world: BPC-157 and KPV remain unproven in humans for gut; the one human BPC-157 UC trial was not clearly positive. [verified]

7. 15 usual questions (draft answers)

  1. Is leaky gut real? Yes, measurably in subsets (IBS-D, post-infection IBS, celiac, IBD). Not a stand-alone diagnosis. [verified, PMID 33717210]
  2. Should I get a zonulin test? No. Most commercial kits don't measure zonulin. [verified, PMID 42940160]
  3. Was BPC-157 really found in stomach juice? It's a fragment of a protein isolated from human gastric juice. [verified]
  4. Has BPC-157 been tested in people with gut disease? Once: 53 UC patients, 2 weeks of enemas, not clearly better than placebo. [verified, FDA]
  5. Does oral BPC-157 survive the stomach? The developers say yes (24 h in gastric juice). No human oral blood level data exist. [reported/verified gap]
  6. Oral or injected for gut? Untested in humans either way. Gut-acting peptide drugs that work (linaclotide) are oral and act locally. [inferred]
  7. What is KPV? A 3 amino acid piece of alpha-MSH; works orally in mouse colitis via PepT1; no human data. [verified]
  8. Why did larazotide fail? Phase 2 helped only at the lowest dose and didn't change permeability on pooled analysis; phase 3 was stopped. [verified]
  9. Is there an approved peptide for IBS? Yes: linaclotide and plecanatide (constipation type). [verified]
  10. Do GLP-1s help IBD? Mixed. One cohort showed more symptom remission (UC 67% vs 25%); a larger 2026 claims study showed no change in hard relapse. Patients on forums mostly report calmer bowels, but 2 of 16 flared. RCTs are running. [verified studies; reported community]
  11. Can GLP-1s wreck my gut? Rarely: higher risk of gastroparesis (about 3.7x) and obstruction vs an older weight drug; common nausea. [verified/reported, 8]
  12. Does glutamine heal the gut? One strong RCT in post-infection IBS-D with leaky gut (placebo was whey, no replication); no benefit in Crohn's (Cochrane). Some users get cramps and loose stools. [verified]
  13. Does collagen heal the gut? No gut trials found. [verified negative]
  14. Why do endo and PCOS patients have gut symptoms? Endo triples IBS odds and can sit on the bowel; PCOS link weaker. [verified]
  15. Can I swap my IBD meds for peptides? No evidence supports that; untreated IBD can scar, perforate or raise cancer risk. [inferred, standard]

8. Warnings (bottom of page)

  • Red flags, see a doctor: blood in stool, black stools, unexplained weight loss, waking at night with pain or diarrhoea, fever, anaemia, symptoms starting after 50, family history of bowel cancer or IBD, trouble swallowing, vomiting blood. [reported, standard alarm features; not re-sourced this pass]
  • IBD needs a doctor. Track calprotectin and scopes. The only BPC-157 UC trial had dropouts for worsening UC in both arms. [verified] On GLP-1s, 2 of 16 forum UC users flared; one wondered if slow stomach emptying cut absorption of oral mesalazine. Tell the IBD team before starting. [reported; mechanism speculated]
  • GLP-1s and the gut: vs bupropion-naltrexone, GLP-1s had higher pancreatitis (HR 9.1, wide CI), bowel obstruction (HR 4.2) and gastroparesis (HR 3.7); gastroparesis 9.1 per 1,000 person-years on semaglutide vs 3.1. [reported, Sodhi JAMA 2023 via TCTMD, link] Between GLP-1 drugs, GI safety was similar (HRs 0.96 to 1.07). [verified, Crisafulli 2025, PMID 41183330] Slowed stomach emptying raises aspiration risk during endoscopy or anaesthesia; tell the team. [verified, Jalleh 2026, PMID 41697736] Long-term trials dispelled pancreatitis/pancreatic cancer concern per that review, conflicting with the JAMA signal. [verified; conflict flagged]
  • Unregulated products: FDA says BPC-157 is not well characterised; impurities, aggregation and endotoxin are unknowns; immunogenicity untested for injection. [verified, FDA briefing] UK OPSS has flagged BPC-157 in wellness products as a prohibited/unapproved drug. [reported, title only, link]
  • Growth signals: teduglutide grows lining; theoretical concern with any pro-growth peptide in people with polyps or bowel cancer history. [inferred]
  • VIP: excess VIP causes severe diarrhoea. [reported]
  • Sport: BPC-157 is on the WADA list (see Achilles v2). [reported]
