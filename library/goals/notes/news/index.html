2. HHS peptide compounding policy (Oct 2026)

  • Federal Register doc 2026-22192, docket HHS-OASH-2026-0496, RIN 0910-ZD94. It was filed for public inspection on 28 Oct and published on 30 Oct 2026. The action is labelled "Notice of availability". The SUMMARY line does not use the word "draft", but the Supplementary Information says HHS is "announcing the availability of a draft 'Enforcement Discretion Policy…'". Comments are due "2 weeks after date of publication", which works out to 13 Nov 2026 [verified text; date inferred]. No effective date is stated, and the policy is "not binding on HHS or the public". Source: https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-22192.pdf [verified]
  • Coverage differs by outlet. AJMC (29 Oct) and RAPS (29 Oct) call it an interim policy now in force, and AJMC says RFK Jr. signed it [reported]. The Federal Register itself calls it a draft. Page wording should be "draft interim policy, open for comment until about 13 Nov 2026".
  • Policy text on hhs.gov: https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/hhs-503a-peptide-enforcement-discretion-policy.pdf [verified]. It covers only a "State-licensed pharmacy, Federal facility, or licensed physician". It lasts "until a peptide bulk drug substance is addressed in a final rule" and can be revisited if a safety or quality signal appears. The 10 conditions: 1. The peptide is on Appendix A, the "503A Interim Access List". 2. Its identity and route match Appendix B. 3. The bulk comes from an FDA-registered facility with a valid COA showing the specified tests. 4. The original manufacturer is FDA-registered and CGMP-compliant, with its site listed on the COA. Intermediaries must validate it. 5. Products, serious adverse events and quality issues are reported to MedWatch and the states. 6. The label reads "This is a compounded drug product. Not for resale." 7. One active ingredient only. 8. Oral forms need documentation that the formulation reaches adequate oral bioavailability in humans. 9. The only device allowed is an FDA 510(k)-cleared syringe. 10. Otherwise compliant with the FD&C Act, including a patient-specific prescription and sanitary conditions.
  • Appendix A peptides: BPC-157, epitalon, KPV, MOTS-c, Semax, TB-500. [verified]
  • Appendix B routes: https://www.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/hhs-503a-peptide-enforcement-discretion-policy-appendix-b.pdf [verified] BPC-157 acetate SC, IM, oral, topical. TB-500 acetate SC or IM. Epitalon acetate SC or IM "dispensed in intermittent courses". Semax free base intranasal. KPV acetate topical and oral. MOTS-c acetate SC or IM. This matches the BakerHostetler summary the files used. Appendix B also says several of these peptides sat in Category 2 from Sep 2023 until 22 Apr 2026, and that CDER's safety concerns were "largely theoretical". [verified]
  • Research-chemical (RUO) sellers fall outside it because they are not covered parties. HHS describes the policy as a "more controlled alternative" to foreign RUO peptides. It contains no explicit exclusion clause. [verified + inferred]

5. FDA suicidality label change, Jan 2026

Source: https://www.fda.gov/drugs/drug-safety-and-availability/fda-requests-removal-suicidal-behavior-and-ideation-warning-glucagon-peptide-1-receptor-agonist-glp (Drug Safety Communication 13 Jan 2026; page dated 3 Apr 2026) [verified] - Title: "FDA Requests Removal of Suicidal Behavior and Ideation Warning from Glucagon-Like Peptide-1 Receptor Agonist (GLP-1 RA) Medications". - Products: Saxenda, Wegovy, Zepbound. The SI/B information comes out of Warnings and Precautions. T2D GLP-1 labels never carried it. - Evidence: - 91 placebo-controlled trials, 107,910 patients (60,338 on a GLP-1 RA, 47,572 on placebo). - Sentinel: 2,243,138 new users from 10 data partners, Oct 2015 to Sep 2023, GLP-1 RA (1,161,983) versus SGLT2 inhibitors (1,081,155). - Finding: no increased risk of intentional self-harm. - FDA still advises patients to report new or worse depression, suicidal thoughts or mood changes, and points to the 988 lifeline.
