0. Headline numbers for the page (quick pull)

  • A healthy scalp has about 100,000 follicles; 85 to 90% growing, 1 to 3% transitioning, 5 to 10% resting; normal shedding 50 to 100 hairs a day [verified, DermNet].
  • Shedding after a trigger (illness, crash diet, surgery, birth) typically shows about 3 months later; after COVID the median was 2 months (range 1 to 11) in 181 shedding patients [verified, doi:10.1111/jocd.14883].
  • GLP-1 drugs: pooled RCTs show 3.25x the risk of hair loss vs placebo; about 3.9% of users report it in trials [verified, meta-analysis, 4,114 users, doi:10.1016/j.diabres.2026.113333]. Tirzepatide 15 mg in SURMOUNT-1: 5.7% vs 0.9% placebo [verified, manufacturer medical page, see 3.10].
  • Low-dose oral minoxidil in 1,404 patients: only 1.7% stopped for side effects; extra body/face hair 15.1% [verified, doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2021.02.054].
  • Finasteride 1 mg: durable benefit over 5 years vs progressive loss on placebo [verified, Eur J Dermatol 2002, PMID 11809594]. Sexual side effects 1.57x placebo across 15 RCTs [verified, doi:10.2340/00015555-3035].
  • Alopecia areata JAK pills, near full scalp (SALT 20 or less): baricitinib 4 mg 38.8% / 35.9% vs 6.2% / 3.3% at week 36 [verified, NEJM 2022, PMID 35334197]; ritlecitinib 50 mg 23% vs 2% at week 24 [verified, doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(23)00222-2]; deuruxolitinib 8 mg 29.6% vs 0.8% [reported, AJMC].
  • Clascoterone 5% scalp solution (first new topical anti-androgen): 1,465 men, two phase 3 trials; US filing targeted early 2027 [reported, Dermatology Times Apr 2026].
  • Peptides: no peptide has a published placebo-controlled human trial for any hair loss type. The strongest human peptide data are an uncontrolled 7-man study where copper peptides were mixed with minoxidil and dutasteride, and open-label growth-factor cocktails containing copper tripeptide and thymosin β4.
  • Thymosin β4 is the split result: it grew hair in rats and mice (NIH lab) but slowed growth and pushed human scalp follicles into the shedding phase in a dish [reported, see 3.3].

1. What each type is (so readers can match)

1.1 The hair cycle in plain language

  • Each follicle cycles on its own: growth (anagen) 2 to 6 years, transition (catagen) 2 to 3 weeks, rest (telogen) about 3 months, then the old hair falls as a new one pushes up [verified, DermNet].
  • Length depends on how long growth lasts. Thickness depends on the size of the dermal papilla, the cluster of cells at the bulb that signals the follicle [verified, Sinclair, doi:10.1093/ced/llaf012].
  • Why shedding lags 2 to 3 months: a shock pushes many growing follicles into rest at once (DermNet says up to 50%). They sit in rest for about 3 months before the hair drops, so the trigger is usually forgotten by the time the brush fills [verified, DermNet]. Regrowth then needs months to reach visible length, at about 1 cm a month [reported, clinic guide].

1.2 Male and female pattern hair loss (androgenetic alopecia, AGA)

  • Mechanism: in genetically sensitive follicles, DHT (made from testosterone by the enzyme 5α-reductase type 2) shortens each growth phase. Each cycle the follicle comes back smaller, until thick terminal hairs become fine vellus hairs. This shrinking is called miniaturisation [verified, doi:10.1016/j.jdin.2023.07.005; doi:10.3390/neurosci7010020].
  • Men: receding temples and crown. On placebo, hair keeps thinning year on year [verified, finasteride 5 year data].
  • Women: affects nearly 50% of women in their lifetime; usually a widening part with the front line kept; underdiagnosed. Topical minoxidil is the only FDA-approved drug for it [verified, doi:10.1007/s40257-026-01009-2].
  • Inflammation and blood supply: balding scalp shows micro-inflammation around follicles and less vascular support; TGF-β1 (driven by androgens) slows keratinocyte growth [reported, cell data summarised by Perfect Hair Health]. Minoxidil was a blood pressure drug and opens potassium channels in dermal papilla cells; its hair effect is not just "more blood flow" [verified, review doi:10.1007/s40257-025-00990-4].
  • The Wnt/β-catenin pathway is the follicle's "grow" switch; a brake protein called CXXC5 is raised in miniaturised human follicles [verified, doi:10.1016/j.jid.2017.04.038]. This is the target of PTD-DBM (3.5).
  • How to tell: gradual (years), patterned, with hairs of mixed thickness under a scope [verified, women's review above].

1.3 Telogen effluvium (TE): diffuse shedding after a trigger

  • Triggers: illness with fever, COVID, surgery, childbirth, crash diets, low iron or protein, new medicines, big stress [verified, DermNet].
  • Looks like: all-over shedding, handfuls in the shower, a thinner ponytail, but no bald patches; hairs come out with a small white bulb [inferred].
  • Post-COVID TE: in a Turkish clinic, 35.4% (64/181) of TE patients had COVID first; 87.5% acute, 12.5% chronic [verified, doi:10.1111/jocd.14883]. A 2026 trichoscopy study found unusual blood vessel patterns and immune cells around follicles, and the giant vessels linked to having needed oxygen in hospital [verified, doi:10.1159/000552511].
  • GLP-1 shedding: mostly TE from fast weight loss, not the drug poisoning follicles; female sex and more than 10% weight loss were the strongest predictors [verified, review doi:10.1007/s00592-026-02812-3]. Some studies also log pattern loss being unmasked [verified, doi:10.1097/DSS.0000000000005176].
  • Course: usually self-limits. Dermatologist timeline for GLP-1 shedding: noticed at 2 to 3 months, can plateau 2 to 3 months, regrowth over 3 to 6 more months, near normal by about a year; if pattern loss sat underneath, the hair may return thinner [reported, Dr Garshick in The Zoe Report, Jun 2026].
  • Chronic TE (over 6 months) is a separate diagnosis that can mimic early female pattern loss [verified, women's review].

1.4 Alopecia areata (AA): autoimmune patches

  • The follicle normally hides from the immune system ("immune privilege"), guarded by local signals including α-MSH, TGF-β and IL-10. In AA that shield collapses and CD8 T cells attack the bulb; interferon-γ and IL-15 signal through JAK pathways [verified, doi:10.3390/ijms25084409; doi:10.1016/j.jaut.2024.103217].
  • Looks like: round smooth patches, sometimes eyebrows or beard; can progress to whole scalp (totalis) or body (universalis). Course is unpredictable [verified, doi:10.1007/s40265-026-02371-5].
  • Thyroid link: over 40% of AA patients carry thyroid antibodies [verified, doi:10.1016/j.msard.2026.107974].

1.5 PCOS hair loss

  • PCOS combines irregular cycles, high androgens and often insulin resistance; hair loss here is female pattern loss driven by androgen excess, often with acne and excess body hair [verified, doi:10.7759/cureus.72987].
  • Male relatives of women with PCOS have 1.65x the odds of pattern baldness (21 studies), supporting a shared androgen/metabolic trait [verified, doi:10.1186/s12610-025-00290-1].
  • In 1,249 women with PCOS, autoimmune thyroid disease was 20.3% and alopecia areata 0.24% [verified, doi:10.3389/frph.2026.1806450]. So patchy loss in PCOS needs a separate check.
  • Cross-link: PCOS Research v1/v2 pages.

1.6 Hair transplant recovery

  • What happens: follicles from the back (DHT-resistant) are moved to thin areas. Most transplanted hairs shed within about 6 weeks, rest about 3 months, start growing at months 3 to 5, and the result is judged at 12 to 18 months [reported, clinic guide citing MedlinePlus].
  • Shock loss (native hair near the grafts shedding): 23 of 621 FUE patients (3.7%); women had 30x the odds of men, and risk rose with age in women [verified, doi:10.1007/s00266-023-03699-z]. Donor-area shedding was rarer still: 1 of 2,896 patients in one surgeon's 10-year series; recipient effluvium 3 [verified, doi:10.1055/s-0041-1739255].
  • Graft survival: in scarred scalp (inactive scarring alopecias), survival was 82.7% at 7 to 12 months, falling to 39.6% at 49 to 72 months [verified, systematic review of 8 studies, 123 patients, doi:10.1097/DSS.0000000000004707]. A 2026 review of transplants into lichen planopilaris and frontal fibrosing alopecia found the same pattern: 80 to 100% at 6 to 12 months, below 41% after year two [verified, doi:10.1016/j.abd.2026.501426].
  • Graft survival in normal pattern-loss scalp (v2 check of the "85 to 95%" figure): the range holds in primary studies [verified, abstracts read today]:
  • 158 men, FUE: over 90% of follicles survived; over 85% of patients exceeded 95% at 12 months [verified, doi:10.1186/s12893-024-02655-1].
  • Long-hair FUE for hairlines: 93.1% (±1.3) at 9 months [verified, doi:10.1111/jocd.16136].
  • Sapphire vs steel blade incisions: 94.7% vs 88.9% [verified, doi:10.1007/s12663-025-02874-w].
  • 60 patients, growth-factor soak vs none: 91.1% vs 81.0% at 12 months (the 81% is the plain-surgery control arm) [verified, doi:10.1111/jocd.70464].
  • 2010 review of graft survival factors: published rates run from "90% to well over 100%" but come from small marked test boxes with ideal grafts; the authors note few surgeons reach that when every graft is counted [verified, Parsley and Perez-Meza, J Cutan Aesthet Surg 2010, PMC2956960]. The same review cites grafts kept out of the body 95% surviving at 2 hours, 79% at 24 hours (Limmer) [verified, same review].
  • Packing density matters: one surgeon's 2-patient test found 88 to 95% at 20 to 30 grafts/cm² but 67 to 100% at 40 to 50/cm² [reported, ISHRS 2005 conference abstract summarised on a surgeon site, Bernstein Medical].
  • Reading: about 85 to 95% is a fair page figure for a good clinic in normal scalp, with the honest caveats that most studies are single-clinic, measure in small test areas, and that 81% showed up in a modern control arm [inferred]. Use "most studies report 85 to 95%" rather than "you will keep 95%". Technique choices (stick-and-place vs premade incisions) changed shedding and density but not survival in 191 patients [verified, doi:10.1007/s00266-025-04871-3].
  • Most common complication: sterile folliculitis (203 of 2,896); serious complications zero [verified, Garg 2021].

2. Standard treatments, with numbers

2.1 Topical minoxidil

  • Women (Cochrane, 47 RCTs): moderate to marked regrowth RR 1.93; +13.18 hairs/cm² vs placebo; 2% and 5% performed about the same in women [verified, doi:10.1002/14651858.CD007628.pub4].
  • Side effect that matters: scalp itch and dryness in 75% of topical users in one RCT [verified, Janaani 2025, below].

2.2 Low-dose oral minoxidil (LDOM): the 2022 to 2026 boom

  • Popularity: after a New York Times article (18 Aug 2022), first-time US prescriptions rose 2.4x in men and 1.7x in women within 8 weeks; finasteride prescriptions did not move [reported, JAMA Netw Open via US Pharmacist]. Publications on non-topical minoxidil burst after 2019 (33 papers) but only 3 RCTs by 2024 [verified, doi:10.1111/jocd.16427].
  • Head-to-head RCT (n=75, 32 weeks): oral vs topical vs PRP+topical; one-grade improvement 25% / 10% / 25% (not significant); terminal hair density up 47.5% / 48% / 57% [verified, doi:10.1007/s00403-025-03938-0].
  • Does it reverse shrinking? Sublingual minoxidil RCT: fibre diameter -2 µm placebo vs +3 µm at 1.35 mg and +6 µm at 4.05 mg; 0.45 mg did nothing [verified, doi:10.1093/ced/llaf012].
  • Consensus doses reported: start 1.25 mg/day women (range 0.625 to 5), 2.5 mg/day men (1.25 to 5) [verified, doi:10.1007/s40257-025-00990-4]. Pediatric consensus (Sep 2026) supports use from age 4 with weight-based dosing [verified, doi:10.1001/jamadermatol.2026.3749].
  • Also used for chemo-related persistent loss: benefit in 7 of 7 LDOM studies vs 7 of 12 topical [verified, doi:10.1097/SPC.0000000000000832].

2.3 Finasteride, dutasteride, topical finasteride

  • Finasteride 1 mg: 1,553 men randomised, 1,215 into 5-year extensions; durable gains vs placebo on every endpoint [verified, PMID 11809594].
  • In women, finasteride 1 mg was no better than placebo (30/67 vs 33/70) in the Cochrane pool [verified, Cochrane 2016].
  • Dutasteride 0.5 mg vs oral minoxidil (non-randomised, 6 months, n=46 completers): minoxidil groups improved on clinical staging; dutasteride did not change measurably in 6 months [verified, doi:10.3389/fmed.2026.1751116]. Six months is short for an anti-DHT drug [inferred].
  • Topical finasteride: phase III in China (n=270, 24 weeks) beat placebo on target hair count and terminal hairs [verified, doi:10.1097/CM9.0000000000003495]. A European phase III found 0.25% spray about equal to oral 1 mg (+20.2 vs +21.1 hairs/cm²) but it still lowers blood DHT [verified, review doi:10.1111/jocd.14895]. No FDA-approved topical finasteride exists (2.11).

2.4 Clascoterone 5% solution (topical androgen-receptor blocker)

  • SCALP 1 and 2: 1,465 men, 51 US/EU sites, randomised 2:1 vs vehicle, twice daily. Target hair count improvement vs vehicle was 5.39-fold (539%) in one trial and 1.68-fold in the other; absolute hair counts not released [reported, AJMC Dec 2025; HCPLive].
  • 12 months: staying on drug gave 2.39x more gain than switching to vehicle at 6 months; side effects similar to vehicle, no systemic hormonal effects seen [reported, Dermatology Times Apr 2026].
  • The scalp solution does reach the bloodstream (unlike the acne cream); a supratherapeutic dose study found no QT effect [verified, doi:10.1002/cpdd.935].
  • Women: no trial announced [reported]. Percent-improvement headlines over a near-zero vehicle change can look huge; wait for absolute counts [inferred].

2.5 Spironolactone (women)

  • Widely used off-label for female pattern and PCOS hair loss; the Cochrane review (2016) found no RCTs and called for them [verified, Cochrane 2016].
  • In PCOS, 5 open-label RCTs found spironolactone no better than metformin for excess hair, testosterone or BMI; hair loss itself was barely measured [verified, doi:10.1111/cen.70062].
  • Observational cohorts (Sinclair group) report most women stabilise or improve [reported, recalled, not re-verified]. Contraception needed (male fetus risk) [reported].

2.6 PRP

  • PRP vs topical minoxidil (9 RCTs, 451 people): no clear difference in density or terminal hairs; patients more satisfied with PRP (OR 2.77); negative pull test 82.75% vs 52.94% [verified, doi:10.1007/s00266-025-05394-7].
  • Best ranked combination in a 2025 network meta-analysis: PRP + bFGF + minoxidil, +35.12 hairs/cm² over minoxidil alone; in men, finasteride + minoxidil ranked top; in women, microneedling + minoxidil [verified, doi:10.3389/fmed.2025.1638496].

2.7 Microneedling

  • Added to minoxidil (12 RCTs, 631 people): hair count SMD 1.32, diameter SMD 0.34; depth (1 mm or under vs over) and device did not matter [verified, doi:10.1007/s00403-025-04032-1]. Microneedling also boosts skin penetration of topicals, which matters for copper peptides (3.1) [reported].

2.8 Low-level laser/light (LLLT)

  • Alone vs sham: laser comb +17.4 to +17.6 hairs in two women's trials, though users could not tell it from sham [verified, Cochrane 2016].
  • Added to minoxidil (4 RCTs): no extra benefit in hair count or diameter [verified, doi:10.1080/09546634.2024.2447355]. An older network meta-analysis ranked LLLT top, but on very low quality evidence [verified, doi:10.1111/jdv.15081].

2.9 JAK inhibitors for alopecia areata (approved: baricitinib, ritlecitinib, deuruxolitinib)

  • Baricitinib (BRAVE-AA1/2): SALT 20 or less at week 36: 4 mg 38.8% / 35.9%; 2 mg 22.8% / 19.4%; placebo 6.2% / 3.3%; acne, raised CK and cholesterol more common [verified, PMID 35334197].
  • Ritlecitinib (ALLEGRO 2b/3, n=718, age 12+): week 24 50 mg 23%, 200/50 mg 31%, placebo 2%; adverse events similar across arms, no deaths [verified, Lancet 2023].
  • Deuruxolitinib (THRIVE-AA1, n=706): week 24 8 mg twice daily 29.6%, 12 mg 41.5%, placebo 0.8%; FDA approval July 2024 [reported, AJMC].
  • Real world tofacitinib (202 patients, 18 months): 55.9% / 42.6% / 29.2% reached 50/75/90% scalp improvement; longer disease predicted worse response [verified, doi:10.1111/ajd.14325].
  • A substantial share never reaches near-full regrowth and relapse on stopping is common [verified, Drugs 2026 review].

2.10 Hair transplant adjuncts

  • PRP at surgery: systematic review of 3 controlled studies (217 people) found better density and earlier growth, but no trichoscopy and mixed methods [verified, doi:10.7759/cureus.94116]. One RCT (60 men) reported 100% vs 40% reaching 75% regrowth at 6 months [verified, doi:10.1007/s12663-026-02997-8]; the size of that gap is unusual and the journal is outside hair surgery [inferred].
  • Fat-derived stem cells before transplanting into scars: graft survival 87% vs 60% (60 patients, clinic-run) [verified, doi:10.25122/jml-2024-0303].
  • Finasteride/minoxidil after surgery to protect native hair is standard surgeon practice [reported].

2.11 Biotin and collagen (non-peptide and peptide supplements)

  • Biotin: no good evidence it grows hair unless you are deficient (rare) [reported, recalled]. Real harm: doses of 5 to 20 mg/day can fake thyroid blood results (falsely high T3/T4, low TSH); stop 24 to 72 hours before tests [verified, doi:10.1016/j.msard.2026.107974]. This matters most in AA, where thyroid disease is common.
  • Collagen peptides (oral): in a 6-month RCT in menopausal women, 5 g collagen, calcium + vitamin D, and both together all slowed shedding vs placebo; collagen's clearest effect was skin elasticity (+12.23%) [verified, doi:10.3390/clinpract15090168]. Since calcium + vitamin D alone also helped, the shedding effect is not collagen-specific [inferred].

3.1 GHK-Cu and copper peptides

  • What: a natural 3-amino-acid peptide that carries copper; levels fall with age. Developer reviews list larger hair follicle size and better hair transplant success among its effects [verified, Pickart 2008, doi:10.1163/156856208784909435]. That review is by the molecule's commercial developer and gives no transplant numbers [inferred].
  • Ex vivo human follicles: a close cousin, AHK-Cu, at 10⁻¹² to 10⁻⁹ M lengthened human hair follicles in culture and increased dermal papilla cell growth, while lowering cell-death signals [verified, doi:10.1007/BF02978833]. Note: this is AHK-Cu, not GHK-Cu.
  • Animal: a 1993 study found a GHK-copper complex increased growing follicles, reportedly "comparable to 5% minoxidil" [reported, via Perfect Hair Health, 1 Nov 2026].
  • Humans (closest): 7 men (age 28 to 55, Norwood III to IV) who had an unsatisfactory response to at least a year of standard treatment had a solution of minoxidil sulfate 0.5%, dutasteride 0.1% and copper peptides 1.2% driven into the scalp with a tattoo machine (2 mm needles) monthly x5, while staying on topical minoxidil. Top-scalp bald area (SALT score) fell from a median 40% to 7.5%; median regrowth 26.5% of the top scalp; 5 of 7 over 10%. Graded by 4 blinded dermatologists on photos taken at baseline and 1 month after the last session, plus an AI estimate that landed within 7.5 points of them. No scarring or infection [verified, full text read today, Kuceki 2025, doi:10.1016/j.jdin.2025.01.012].
  • Limits the authors state: retrospective, no control arm, no trichoscopy density counts [verified]. Compared with an earlier 3-session minoxidil + dutasteride (no copper) series, the median gain was higher (10% vs 26.5%), but sessions, needle depth and copper all changed at once, so copper's share is unknown [verified/inferred]. The senior author lists advisory roles including a compounding pharmacy [verified, conflict statement].
  • Growth factor cocktail with copper tripeptide-1 + thymosin β4 + VEGF, bFGF, IGF-1, KGF: open-label, 1,000 patients, injected every 3 weeks x8; pull test improved in 83%, hair count up at 1 year (p=0.002), "beneficial in post-transplant patients" [verified, doi:10.1080/14764172.2018.1439965]. No control group; clinic-developed product. Mouse follow-up showed hair retained around injection sites [verified, doi:10.1097/GOX.0000000000002843].
  • Skin penetration: concentrated solution moved copper into skin over 48 h; lower concentration showed none in 9 h; microneedles increased it [reported, via PHH].
  • Safety: a 2018 Cosmetic Ingredient Review panel judged copper cosmetic peptides safe as used [reported, via PHH].
  • Transplant: copper-peptide aftercare sprays have been sold since the 1990s; a hair-loss information site found no study or numbers behind them and said hundreds of patients healed fine without [verified, HLC 2022]. A 2009 forum moderator called the benefit "debatable" [verified, HRN].
  • Injected GHK-Cu for hair: no human study found [verified, by absence in PubMed today].
  • Grade: plausible mechanism, no controlled human proof of GHK-Cu alone [inferred].

3.2 Copper peptides in cosmetics

  • Covered above. Most commercial "copper peptide" hair serums have no product-level trial; the 2025 trichology review lists them among heavily marketed topicals with small, conflicted studies [verified, doi:10.4103/ijt.ijt_8_23].

3.3 Thymosin β4 (TB-500's parent) and TB-500

  • Mice/rats (NIH, 2004): Tβ4 accelerated hair growth in normal rats and mice; it is made by bulge stem cell descendants during the growth cycle and, at nanomolar levels, increased stem cell migration and differentiation plus MMP-2 [verified, doi:10.1096/fj.03-0244fje]. Confirmed in a transgenic overexpressing mouse [verified, doi:10.1196/annals.1415.009].
  • Goats: exogenous Tβ4 increased secondary follicles and cashmere output [verified, review doi:10.1111/jcmm.16241].
  • Human follicles in a dish (2012, J Invest Dermatol, Paus lab, scalp from a UK transplant clinic): Tβ4 cut hair shaft output by 10 to 20% vs control; at 1,000 ng/ml for 7 days it shortened the growth phase and pushed follicles into catagen early. In the same letter, thymulin (a different thymic peptide, 10 pg/ml) raised shaft growth and kept follicles in growth phase longer, with more dividing and fewer dying cells [reported, PubMed lists it as a letter with no abstract; numbers read via the excerpt quoted in an HLT thread; PMID 22402437, doi:10.1038/jid.2012.2]. Authorship confirmed on PubMed (Meier, Farjo, Paus and others) [verified].
  • TB-500 is a short fragment marketed as the "active" part; no hair study of the fragment itself exists [reported].
  • Reading: the mouse result is real, the only human tissue result went the other way. Flag clearly [inferred].

3.4 BPC-157

  • Zero PubMed results for BPC-157 and hair [verified, search today]. Any hair claim is extrapolated from wound and blood-vessel studies in rats [inferred]. Clinic blogs market it for "follicle health" without citations [verified, search result titles only].

3.5 PTD-DBM (Wnt-releasing peptide)

  • Mechanism: CXXC5 brakes Wnt/β-catenin by binding Dishevelled. CXXC5 was raised in miniaturised human follicles; a competitor peptide (PTD-DBM) that blocks this binding sped hair regrowth and new follicle formation after wounding in mice, and added valproic acid boosted it [verified, doi:10.1016/j.jid.2017.04.038].
  • Follow-ups by the same Yonsei group and its spin-off moved to a small molecule (KY19382) that did the same in mice and human follicle culture [verified, doi:10.1111/bph.15438].
  • A 2025 review says PTD-DBM has "demonstrated clinical efficacy" [verified, doi:10.3390/cells14110779] but no published human trial or registry record was found today [verified by absence, PubMed + ClinicalTrials.gov]. Treat the review claim as unsupported until a trial appears.

3.6 KPV and α-MSH

  • α-MSH is one of the follicle's own immune-privilege guardians; its loss is part of AA [verified, doi:10.3390/ijms25084409]. IL-15 raised α-MSH and restored privilege in human follicles [verified, doi:10.1016/j.jaut.2024.103217].
  • The related tripeptide K(D)PT (a close cousin of KPV) restored pigment production in human follicles under inflammatory stress in organ culture, proposed for white regrowth after AA [verified, doi:10.1111/j.1365-2133.2008.08872.x].
  • No KPV hair-growth study in animals or people [verified by absence]. Hypothesis grade [speculated].

3.7 Thymosin α-1 for alopecia areata

  • Zero PubMed hits for thymosin α-1/thymalfasin with alopecia areata [verified, search today]. In the 2012 human follicle study (3.3), thymosin α-1 at 100 to 1,000 ng/ml slightly lowered hair shaft growth and did not change cycling much [reported, excerpt]. It is an immune stimulator; AA is an overactive immune disease, so the logic is unclear [inferred]. Low-dose IL-2 (to boost regulatory T cells) has a completed phase 3 in AA (NCT02557074, n=44) [verified, registry], which is the nearest real "immune-rebalancing" approach.

3.8 Melanotan and hair

  • No hair-growth evidence. A 2026 case report described depigmented patches on face and neck after melanotan in a patient with atopic dermatitis and alopecia areata [verified title, doi:10.1093/ced/llag221; abstract not available]. Forum users also report darker body hair [reported, HLT, see 4.2].

3.9 GH peptides and IGF-1

  • IGF-1 sits in the injected cocktail above (3.1) and in cosmetic scalp serums now in trials (NCT06985121, NCT07271212) [verified, registry]. No study of GH secretagogues (CJC-1295, ipamorelin, MK-677) on hair counts found [verified by absence]. IGF-1 supports the growth phase in follicle biology [reported, recalled]. A forum post titled "GH peptides + blend saved my hair" exists on a Reddit mirror and was excluded [verified title only].

3.10 GLP-1s: shedding odds (sema, tirz)

  • RCT meta-analysis (9 studies, 4,114 users): RR 3.25 vs placebo, 3.59 in obesity RCTs; event rate 3.9% [verified, doi:10.1016/j.diabres.2026.113333].
  • Tirzepatide trials (manufacturer data): SURMOUNT-1 5 mg 5.1%, 10 mg 4.9%, 15 mg 5.7% vs 0.9%; SURMOUNT-2 (diabetes) 2.9 to 3.2% vs 0.6%; SURMOUNT-3 7.0% vs 1.4%; diabetes program pooled 0.25% [verified, Lilly medical information]. Hair loss tracks weight loss size, not just the drug [inferred].
  • Semaglutide: doses under 2 mg/week rarely implicated; obesity doses more often [verified, systematic review of 24 studies, doi:10.1177/00368504261444578].
  • Real world vs trials: 3 to 7% in trials vs over 70% in some real-world cohorts [verified, doi:10.1007/s00592-026-02812-3] (self-selected and differently defined).
  • Records study vs metformin (12 months): sema RR 1.43, tirz RR 1.68, liraglutide and dulaglutide not raised [reported, via clinic blog summary with internal inconsistencies, CED Clinic].
  • FDA reports Jan 2022 to Jun 2026: alopecia relatively more reported with tirzepatide (ROR 0.74 sema vs tirz) [verified, doi:10.1007/s12020-026-04840-z].
  • Paradox: some reports of improved pattern and scarring alopecias on GLP-1s, likely via metabolic and anti-inflammatory effects [verified, Acta Diabetol 2026]. Cross-link GLP1 pages.

3.11 Cosmetic peptides (Redensyl, Capixyl, acetyl tetrapeptide-3, Procapil)

  • Redensyl + another active vs vehicle, single-blind RCT (n=44, 24 weeks): anagen:telogen ratio 2.25 to 6.02; 7.7% great, 73.1% moderate improvement [verified, doi:10.1111/dth.13734]. Vehicle group results not given in abstract.
  • Acetyl tetrapeptide-3 + biochanin A + ginseng (the Capixyl-type mix) vs 3% minoxidil, triple-blind RCT (n=32): terminal hairs +8.3% vs +8.7%, no significant difference [verified, J Clin Aesthet Dermatol 2020, PMID 33584955]. No placebo arm.
  • Open-label industry studies report big percentages (e.g., hair density +37.92%) with no control [verified, doi:10.7759/cureus.77066; doi:10.1111/jocd.16084].
  • Review verdict: small samples, no standard-therapy comparison, unspecified hair loss types, conflicts of interest; maybe an add-on [verified, doi:10.4103/ijt.ijt_8_23].
  • New: a Thai double-blind RCT of a peptide serum vs topical minoxidil (n=80) is recruiting, completion Nov 2026 [verified, NCT07536100].

3.12 Exosomes

  • Pilot RCT, 20 men (Norwood 2 to 3), plant-extract exosome formula vs saline x4 sessions: median hair gain 9.5 vs 1.5 at week 16 [verified, doi:10.3390/life15030500].
  • At least 9 registered exosome hair trials, including a 120-man double-blind umbilical cord exosome RCT completing Nov 2026 (NCT07693244) and exosomes vs PRP (NCT06239207, completed, no results posted) [verified, registry].
  • Regulatory: no FDA-approved exosome product; FDA warning letters to sellers in 2025 [reported, search result titles only; not fetched]. A hair dermatologist (Donovan, 2022) said exosomes for hair were not approved by FDA or Health Canada and that in North America they should only be given inside a trial; he had not used them [verified, Donovan Medical].
  • Alopecia areata (new in v2): a 2026 systematic review found no RCTs, only 2 clinical studies (1 retrospective cohort, 1 case report) plus 5 animal AA models showing regrowth [verified, doi:10.3390/ijms27010021]. Clinician-documented cases with photos:
  • 2 women with severe AA who had failed JAK pills (tofacitinib, baricitinib) and other drugs: one session of fat-derived exosome injections; SALT 79.6 to 59.6 and 70.2 to 58.6 at 3 months [verified, full text, doi:10.1155/crdm/4908438]. That is a 12 to 20 point improvement, real but far from full regrowth; both then started minoxidil [inferred].
  • 1 man, 35, beard AA for 4 years, failed steroid injections and minoxidil: 2 sessions; regrowth and white hairs turning dark from week 6, held at 3 months [verified, full text with photos, doi:10.1002/ccr3.71558]. AA can remit by itself, so single cases cannot separate drug from course [inferred].

4.6 Takeaway for the page (rewritten in v2)

  • What worked, by count (non-Reddit): exosomes 5 of 5 (including 3 clinician-documented AA cases, likely publication bias); copper aftercare sprays 3 of 5 for healing and redness, with one surgeon seeing unusually fast healing but unsure it was the copper; TB-500 2 of 3 (1 worse); GLP-1 shedders mostly recover once weight stabilises, faster with oral minoxidil.
  • Strongest human result with a peptide in it is still the 7-man tattooed minoxidil + dutasteride + copper series graded by blinded dermatologists (3.1). It is not a copper-alone result.
  • The pattern across hair-loss positives is unchanged from v1: thicker existing hair and less shedding, rarely new hair in bald areas [inferred].
  • Transplant patients: no peptide has graft-survival data. Normal survival is already 85 to 95% (1.6), so any add-on has little room to show a gain [inferred]. The safety point that recurs is a surgeon one: tell your clinic, avoid unknown injectables near fresh grafts in the first 10 days, and don't spray anything that runs across healing incisions without asking.
  • No first-hand outside-Reddit data for KPV, injected GHK-Cu, or GH peptides other than one confounded MK-677 post. Those gaps are real, not search failures: the volume lives on Reddit and its mirrors.

5. Pairings and related conditions

  • Most common real-world stacks: minoxidil (topical or oral) + finasteride/dutasteride; add microneedling or PRP; peptides used on top, never alone [verified, forum regimens and network meta-analysis].
  • Strongest evidence pairing: finasteride + minoxidil in men; microneedling + minoxidil in women [verified, doi:10.3389/fmed.2025.1638496; combination meta-analysis doi:10.1111/dth.13741].
  • GLP-1 + pattern loss: shedding can unmask pattern loss; dermatologists start minoxidil early [reported].
  • AA + JAK + oral minoxidil: 168 of 202 tofacitinib patients also took LDOM [verified].
  • Chemo hair loss: scalp cooling is the only proven preventer of persistent loss; LDOM helped in 7 of 7 studies [verified].
  • Related pages: PCOS, GLP-1, Acne (androgens, clascoterone), Achilles/Tendon (TB-500, BPC-157 compound pages), GHK-Cu Library page.

6. Options (ordered by evidence)

  1. Find the type first. Pattern vs shedding vs patches vs scarring change everything. Bloods for ferritin, thyroid, vitamin D, zinc in diffuse shedding [reported, dermatologist practice].
  2. Pattern loss, men: finasteride (oral or topical) + minoxidil (topical or low-dose oral) [verified]. Clascoterone pending (filing 2027) [reported].
  3. Pattern loss, women: topical minoxidil (only approved drug) or low-dose oral minoxidil; spironolactone off-label (weak trial base) [verified].
  4. Add-ons with RCTs: microneedling (best), PRP (similar to minoxidil), LLLT (alone helps, adds little to minoxidil) [verified].
  5. Telogen effluvium (including GLP-1, post-illness): fix trigger, protein and iron; it usually self-resolves in 3 to 12 months; slower weight loss; minoxidil if prolonged [verified/reported].
  6. Alopecia areata, moderate to severe: JAK inhibitors (20 to 40% near full regrowth in 6 to 9 months) [verified].
  7. Transplant: keep native hair on medical therapy; PRP adjunct has weak but positive data [verified].
  8. Peptides (experimental): topical GHK-Cu (low risk, weak evidence, best paired with microneedling); TB-500 (conflicting: mice yes, human follicles no); PTD-DBM (mouse only, unstable); BPC-157, KPV, Tα1 (no hair data) [verified/inferred].

7. Sixteen usual questions (evidence answers)

  1. Is it normal to lose 100 hairs a day? Yes, 50 to 100 is normal [verified, DermNet].
  2. Why did my hair fall out months after I was sick? Follicles pushed into rest wait about 3 months before dropping; COVID median 2 months [verified].
  3. Will my GLP-1 hair loss stop? Usually: trichologists expect 3 to 6 months of shedding; full fullness can take about a year [reported]. Pattern loss underneath will not fix itself [reported].
  4. How likely is hair loss on Ozempic/Wegovy/Mounjaro? Trials: about 3 to 7% vs about 1% placebo; higher with bigger weight loss and in women [verified].
  5. Is oral minoxidil safe? In 1,404 patients only 1.7% stopped for side effects; extra hair 15%, fluid retention 1.3%, fast heart 0.9% [verified]. Pericardial effusion is rare and mostly linked to dosing errors [verified, review].
  6. Oral or topical minoxidil, which is better? Similar regrowth in an RCT; oral avoids scalp irritation (75% with topical) [verified].
  7. Does finasteride ruin your sex life? RCTs show a 1.57x higher rate of sexual side effects, mostly reversible; a small subset report lasting effects (post-finasteride syndrome) [verified].
  8. Is topical finasteride safer? It lowers blood DHT less but not to zero; FDA received 32 adverse reports for compounded versions 2019 to 2024 [verified].
  9. Does biotin work? Not unless deficient; it can fake thyroid test results [verified/reported].
  10. Does collagen help hair? One RCT showed slower shedding, but calcium + vitamin D did the same [verified].
  11. Do copper peptides regrow hair? No controlled human trial of GHK-Cu alone; the best data (7 men, blinded photo grading) mix it with minoxidil and dutasteride; forum users mostly report thicker existing hair, not new hair [verified/reported].
  12. Does TB-500 grow hair? It did in mice; in human follicles in a dish thymosin β4 slowed growth [verified/reported].
  13. What is PTD-DBM? A peptide that releases the Wnt "grow" switch; worked in mice; no published human trial [verified].
  14. What works for alopecia areata? JAK pills: 23 to 41% reach near full scalp in 24 to 36 weeks vs 1 to 6% on placebo [verified].
  15. Is shedding after a hair transplant normal? Yes, grafts shed within about 6 weeks and regrow from month 3 to 5; native-hair shock loss in about 4%, much more in women; most studies report 85 to 95% of grafts surviving in normal scalp [verified/reported].
  16. Do BPC-157 or TB-500 help a transplant heal? No study in transplant patients; one surgeon blog advises not starting them in the first 10 days unless your surgeon plans it, and to tell the clinic about anything you inject [verified, clinic blog].

8. Warnings (bottom of page)

  • Finasteride/dutasteride: sexual side effects 1.57x placebo (finasteride RR 1.66) [verified]. FDA reports show more depression, anxiety and suicidality reports for finasteride, especially young men on hair doses [verified, doi:10.3390/brainsci16040394]; an observational meta-analysis found no significant link (HR 1.07) [verified, doi:10.3389/fruro.2026.1881635]. Post-finasteride syndrome (lasting sexual and psychiatric symptoms after stopping) is reported in a subset; mechanism proposed via lower neurosteroids [verified, doi:10.3390/neurosci7010020]. A public health essay argues regulators under-studied it [verified, doi:10.1093/pubmed/fdag077]. Screen mood before and during [verified].
  • Oral minoxidil heart effects: fluid retention 1.3 to 10%, fast heart or dizziness under 5%, pericardial effusion extremely rare, often linked to compounding dose errors [verified, doi:10.3390/jcm14061805]. A large records study found no association with pericardial effusion [verified title, doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2025.02.055]. Contraindicated with pericardial disease, uncontrolled blood pressure, pheochromocytoma [verified]. Early shedding in 16 to 22% is expected [verified].
