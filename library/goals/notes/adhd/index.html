1. What ADHD is

Definition. ADHD is a neurodevelopmental condition with lasting patterns of inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interfere with daily life. It has three presentations: predominantly inattentive, predominantly hyperactive-impulsive, and combined. [verified, DSM-5 framework as summarised in Zavadenko 2014, DOI 10.15690/vsp.v13i4.1084]

Adult diagnosis (DSM-5). [reported, standard DSM-5 criteria; the DSM text was not fetched this pass] - Adults (17 and over) need 5 symptoms in either domain (children need 6). - Several symptoms must have been present before age 12. - Symptoms must show up in 2 or more settings. - Symptoms must clearly impair functioning and not be better explained by another condition.

How common it is - US children, 2022: 11.4% of 3–17-year-olds had ever been diagnosed, about 7.1 million children. 10.5% currently have ADHD. Of those, 53.6% take medication, 44.4% had behavioural treatment in the past year, and 30.1% get no ADHD-specific treatment. [verified via press release, National Survey of Children's Health (NSCH) 2022, n=45,483 interviews, J Clin Child Adolesc Psychol 2024, DOI 10.1080/15374416.2024.2335625] - Children worldwide: about 7.2% (Thomas 2015 meta-analysis). [reported, from a search snippet; not opened] - Adults worldwide: 2.58% have persistent ADHD that began in childhood. 6.76% have adult ADHD symptoms whether or not they started in childhood. That is about 140 million and 366 million adults in 2020. Prevalence falls with age. [verified, Song 2021, J Glob Health, PMID 33692893, DOI 10.7189/jogh.11.04009] - Typical age of onset: median 8–13 years, along with phobias and autism. [verified, Solmi 2021, 192 studies, Mol Psychiatry, PMID 34079068, DOI 10.1038/s41380-021-01161-7]

Does it go away? Usually not cleanly. - The MTA cohort followed 558 children with ADHD into adulthood. About 30% reached full remission at some point, but 60% of those saw symptoms return. - 63.8% had a fluctuating course. - Only 9.1% were in sustained remission at the end. - 10.8% stayed persistently symptomatic. [verified, Sibley 2022, Am J Psychiatry, PMID 34384227, DOI 10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.21010032] - Plain meaning: about 1 in 10 truly "grows out of it". Most people go through good and bad stretches, often tracking life demands.

Women diagnosed later - In 900 adults first diagnosed at a Barcelona clinic, women were diagnosed on average at 28.96 years and men at 24.13 years. That is about 5 years later, even though symptoms started at the same age. - Women had more depression (P=0.003), more anxiety (P<0.001), and worse functioning. [reported, Oltra-Arañó et al, ECNP congress abstract Oct 2025, not peer-reviewed; via Psychiatric Times] - The usual explanation is that girls more often have the inattentive presentation, which is less disruptive, so it gets missed or mislabelled as anxiety or depression. [inferred; widely stated]

Brain model (dopamine, norepinephrine, prefrontal networks). ADHD is understood as weaker regulation by the prefrontal cortex (planning, holding information in mind, braking impulses) and its loops with the striatum, which are tuned by dopamine and norepinephrine. [reported; consensus model, summarised in the WFADHD Consensus 2021] - Stimulants raise dopamine and norepinephrine at the synapse. - Alpha-2A agonists (guanfacine) strengthen norepinephrine signalling in the prefrontal cortex. - Every effective medication acts on at least one of these two systems. [inferred, from the mechanisms in section 2]

Heritability. - Twin studies put heritability at about 74% (mean of 37 studies). DNA-based (SNP) heritability is 0.22, and 12 genome-wide significant genetic regions were found in 20,183 cases. - Adult clinical diagnoses give about 72%. Adult self-report alone gives 30–40%, mostly because of measurement error. [verified, Faraone & Larsson 2019, Mol Psychiatry, DOI 10.1038/s41380-018-0070-0] - Plain meaning: genes explain most of the variation between people, through many genes of small effect. It is not caused by parenting or sugar.

Executive function. Weak working memory, inhibition, planning and flexibility are typical, but not universal. [reported, WFADHD 208-statement consensus, Neurosci Biobehav Rev 2021, PMID 33549739, DOI 10.1016/j.neubiorev.2021.01.022]

Emotional dysregulation. - Adults with ADHD scored much higher than controls on general emotion dysregulation (Hedges' g 1.17) and on emotional lability (g 1.20). - Dysregulation tracked symptom severity (r=0.54). [verified, Beheshti 2020, 13 studies, n=2,535, BMC Psychiatry, PMID 32164655, DOI 10.1186/s12888-020-2442-7] - Plain meaning: g≈1.2 is a large effect, larger than many attention measures. Mood swings and quick anger are a core part of adult ADHD, not an add-on.

Overlap with other conditions - Autism: 28% of autistic people also have ADHD (96 studies). Among autistic people, 20% have anxiety disorders and 13% sleep-wake disorders. [reported, Lai 2019, Lancet Psychiatry, PMID 31447415; via The Transmitter] - Epilepsy: 22.3% of people with epilepsy have ADHD, rated strong credibility. [verified, Fornaro 2026 umbrella review, PMID 41895003, DOI 10.1016/j.euroneuro.2026.112820] - Addiction: 23.1% of people in treatment for substance use disorders meet ADHD criteria (29 studies). [verified, van Emmerik-van Oortmerssen 2012, PMID 22209385, DOI 10.1016/j.drugalcdep.2011.12.007] - Eating disorders: ADHD comes with a 3.8-fold higher risk of an eating disorder. People with binge eating disorder have a 5.8-fold higher rate of ADHD. [reported, Nazar 2016 meta-analysis, 4,013 ADHD cases, via APSARD summary; PMID 27859581 not opened] - Sleep: sleep problems are common. A Cochrane review of non-randomised studies found methylphenidate itself raised insomnia and sleep problems (risk ratio 2.58), with trouble falling asleep in 17.9% of children taking it. [verified, Storebø 2018, Cochrane, PMID 29744873, doi:10.1002/14651858.CD012069.pub2] A pooled prevalence of sleep disorders in untreated ADHD was still not found in v2 (see Gaps). Melatonin: see Usual questions.

2. Studies: standard medications

2a. The key comparison: Cortese 2018 network meta-analysis

[verified, Lancet Psychiatry, PMID 30097390, DOI 10.1016/S2215-0366(18)30269-4] - 133 double-blind RCTs: 10,068 children and adolescents and 8,131 adults in the efficacy analysis. - Effect sizes are standardised mean differences (SMD) vs placebo on clinician ratings at about 12 weeks. A negative number means symptoms improved more than placebo.

Drug Children/teens SMD Adults SMD
Amphetamines (incl. lisdexamfetamine) −1.02 −0.79
Methylphenidate −0.78 −0.49
Atomoxetine −0.56 −0.45
Bupropion (all drugs beat placebo) −0.46
Modafinil beat placebo +0.16 (no effect)
  • Tolerability: amphetamines had more dropouts than placebo at both ages: children odds ratio (OR) 2.30, adults OR 3.26. In adults, methylphenidate (OR 2.39), atomoxetine (2.33) and modafinil (4.01) also had more dropouts. In children, guanfacine had more dropouts (OR 2.64).
  • Conclusion: methylphenidate is first choice for children, amphetamines for adults.
  • Big caveat: there was not enough data at 26 or 52 weeks.
  • Plain meaning: an SMD of about 0.8–1.0 is among the largest effects in psychiatry. Antidepressants are about 0.3.

2b. Adult update: Ostinelli 2025 (Lancet Psychiatry, 113 RCTs in total, 14,887 adults)

v2 correction: v1 said "113 drug trials and 38 non-drug trials". The abstract says 113 RCTs in total: 63 drug trials (6,875 people), 28 therapy trials, 10 neurostimulation or neurofeedback trials. - Only stimulants and atomoxetine reduced core symptoms at 12 weeks on both self-ratings and clinician ratings. - Stimulants: self-rated SMD −0.39, clinician-rated −0.61. - Atomoxetine: self-rated −0.38, clinician-rated −0.51. - CBT (−0.76), mindfulness (−0.79), psychoeducation (−0.77), cognitive remediation (−1.35) and tDCS (−0.78) beat placebo only on clinician ratings, not on self-ratings. - Atomoxetine (OR 1.43) and guanfacine (OR 3.70) had more dropouts than placebo. - Medications did not improve quality of life. Long-term evidence is thin. [verified, PMID 39701638, doi:10.1016/S2215-0366(24)00360-2] - Plain meaning: in adults, the stimulant effect looks smaller here (about 0.4 to 0.6) than in Cortese 2018 (0.79 for amphetamines), because stimulants were pooled and self-ratings were included. [inferred]

2c. How each one works

[reported unless marked] - Methylphenidate: blocks the dopamine and norepinephrine transporters (reuptake inhibitor). - Amphetamines: block reuptake and also push dopamine and norepinephrine out of storage vesicles. Lisdexamfetamine is an inactive prodrug converted in the blood, which smooths onset and lowers snorting or injection appeal. - Atomoxetine: selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor. Because the prefrontal cortex clears dopamine through the norepinephrine transporter, it also raises prefrontal dopamine. It takes weeks to work. - Viloxazine ER (Qelbree): modulates norepinephrine (plus serotonin receptors). - Adult phase 3: n=374, 200–600 mg/day for 6 weeks. AISRS response rate was described as 30% higher than placebo; the exact effect size was not in the coverage. [reported, Nasser 2022, CNS Drugs, DOI 10.1007/s40263-022-00938-w] - Guanfacine ER and clonidine ER: alpha-2 agonists that strengthen prefrontal norepinephrine signalling. - Effect size about 0.5 vs about 0.8 for stimulants. Benefit builds over 2–5 weeks. - In one study, 59% had sedation on guanfacine alone vs 11% when it was combined with a stimulant. - 20% of adults dropped out in the adult trial. - Stopping clonidine abruptly can cause rebound high blood pressure. [reported, Carlat Report review] - Bupropion: weak dopamine and norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor. Off-label for ADHD. Adult SMD −0.46 (Cortese). [verified] - Centanafadine (Simtriyo), new: triple reuptake inhibitor (norepinephrine, dopamine, serotonin). - FDA approved about 24 July 2026 for adults and for children 6 and over weighing more than 20 kg. [verified across Psychiatric Times, HCPLive, Drug Topics, July–Aug 2026] - Paediatric phase 3: the high dose separated from placebo by week 1, with a 46–65% higher chance of meaningful symptom change. The low dose failed. Industry-authored. [verified, Ward 2026, J Atten Disord, PMID 42779129, DOI 10.1177/10870547261483444] - Controlled-substance schedule: as of 1 Oct 2026 it was still awaiting DEA scheduling review, and is expected to be scheduled. Label carries class warnings on abuse and misuse, and suicidal thinking in children and teens. [reported, Carlat Report, 1 Oct 2026] - Adult effect size: Carlat describes it as roughly half that of stimulants, like other non-stimulants; primary adult papers not pulled (see Gaps). [reported]

2d. Heart and blood pressure numbers

  • Farhat 2025 (102 RCTs, 13,315 children and 9,387 adults, median 7 weeks, Lancet Psychiatry, PMID 40203844, doi:10.1016/S2215-0366(25)00062-8): effects were small. [verified in v2 against the abstract; adult estimates are very low certainty] Only 4 RCTs covered the medium term and none the long term.
  • Methylphenidate in children: systolic +1.81, diastolic +2.42 mmHg.
  • Atomoxetine in children: pulse +5.58 bpm.
  • Amphetamines in adults: systolic +2.3, diastolic +3.07 mmHg.
  • Viloxazine in adults: pulse +5.8 bpm.
  • Guanfacine lowered blood pressure and pulse (adults: systolic −10.1 mmHg, very low certainty).
  • Stimulants did not raise blood pressure more than atomoxetine or viloxazine. The authors advise monitoring on any ADHD drug, not just stimulants. [verified]
  • Long-term Swedish data (Zhang 2024, 278,027 people with ADHD; 10,388 heart and blood-vessel disease cases): [verified, JAMA Psychiatry, PMID 37991787, DOI 10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2023.4294]
  • Each year of use raised risk by 4% (adjusted OR 1.04).
  • 3–5 years of use: OR 1.27. More than 5 years: OR 1.23.
  • Hypertension OR 1.72–1.80; arterial disease OR 1.65.
  • Short-term methylphenidate (Swedish registry, 26,710 users vs 225,672 controls): incidence rate ratio (IRR) 1.41 in the 6 months after starting, against 1.18 in controls. There was little evidence of a 20% or greater rise in risk. [verified, JAMA Netw Open 2024, PMID 38446477, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2024.1349]
  • Plain meaning: a few mmHg and a few beats per minute is usually harmless for one person but adds up across years and populations. Monitor blood pressure, especially past 3 years of use.

2e. Benefits beyond symptoms

Swedish target-trial emulation (n=148,581, 2007–2020): in the first 2 years, medication was linked to fewer first events. [reported via BMJ press release; BMJ 2025, DOI 10.1136/bmj-2024-083658; observational] - Suicidal behaviour −17% - Substance misuse −15% - Transport accidents −12% - Criminality −13% - Accidental injury: no significant change

2f. Psychosis and mania risk

High-dose amphetamine was linked to psychosis or mania. [verified in v2, Moran 2024, Am J Psychiatry, PMID 39262211, doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.20230329; McLean Hospital case-control, 1,374 cases vs 2,748 hospitalised controls aged 16 to 35. The authors flag selection bias from using hospitalised controls.] - Any amphetamine: adjusted OR 2.68. - More than 30 mg dextroamphetamine-equivalent: adjusted OR 5.28. - Methylphenidate: OR 0.91 (no increase).

2g. Misuse and abuse potential

  • Stimulant side effects overall: 93 RCTs found more side effects than placebo (risk ratio 1.34). [verified, Oliva 2025, JAMA Netw Open, PMID 40343695, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2025.9492]
  • US adults misusing prescription stimulants: 7.5% of under-30s in 2016, 3.7% in 2023; 1.9% of all adults in 2023. [reported, Maglione 2026, J Clin Psychopharmacol, DOI 10.1097/JCP.0000000000002202]

2h. Tolerance

  • MTA study: the medication effect held at d≈0.6 at 3, 9 and 14 months.
  • But: methylphenidate doses rose from 31 to 38 mg/day (about 20%) in year 1.
  • At 15 years: only 6.8% were still medicated as adults. Cumulative dose did not predict adult symptom severity.
  • Swanson & Grace argue this pattern points to long-term tolerance. Swanson holds a pending patent on an alternative regimen, a conflict of interest. [reported, Psychiatric Times Nov 2025]
  • Within-day (acute) tolerance: documented, and the reason rising-release formulations like Concerta were designed. [reported]
  • Plain meaning: short-term tolerance is real. Long-term tolerance is plausible but argued from indirect data.

2i. Shortages 2022–2026

  • The FDA listed IR amphetamine shortages from 12 Oct 2022, lisdexamfetamine from 14 Jul 2023, and methylphenidate ER from 26 Jul 2023. All three were still listed on 16 Oct 2026. [reported, secondary page summarising the FDA database; verify on fda.gov]
  • The DEA's 2026 production quota (effective 5 Jan 2026) raised amphetamine and lisdexamfetamine limits above proposed levels. [reported]
  • US children: the Oct 2022 shortage gave no significant overall drop in children receiving stimulants. IR mixed-salt dispensing fell while dexmethylphenidate rose, meaning children switched drugs. [verified, Pediatrics 2025, PMID 39864457, DOI 10.1542/peds.2024-068558]
  • Medicaid: generic IR prices rose 6% during the shortage. [verified, Health Aff Sch 2026, PMID 42312118, DOI 10.1093/haschl/qxag136]
  • Telehealth: DEA telehealth prescribing flexibilities expire 31 Dec 2026 unless extended. [reported]

3. Studies: peptides and nootropics people use for focus

Evidence grade key: A = RCTs in ADHD. B = human trials, but not in ADHD or poorly controlled. C = animal or mechanism data only. D = nothing, or compromised data.

Semax (Met-Glu-His-Phe-Pro-Gly-Pro, an ACTH(4-10) analogue). Grade: B− for ADHD (Russian only), C for mechanism

  • Russian registration: registered as 0.1% and 1% nasal drops, prescription-only and on Russia's vital medicines list. The manufacturer is a Russian centre.
  • The 0.1% label lists minimal brain dysfunction in children 5 and over, explicitly including ADHD.
  • Contraindications include anxiety disorders, seizure history and acute psychosis.
  • The only listed side effect is mild nasal irritation.
  • v2 re-check of the 0.1% label (RLS reference, label text dated 2003, page updated 2018): paediatric use 5 to 14 years for minimal brain dysfunction including ADHD; side effects listed as agitation and insomnia; contraindications include anxiety disorders, acute psychosis, endocrine disease, pregnancy. The page cites no clinical trial behind the ADHD indication. [verified, RLS] The two label versions differ on side effects; the newer page should be preferred and the difference noted. [inferred] [verified, RLS drug reference page, label approved 2008, page updated May 2026; registration certificate number not confirmed]
  • Russian guidance, 2026: a 16 Jul 2026 Expert Council of Russian paediatric neurologists put Semax in Group B ("limited evidence base") for add-on ADHD treatment. Only Prospekta reached Group A (double-blind RCT). The council said add-ons do not replace atomoxetine. [verified, Lapochkin 2026, Zh Nevrol Psikhiatr, PMID 42910684, DOI 10.17116/jnevro2026126092126]
  • ADHD RCTs: no randomised, placebo-controlled Semax trial in ADHD was found on PubMed or ClinicalTrials.gov. The first English paper proposing Semax for ADHD is a 2006 hypothesis piece with no data. [verified, Tsai 2006, Med Hypotheses, PMID 16996699]
  • v2 Russian-literature search (priority gap): [verified absence for the searches run]
  • PubMed "Semax AND children AND attention" returns only the 2026 Expert Council and the 2006 hypothesis paper. Of 45 Semax records with patients or clinical terms, the human studies are stroke (Gusev 2018, n=110, open, BDNF rose), eye disease, psoriasis with metabolic syndrome (n=118), and healthy-volunteer imaging. None is ADHD.
  • ClinicalTrials.gov: zero Semax or Selank trials registered for any condition.
  • The Russian ADHD trials that turned up test other products: Prospekta (Zavadenko 2022, double-blind RCT, 363 children, 35 centres: ≥25% symptom drop in 55.9% vs 43.3% on placebo; mean difference 2.09 points on ADHD-RS; maker-funded) [verified, doi:10.17116/jnevro202212211162], Mexidol (MEGA study, 333 children, SNAP-IV change −9.3 vs −3.9 placebo, maker-supported) [verified via medi.ru] and Noofen (open, n=50) [verified via mediasphera].
  • Context on Prospekta, the only Group A product: it belongs to the "release-active" ultra-dilute antibody class that a BMJ Evidence-Based Medicine paper classed as a form of homeopathy (stated dilutions up to 1:10^1991). [verified, Panchin et al, BMJ EBM] Plain meaning: Russia's strongest-rated ADHD add-on is itself contested, and Semax ranks below it.
  • eLibrary.ru and CyberLeninka were not searchable directly from this session (see Gaps).
  • Mechanism (animals):
  • A single intranasal dose in rats changed BDNF and NGF gene expression. It rose in the frontal cortex at 20 min and in both regions by 90 min, back to baseline by about 8 h. [verified, Agapova 2008, PMID 18756821]
  • Alone, Semax did not change striatal dopamine in rodents. It raised serotonin turnover (extracellular 5-HIAA up to 180%). [verified, Eremin 2005, PMID 16362768]
  • Given before d-amphetamine, Semax amplified amphetamine's dopamine release. Mouse locomotion rose to 261% vs 182% with amphetamine alone. [verified, Eremin 2004, PMID 15188751]
  • Chronic intranasal dosing (0.05 mg/kg for 10–14 days) had anti-anxiety and antidepressant-like effects in rats. [verified, PMID 17850024]
  • Healthy humans: resting-state fMRI in 52 healthy people (Semax, Selank or placebo) changed amygdala and temporal connectivity at 5–20 min. No cognitive outcome was reported. [verified, Panikratova 2020, PMID 32342318]
  • US status (updated v2):
  • 24 Jul 2026: FDA's Pharmacy Compounding Advisory Committee voted to recommend Semax for the 503A bulks list, by a narrow margin (count not published in coverage). FDA staff had recommended against all seven peptides reviewed. [reported, AJMC 31 Jul 2026]
  • No final FDA decision or rule as of 3 Nov 2026. [verified via law-firm summaries]
  • 28 to 30 Oct 2026: HHS published a draft interim enforcement-discretion policy (Docket HHS-OASH-2026-0496). It says HHS will not act against licensed pharmacies or physicians compounding six peptides, including Semax (intranasal only), if conditions are met: FDA-registered bulk source with a certificate of analysis, patient-specific prescription, single active ingredient, adverse-event reporting, "compounded drug, not for resale" labelling. It is non-binding, can be withdrawn, and is "not FDA approval". It does not cover products sold as research chemicals or supplements. Comments close about 13 Nov 2026. [verified, Federal Register public inspection; conditions via Baker Hostetler and RAPS]
  • Plain verdict: registered and used for childhood attention problems in Russia, but there is no Western-standard ADHD trial. The amphetamine-boosting finding matters for anyone stacking it with Adderall or Vyvanse (see Warnings).

NA-Semax-amidate (N-acetyl Semax amidate). Grade: D

  • No published human or ADHD data. [reported, community wiki states this; my PubMed searches found nothing]
  • It is a modified research-market variant, not the registered drug. Claims of higher potency are unverified. [inferred]

Selank (TP-7, a tuftsin analogue). Grade: B for anxiety, D for ADHD

  • Anxiety trials:
  • GAD and neurasthenia: Selank (n=30) vs medazepam (n=32) gave similar anxiety relief. Selank also showed "antiasthenic and psychostimulant" effects. [verified, Zozulia 2008, RCT, PMID 18454096]
  • 60 patients, Selank vs phenazepam: Selank was anxiolytic with a mild nootropic effect, and benefit lasted a week after stopping. [verified, Medvedev 2014, PMID 25176261]
  • Status: registered in Russia as 0.15% nasal drops. Current registration dated 15 Jul 2025 (earlier number from 2009). Indications: anxiety, panic attacks, neurasthenia, asthenia, mood instability, sleep problems, adjustment disorders. Adults only (18+). Listed side effects: allergy, unpleasant taste. No interaction section on the reference page. [verified in v2 via Vidal Russia] Not part of the July 2026 FDA review or the Oct 2026 HHS policy. [verified absence] US reviewers describe Selank as a "poorly studied Russian drug" with GABAergic mechanisms that is sold to US consumers as a supplement. [verified, Doyno 2021, J Clin Pharmacol, PMID 34396551]
  • ADHD: no ADHD-specific human data. [verified, by absence in PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov]
  • Relevance: anxiety co-occurs with ADHD, and Semax's label excludes anxiety disorders. That is why people pair the two. [inferred]

Cerebrolysin (pig brain peptide mix, injected). Grade: B− (Russian open studies)

  • Chutko 2016, 60 children aged 9–12 with ADHD: "improvement" in 70% with idiopathic ADHD and 86.7% with "residual-organic" ADHD. Abstract only; no control group described. [verified, PMID 27845323]
  • Gromova 1998, 36 children aged 3–8 with "minimal brain dysfunction": 2–5 ml intramuscularly for 1 month. Reported clinical and antioxidant benefit; uncontrolled. [verified, PMID 9505400]
  • In Group B (limited evidence) of the 2026 Russian Expert Council. [verified, PMID 42910684]
  • Approved in Russia, Austria and other countries for stroke and dementia; not FDA-approved. [reported]
  • Plain verdict: no placebo-controlled ADHD trial. Response rates of 70–87% without a control arm are typical of placebo plus natural fluctuation (see MTA: 63.8% fluctuate).

Dihexa (N-hexanoic-Tyr-Ile-(6)-aminohexanoic amide). Grade: D

  • Retracted: the key mechanism paper (Benoist 2014, JPET, PMID 25187433) is retracted. PubMed flags it as a "Retracted Publication" (re-checked 3 Nov 2026; the paper was from Washington State University with authors linked to a company developing the compound). [verified, doi:10.1124/jpet.114.218735] The retraction notice itself (date and reason) was still not retrieved. A community source reports a Washington State University finding of falsified or fabricated figures. [reported]
  • An animal review found angiotensin IV analogues, including dihexa, improved memory in 8 of 9 cognitive-deficit models. [verified, Ho & Nation 2018, PMID 29733881]
  • A 2025 mouse study of repeated mild brain injury found an HGF/MET modulator rescued working memory. [verified, PMID 42825082]
  • No human trials and no ADHD data. [reported + verified absence]
  • Theoretical concern: it acts on HGF/c-Met, a pathway linked to cancer growth. [speculated; not tested]

P21 (P021, a CNTF-derived peptide mimetic). Grade: C (animals only)

  • In Down-syndrome mice it rescued developmental delay and memory and raised BDNF. [verified, Kazim 2017, PMID 28368015]
  • In a CDKL5-deficiency mouse model, chronic treatment failed to raise BDNF and gave limited benefit. [verified, Mottolese 2024, PMID 39592934]
  • No human or ADHD data.

Noopept (omberacetam). Grade: B− for mild cognitive impairment, D for ADHD

  • The only human trial: 31 patients, open-label, 56 days, 10 mg twice daily vs piracetam. MMSE went from 26 to 29.
  • Side effects: blood pressure rose in 7/31, sleep disturbance in 5/31, irritability in 3/31. [reported, Neznamov 2009, via ADDF Cognitive Vitality report]
  • Oral bioavailability is about 10%. [verified, PMID 34126240]
  • Rat hippocampus data show an α7 nicotinic mechanism. [verified, PMID 36195298]
  • Prescription drug in Russia; not FDA-approved. No ADHD data.

BPC-157 (the "dopamine" claims). Grade: C

  • All from one Zagreb group, in rats and mice.
  • Blocked amphetamine-induced stereotypy and the supersensitivity that haloperidol produces. [verified, Jelovac 1998, Biol Psychiatry, PMID 9547930]
  • Reduced neuroleptic catalepsy. [verified, PMID 10499368]
  • Reversed "vascular failure" in rats given amphetamine or neuroleptics. [verified, Strbe 2023, PMID 37375736]
  • Plain meaning: BPC-157 seems to dampen or normalise extreme dopamine states in rodents. It has never been tested for attention, in ADHD, or in humans for any brain outcome. [verified absence]
  • US: FDA advisers voted 8 to 6 (1 abstention) on 23 Jul 2026 to recommend BPC-157 (with KPV and TB-500). Since late Oct 2026 it is covered by the HHS draft interim enforcement-discretion policy (injected, oral or topical, under conditions). Not FDA-approved, not on the 503A list. [verified + reported, see Semax US status for sources]

DSIP (delta sleep-inducing peptide, emideltide). Grade: C/D

  • Mouse insomnia-model data. [verified, PMID 39444618] No ADHD data.
  • FDA advisers rejected emideltide (DSIP) 7 to 6 (1 abstention) on 24 Jul 2026, the only one of 7 peptides rejected. Not in the Oct 2026 HHS policy. [reported, AJMC]
  • Relevance: ADHD sleep problems. The evidence does not support DSIP for them.

Oxytocin (intranasal). Grade: B− (tiny pilots)

  • Children with ADHD, n=8, double-blind crossover, single dose: faster and more consistent responses on theory-of-mind and emotion-recognition tasks. Also given alongside stimulants, with no adverse effects. [verified, Saker 2025, J Psychiatr Res, PMID 41138507, DOI 10.1016/j.jpsychires.2025.10.031]
  • MGH adult ADHD trial (NCT03136263), v2 check: randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover; men 18 to 55 only; single 24 IU intranasal dose; primary outcome stop-signal task (impulse braking), plus four other cognitive-control tasks and an fMRI substudy. n=24. Completed 5 Nov 2020. Registry shows no results (re-checked 3 Nov 2026). PubMed search for oxytocin + ADHD + adults + RCT (7 hits) and web search found no matching paper. [verified, ClinicalTrials.gov]
  • Plain meaning: a completed, small, well-designed trial unpublished for nearly six years. Unpublished trials lean toward null results, but this is not proof. [inferred]
  • Plain meaning: may target the social side of ADHD, not attention itself. n=8 is a pilot, not evidence of effect.

GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide) and ADHD. Grade: D for ADHD symptoms

  • No trials: no RCT of a GLP-1 drug for ADHD symptoms was found on ClinicalTrials.gov (0 results) or PubMed. [verified]
  • Genetics: a Mendelian randomisation study (using gene variants as a natural experiment) found no evidence that GLP-1 receptor activation changes ADHD risk. It did find lower schizophrenia risk (OR 0.84). [verified, PMID 40141382]
  • Chart review: 226 GLP-1 users (mean 53 years). A minority developed new psychiatric diagnoses; ADHD appeared at a median of about 7.8 months. Uncontrolled, and it cannot show cause. [verified, PMID 41616750]
  • Adolescents on liraglutide (Israel, n=966): only 18% stayed on it 7 or more months. Those who did lost about 10% of BMI. Teens with ADHD who were on ADHD medication stuck with it better. [verified, Child Obes 2025, PMID 41047941]
  • Children's weight programmes: untreated ADHD is linked to slower BMI response. [verified, Nutrients 2026 review, PMID 42943020]
  • Binge eating: GLP-1s and naltrexone-bupropion "show promise" for binge eating with high BMI. [verified, Hay 2024 review, PMID 39568427] Tirzepatide and retatrutide are unproven for binge eating disorder. [verified, Himmerich 2024, PMID 39096466]
  • Lisdexamfetamine, the established option, is the only drug approved for binge eating disorder, in some countries (see section 4).
  • Impulsivity: GLP-1s may reduce "food noise" and impulsive eating through reward pathways. [speculated, mechanism-based; no ADHD outcome data]

4. Usual questions (with numbers)

  • Adult vs child? The same condition with a different look. Hyperactivity turns into inner restlessness. In adults, stimulant effects are somewhat smaller (amphetamine SMD −0.79 vs −1.02 in children) and dropout from side effects is higher (amphetamine OR 3.26 vs 2.30). [verified, Cortese 2018]
  • Does it go away? About 9% reach sustained remission. Most fluctuate. [verified, Sibley 2022]
  • Stimulants and the heart? Short-term: a few mmHg and about 5 bpm. Long-term: about 4% higher heart and blood-vessel disease risk per year of use, plateauing around OR 1.2–1.3, driven mostly by hypertension. [verified + reported, section 2d]
  • Tolerance? Within-day tolerance is established. Long-term tolerance is suggested by MTA dose creep (+20% in year 1) and adult dropout. [reported]
  • Caffeine? The only systematic review covers animal models. Caffeine helped attention and memory there, with unclear effects on hyperactivity and impulsivity. No good human ADHD trial data. [reported, Vázquez 2022, Nutrients 14:739]
  • Omega-3?
  • 22 RCTs (1,789 people): overall SMD −0.16, not significant. Courses of 4 months or longer: −0.35, significant. [verified, Liu 2023, J Clin Psychiatry, PMID 37656283, DOI 10.4088/JCP.22r14772]
  • 16 RCTs, 2026: g −0.24, borderline. [verified, Huang 2026, Eur Child Adolesc Psychiatry, PMID 42776218]
  • Children with low baseline omega-3 levels: SMD 0.52. Normal levels: 0.03. Only 7 RCTs, so preliminary. [verified, Fu 2026, PMID 42433395, DOI 10.3389/fpubh.2026.1844881]
  • Plain meaning: about a fifth to a quarter of a stimulant's effect. It is worth testing your level first.
  • Exercise? Across 118 RCTs of non-drug approaches in children with neurodevelopmental disorders (83 in ADHD), "cognitively engaging exercise" improved inhibitory control (SMD 0.65). Certainty was low, with possible publication bias. [verified, EClinicalMedicine 2026, PMID 42857135, DOI 10.1016/j.eclinm.2026.104228]
  • ADHD and binge eating? Binge eating disorder carries a 5.8-fold higher ADHD rate. [reported] Lisdexamfetamine cut binge days by 1.29 per week vs placebo (5 RCTs, n=988); diarrhoea risk ratio 4.06. [verified, Ellwanger 2025, PMID 41423727, DOI 10.1002/erv.70070]
  • ADHD and weight-loss drugs? No ADHD efficacy data for GLP-1s. Adherence may be better when ADHD is treated. [verified, section 3]
  • Best non-stimulant? Adults: atomoxetine (SMD −0.45) and bupropion (−0.46) are similar on paper. Viloxazine and centanafadine are newer options with less comparative data. Guanfacine helped children but not adult core symptoms in the Ostinelli review. [verified + reported]
  • Does therapy work for adult ADHD? CBT, mindfulness and psychoeducation beat placebo when a clinician rates symptoms (SMD about −0.8) but not when the person rates themselves. Only stimulants and atomoxetine worked on both. [verified, Ostinelli 2025]
  • Is Semax approved for ADHD anywhere? Its Russian label lists ADHD in children 5 to 14, but no controlled ADHD trial backs it, and the 2026 Russian expert council put it in the "limited evidence" group, below Prospekta and not a replacement for atomoxetine. It is not approved in the US or EU. [verified]
  • Can I legally get Semax or BPC-157 in the US now? Since late Oct 2026 HHS says it will not act against licensed pharmacies compounding them with a prescription under set conditions. That is a draft, non-binding policy, not FDA approval, and it excludes research-chemical sellers. [verified]
  • Does melatonin help ADHD sleep? In 21 RCTs of children with neurodevelopmental conditions (n=984), melatonin shortened time to fall asleep and lengthened total sleep, with tolerability similar to placebo. It did not reduce night waking. [verified, Salanitro 2022, PMID 35691474, doi:10.1016/j.neubiorev.2022.104723]
  • What happened to the oxytocin ADHD trial? The MGH adult trial finished in 2020 and has never posted or published results. The only published data are an 8-child pilot. [verified]

6. Pairings and related conditions

  • Semax with Selank: the most common community pairing. Semax for drive, Selank to offset anxiety. Note that the Semax label contraindicates anxiety disorders. [reported + verified label]
  • Semax with a stimulant: in animals Semax amplifies amphetamine's dopamine release (locomotion 261% vs 182%). That is a real interaction signal, not a reason to combine. [verified animal; human effect speculated]
  • Oxytocin with methylphenidate: combined in the n=8 pilot with no adverse events. [verified]
  • Omega-3 with medication: the omega-3 trials were mostly add-on designs; the effect is small. [reported]
  • Related conditions to interlink:
  • Anxiety (Selank)
  • Sleep (DSIP rejected; melatonin is standard)
  • Binge eating (lisdexamfetamine, GLP-1s)
  • Addiction (23% ADHD rate)
  • Autism (28% overlap)

7. Options, in order of evidence strength

  1. Stimulants: amphetamines or methylphenidate (SMD 0.5–1.0). Grade A.
  2. Non-stimulants: atomoxetine, viloxazine, bupropion, guanfacine or clonidine (in children), centanafadine (new 2026). Grade A, smaller effects.
  3. Behavioural and executive-function training, cognitively engaging exercise. Grade B, low certainty.
  4. Omega-3, mainly if your baseline level is low. Grade B, small effect.
  5. Semax (Russia-registered for childhood attention problems, no controlled ADHD trial), Cerebrolysin. Grade B− for label and open studies only.
  6. Selank (for anxiety alongside ADHD), oxytocin (social cognition). Grade B−/C.
  7. Noopept, P21, BPC-157, DSIP, NA-Semax, Dihexa. Grade C/D. Dihexa's core paper is retracted.

8. Warnings (bottom of page)

  • MAOIs: stimulants are contraindicated with MAOIs and within 14 days of stopping one, because of hypertensive crisis risk. This includes linezolid and selegiline. [reported, US labelling summaries] Bupropion is also contraindicated with MAOIs. [reported] No MAO data exist for Semax.
  • Semax + amphetamine: amplified dopamine release in rodents (section 3). In humans this could mean more stimulation, higher blood pressure, anxiety or insomnia. [speculated] The Semax label lists anxiety disorders, seizure history and acute psychosis as contraindications. [verified]
  • Stimulant psychosis risk rises with amphetamine dose: OR 5.28 above 30 mg. Adding dopamine-active peptides on top is untested. [reported + inferred]
  • Selank's GABAergic action: additive sedation with benzodiazepines, alcohol or gabapentinoids is plausible but untested. [speculated] No interaction study found.
  • Product quality: online peptides are unregulated. In a JMIR 2024 test-purchase study of semaglutide sold without prescription:
  • All 3 vials received were likely substandard or falsified, and the 3 pens were never delivered (scams).
  • Measured purity was 7.7–14.4% against 99% claimed, content was 29–39% over label, and endotoxin was found in every sample. [verified, PMID 39509151, DOI 10.2196/65440] No equivalent test exists for Semax or Selank, so expect similar risk. [inferred]
  • Nasal spray stability: the registered Semax is a sealed 3 mL dropper bottle. Home-reconstituted peptide nasal sprays lack stability data. Short peptides break down in water and are open to contamination once opened. [inferred; no peer-reviewed stability study found for Semax or Selank sprays] The Semax label advises against using nasal decongestant (vasoconstrictor) sprays alongside it. [verified]
  • Regulatory (updated v2): none of these peptides is FDA-approved. In July 2026 FDA advisers backed Semax and BPC-157 for compounding (non-binding) and rejected DSIP. Since late Oct 2026 an HHS draft interim policy tolerates pharmacy compounding of Semax (nasal) and BPC-157 under strict conditions; it excludes research-chemical sales. Selank, Dihexa, P21, NA-Semax and DSIP are outside it. [verified, Federal Register notice 2026-22192]
  • Children: no peptide here has Western paediatric safety data. The Russian label allows Semax 0.1% from age 5. [verified]
  • Heart: have blood pressure and pulse checked before and during stimulant use. Risk climbs over the first 3 years. [verified]

9. Community findings outside Reddit (folded in from ADHD Addiction Peptides v1)

All [reported]; tallies were counted from the pages as fetched on 3 Nov 2026. Sources: Mumsnet, LongeCity, Skool, Substack, LessWrong, Understood.org, Drugs.com. Reddit excluded. Vendor and AI-written pages discarded.

GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide) and ADHD - 5 Mumsnet threads plus 1 Substack, 1 LessWrong, 1 Understood feature: about 30 posters with ADHD. - Rough tally: about 11 improved, 6 mixed, 5 no change, 7 worse. - Improved (their words, paraphrased): "head quieter", calmer, more organised, less impulsive spending and scrolling, food noise gone. Often within the first weeks. - Unchanged: task-starting, procrastination and executive function were the most common "no change" items. - Worse: flatness, low motivation, exhaustion, mostly on tirzepatide at dose steps or around month 2. In one thread 5 of 6 "worse" posts were Mounjaro, while 2 Wegovy users improved. In a separate low-mood thread 8 of 13 Mounjaro posters described flatness, 5 of whom improved after a dose cut or stopping. Matches a 3-case tirzepatide report (Nadolsky 2026, PMID 42518361). [verified case report] - Expert view: a Johns Hopkins psychiatrist quoted on Understood.org (19 May 2026) said GLP-1s may help impulsivity but not attention or executive function. [reported] - With stimulants: one called the combination "remarkable", one had binge eating stop but ADHD unchanged, one stopped Concerta and executive function dropped. No interaction study exists. [reported; verified absence] - Studies: no registered GLP-1 ADHD trial (ClinicalTrials.gov re-checked 3 Nov 2026: 0). Mendelian randomisation shows no genetic link. [verified]

Semax (and NA-Semax amidate) - LongeCity (6 threads), Skool, Substack: about 22 first-hand reports, roughly 16 positive, 5 mixed, 1 no effect, 1 negative. - Only 3 to 4 came from people with diagnosed ADHD or ADD. Most were healthy self-experimenters. - Positives: focus, clarity, verbal fluency, drive, "alert without anxiety". Nasal: minutes, lasting 4 to 9 hours. Injected: subtler, over about a week. - Negatives: insomnia (3), irritability (2), anxiety (2 to 3, one on an SSRI), depersonalisation (1), a 1-hour panic with racing heart after a high first dose (1). - Stimulant interaction: a Vyvanse user said Semax "will potentiate any stimulant". Matches the mouse data (amphetamine activity 261% vs 182%). [reported + verified animal] - MAOI: one user combined it with a low dose of tranylcypromine and reported flatness and detachment. No pharmacology data. [reported] - NA-Semax amidate: 2 to 3 mild-positive reports; no published data at all.

Selank, Cortexin, Cerebrolysin, oxytocin, Dihexa, P21, BPC-157, DSIP and others - Selank: about 5 reports, none from diagnosed ADHD (anxiety down; 2 no effect). [reported] - Cortexin: no community reports. Study: Chutko 2026, 40 children with sluggish cognitive tempo, open-label, improvement in 82%, no placebo arm. [verified, PMID 42899917] - Cerebrolysin: 1 recalled parent account (autism). Open autism study n=36, CARS −11%, 4 of 40 dropped for agitation. [verified, Ashrafi 2019] - Everything else: zero non-vendor first-hand ADHD reports found. [verified absence]

What the community adds to the page [inferred] - Both Semax and GLP-1 "it helped" stories are real and consistent, but almost none are from controlled settings, and ADHD symptoms fluctuate naturally. - The flatness signal on tirzepatide is the most repeated harm and matters most for ADHD, where motivation is already fragile. - No-change reports are under-posted. Expect the true positive rate to be lower than forum tallies.
