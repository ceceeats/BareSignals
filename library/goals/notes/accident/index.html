0. Headline findings (page top)

  1. Most crash injuries heal; the long tail is the problem. About half of whiplash cases still report neck pain at 1 year (Carroll 2008); recovery mostly happens in the first 3 months then flattens (Kamper 2008). [verified]
  2. "Mild" brain injury is not mild for many. 53% of mild TBI patients at US trauma centres still had some functional limitation at 12 months vs 38% of people with only orthopaedic injuries (Nelson 2019, n = 1,453). [verified]
  3. Move early beats rest and a collar for whiplash (Rosenfeld 2000/2003); a 6-session physio package only sped early recovery and was gone by 8 months (Lamb 2013, n = 3,851). [verified]
  4. The only approved "bone peptides" are teriparatide and abaloparatide, and they are not proven fracture healers. Teriparatide 20 µg shortened distal radius healing from 9.1 to 7.4 weeks in a post hoc comparison; the planned 40 µg comparison failed (Aspenberg 2010). Abaloparatide did not beat placebo for pelvic fractures; more placebo patients healed (64% vs 39%, n.s.) (Nieves 2026). [verified]
  5. Ultrasound bone stimulators (LIPUS) do nothing after nailed tibial fractures (TRUST, n = 501, BMJ 2016). [verified]
  6. Short NSAID courses look fine for bone; long or high-dose ones do not. Nonunion OR 1.56 overall, not raised with 14 days or less (2026 umbrella review). Chronic opioids carried a bigger signal (OR 5.10). [verified, observational]
  7. The best peptide trial data after head injury belong to Cerebrolysin, a prescription pig-brain peptide mix, not to the injectable "research" peptides. CAPTAIN trials (moderate to severe TBI): small to medium benefit, MW 0.60 at day 90 (n = 185 pooled). One mild TBI pilot (n = 32) showed better cognition at 12 weeks. [verified]
  8. BPC-157 has animal data for every crash injury on this page (bone, nerve, brain, spinal cord, burn), and all of it comes from one Zagreb group. No human trial in fracture, TBI, nerve or wounds exists. [verified authorship and registry absence]
  9. Thymosin beta-4 (the full protein, not TB-500) has real human wound trials, but in chronic leg and pressure ulcers, with modest or unclear results; TBI data are rats from one Detroit group. [verified]
  10. Community (outside Reddit, v1 + v2): 32 first-hand reports; 17 worked, 7 unclear, 6 no change, 2 worse (side effects); 0 clinician-attributed effects. Most "worked" reports are soft tissue or confounded by GH, MK-677 or physio. Best fracture story: a CT-confirmed rib fracture with small collapsed lung after a water-ski crash felt "dramatically better within a few days" of starting BPC-157 in week 3 (poster unsure it was the peptide). The two most severe crash fractures in v1 (ribs plus lung plus pelvis; broken back with fusion) were both "no change". Still zero first-hand peptide reports for whiplash, nerve injury or road rash scars, and zero in 6 motorcycle brachial plexus stories. [verified per page; tally inferred] (v1 said 19; corrected, see What changed)

1. What each injury is (how common, symptoms, natural history)

1.1 Bali and Indonesia context

  • Bali recorded 8,498 traffic accidents and 261 deaths in 2025; 142 accidents involving foreigners (21 deaths) in 2024; no scooter-specific count exists; minor crashes are under-reported because tourists settle with clinics or rental shops (Bali Holiday Secrets, 1 Oct 2026, citing Bali Police and BPS "Bali Province in Figures 2025"). [reported, secondary page; 2025 figures unsourced in text]
  • Indonesia national survey 2018 (59,423 people with road injury): helmet use was a dominant protective factor (OR 0.66 urban, 0.68 rural) (Zainafree 2022, DOI). [verified]
  • North Maluku neurosurgery register: 91.8% of road-crash brain injuries were motorcycle crashes; 91.2% helmetless; 75% were mild TBI; 9.3% died in hospital (Nugroho 2025, DOI). [verified] Not Bali, but the closest Indonesian primary data found. [inferred]

1.2 Whiplash and neck pain (whiplash-associated disorders, WAD)

  • What: acceleration/deceleration strain of neck soft tissue; graded I (pain only) to III (neurological signs); grade IV = fracture (classic Quebec grading). [reported]
  • Symptoms: neck pain and stiffness, headache, shoulder pain, dizziness, sometimes arm tingling. [reported]
  • Natural history: recovery is fast in the first 3 months, then levels off (38 cohorts, Kamper 2008, DOI). About 50% still report neck pain at 1 year. Slower recovery predicted by higher initial pain, more symptoms, more disability, passive coping, low mood and fear of movement; crash details (direction, headrest) barely predicted anything (Carroll 2008 Neck Pain Task Force, DOI). [verified]
  • Page line: "Your mood and fear of moving predict your neck a year from now better than how hard the scooter hit." [inferred from Carroll 2008]

1.3 Back injury

  • Ranges from muscle strain to vertebral fracture. Spinal fractures in this pass appear only in community reports (T4 to T8 fusion after a crash). [verified per thread]
  • Back strain natural history, compression fracture rates in young riders: not gathered this pass. [gap]
  • Osteoporotic vertebral fractures (older riders): teriparatide conservative care beat vertebroplasty on pain after 6 months and cut new fractures (OR 0.15), low-quality evidence (2025 meta, 326 patients, DOI). [verified]

1.4 Fractures and bone healing

  • How bone heals: bone truly regenerates (repeats skeletal development); about 10% heal abnormally (Einhorn 2015, see Tendon Base 1.2). [verified]
  • Nonunion rate: 2.5% of 56,492 fractures in older US adults; younger patients had more nonunion; risk factors: smoking (past or current), alcoholism, obesity, diabetes, rheumatoid arthritis, open fracture (Zura 2016, DOI). [verified]
  • Union times: placebo distal radius (wrist) fractures bridged in a median 9.1 weeks (Aspenberg 2010, below). [verified] Typical 6 to 12 weeks for most bones; tibia often months. [reported, no primary pulled this pass, carried from Tendon Base gap]
  • Ribs, collarbone and pelvis are common in scooter falls [reported, community and news]; collarbone up to 10% of adult fractures [reported, promotional page, unverified]. v2 correction: collarbone is about 4% of all fractures, 76% in the middle third; sling and figure-of-eight bandage gave similar results (Cochrane, Lenza 2016, DOI). [verified]
  • Union times by bone (v2, closes v1 gap 3) [verified unless marked]: | Bone | Typical time to union | Source | |---|---|---| | Wrist (distal radius), adults | 9.1 weeks (placebo arm, older women); 6.9 weeks after plate fixation; 8.3 weeks surgery vs 10.2 weeks cast in older adults (n = 63) | Aspenberg 2010; Kang 2026 DOI; Wang 2026 DOI | | Scaphoid (wrist bone, falls on hand) | Cast heals 94%; 6% nonunion; surgery cut nonunion risk (RR 0.35) but no better long-term function | 2026 meta, 4 RCTs, 559 patients DOI | | Collarbone, midshaft displaced | Surgery healed 5.1 weeks faster and cut nonunion (RR 0.10) but had more complications (31% vs 21%) | Guerra 2019 AJSM meta, 14 RCTs, 1,546 patients DOI | | Tibia (shin), nailed | About 26 weeks mean (25 studies, 790 patients, femur/tibia/humerus); adolescents 14 weeks (69% were road crashes); early weight bearing shaved 2.4 weeks | Papadakis 2025 DOI; Shah 2026 DOI; Bhanushali 2022 DOI | | Open tibia (bone through skin, Gustilo IIIB) | 31 weeks mean in children; 22% delayed, 13% nonunion | Glass 2009 DOI | | Ribs (multiple, admitted to hospital) | No primary union-time paper found; quality of life stayed below population norms for 2 years; only 71% returned to any work | Marasco 2014, 397 patients DOI | Page line: "A wrist takes about 2 months, a collarbone 2 to 4, a shin bone often half a year. Ribs hurt long after they knit." [inferred from table; rib knitting time reported, not verified]
  • Tibial nonunion can be predicted early: a score using smoking, infection, flap need and 6 and 12 week X-ray scores sorted patients from 0% to 48% nonunion risk (O'Toole 2020, DOI). [verified]

1.5 Road rash (friction burns) and scarring

  • What: abrasion plus heat injury from sliding on asphalt; depth decides everything (superficial heals from the base; deep needs grafting). [reported]
  • How common/serious: 810 motorcycle trauma admissions with road rash at one US level 1 centre (2018 to 2022): 92% male, extremities most hit. Burn surgeons were needed in 7%; of those 23% had surgical excision and 16% skin grafts (about 1% of all road rash). "Local wound care is sufficient for a vast majority" (2025, DOI). [verified]
  • Scar maturation: scars keep changing for up to about 18 months, so short trials under-read treatments (Cochrane pressure-garment review 2024, DOI). [verified statement in abstract]
  • Bali-specific: gravel and dirt embed and must be cleaned out; avoid swimming and sea water; protect new skin from sun (dark marks can last years) (bali.live community post, Feb 2024). [reported, non-clinician author] It also suggests letting wounds dry to a scab, which conflicts with moist-healing practice (see 2.4). [inferred]

1.6 Concussion and mild traumatic brain injury (mTBI)

  • What: brain dysfunction from impact or whiplash forces; Glasgow Coma Scale 13 to 15 = "mild". [verified definition in TRACK-TBI]
  • Symptoms: headache, dizziness, fogginess, poor sleep, irritability, light and noise sensitivity, neck pain. [reported]
  • Natural history (adults, trauma centres): at 2 weeks 87% had functional limits; at 12 months 53% still did (vs 38% orthopaedic controls); 61% if the CT showed bleeding vs 49% with a clean CT (Nelson 2019, DOI). [verified]
  • Early warning score: a Rivermead symptom score of 14+ at 2 weeks raised odds of persistent symptoms at 6 months 7-fold (n = 2,000, 2025, DOI). [verified]
  • People who skip follow-up do badly: of those who said "I did not think I need follow-up" at 2 weeks, 44% had incomplete recovery and 40% persistent symptoms at 12 months (2025, DOI). [verified]
  • Adolescent sport concussion with heavy cognitive symptoms: recovery 34 vs 15 days; 6-fold odds of symptoms beyond a month (2024, DOI). [verified] Sports data are faster and cleaner than crash data. [inferred]

1.7 Nerve injury

  • Example with numbers: radial nerve palsy (wrist drop) occurs in 12.3% of humeral shaft fractures; 77.2% recover without surgery; early exploration within 3 weeks: 89.8%; late exploration after 8 weeks: 68.1% (Ilyas 2020, 7,262 fractures, DOI). Another review: 70.7% recover spontaneously within 6 months (Hegeman 2020, DOI). [verified]
  • Regrowth speed about 1 mm per day, so distance to the muscle sets the timeline. [reported, standard teaching, no primary pulled]
  • Brachial plexus injury (classic motorcycle injury): not gathered this pass. [gap]

1.8 Post-surgery recovery

  • Covers plates, nails, fusions, skin grafts, tendon repairs. Relevant cross-links: Tendon Base 1.4 (surgery and injections), Muscle Injury 4.2 (BPC-157 after cuff repair trial NCT07803250). [verified in those files]

[inferred]

LIPUS (low-intensity pulsed ultrasound): TRUST, 501 patients with nailed tibial fractures, sham-controlled: no faster X-ray healing (HR 1.07) and no function gain (Busse 2016, DOI). A BMJ Rapid Recommendation guideline followed (Poolman 2017, DOI; abstract unavailable, "strong recommendation against" wording [reported]). [verified for TRUST] - v2: the systematic review behind that guideline (26 RCTs): low-bias trials showed no gain in return to work, weight bearing, pain or X-ray healing; only high-bias trials looked positive (Schandelmaier 2017 BMJ, DOI). Clavicle LIPUS trial (n = 120) also null (Lenza 2016). [verified] A useful mirror for peptide claims: benefit shows up mainly where bias is high. [inferred] - v2: electromagnetic stimulation for nonunion: 4 RCTs, 125 people, union RR 1.96 (CI 0.86 to 4.48), not significant, no pain benefit (Cochrane, Griffin 2011, DOI). [verified]

Painkillers and bone (fills Tendon Base gap "NSAIDs and bone healing"): - 2026 JAAOS umbrella review + 38-study meta: NSAIDs nonunion OR 1.56 (1.18 to 2.11); adults OR 1.67, children none; worse in long bones and trauma, higher doses; not raised with 14 days or less (DOI). [verified] - 2024 meta, 20 studies, 523,240 patients: no significant difference (adjusted OR 1.11, 0.99 to 1.23) (DOI). [verified] The two reviews disagree on overall harm, agree that short courses are low risk. [inferred] - Opioids: chronic use OR 5.10 for nonunion (7 cohorts, >1 million fractures; 2026, DOI); acute and chronic opioid and NSAID prescriptions linked to nonunion in a 2018 claims cohort (DOI). Observational; sicker patients get more painkillers. [verified; confounding inferred]

2.3 Road rash wound care

  • Most road rash heals with local care; grafting about 1% (see 1.5). [verified]
  • Moist dressings over dry scabbing: standard wound-care principle (faster re-epithelialisation, less pain). [reported; primary RCT not retrieved this pass, gap]
  • v2 fill (moist vs dry, closes v1 gap 2): no RCT on road rash or simple abrasions was found (2 searches). The nearest models, graft donor sites and superficial burns, all point the same way: [verified]
  • Review of 35 publications on split-skin graft donor sites (shallow skin wounds much like road rash): moist products had "a clear advantage" over non-moist ones for both pain and healing rate (Brown & Holloway 2018, DOI).
  • Superficial partial-thickness burns, RCT, 38 analysed: plain petroleum jelly, no top dressing, closed in 6.2 days vs 7.8 days for silver cream plus gauze (p = 0.050); no infections in either arm; dressing changes easier and less sticky (Genuino 2014, DOI). Done in Manila, a tropical setting. [verified; relevance to Bali inferred]
  • Donor sites, double-blind split-site RCT, 50 patients: film dressing 86% healed by day 10 vs 55% with a fibre dressing (Dornseifer 2011, DOI). Different moist products differ too. [verified]
  • Pressure ulcers, randomised, 95 home-care patients: moist dressings healed in 86 vs 121 days with gauze, with 4× fewer dressing changes and lower cost (Souliotis 2016, DOI). [verified] Page line: "Keep road rash clean, covered and moist (petroleum jelly or a film dressing), not dried into a scab. In a burn trial plain jelly healed a day and a half faster than silver cream and gauze." [inferred from above]
  • Clean out grit early (tattooing risk), tetanus booster if contaminated, watch for infection signs: rising pain, swelling, redness, heat, fever, enlarged nodes (bali.live, above). [reported]

2.4 Scars: silicone and pressure

  • Fluid silicone gels: prophylactic and curative effect on hypertrophic scars in 18 RCTs, but many "high evidence" trials at high bias risk; unclear if gels equal sheets (Burns 2022, DOI); 2026 update of 35 papers, 20 meta-analysed (DOI, effect sizes not in abstract). [verified]
  • Silicone gel with onion and aloe = silicone sheet for post-surgical scars, 40 patients, 3 months (Thai RCT 2021, DOI). [verified]
  • Pressure garments after burns: evidence "very uncertain" vs no treatment or silicone (Cochrane 2024, 15 RCTs). [verified] Page line: "Silicone is the default first step for raised scars. Start once the skin has closed, keep it on for months, and judge it at a year, not a week." [inferred; start-timing advice reported]

2.5 Concussion: graded return

  • Amsterdam 2022 consensus (6th International Conference): rather than strict rest, early return to light physical activity and less screen time help recovery; cervicovestibular rehab for dizziness, neck pain or headaches beyond 10 days; active rehab and collaborative care if symptoms beyond 30 days (Patricios 2023, DOI; paediatric summary DOI). [verified]
  • Aerobic exercise: 9 RCTs, 346 people; moderate to high intensity, ideally early, improved symptoms; suggested 20-minute supervised sessions 3 to 5 times weekly (2025, DOI). [verified]
  • Non-athlete adults (Toronto, n = 75): supervised early exercise testing shortened recovery vs usual care (p = 0.046) (2025, DOI). [verified]
  • Women may do better on lower exercise volume (pilot, n = 32, p = 0.058) (2025, DOI). [verified]

2.6 Nerve injury timelines

See 1.7: most closed-fracture nerve palsies recover on their own within 6 months; early surgical exploration (within 3 weeks) had the highest recovery rate in pooled data. [verified]

3.2 Teriparatide and abaloparatide

See 2.2 for all numbers. Key framing: these are the only peptides on this page with regulator approval and human fracture trials, and even they have not proven they speed healing in healthy adults. Doses in the trials: teriparatide 20 or 40 µg daily SC for 8 weeks (Aspenberg 2010). [verified] Abaloparatide dose not in abstract. [gap]

3.3 Cerebrolysin

  • What it is: a mix of low-molecular-weight peptides and amino acids from purified pig brain proteins; IV or IM; approved for TBI in some countries (Jarosz 2023, DOI). [verified] Not FDA approved; not available in the US. [reported, NeuroTalk moderators and reviews]
  • CAPTAIN I (Asian-Pacific, n = 46, GCS moderate to severe): primary multi-scale endpoint just missed in ITT (MW 0.63, p < 0.1), significant per protocol (MW 0.69); 3 cognitive tests significant; safety = placebo (Poon 2019, DOI). [verified]
  • CAPTAIN II (single centre, Romania, n = 139, GCS 7 to 12): small to medium benefit at day 90 (MW 0.59, p = 0.012) (Muresanu 2020, DOI). [verified]
  • CAPTAIN meta (n = 185): MW 0.60 at day 30 and 90; SMD 0.31 to 0.34; OR 1.69 to 1.77 (Vester 2021, DOI). Dosing in trials: 50 mL daily for 10 days, then two 10-day cycles of 10 mL daily. [verified]
  • Mild TBI pilot (Taiwan, n = 32, alert patients with contusion bleeding): 30 mL IV daily for 5 days; cognitive score gain 21.0 vs 7.6 at 12 weeks (p = 0.046) (Chen 2013, DOI). [verified]
  • Null trial: Cerebrolysin with or without rTMS vs placebo, n = 93 TBI: no significant primary difference (Verisezan Rosu 2023, DOI). [verified]
  • Meta-analyses: GOS SMD 0.30 but mostly cohort studies, heavy heterogeneity (Ghaffarpasand 2019, DOI); 10 studies, 8,749 patients, better GCS and GOS, no mortality or stay difference (Jarosz 2023). [verified]
  • Conflict check: CAPTAIN authors overlap with one Romanian research institute and a biostatistics consultancy across all three papers; a 2018 rat mild-TBI study had manufacturer employees as co-authors (Zhang 2018, DOI). [verified authorship] Independent large RCT in mild TBI: none found. [verified absence, 2 searches]
  • Stroke aphasia RCT (n = 123): +14.8 points on aphasia battery vs placebo with speech therapy (ESCAS 2025, DOI). [verified] Stroke, not trauma. [inferred] Grade: B for moderate to severe TBI as add-on; C for concussion. Hospital IV product, not a home peptide. [inferred]

3.4 Thymosin beta-4 (full protein) and TB-500

Wounds (human) - Venous leg ulcers, phase 2, 73 patients, 8 European sites, topical: safety = placebo; 0.03% "may have the potential" to accelerate healing, about 25% fully healed by 3 months; no clear significance stated (Guarnera 2010, DOI). [verified] - Pressure ulcer phase 2 (NCT00382174, n = 72, completed 2008): results not read. Epidermolysis bullosa phase 2 (NCT00311766): terminated. [verified registry] - v2 detail: NCT00382174 was a double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation topical trial at 12 US sites (several VA hospitals), adults 18 to 85 with a full-thickness pressure ulcer 5 to 70 cm² present at least a month. Primary endpoint was safety; healing at day 84 was only secondary. Registry says results are posted; the results tab could not be opened from this session. [verified registry, read 3 Nov 2026] - The developer-linked summary of both phase 2 trials (stasis and pressure ulcers) says Tβ4 sped healing "by almost a month in those patients that did heal" (Treadwell 2012, DOI; co-authors include the developer's founders and staff [verified authorship]). Measuring speed only among people who healed is a subgroup chosen by outcome, which can manufacture a difference; the headline healing rates are not given in that abstract. [inferred] A later review by the same group repeats "accelerated the rate of repair" in pressure, stasis and EB wounds without numbers (Kleinman & Sosne 2016, DOI). [verified] - Dry eye phase 3 (ARISE): a 2023 paper still described Tβ4 as "in Phase 3 trials" (Sosne 2023, DOI); no ARISE-2/3 result paper on PubMed (1 search). [verified absence] - Developer reviews describe faster repair in pressure, stasis and EB wounds (Kleinman 2016, DOI; Yang 2019, company-affiliated, DOI). 3.5 GHK-Cu

  • 2026 evidence-mapping review: preclinical support consistent; clinical evidence "sparse"; a 13-person post-CO2-laser study was negative on objective endpoints; GHK-Cu should be regarded as "promising but unproven" (Mateescu 2026, DOI). [verified]
  • Microneedle review: no adequately powered human RCT published (Najafi 2026, DOI). [verified]
  • Registered trial NCT07437586 (CuHeal): phase 2, double blind, split-wound (two 5 mm punch biopsies on the upper arm, one GHK-Cu gel, one vehicle, daily for 14 days), 60 healthy adults, single hospital in Shenzhen; primary = days to full re-epithelialisation; scar (POSAS) at 12 weeks; primary completion 14 Feb 2027. Same commercial sponsor as the BPC-157 hamstring trial and the TB-500 heart trial. [verified registry, read 3 Nov 2026]
  • Excludes smokers, diabetics, keloid formers: so results will not directly cover infected road rash. [inferred]

3.6 LL-37 (antimicrobial peptide)

  • First-in-human RCT, 34 hard-to-heal venous leg ulcers, 4 weeks: healing rate about 6× (0.5 mg/mL) and 3× (1.6 mg/mL) placebo; ulcer area down 68% and 50%; highest dose 3.2 mg/mL no better than placebo (Grönberg 2014, DOI). [verified]
  • Phase IIb HEAL LL-37, 148 patients: no significant improvement overall; post hoc benefit in wounds ≥10 cm² (Mahlapuu 2021, DOI). [verified]
  • Inverted dose response and failed phase IIb = warning that "more is better" logic fails. Chronic ulcers in older adults, not acute road rash. [inferred]
  • The body makes LL-37 in injured skin; topical vitamin D analogue raised it further after wounding (Heilborn 2009, DOI). [verified]

3.8 KPV

Only animal and formulation data: anti-inflammatory and antibacterial gel healed rat chemotherapy mouth ulcers (2021, DOI). No human wound study found. [verified absence, 1 search] Not to be confused with tanning or pigment tripeptides that share the "alpha-MSH tripeptide" label. [inferred]

6. Options (page section, evidence order) [inferred from sections 2 and 3]

  1. Get checked the same day for any head knock with symptoms, neck pain with numbness or weakness, suspected fracture, or deep or dirty road rash (see Warnings).
  2. Whiplash: keep the neck moving from day one; skip the collar; expect most improvement within 3 months; if still bad at 6 weeks, a short physio course may speed things (MINT).
  3. Concussion: light activity within days, less screen time; aerobic exercise as tolerated; vestibular and neck rehab if dizziness or headache past 10 days; a follow-up visit even if you feel fine (44% of "no follow-up needed" people had incomplete recovery at a year).
  4. Fractures: stop smoking, control blood sugar, eat enough protein; paracetamol first, short NSAID courses acceptable, avoid long opioid use; skip LIPUS; teriparatide is a doctor conversation only for osteoporosis or a fracture that will not heal.
  5. Road rash: wash grit out fully, moist dressings, tetanus check, no sea or pool until closed, sun protection on new skin for a year.
  6. Scars: silicone gel or sheet once closed, for months; judge at 12 to 18 months.
  7. Nerve palsy with a fracture: most recover by 6 months; ask about early exploration within 3 weeks if the surgeon is unsure.
  8. Peptides: no human trial of BPC-157, TB-500, GHK-Cu or KPV in any crash injury. Cerebrolysin has the only real human trial base after brain injury, as a hospital IV product. Watch for the GHK-Cu acute wound trial (results after Feb 2027).

7. Fifteen usual questions (evidence answers)

  1. How long will whiplash last? Most improvement in 3 months; about half still have some neck pain at 1 year. [verified]
  2. Should I wear a neck collar? No for typical whiplash; early active movement gave less pain and better long-term motion than rest plus collar. [verified]
  3. Does physio help whiplash? A little and early: 6 sessions beat one advice session at 4 months, not at 8 or 12. [verified]
  4. I hit my head but feel OK. Do I need follow-up? Yes: 53% of mild TBI patients had some limitation at a year, and those who skipped follow-up did worse. [verified]
  5. Should I rest in a dark room after concussion? Short rest only; light activity early and reduced screens speed recovery (Amsterdam 2022). [verified]
  6. How long does a broken bone take? Wrist about 7 to 10 weeks to bridge on X-ray; displaced collarbone 2 to 4 months (surgery about 5 weeks faster); nailed shin bone about 6 months; open shin fractures longer. 2.5% of fractures don't unite, more often in smokers, diabetics and open fractures. Ribs can affect quality of life for 2 years after a bad multi-rib injury. [verified, see 1.4 table]
  7. Can I take ibuprofen with a fracture? Short courses (≤14 days) were not linked to nonunion; long or high-dose use was (OR 1.56). [verified]
  8. Does a bone stimulator help? Ultrasound (LIPUS) did not speed tibial healing or function in a 501-patient sham trial. [verified]
  9. Is there a peptide that heals bone? Teriparatide is approved for osteoporosis and may shorten osteoporotic fracture healing; it is not proven in young healthy people. Abaloparatide's fracture trial was negative. BPC-157 bone data are one rabbit study from one lab. [verified]
  10. Does BPC-157 help concussion? One mouse study, dosed before or right after impact, from the same lab. No human data; concussion forums show almost no first-hand use. [verified]
  11. What about Cerebrolysin? Small to medium benefit in moderate to severe TBI trials (CAPTAIN); one 32-person mild TBI pilot positive; one 93-person trial null; IV hospital product, not FDA approved. [verified]
  12. Will TB-500 or thymosin beta-4 heal my road rash? The full protein was tested on chronic leg ulcers with modest, unclear results; TB-500 (a fragment) has no skin trial. [verified]
  13. Does GHK-Cu fade accident scars? Not shown in humans; a small laser-wound study was negative; a 60-person acute wound trial reports after Feb 2027. Silicone has the evidence. [verified]
  14. How long until a nerve comes back? Most fracture-related radial nerve palsies (70 to 77%) recover within 6 months on their own; regrowth about 1 mm per day. [verified; speed reported]
  15. Is it safe to put research peptides on or near an open wound? No regulator checks identity, purity or sterility; an open crash wound is already an infection risk; orthopaedic reviewers flag sterility and contamination specifically. [verified for review statements; risk inferred]

8. Warnings (bottom of page)

Go to hospital now (head) [reported, standard emergency guidance; Bali page above agrees]: worsening headache, repeated vomiting, seizure, confusion or drowsiness, slurred speech, weakness or numbness, unequal pupils, clear fluid from nose or ears, loss of consciousness, age over 65, on blood thinners, or drinking before the crash. CT-positive mild TBI had worse 1-year outcomes (61% vs 49% limited) (Nelson 2019). [verified]

Spine [verified decision-rule data; symptom list reported]: neck pain after a crash plus numbness, tingling or weakness in arms or legs, loss of bladder or bowel control, or midline neck tenderness needs imaging. Canadian C-spine rule and NEXUS catch about 99 to 100% of significant injuries when applied properly; CT has >98% sensitivity (Shah 2026, DOI). Do not self-manage with a collar and peptides.

Fractures: open wound over a break, deformity, can't bear weight → emergency. Open fractures raise nonunion risk (Zura 2016). Long NSAID or opioid courses linked to nonunion. [verified]

Wounds and infection: rising pain, redness, swelling, pus, fever or red streaks → doctor; tetanus booster if dirty; deep road rash may need a burn surgeon. [reported] Tropical heat, sea water and humidity add risk. [reported, Bali page]

Unregulated products near open wounds [verified review statements; risk inferred]: identity, purity, concentration and sterility of "research" peptides unverified (Tham 2026); injecting near a fresh surgical site or wound adds contamination risk to an already vulnerable area; tell your surgeon before using anything (one community user did, and the surgeon agreed; most don't ask).

Banned in sport: BPC-157, TB-500 and GH secretagogues prohibited at all times (see Tendon Base 1.6). Cerebrolysin WADA status not checked. [gap]

Side effects people actually reported (v2): teriparatide: hair loss (2 of 3 Drugs.com fracture reviews), reflux, tooth loss; oral BPC-157: 2 weeks of gas, bloating, constipation (1 report). [verified per page]

Insurance and records (Bali): hospitals may ask for deposits or insurer confirmation first; keep all invoices and records. [reported] Riding drunk or without a licence can void travel insurance [reported, via Smartraveller as cited by the Bali page].
