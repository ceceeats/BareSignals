1. The condition

1.1 Three different problems under one name

  • Mid-portion Achilles tendinopathy: pain and thickening 2 to 7 cm above the heel insertion (the definition used in de Vos 2010, PMID 20068208). Most common form in runners. Morning stiffness, pain at the start of a run that may warm up, then worse the next day. [verified for location; symptoms reported via Silbernagel 2020 review, PMID 32267723, DOI 10.4085/1062-6050-356-19]
  • Insertional Achilles tendinopathy: degeneration from the heel-bone insertion up to about 2 cm. Often calcified; compression of the tendon against the heel bone in deep dorsiflexion is the extra driver, which is why full heel drops off a step are usually avoided and exercises are done to floor level [reported]. Evidence is thin: a 2023 network meta-analysis of 9 trials (464 people) found very low confidence overall; eccentric exercise plus soft tissue work ranked best for short-term pain (Ko 2023, PMID 36750789, DOI 10.1186/s12891-023-06170-x). [verified]
  • Partial tear: localized fibre disruption inside a tendinopathic tendon. Literature is sparse and mostly imaging-based; this pass did not find a good treatment trial. [flag: gap]
  • Full rupture: sudden "kicked in the calf" event, often in middle-aged recreational players during push-off. Covered in section 4.4.

1.2 How common

  • Recreational runners (Netherlands, 1,929 followed): 5.2% developed Achilles tendinopathy around one event; 7.4% in marathon registrants. Strongest risk factor: Achilles tendinopathy in the previous 12 months (OR 6.3). Using a training schedule (OR 1.8) and compression socks (OR 1.7) were also linked; age, BMI and training volume were not (Lagas 2019, PMID 31892510, DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2019.12.013). [verified] v2 retag: the outcome was self-reported Achilles tendinopathy by questionnaire, up to 1 month after the event, not a clinical diagnosis; 2,378 enrolled, 1,929 with follow-up, 100 cases. Treat 5.2% as "reported symptoms", not confirmed tendinopathy. [verified] Note: compression socks are likely a marker of people who already had calf/Achilles niggles, not a cause [inferred].
  • Former Finnish elite athletes: middle and long distance runners had 31x the odds of Achilles tendinopathy before age 45 versus controls; sprinters had 14.9x the odds of rupture (Kujala 2005, PMID 15867554, DOI 10.1097/01.jsm.0000165347.55638.23). [verified]
  • General adult prevalence cited in the 2024 US physical therapy guideline as roughly 4 to 7%, higher with age and sport (secondary summary of Chimenti 2024, PMID 39611662). [reported; guideline full text not read]
  • Ruptures are rising: Finland 17.3 to 32.3 per 100,000 person-years from 1997 to 2019, steepest in older adults, while surgery fell from 13.6 to 4.9 per 100,000 (Leino 2022, PMID 36389616, DOI 10.1177/23259671221131536). [verified]
  • Basketball: NBA, 22 confirmed ruptures 2000 to 2025; 86.7% of those with follow-up returned, mean 10.8 months, with clear drops in minutes and scoring; a cluster in 2024 to 25 (Maman 2026, PMID 42436963, DOI 10.1177/23259671261461403). A second series found minutes per game fell about 20% after return (Kennedy 2026, PMID 42708016, DOI 10.7759/cureus.114162). [verified]
  • Racket sports: pickleball ruptures rising since 2016 in one US referral network; average age 64.5 vs 48.6 for other ruptures; 67% had surgery (McCahon 2024, PMID 39377135, DOI 10.1177/19386400241286591). [verified] Padel: about 3 injuries per 1,000 training hours and 8 per 1,000 matches; elbow is the top site; review could not separate Achilles figures (BMJ Open SEM review, link; title and authors not visible to me). [reported] Tennis: no Achilles-specific incidence found this pass. [gap]
  • Sex: ruptures are about 81% male across 74 years of literature (Potter 2026, PMID 42405884, DOI 10.1002/ksa.70520). [verified]

1.3 Risk factors

  • Fluoroquinolone antibiotics (ciprofloxacin, levofloxacin etc.): Achilles rupture risk 3.1x during current use, lasting about 60 days; 19.4x when combined with oral corticosteroids. Absolute extra risk is small: about 2 per 10,000 treated, rising to 6.6 per 10,000 in people 60+ also on steroids (Morales 2019, UK primary care, PMID 30465300, DOI 10.1007/s40261-018-0729-y). [verified] After a tendon repair, a fluoroquinolone within 90 days doubled Achilles reoperation (3.8% vs 1.8%) (Waters 2023, PMID 36843858, DOI 10.52965/001c.67914). [verified]
  • Oral corticosteroids: risk multiplier with fluoroquinolones as above. Injected cortisone: see section 4.5.
  • Statins: the same UK dataset found no association with rupture (Morales 2019) and an ultrasound study found statin users' Achilles structure equivalent to non-users (de Sá 2018, PMID 29928054, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0199645). [verified] Statins are often listed as a risk on websites; the better data do not support that. Higher BMI was linked to worse tendon structure. [verified]
  • Age: rupture incidence rising most in the elderly (Leino 2022). [verified]
  • Previous tendinopathy: strongest predictor of new symptoms (Lagas 2019). [verified]

1.4 Diagnosis and imaging

  • Diagnosis is clinical (location of pain, morning stiffness, pain on loading, palpable thickening). Imaging (ultrasound, MRI) confirms and was required for entry in trials such as Kearney 2021 and Johannsen 2022. [verified for trial entry criteria]
  • Ultrasound changes and Doppler signal often do not track symptoms: in Achilles, most high-quality studies found no link between imaging improvement and clinical improvement (Malliaras 2013 systematic review, PMID 23494258, DOI 10.1007/s40279-013-0019-z). [verified] Page implication: a scan "looking worse" is not the same as the tendon being worse.
  • Rupture: Thompson calf squeeze test plus ultrasound are standard [reported, not sourced this pass].

2. Tendon vs muscle vs bone healing

2.1 Key verified facts

  • Tendon core does not renew in adults. Carbon-14 left by 1955 to 1963 nuclear tests showed the Achilles core carries the carbon of the first ~17 years of life; muscle samples showed continuous turnover (Heinemeier 2013, FASEB J, PMID 23401563, DOI 10.1096/fj.12-225599). [verified] v2 caveat: 28 forensic tendon core samples vs only 4 muscle samples; donors born 1945 to 1983. The finding applies to the tendon core, not the outer sheath, and the muscle contrast is based on a very small sample. [verified] This is the single best explanation of why tendons heal slowly and incompletely.
  • The outside of the tendon does respond to load. Training raised type I collagen synthesis (PICP) in the tissue around the Achilles about 5 to 7 fold at 4 weeks and kept it high at 11 weeks; early on both synthesis and breakdown rise, later synthesis dominates (Langberg 2001, PMID 11433010, DOI 10.1111/j.1469-7793.2001.00297.x). [verified]
  • Injured tendons respond more than healthy ones. 12 weeks of eccentric training raised collagen synthesis from 3.9 to 19.7 µg/L in injured Achilles tendons of elite footballers, with no change in healthy tendons; pain fell from 44 to 13 on a 100 mm scale and all returned to football (Langberg 2007, PMID 16787448, DOI 10.1111/j.1600-0838.2006.00522.x). [verified; n = 6 injured]
  • Load magnitude matters more than contraction type. Meta-analysis of 27 studies: tendon stiffness rises with training (effect size 0.70), driven by loading intensity, not whether the exercise is eccentric, concentric or isometric; programs of 12+ weeks trended larger (Bohm 2015, PMID 27747846, DOI 10.1186/s40798-015-0009-9). [verified]
  • There is a strain threshold. Over 14 weeks, only the leg trained at high Achilles strain (~4.6%) got stiffer and thicker; the low-strain leg (~2.9%) did not adapt beyond daily life (Arampatzis 2007, PMID 17644689, DOI 10.1242/jeb.003814). [verified] The commonly quoted protocol from this group (about 90% of max, 3 s load / 3 s rest cycles) is [reported], not in the abstract.
  • Mechanotransduction: cells sense load through the matrix and cytoskeleton and convert it to protein synthesis; this is the basis of "mechanotherapy" for tendon, muscle, cartilage and bone (Khan and Scott 2009, PMID 19244270, DOI 10.1136/bjsm.2008.054239). [verified]
  • Growth hormone raises tendon collagen: 14 days of rhGH increased tendon collagen I mRNA 3.9x and tendon collagen synthesis 1.3x in healthy young men, with no effect on muscle protein (Doessing 2010, PMID 19933753, DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.2009.179325). [verified] Relevant to the GH-peptide question.
  • v2: Achilles blood supply. A cadaver study (20 limbs) found three vascular territories; the midsection is fed by the peroneal artery and is markedly less vascular than the rest; the midsection is also where ruptures and surgical complications cluster (Chen 2009, PMID 19173244, DOI 10.1002/ca.20758). [verified] The exact "2 to 6 cm" figure is not in the abstract. [reported]
  • v2: Muscle repair. Satellite cells (resident muscle stem cells) activate, multiply and fuse into new fibres after injury, in a process that repeats embryonic muscle development (Karalaki 2009 review, PMID 19779115, no DOI listed). [verified] Most muscle injuries heal well without surgery, but failed healing can cost weeks to months (Järvinen 2005, PMID 15851777, DOI 10.1177/0363546505274714). [verified] Timelines in elite football: hamstring grade 1 about 18 days and grade 2 about 24 days lay-off (255 MRI-graded injuries; Ekstrand 2016, PMID 27084882, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2016-095974); median absence 13 days for hamstring and calf strains across 5,603 muscle injuries (Hallén and Ekstrand 2014, PMID 24784885, DOI 10.1080/02640414.2014.905695). [verified]
  • v2: Anti-inflammatories and muscle. Any early NSAID benefit seems not to last and may turn into a long-term repair deficit; there is evidence that NSAIDs harm satellite cells (Mackey 2012 review, PMID 22449131, DOI 10.1111/j.1600-0838.2012.01463.x). [verified]
  • v2: Bone repair. Fracture repair is true regeneration: it usually restores the bone's original cells, structure and strength, but about 10% of fractures do not heal normally (Einhorn and Gerstenfeld 2015, Nat Rev Rheumatol, PMID 25266456, DOI 10.1038/nrrheum.2014.164). [verified] The common "6 to 12 weeks to union" is still not in any abstract read. [reported]

2.3 Healing phases (tendon) [reported, general literature]

  1. Inflammatory: days 0 to ~7.
  2. Proliferative: ~1 to 6 weeks; fibroblasts lay down type III collagen.
  3. Remodelling: from ~6 weeks for 12 months or more; type III to type I, fibres align with load. Peptide rat studies mostly measure weeks 1 to 4 (phases 1 and 2); none measures the 12-month remodelling that matters most in humans [inferred].

3. Verifying the doctor's isometric protocol

3.1 The source studies

  • Rio 2015 (patellar tendinopathy, 6 volleyball players, crossover): 5 x 45 s isometric holds at 70% of max (MVIC), 2 min rest [protocol values reported; outcome verified]. Pain on single-leg decline squat fell from 7.0 to 0.17/10 after isometrics vs 6.3 to 3.75 after isotonic; relief lasted at least 45 min; strength rose 18.7% (PMID 25979840, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2014-094386). [verified]
  • Rio 2017 in-season (20 jumping athletes, 4 weeks, 4x/week): isometric leg extension at 80% MVIC produced greater immediate pain relief than isotonic; both improved function (PMID 27513733, DOI 10.1097/JSM.0000000000000364). [verified]
  • Bradford 2021 (Achilles, 11 people): isometric plantarflexion holds reduced pain on a loading test by 1.6 points (knee straight) and 1.3 (knee bent), about 50% (PMID 33782936, DOI 10.1055/a-1398-5501). [verified]

3.2 Replication failures and controversy

  • O'Neill 2019 (Achilles, 16 people): one heavy 45 s isometric hold gave no meaningful pain relief (4.2 to 4.9/10, not significant); authors concluded it cannot be recommended for immediate pain relief (PMID 29974171, DOI 10.1007/s00167-018-5050-z). [verified]
  • Holden 2020 (patellar, 21 people, pre-registered crossover): both isometric and dynamic exercise reduced pain by only 0.9/10, not sustained at 45 min, no difference between types (PMID 31735531, DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2019.09.015). [verified]
  • Clifford 2020 meta-analysis (10 trials, 5 tendons): isometric not superior to isotonic, immediately or up to 12 weeks; response highly variable (PMID 32818059, DOI 10.1136/bmjsem-2020-000760). [verified]
  • Challoumas 2021 patellar network meta-analysis: isometric as effective as isotonic for immediate relief (MD -1.03, CI crosses zero) (PMID 34900334, DOI 10.1136/bmjsem-2021-001110). [verified]
  • v2: Soliman 2026 systematic review (13 RCTs, 336 people, 6 tendon sites): pain and function improved significantly in only 3 studies; 3 of 13 good quality, 10 poor; adherence fell over time (70% isometric vs 58% isotonic did 80% or more of sessions); interventions ran from one session to 4 months; no hold-duration standard emerged (PMID 42203537, DOI 10.1016/j.clinthera.2026.04.027). [verified]
  • Takeaway: isometrics are a valid, tolerable way to load a painful tendon; the dramatic "instant pain off" from Rio 2015 (n = 6) is contested. [inferred from above]

3.4 Other Achilles loading programs

  • Alfredson 1998 eccentric heel drops (15 recreational athletes, 12 weeks): all back to full running; strength equalized; conventional-treatment comparison group all ended in surgery (PMID 9617396, DOI 10.1177/03635465980260030301). [verified] The standard dose, 3 x 15, straight and bent knee, twice daily, 7 days a week, 12 weeks, is [reported] (described in later protocols, e.g. Rabusin 2019 "twice a day, 7 days a week, for 12 weeks", PMID 30949243). Not randomized; early results not matched by later trials.
  • Beyer 2015 heavy slow resistance (HSR) vs eccentric (58 people, 12 weeks): equal improvements in function and pain, maintained at 52 weeks; HSR had better adherence (92% vs 78% of sessions) and a trend to higher satisfaction (100% vs 80% at 12 weeks) (PMID 26018970, DOI 10.1177/0363546515584760). [verified] HSR is 3 sessions/week vs eccentric's 14 [reported].
  • Silbernagel 2007 pain-monitoring model (38 people): continuing running and jumping while keeping pain within limits gave the same 12-month outcome as 6 weeks of rest (VISA-A 57 to 85 vs 57 to 91) (PMID 17307888, DOI 10.1177/0363546506298279). [verified] The rule usually quoted (pain up to 5/10 during activity allowed, settles by next morning, no week-on-week worsening) is [reported].
  • Negative mirror (2026): unsupervised home eccentric training was no better than sham upper-body exercise at 12 weeks (VISA-A +10 vs +6, difference 4, CI -7 to 15; 68 people) (Janssen 2026, PMID 42575766, DOI 10.1016/j.jsams.2026.07.012). [verified] Implies supervision, load progression and time matter; the exercise alone is not magic.
  • v2: HEALTHY trial result (100 people, mid-portion, mean age 46): in-shoe heel lifts improved VISA-A by 26.0 vs 17.4 for eccentrics at 12 weeks; difference 9.6 (CI 1.8 to 17.4), just below the trial's own 10-point minimum important difference; 80% follow-up (Rabusin 2020, PMID 32988930, DOI 10.1136/bjsports-2019-101776). [verified] Page angle: a cheap, low-effort option at least as good as heel drops in the short term.
  • v2: 5-year outcome with exercise alone (34 people): 80% fully recovered (65% symptom-free, 15% had a new episode), 20% still had symptoms; more fear of movement went with poorer heel-rise recovery (Silbernagel 2011, PMID 21084657, DOI 10.1177/0363546510384789). [verified; case series, level 4]
  • Adherence: in a 127-person trial, doing the prescribed sets, reps and added weight predicted better VISA-A; simply showing up (frequency) did not (Kenny 2025, PMID 40494036, DOI 10.1016/j.msksp.2025.103362). [verified]
  • Malliaras 2013 review: no clear superiority for isolated eccentrics; neuromuscular performance gains were the mechanism most consistently linked to recovery (PMID 23494258). [verified]

3.5 Rupture rehab and early weight bearing

  • UKSTAR (540 patients, non-surgical): functional brace with early weight bearing equal to plaster cast at 9 months (ATRS 72.8 vs 74.4); re-rupture 5% vs 6%; cheaper (Costa 2020, Lancet, PMID 32035553, DOI 10.1016/S0140-6736(19)32942-3). [verified]
  • Accelerated rehab in non-operative care: no difference in outcomes or re-rupture (OR 0.97) across 6 RCTs, judged safe (Coopmans 2021, PMID 34400090, DOI 10.1053/j.jfas.2021.07.007). [verified]

3.6 Rehab vs strengthening (doctor's point)

Same biology (load raises collagen synthesis and stiffness). Difference is the starting point and pain ceiling. Risk: loading beyond capacity flares tendinopathy, which is why every successful program above is progressive and pain-monitored. [inferred from verified studies] No trial found that tests "prevention strengthening" for Achilles injury in racket sports. [gap]

4. Other treatments, with numbers

4.1 PRP (platelet-rich plasma)

  • de Vos 2010 (JAMA, 54 people, mid-portion): PRP + eccentrics improved VISA-A 21.7 vs saline 20.5; no difference (PMID 20068208, DOI 10.1001/jama.2009.1986). [verified]
  • Kearney 2021 ATM trial (JAMA, 240 people, 24 sites): PRP 54.4 vs sham 53.4 VISA-A at 6 months; does not support use (PMID 34255009, DOI 10.1001/jama.2021.6986). [verified]
  • PATH-2 (BMJ 2019, 230 acute ruptures): no benefit on heel-rise function (difference -3.9%) or any secondary outcome (PMID 31748208, DOI 10.1136/bmj.l6132). [verified]
  • Cochrane 2015 (18 trials, 732 people): no meaningful VISA-A difference for injection therapies vs control at 6 weeks, 3 months or 6 to 12 months (PMID 26009861, DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD010960.pub2). [verified]

4.2 Shockwave (ESWT)

  • Rompe 2009 (68 people): eccentric + low-energy shockwave beat eccentric alone at 4 months (VISA-A 51 to 87 vs 50 to 73; 82% vs 56% much improved); no difference by 1 year (PMID 19088057, DOI 10.1177/0363546508326983). [verified; same research group developed the device protocol, single trial]
  • Katolický 2026 (41 people): both groups improved; shockwave added more ultrasound-measured remodelling but clinical difference not highlighted (PMID 42120288). [verified]
  • Umbrella review: shockwave improves pain and function across tendinopathies, clinical significance undetermined (Girgis 2020, PMID 32877858). [verified] 2024 guideline summary does not mention it. [reported]

4.3 High-volume injection (HVI)

  • Inject trial 2026 (BMJ, 192 people, 12 months): HVI with or without hydrocortisone no better than sham; steroid version helped at 6 weeks (+9.2) but was worse at 26 weeks than HVI without steroid (-8.6) (Malliaras 2026, PMID 42951276, DOI 10.1136/bmj-2026-100807). [verified]
  • Military trial (62 men, Achilles and patellar): HVI not superior to a small control injection (Barker-Davies 2022, PMID 35425844, DOI 10.1177/23259671221088326). [verified]

4.4 Rupture: surgery vs non-surgery

  • Ochen 2019 meta-analysis (29 studies, ~15,900 patients): re-rupture 2.3% surgery vs 3.9% non-surgery (difference 1.6 points); complications 4.9% vs 1.6%, mostly infection; with accelerated functional rehab, no significant re-rupture difference (PMID 30617123, DOI 10.1136/bmj.k5120). [verified]
  • Myhrvold 2022 NEJM (554 patients, 3 arms): no difference in function at 12 months; re-rupture 6.2% non-operative vs 0.6% in each surgical group; nerve injury 5.2% minimally invasive, 2.8% open, 0.6% non-op (PMID 35417636, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2108447). [verified] v2 recheck: 526 analysed; all three groups were still below baseline at 12 months (ATRS change -17.0, -16.0, -14.7) [verified]; that baseline was the patients' recalled pre-injury score [reported, not in abstract].
  • Non-operative with functional bracing: re-rupture 5 to 12% in a 2026 review; functional deficits persist for years either way (Maffulli 2026, PMID 42949430, DOI 10.1093/bmb/ldag041). [verified]

4.5 Cortisone

  • Johannsen 2022 (JAMA Netw Open, 100 people): ultrasound-guided steroid + exercise gave 17.7 points more VISA-A improvement at 6 months than placebo + exercise, no deterioration at 2 years, no severe adverse events (PMID 35816306, DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2022.19661). [verified]
  • Counterweights: Inject 2026 showed steroid HVI worse at 26 weeks (above); a 2024 expert viewpoint recommends against steroid injections, citing recurrence and rupture risk (Visser 2024, PMID 37506303, DOI 10.2519/jospt.2023.11875); the only major adverse event in the Cochrane review was a rupture in a steroid trial (PMID 26009861). [verified] Rat data: methylprednisolone worsened tendon-to-bone healing (Krivic 2006). [verified, animal]
  • Status: contested; short-term benefit likely, long-term cost possible.

4.6 Other

  • Heel lifts: may temporarily reduce load; orthoses evidence contradictory (2024 guideline summary) [reported]. The HEALTHY trial (heel lifts vs eccentrics) protocol is verified (PMID 30949243); results not retrieved. [gap]
  • Low-level laser: guideline says do not use [reported]. PEMF: both arms improved, no sustained extra benefit reported in abstract (Ko 2026, PMID 41651914). [verified]

5. Peptides: evidence grade

Grade key: A = multiple good human RCTs; B = one human RCT or consistent small human trials; C = human pilot/retrospective only; D = animal/cell only.

5.1 BPC-157. Grade D for Achilles (C overall, non-tendon)

Animal (rat) Achilles work, nearly all from one Zagreb group: - Staresinic 2003: transected rat Achilles; BPC-157 10 µg, 10 ng or 10 pg/kg intraperitoneal daily; higher load to failure, stiffness and functional index, smaller defect, by day 14 (PMID 14554208, DOI 10.1016/S0736-0266(03)00110-4). [verified] - Krivic 2006: Achilles detached from bone; BPC-157 improved tendon-to-bone healing and reduced the damage from methylprednisolone (PMID 16583442, DOI 10.1002/jor.20096). [verified] - Krivic 2008: early function days 1 to 4 better, less inflammation, more new vessels; steroid reduced inflammation but also vessels, with no functional gain (PMID 18594781, DOI 10.1007/s00011-007-7056-8). [verified] - Brcic 2009: no direct effect on cells in a dish; in vivo VEGF up and angiogenesis modulated (PMID 20388964). [verified] - Chang 2011 (independent, Taiwan): faster tendon fibroblast outgrowth, survival under oxidative stress and migration via FAK-paxillin; no direct proliferation (PMID 21030672, DOI 10.1152/japplphysiol.00945.2010). [verified] - Bicer 2026 (independent, Turkey, 32 rats, repaired Achilles, 4 weeks): BPC-157 10 µg/kg/day ip gave numerically better histology but not statistically significant on total scores; TB-500 did better (below); combination added nothing (PMID 42542926, DOI 10.52312/jdrs.2026.2951). [verified] This is the first non-Zagreb in vivo Achilles test found and it is weaker than the Zagreb results. Important negative mirror. v2 reframe: BPC-157 load to failure was also higher than control, but not significantly; the authors themselves conclude both peptides were associated with better histology and matrix organization, and call the study exploratory. 8 rats per group, split between strength testing and histology, so each test used fewer than 8 [inferred from methods wording]. Fair page wording: "positive direction for both, significant only for TB-500, tiny study". [verified + inferred] Human: - Systematic review to 1 June 2026: 3 human studies, n = 30 total, none in tendon; all poor quality; certainty very low (Poolton 2026, PMID 42885452, DOI 10.52628/92.3.15837). [verified] - Knee injections, retrospective phone survey: 14 of 16 reported relief (Lee and Padgett 2021, PMID 34324435). [verified; no controls, no validated measures] - Registered trials: a phase 2 RCT in MRI-confirmed grade II hamstring strain (n = 120, recruiting since Feb 2026, NCT07437547 (registry record doubtful: its sponsor's other records include one that calls itself fictional; see the muscle injury notes)); a phase 1 rotator cuff repair study (n = 30, not yet recruiting, NCT07803250); an older phase 1 safety/PK study (NCT02637284, status unknown, no results). [verified registry] No Achilles trial registered (rechecked 3 Nov 2026). [verified] - v2: rotator cuff trial detail (NCT07803250): university sponsor, randomized double-blind placebo-controlled pilot; daily subcutaneous self-injection for 90 days after arthroscopic repair; start Jan 2027, primary outcome shoulder strength; registry enrollment 30 but summary says 20 patients (discrepancy). Dose not stated. [verified registry] The hamstring phase 2 trial has the same commercial sponsor as the TB-500 fragment trial. [verified registry] - v2: 2026 reviews. JAAOS (27 Oct 2026): human musculoskeletal evidence for BPC-157 is one small retrospective uncontrolled case series; no validated dosing, PK or long-term safety; routine clinical use cannot be recommended (Tham 2026, PMID 42937313, DOI 10.5435/JAAOS-D-26-00049). [verified] Sports Med (Apr 2026) flags placebo effects amplified by social media as a likely driver of reported benefit (Mendias 2026, PMID 41966639, DOI 10.1007/s40279-026-02437-0). [verified] A Feb 2026 review claiming consistent efficacy is written by the Zagreb group itself (Matek 2026, PMID 41754849, DOI 10.3390/ph19020309). [verified authorship] Treat it as the originators' view, not independent confirmation. [inferred]

5.2 TB-500 / thymosin beta-4. Grade D for tendon

  • Clarify on page: TB-500 is not thymosin beta-4. It is a synthetic acetylated 7-amino-acid fragment (Ac-LKKTETQ, residues 17 to 23 of the 43-amino-acid protein), the actin-binding region (Ho 2012, PMID 23084823, DOI 10.1016/j.chroma.2012.09.043). [verified]
  • Bicer 2026 rats: TB-500 60 µg/kg/day ip for 4 weeks gave significantly higher load to failure and better Bonar (p = 0.016) and Movin (p = 0.017) scores; more mature collagen organization. [verified] Best single animal Achilles result for TB-500, one small study.
  • Human: full thymosin beta-4 has human trials in eye, heart and skin (e.g. phase 3 dry eye, n = 601, NCT02974907; recombinant Tβ4 phase 1a/1b in healthy volunteers, NCT04555824/NCT04555850). No tendon trials. A phase 1/2 dose-escalation trial of the TB-500 fragment itself in cardiovascular disease is recruiting (n = 80, NCT07487363, commercial sponsor). [verified registry; results not available]

5.3 GHK-Cu. Grade D, weak

  • Rat ACL reconstruction: intra-articular GHK-Cu improved laxity and stiffness at 6 weeks, benefit gone by 12 weeks after stopping (Fu 2015, PMID 25731775, DOI 10.1002/jor.22831). [verified] No Achilles or tendon study found. Fits the editor's earlier decision to drop the GHK-Cu card on the tennis elbow page.

5.4 Collagen / gelatin + vitamin C. Grade B-minus (only peptide-adjacent option with human tendon data)

  • Shaw 2017: 15 g vitamin C-enriched gelatin 1 h before 6 min of rope skipping doubled PINP (collagen synthesis marker) vs placebo; serum applied to engineered ligaments raised collagen content (8 healthy men, crossover) (PMID 27852613, DOI 10.3945/ajcn.116.138594). [verified] Surrogate markers, not injury outcomes.
  • Praet 2019 (20 Achilles tendinopathy patients, crossover with calf program): specific collagen peptides first 3 months: VISA-A +12.6 vs +5.3 placebo; after crossover the late group caught up (+17.7) (PMID 30609761, DOI 10.3390/nu11010076). [verified; small, industry product]
  • 2026 systematic review: "cautiously positive signal", low certainty, most products multicomponent (Latini 2026, PMID 42666580, DOI 10.3389/fmed.2026.1891011). [verified]
  • Not WADA-relevant; a legal food. Strong candidate for an "options" card. [inferred]

5.5 GH secretagogues (CJC-1295, ipamorelin, MK-677). Grade D for tendon

  • Mechanistic link: real GH raises tendon collagen synthesis (Doessing 2010, above). [verified]
  • 2026 AJSM scoping review of BPC-157, TB-500, CJC-1295, MK-677, ipamorelin, GHK-Cu: 67% of papers animal; human data "modest improvements at best"; MK-677 linked to congestive heart failure risk and insulin resistance; should not be recommended (Tewari 2026, PMID 42578445, DOI 10.1177/03635465261464420). [verified]

7. Pharmacokinetics and dosing (as reported in studies only)

  • BPC-157 animal PK (He 2022): elimination half-life under 30 minutes after IV or IM in rats and dogs; linear kinetics; IM bioavailability 14 to 19% in rats, 45 to 51% in dogs; broken into small peptides and amino acids, cleared via urine and bile (PMID 36588717, DOI 10.3389/fphar.2022.1026182). [verified]
  • Human IV pilot 2025 (Lee and Burgess): 2 people (58 and 68 y), who had used IV BPC-157 before; 10 mg in 250 mL saline over 1 h on day 1, 20 mg on day 2; no change in heart, liver, kidney, thyroid or glucose markers; no side effects reported (PMID 40131143). [verified] No PK sampling reported in abstract; two prior users at a private clinic is a very weak safety signal.
  • Animal doses used in Achilles studies: BPC-157 10 µg/kg/day ip (Staresinic 2003; Bicer 2026), with effects also claimed at 10 ng and 10 pg/kg (Staresinic 2003); TB-500 60 µg/kg/day ip (Bicer 2026). [verified] The Zagreb claim of effect across a million-fold dose range is unusual and a reason for caution [inferred].
  • Routes: rat studies used intraperitoneal or local cream; human reports used intra-articular, intravesical, IV (Poolton 2026). Local vs systemic: no human data comparing them. With a <30 min half-life and systemic distribution, "inject near the tendon" is unproven [inferred].
  • TB-500 / Tβ4 human PK: phase 1 studies of recombinant full Tβ4 completed (NCT04555824, NCT04555850) but results not retrieved; a TB-500 fragment PK trial is recruiting (NCT07487363). [gap]
  • Why "start low, go slow": standard first-in-human logic when human PK, dose-response and toxicity are unknown; lets side effects show at low exposure; and with no purity regulation, a low first dose limits harm from a contaminated or mislabeled vial [inferred]. FDA's 2023 concerns were impurities and characterization, which supports the product-quality reason [reported].
  • No human dose for tendon healing is established for any of these peptides. [verified absence across the 2025 and 2026 reviews; reconfirmed by Tham 2026 JAAOS]
  • v2: the only registered human tendon protocol (NCT07803250) uses daily subcutaneous injection for 90 days; the dose is not posted. [verified registry]

8. Usual questions (draft answers with sources)

  • Can I keep running/playing? Often yes, if pain stays within limits: continuing with pain monitoring gave the same 12-month result as rest (Silbernagel 2007). Complete rest is discouraged (2024 guideline summary). [verified / reported]
  • How long? Most trials run 12 weeks of loading with gains continuing to 12 months (Beyer 2015; Silbernagel 2007); at 5 years 80% had fully recovered with exercise alone (Silbernagel 2011). Rupture: elite basketball return ~10.8 months, 13 of 15 with follow-up returned (Maman 2026). [verified]
  • Stretching? Guideline summary: only when ankle dorsiflexion is restricted. [reported]
  • Shoes / heel drop? v2: heel lifts did at least as well as eccentric exercise at 12 weeks (+26 vs +17 VISA-A; difference just under the clinically important threshold) (Rabusin 2020). [verified] Footwear beyond heel lifts: no strong evidence found. [gap]
  • Ice? No Achilles trial found this pass. For acute rotator cuff, isometrics were no better than ice (Clifford 2020). [gap for Achilles]
  • Injections? PRP: no benefit in two JAMA trials and PATH-2. HVI: no benefit at 12 months (Inject 2026). Cortisone: short-term help, possible long-term harm, contested. [verified]
  • Return to sport? Use symptoms plus strength: recovery tracks neuromuscular performance (Malliaras 2013); the NBA data show performance often does not fully return after rupture. [verified]
  • Side effects of peptides? Human data cannot answer this: n = 30 total in published reports, poor quality (Poolton 2026). Known risks are product quality, injection site issues, and for MK-677 heart failure and insulin resistance (Tewari 2026). [verified]
  • Will it get me banned? Yes for BPC-157, TB-500 and GH secretagogues under WADA rules, any time, prescription or not; MOTS-c too (S4.4.1). [verified for 2026 List; 2027 per USADA: peptides named as a group in S0, BPC-157 S0, TB-500 S2, reported; WADA's own 2027 text not read, see 6.0] GLP-1 drugs are not banned, only monitored [reported]. A US sanction came from a whistleblower, not a test (USADA 2025). [verified]
  • Is it legal now in the US? v3: under a draft HHS policy (published 30 Oct 2026, comments until about 13 Nov 2026), HHS says it will not act against state-licensed pharmacies, federal facilities or licensed physicians compounding BPC-157 or TB-500 under 10 conditions. That is enforcement discretion, not FDA approval, does not cover research-chemical sellers, and has no bearing on sport (see 6.1). [verified, HHS policy text and FR notice]
  • Do antibiotics matter? Fluoroquinolones raise rupture risk ~3x for ~60 days, ~19x with oral steroids (Morales 2019). [verified]
