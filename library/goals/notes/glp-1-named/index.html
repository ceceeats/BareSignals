GLP1 Named Effects v1

Fun-facts research for the baresignals.co GLP-1 hub: the viral nicknames ("Ozempic personality", "food noise", "Ozempic face" and friends). Compiled 3 Nov 2026. Version 1 (first pass). Companion to GLP1 Research v1 (sections 4.3, 4.4, 4.9), GLP1 Claims Brain v1 (Cards 2, 4, 5), GLP1 Claims Hormones v1 (Ozempic babies). Overlaps are cross-referenced, not repeated in full.

What changed in v1: New file. About 50 searches and fetches (PubMed, ClinicalTrials.gov, FDA label PDF, news primary sources, web).

Evidence tags: [verified] = primary abstract, FDA label or registry read this pass. [reported] = news, review or secondary site. [inferred] = my reasoning from verified facts. [speculated] = plausible, unsupported. Verdict scale: Proven (label rate vs placebo or replicated controlled data) / Likely (consistent controlled signal, not settled) / Plausible (mechanism plus case or survey data) / Unproven (anecdote only) / Myth (contradicted or a misreading). "Drug or weight loss?" is answered on every card, because that is the question readers actually have. Rules kept: paraphrased throughout, no grey-market or compounding-seller sources used as evidence (several came up in searches and were skipped), no dosing instructions. Doses appear only as what a trial or case report tested.

Quick list (fact lines for the hub page)

  1. Ozempic personality: Some people on high doses say life feels "meh"; the same brain brake that quiets food cravings may dull other rewards. Plausible, dose-linked, usually reversible.
  2. Food noise: The nonstop mental chatter about food can go quiet within days, often before much weight is lost. Likely.
  3. Ozempic face: About 7% of midface volume is lost per 10 kg of body weight lost, mostly from the fat just under the skin. Proven as weight loss, not a drug effect.
  4. Ozempic butt, breasts, hands, feet, vulva: Same story as the face: fat pads shrink faster than skin tightens. Weight loss, not the drug.
  5. Ozempic teeth: Dry mouth is a real reporting signal for semaglutide; tooth damage itself has never been measured in a study. Plausible.
  6. Sulfur burps: Burping is on the label: 7% on Wegovy vs under 1% on placebo. The "rotten egg" smell part is inferred, not measured. Proven (burping), Plausible (sulfur).
  7. Ozempic dreams: Vivid, weird dreams went viral in April 2023; no trial or label lists them. Unproven.
  8. Mounjaro rage: Irritability reports exist, but no study measures it; low blood sugar and hunger are the likely culprits. Unproven.
  9. Ozempic fatigue: Tiredness is on the Wegovy label at 11% vs 5% on placebo. Proven.
  10. Skin tingling (dysaesthesia): Burning or "sunburn without sun" skin rises with dose: 22% on high-dose semaglutide 7.2 mg vs 0% on placebo. Proven, mechanism unknown.
  11. Feeling cold: Common complaint; the most likely cause is eating far less, not the drug acting on temperature. Plausible.
  12. Food aversions: Users eat much less processed food, soda and beef; taste itself barely changes in tests. Likely.
  13. Alcohol loses its pull: About 6 in 10 drinkers on GLP-1s say they drink less; "it tastes bad" is rarer than "I just don't want it". Likely.
  14. Ozempic penis: A viral 2026 claim that it "looks bigger" is simply pubic fat loss. Weight loss.
  15. Shopping and habits: About 1 in 5 Reddit comments on compulsive shopping described it stopping. Plausible.
  16. Ozempic babies: Unplanned pregnancies, from restored ovulation and possibly reduced pill absorption on tirzepatide. See GLP1 Claims Hormones v1.

Card 1. "Ozempic personality" (feeling flat, losing the spark, or calmer)

Fun fact: The same brain circuit that switches off cravings for cake can, in some people at high doses, turn down the pleasure of hobbies, sex and sunsets too.

Where the term came from - Earliest dated use found: New York Post, 17 Apr 2024 (author Adriana Diaz), which framed it as the latest nickname after "Ozempic face" and "Ozempic butt". [verified, Yahoo UK syndication of NY Post] - Within a week: Healthline (23 Apr 2024) and Prevention (25 Apr 2024, syndicated by AOL). Healthline says the term spread through social media, NY Post and Daily Mail. [verified, both pages read] - Nobody is credited with coining it. A search for 2023 uses returned nothing earlier. [verified absence this pass; earlier TikTok use is likely but unconfirmed, inferred] - Second wave, April 2026: Boston Globe (16 Apr 2026, an emotional-blunting feature also syndicated by KTLA and Daily Herald; the GLP1 Claims Brain v1 file notes it as Washington Post origin), MindSite News (22 Apr 2026), Washington Times (30 Apr 2026). This wave reframed it from "depression risk" to "anhedonia". [verified, pages read] - Several clinicians quoted in the 2026 coverage call the label stigmatising. [reported, Washington Times]

What people describe - Flat side: less joy in reading, music, gardening, sport, dancing; sunsets and birthday cake feel unappealing; less interest in sex, some say they "fell out of love"; trouble getting motivated to exercise; "bed rot". [reported, Boston Globe 2026] - Less shopping, less drinking, less snacking, less nail biting. [reported; Reddit data in Arillotta 2024 below] - The opposite side, equally common in reports: calmer, less anxious, less impulsive, more optimistic. [reported, Prevention 2024 citing Reddit users] - Libido goes both ways: up for some (body image, energy), down for others. [verified, Arillotta 2024 and Merhi 2026 review]

How it works mechanically (the core of this card) 1. Receptors in the reward system. GLP-1 receptors sit in the ventral tegmental area (VTA, where dopamine cells start), the nucleus accumbens (NAc, the "wanting" hub) and prefrontal cortex. Activating them shifts dopamine, glutamate and GABA signalling. [verified, Amorim Moreira Alves 2025 review, Med Sci, PMID 40843757, DOI] 2. How the drug gets there. Semaglutide barely crosses the blood-brain barrier. It hits the brainstem (area postrema, nucleus tractus solitarius) and the message is relayed. The NTS projects to GABA "brake" cells in the VTA, which dial dopamine cells down. [verified, Krupa 2025 commentary, Neurosci Appl, PMID 40654594, DOI; brain-access detail in GLP1 Claims Brain v1 section 0] 3. Dopamine release falls in the reward hub. Nature, May 2026 (mice with human GLP-1 receptors, given small-molecule oral-type GLP-1 drugs): a group of GLP-1-receptor neurons in the central amygdala cut dopamine release in the NAc and selectively reduced eating of tasty food. Deleting the receptor from those cells weakened the drug's effect on reward eating only. [verified, Godschall 2026, Nature, PMID 42092139, DOI] This is the cleanest circuit map so far for "the drug turns down wanting". 4. Food reward spilling over to other rewards. The same circuits handle sex, social reward, alcohol and drugs. In rodents, the GLP-1 drug exendin-4 suppressed sexual interaction when given into the VTA, laterodorsal tegmentum and NAc shell. [verified, Merhi 2026 review, Sex Med Rev, PMID 41870138, DOI] In healthy mice, daily semaglutide for 8 weeks cut sniffing of female scent (a natural social and sexual reward) and slowed reward-related brain waves in the NAc; weekly dosing did not. Small study. [reported, PsyPost summary of Mosqueira 2026, Mol Brain, DOI; primary not opened] 5. It is not simply "less dopamine". Two rodent findings complicate the story: semaglutide increased VTA dopamine during the act of consuming sucrose (more satisfaction from each bite) while leaving anticipation unchanged [reported, Kooij 2024 via Krupa 2025]; and in rats, chronic semaglutide made small food rewards and their cues more motivating, so they reached fullness sooner (Michigan-approved preprint, Dec 2025). [verified, bioRxiv preprint DOI, not peer reviewed] A human exenatide brain-scan study found lower anticipatory and higher consummatory reward. [reported, van Bloemendaal 2015 via Krupa 2025] Plain version: the drug may make "a little" feel like "enough". In most people that is the point; in some it may tip into "nothing feels worth chasing". [inferred] 6. Low calories and low energy. Eating 30 to 50% less, plus fatigue (11% vs 5% placebo on the Wegovy label, see Card 9), can feel like low mood on its own. [verified label; inferred link] 7. Identity and social shifts. Food can be a coping tool, a social glue and a hobby. Removing it can leave a gap. Experts in the coverage point to the loss of emotional eating as a coping habit, changed social feedback and shifting identity after big weight loss. [reported, Prevention 2024; MindSite 2026] Weight loss itself can bring irritability and temporary anxiety. [reported, Buettner via Prevention 2024]

Human evidence - Case series, 3 women on tirzepatide 15 mg (the top dose): flatness, low motivation, loss of interest in exercise and hobbies after long time at or near max dose; two had no mood history. Dose reduction to 10 mg or lower improved it in all; one also needed bupropion. Re-escalation in one patient brought symptoms back with no extra weight loss, and stepping down again fixed it while weight held. [verified, Nadolsky 2026, Obes Pillars, PMID 42518361, DOI] This dechallenge-rechallenge pattern is the strongest causal hint published. [inferred] - About 100 further cases being compiled by Nadolsky and Krumm (University of Florida) from thousands treated; unpublished. [reported, Boston Globe and MindSite 2026] - Case report: older woman with dementia developed apathy, weight loss and poor appetite on semaglutide; improved after stopping. [verified, Jamshed 2025, BMJ Case Rep, PMID 40759509, DOI] - Reddit mixed-methods study, Dec 2019 to Jun 2023, 5,859 threads: 21.35% of shopping-related comments described compulsive shopping stopping; 29.75% of alcohol comments described quitting; libido reportedly rose in several users. [verified, Arillotta 2024, Brain Sci, PMID 38928616, DOI] Companion study across Reddit, YouTube, TikTok: insomnia 620 matches, anxiety 353, depression 204; mood both improved and worsened. [verified, Arillotta 2023, Brain Sci, PMID 38002464, DOI] - Negative mirror (motivation goes UP): RCT, n=72 adults with depression and BMI ≥25, oral semaglutide 14 mg vs placebo for 16 weeks. Semaglutide made people more willing to exert effort for rewards (less effort discounting), the opposite of anhedonia. [verified, Gill 2026, JAMA Psychiatry, DOI, secondary analysis of NCT04466345; full text paywalled] - Negative mirror: Parkinson's trial post hoc (exenatide, 48 weeks) found better "mood/apathy" scores vs placebo (-3.3 points, p=0.026), not worse. [verified, Athauda 2018, J Parkinsons Dis, PMID 29843254, DOI] - Mood safety in big trials (STEP, SURMOUNT pooled; FDA removed the suicidality warning in Jan 2026): see GLP1 Claims Brain v1 Card 4. None of these trials measured anhedonia directly. [verified, Brain v1] - Genetics: a GLP-1 receptor gene variant (rs1042044) was linked to anhedonia scores and reward learning in 264 people, but not to depression diagnosis. [verified, Yapici-Eser 2020, Acta Neuropsychiatr, PMID 32213216, DOI] Hints that some people may be wired to respond more strongly. [speculated] - Sex and libido in humans: FAERS reports of low libido and ED exist but disproportionality is not significant; a dulaglutide crossover RCT in lean men found no effect on desire or hormones; female sexual side-effect case reports exist. [verified, Merhi 2026] Mayo review flags that GLP-1s may suppress desire and arousal centrally even as weight loss should help. [verified, Castaneda 2026, Menopause, PMID 42517362, DOI] ED: semaglutide in non-diabetic men linked to more ED (1.47% vs 0.32%, RR 4.5) [verified, Able 2024, Int J Impot Res, PMID 38778151, DOI], while tirzepatide was linked to less ED than semaglutide (RR 0.67). [verified, Cowart 2025, J Diabetes Complications, PMID 40614622, DOI] Both are database studies, so treat as signals.

Prevalence: No number exists. No survey, trial or registry has measured anhedonia on GLP-1s. Journalists and clinicians describe it as "not widespread" and affecting "a minority". Novo Nordisk says anhedonia is not a listed adverse reaction; Lilly says it has no data. [verified absence; company statements reported, Boston Globe 2026]

Dose relationship: Every published and reported case clusters at top doses (tirzepatide 15 mg) and improves with step-down, usually within weeks. [verified Nadolsky 2026; reported MindSite 2026] Mouse behaviour changes appeared only with daily, not weekly, semaglutide. [reported, Mosqueira 2026]

Reversibility: Reported reversible with dose reduction or stopping in all published cases. Long-term data after stopping: none; a Stanford expert notes it is unclear whether it fully resolves. [reported, Washington Times 2026] UT Dallas trial NCT07387796 (n=40, tirzepatide stop and restart, starts Oct 2026) will track neurobehavioural changes. [verified, ClinicalTrials.gov]

Which drugs: Cases: tirzepatide (top dose), semaglutide. Animal: semaglutide, exendin-4, small-molecule oral agonists. Retatrutide and amylin drugs (cagrilintide, eloralintide): no reports found. [verified absence this pass]

Drug or weight loss? Partly the drug. Evidence for a direct drug effect: symptoms tracking dose while weight holds (Nadolsky rechallenge), mouse effects persisting after controlling for weight. Evidence for weight-loss and life-change effects: loss of food as a coping tool, low intake, identity shift. Likely both, in different people. [inferred]

Verdict: Plausible. A real, dose-linked, reversible phenomenon in a minority, with a credible brain mechanism. Unquantified. Not supported as a common or permanent effect, and the one RCT that measured motivation found the opposite in depressed patients.

Card 2. "Food noise"

Fun fact: "Food noise" was a dieting phrase for years, but Google searches only took off in 2022 with GLP-1 drugs; scientists now have a 5-question test for it.

Origin: Origin unknown; a Google hit appears in 2006; searches rose sharply in 2022 alongside GLP-1 use; mainstream with a June 2023 New York Times piece. [reported, Wikipedia mirror citing those sources] Penn State work used social media posts to help define it. [reported, PSU news] Definitions: Formal 2025 definition: persistent food thoughts the person finds unwanted or distressing, and that can cause social, mental or physical harm; distinct from normal food thoughts by intensity and intrusiveness, like rumination. [verified, Dhurandhar 2025, Nutr Diabetes, PMID 40628707, DOI] Questionnaires: Food Noise Questionnaire, 5 items, n=400, reliability α 0.93, retest r 0.79. [verified, Diktas 2025, Obesity, PMID 39828656, DOI; co-authors from WW International, a weight-loss company, so note the interest] Separate RAID-FN Inventory in development. [verified, Dhurandhar 2025] Studies and prevalence: US survey n=1,000, 18% frequent food noise; GLP-1 users lower food noise at 6 months (n=79); qualitative n=30 top theme; full details in GLP1 Claims Brain v1 Card 2. [verified there] Saudi health students and staff, n=593: 29% high food noise, tracked eating-disorder risk and stress more than demographics; convenience sample. [verified, Alarfaj 2026, Nutrients, PMID 42738994, DOI] New review in Diabetes Obes Metab, Oct 2026. [verified title only, Patoulias 2026, PMID 42897102; abstract not available] Mechanism: Fullness signal to hindbrain and hypothalamus plus the reward dampening in Card 1 (central amygdala to NAc dopamine). [verified, Godschall 2026; Brain v1] Drug or weight loss? Mostly the drug: users report it within days, before meaningful weight loss. [reported] Returns after stopping. [reported] Verdict: Likely.

Card 3. "Ozempic face"

Fun fact: Scans show the midface loses about 7% of its volume for every 10 kg of body weight lost, mostly from the fat just under the skin.

Origin: Credited to New York dermatologist Paul Jarrod Frank. Earliest reliable dated use: Town and Country, 29 Nov 2022; New York Times, 24 Jan 2023 made it mainstream. [reported, Barry Popik etymology page listing dated citations; NYT not opened directly] Evidence: - Imaging, n=20 (CT or MRI before and after GLP-1): median 9% midface volume loss; superficial fat 11%, deep 7%; regression about 7% per 10 kg lost; superficial loss correlated with weight lost (rho 0.59), deep did not. [verified, Sharma 2025, Otolaryngol Head Neck Surg, PMID 40407186, DOI] - Review of 46 reports: survey of 1,226 people found more facial volume loss and laxity with greater % weight loss; post-bariatric patients show the same deflation, especially older patients. Conclusion: weight-loss deflation on top of normal ageing, not a drug-specific effect. [verified, Yoo 2026, Aesthet Surg J, PMID 42872378, DOI] - Aesthetic surgery review agrees it more plausibly reflects rapid or substantial weight loss than a proven drug effect. [verified, Ersan 2026, Turk J Surg, PMID 42830623, DOI] - Possible extras under study: changes in dermal fat stem cells, collagen matrix and facial muscle. Hypothesis only. [verified, Cho 2026, PMID 42893183] - "About 9% of users get midfacial volume loss" circulates in press. [reported, Washington Times 2026, unsourced; flag] Drug or weight loss? Weight loss. Verdict: Proven as a weight-loss effect; Myth as a unique drug effect.

Card 4. "Ozempic butt", "Ozempic breasts", "Ozempic hands", "Ozempic feet", "Ozempic vulva", "Ozempic penis"

Fun fact: Every "Ozempic body part" trend has the same explanation: fat pads shrink faster than skin can tighten.

  • Butt: Sagging, flatter buttocks after fast weight loss. Covered by Tufts Medicine (31 Jul 2024) and tabloids through 2025 and 2026. No study measures it. [reported]
  • Breasts: "Deflated" upper-pole volume and ptosis noted by plastic surgeons; literature limited. [verified, Ersan 2026; reported, Yahoo]
  • Hands: Thinner skin, more visible veins and tendons. Press-only; no study found. [reported; verified absence in PubMed this pass]
  • Feet: Loss of foot fat padding; AACE (6 May 2025) summarised Health.com with endocrinologists saying it can follow any rapid weight loss. No data. [verified, AACE page]
  • Vulva: Looser labia and mons from fat loss, plus pelvic floor weakness if muscle is lost; dryness more likely hormonal. Gynaecologist quoted says the drug does not act on the vulva directly. No study. [verified, Healthline 23 Aug 2025]
  • Penis (new 2026): Claim that it looks bigger, because pubic fat that partly hides it ("buried penis") shrinks. A plastic surgeon on a podcast; no data. [verified, PinkNews 11 Sep 2026] Real ED data are in Card 1. Drug or weight loss? Weight loss (plus muscle loss when protein and resistance training are low). [inferred] Verdict: Proven pattern for weight loss generally; Unproven as anything specific to these drugs.

Card 5. "Ozempic teeth" (and hair)

Fun fact: Out of 364,274 US side-effect reports for GLP-1 drugs, 8.6% involved the mouth, teeth or face; semaglutide alone flagged for dry mouth.

  • FAERS, all GLP-1s to Dec 2025: 31,195 oral, craniofacial or dental reports. Semaglutide had a drug-specific dry-mouth signal; vomiting and reflux signals across all five drugs; taste changes flagged for several. [verified, Massarotto 2026, JDR Clin Trans Res, PMID 42935458, DOI]
  • No prospective study has used any dental outcome as an endpoint. Nausea (15 to 50%) and vomiting (5 to 24%) make enamel erosion plausible but unquantified. [verified, Werner 2026, J Dent Res, PMID 42873199, DOI]
  • Proposed mechanisms: GLP-1 signalling in salivary gland cells, drinking less water, reflux and vomiting acid. [verified, Gilligan 2026, J Oral Pathol Med, PMID 42843413, DOI]
  • Negative mirror: in 97,993 matched pairs with diabetes, tirzepatide users had fewer periodontitis and tooth extraction records than other GLP-1 users (periodontitis HR 0.70). [verified, Tu 2026, Diabetes Res Clin Pract, PMID 42764072, DOI] Animal work suggests GLP-1s protect gum bone. [verified, Werner 2026]
  • Press term in The Hill / Nexstar coverage (around March 2026) quoting dentists; no study cited. [reported]
  • Hair: Hair loss 3% vs 1% placebo (Wegovy), 4 to 5% vs 1% (Zepbound); mostly stress shedding after rapid weight loss; detail in GLP1 Research v1 section 4.4 and Longevity v1 Card 10. [verified, FDA labels] Drug or weight loss? Dry mouth: possibly drug (semaglutide-specific signal). Erosion: side effects (vomiting, reflux). Hair: weight loss. Verdict: Dry mouth Likely; tooth damage Plausible; hair shedding Proven.

Card 6. "Sulfur burps"

Fun fact: Burping really is a listed side effect: 7% on Wegovy vs under 1% on placebo; the rotten-egg smell has never actually been measured.

  • Label rates (eructation): Wegovy 2.4 mg 7% vs <1% placebo [verified, Wegovy prescribing information via Drugs.com]; Zepbound 4 to 5% across doses vs 1% placebo [verified, FDA label 2026 PDF]. Related: dyspepsia 9% vs 3% (Wegovy), 9 to 10% vs 4% (Zepbound); reflux 5% vs 3%. [verified, labels]
  • Mechanism: slowed stomach emptying keeps food sitting longer; the "sulfur" part is assumed to be hydrogen sulfide from bacterial breakdown of sulfur-rich proteins. No trial has measured breath or stool hydrogen sulfide on these drugs. [reported, secondary research summary citing Urva 2020 gastric-emptying data; inferred] Drug or weight loss? Drug (gut-slowing effect). Verdict: Proven (burping); Plausible (sulfur smell).

Card 7. "Ozempic dreams" (vivid dreams, nightmares)

Fun fact: In April 2023 people posted dreams about shopping with Clint Eastwood and robbing museums with J.Lo; Novo Nordisk confirmed reports but said it could not tell if the drug caused them.

  • First wave: Wall Street Journal, 18 Apr 2023; Becker's summary 19 Apr 2023. Novo confirmed it had received reports; Lilly declined comment. [verified, Becker's]
  • Expert ideas: GLP-1 receptors in the brain [reported, Apovian]; or not more dreams, just better recall, for example from waking more often (nausea, hunger, bathroom trips). [reported, Barrett, Harvard dream researcher]
  • Other plausible routes: better sleep apnea (tirzepatide is approved for OSA) can cause REM rebound, vivid dreams after years of fragmented REM; night-time low blood sugar in people on other diabetes drugs can cause nightmares. [inferred from sleep literature, cited by a secondary review; not verified this pass]
  • Not listed on Wegovy or Zepbound adverse reaction tables. [verified absence, labels]
  • Reddit/TikTok/YouTube study: insomnia was the most discussed mental-health topic (620 matches). [verified, Arillotta 2023]
  • One case: sleep-related eating disorder resolved alongside GLP-1 weight loss; causality unclear. [verified, Martirosyan 2026, Sleep Med, PMID 42759347, DOI] Drug or weight loss? Unknown; weight-loss route (apnea improvement) is the most testable. [inferred] Verdict: Unproven.

Card 8. "Mounjaro rage" / irritability

Fun fact: "Mounjaro rage" is all over forums, but no trial, label or study has measured it.

  • No PubMed study found linking GLP-1s to irritability or anger as an outcome. [verified absence this pass]
  • Clinician view: irritability can occur, especially with low blood sugar, alongside the numbed-out feeling. [reported, Avigail Lev via Washington Times 2026] Weight loss itself causes irritability for some. [reported, Buettner via Prevention 2024]
  • Negative mirror: among 7,521 US adults, the link between alcohol and violence was 52% weaker in current GLP-1 users (observational, Criminology 2026). [reported, Healthline 2026 update]
  • Pathways: hunger and low intake ("hangry"), poor sleep, nausea, low blood sugar in people also on insulin or sulfonylureas. [inferred] Drug or weight loss? Likely the eating less, not the drug. [inferred] Verdict: Unproven.

Card 9. Fatigue ("Ozempic fatigue")

Fun fact: Tiredness is a real label side effect: 11% on Wegovy vs 5% on placebo.

  • Wegovy 2.4 mg: fatigue and weakness 11% vs 5%; dizziness 8% vs 4%. Zepbound: fatigue 5 to 7% vs 3%; dizziness 4 to 5% vs 2%. [verified, labels]
  • Likely causes: much lower calorie intake, dehydration, nausea; usually during dose escalation. [inferred]
  • "Wegovy wake" (as a term) not found in any source searched. [verified absence, flag] Drug or weight loss? The drug's appetite effect leading to under-eating. [inferred] Verdict: Proven.

Card 10. Skin tingling (dysaesthesia, "sunburn" skin)

Fun fact: At the newest high semaglutide dose, more than 1 in 5 people get burning or tingling skin; nobody knows why yet.

  • Semaglutide 7.2 mg trials: 22% vs 6% at 2.4 mg vs 0% placebo; Wegovy 2.4 mg obesity trials: 2% vs 1%. Rises with dose and blood level. [verified, Wegovy prescribing information]
  • Retatrutide TRIUMPH-2 (obesity with type 2 diabetes): 4% / 6% / 7% at 4 / 9 / 12 mg vs 1% placebo. [verified, Bellido 2026, Lancet, PMID 42810372, DOI] TRIUMPH-1 (12 mg) 12.5% vs 0.9%. [reported, Lilly 2026, in GLP1 Research v1]
  • Tirzepatide: 0.2 to 0.4% vs 0.1% (Zepbound). [verified, FDA label PDF]
  • Systematic review of 101 studies: most strongly tied to semaglutide, about 5% at standard doses and up to 20% at high doses, often during escalation. [verified, Gupta 2026, Med Sci, PMID 42930571, DOI] A dedicated meta-analysis and meta-regression exists. [verified title only, Goldenberg 2026, Diabetes Obes Metab, PMID 42712150; abstract not available]
  • Mechanism: unknown. Separate rare condition: "treatment-induced neuropathy" from fast blood sugar drops in long-standing diabetes. [verified, Bagheri 2026 case, PMID 42959996] Drug or weight loss? Drug (tracks dose and blood level, not weight). [inferred from label wording] Verdict: Proven.

Card 11. Feeling cold

Fun fact: Many users say they are always cold; the likely reason is eating far less, since digesting food itself makes heat.

  • Reddit analysis: "feeling cold" 0.6%, chills 1.1% of 67,008 users. [verified, arXiv 2603.12341, see GLP1 Claims Hormones v1]
  • Not on label tables. [verified absence]
  • Mechanism: fewer calories means less diet-induced heat and lower metabolic rate; less body fat insulation. [inferred; no primary GLP-1 temperature study found this pass] Drug or weight loss? Weight loss and low intake. Verdict: Plausible.

Card 12. Food aversions (meat, coffee, greasy food)

Fun fact: Users eat a lot less processed food, soda and beef, but in lab tests their taste buds work almost the same.

  • US survey, n=1,955 (495 current users): processed foods, soda, refined grains and beef fell most; fruit, leafy greens and water were the only items that rose. [reported, University of Arkansas release, Food Quality and Preference, March 2025]
  • Grocery spending fell 5.5% within 6 months; savoury snacks -11% (Cornell and Numerator, about 150,000 households). [reported, NJBIZ Jan 2025]
  • Taste tests: humans on GLP-1s for 6 months, only sweet detection threshold changed (n=79). [verified, Rutigliani 2026, in Brain v1] Mice on chronic semaglutide had normal taste for sweet, bitter, sour, salty, fat. [verified, Acosta 2026, Mol Metab, PMID 42364710, DOI]
  • Fat specifically: brainstem neurons (GFRAL) fire when mice eat fat; GIP receptor "anti-nausea" neurons respond to sugar. Could explain why greasy food becomes unappealing and why tirzepatide may be gentler on nausea. [verified, López-Cruz 2026, bioRxiv preprint, PMID 42453677, DOI; speculated link to aversions]
  • Practical risk: narrower diet raises iron deficiency risk. [verified, Infante 2026, Nutrients, PMID 42451041] Drug or weight loss? Drug (fullness, nausea learning, reward shift), not a taste change. [inferred] Verdict: Likely.

Card 13. Alcohol losing its appeal ("wine tastes wrong")

Fun fact: In a UK survey, the top reason people on GLP-1s drink less was "I just want it less", not "it tastes bad".

  • UK survey Feb 2026, 207 users: weekly units fell 16%, drinking days 29%; reasons: reduced desire 34%, feel full sooner 23%, feel drunk faster 22%, nausea 18%, less enjoyable 17%. Taste was not a listed reason. [reported, Drinkaware/KAM report]
  • Morgan Stanley survey, about 300 users: 56 to 62% of drinkers drank less, 14 to 18% stopped. [reported, Quartz; survey date unknown]
  • Reddit: 29.75% of alcohol comments described quitting. [verified, Arillotta 2024]
  • Rats: semaglutide, tirzepatide and retatrutide all blunted how alcohol "feels" inside (interoceptive effect). [verified, Windram 2025, Psychopharmacology, PMID 40699363, DOI]
  • Human RCT (n=48, low-dose semaglutide) cut drinks per drinking day and craving. [verified, Hendershot 2025, in GLP1 Research v1 and Addiction Research v1]
  • Mechanism: same NAc dopamine brake as Card 1; slower stomach emptying changes how fast alcohol hits. [verified/inferred] Drug or weight loss? Drug. Verdict: Likely (less wanting); Unproven (actual taste change).
