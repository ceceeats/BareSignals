0. Headline numbers for the page (quick pull)

  • Acne is not only a teen problem. In 1,013 adults surveyed, acne in the last period was reported by 50.9% of women vs 42.5% of men in their 20s, 35.2% vs 20.1% in their 30s, 26.3% vs 12.0% in their 40s [verified, Collier 2008, doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2007.06.045]. Self-reported, not examined.
  • About 1 in 4 "acne" patients also had fungal (Malassezia) folliculitis under the microscope: 28.8% of 320 people diagnosed with acne; 4.1% had fungal folliculitis only. Itch made it 7.4x more likely; scalp/hairline and upper back lesions ~9x [verified, doi:10.2147/CCID.S395654].
  • Milk: drinking 1 glass a day vs less than weekly, OR 1.41; 2+ glasses/day OR 1.43 (14 observational studies, 78,529 people aged 7 to 30) [verified, Juhl 2018, doi:10.3390/nu10081049]. Association, not proof; publication bias was found.
  • Spironolactone in adult women (SAFA, n=410): 82% vs 63% said their acne improved at 24 weeks; doctor-rated success at 12 weeks 19% vs 6% [verified, doi:10.1136/bmj-2022-074349].
  • Isotretinoin: in 19,907 US patients, 22.5% relapsed and 8.2% needed a second course [verified v2, Lai & Barbieri, JAMA Dermatol 2025, doi:10.1001/jamadermatol.2024.5416]. Relapse here means insurance-claims evidence of new acne treatment, with at least 1 year of follow-up.
  • Triple gel (clindamycin + adapalene + benzoyl peroxide, FDA approved Oct 2023): treatment success 49.6% vs 24.9% and 50.5% vs 20.5% vs vehicle in two phase 3 trials (n=183 and 180) [verified v2, Stein Gold 2023, doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2022.08.069].
  • Clascoterone (topical anti-androgen): IGA success at 12 weeks 23.5% vs 7.7% in females, 14.2% vs 5.2% in males [verified, post hoc of 2 phase 3 RCTs, doi:10.36849/JDD.9388].
  • GLP-1 users surveyed by dermatologists: 17 of 41 (41%; 95% CI 27 to 57%) with pre-existing acne said it improved [verified, Rao 2026, doi:10.1007/s13555-026-01882-z]. Small subgroup, self-report, no control group.
  • Peptides: no peptide has a published, controlled human trial with acne lesion counts as the outcome. The closest is omiganan (an antimicrobial peptide gel): 4 registered acne trials (about 977 people, 2000 to 2016), none with results posted or published [verified v2, registry records listed in 3.4].

1. What acne is (so readers can match their skin)

1.1 The four steps, in plain language

  1. Too much oil (sebum). Oil glands in the hair follicle get bigger and busier at puberty, driven by androgens (testosterone and its relatives) [reported, standard textbook model].
  2. The pore plugs. Skin cells lining the follicle shed abnormally and stick together, forming a microcomedone, the invisible start of every spot [reported].
  3. A skin bacterium thrives. Cutibacterium acnes (old name Propionibacterium acnes) lives on everyone. In acne, the mix of its strains narrows ("dysbiosis") rather than the bug simply being present [verified, review doi:10.3389/fimmu.2024.1502242].
  4. Inflammation. The immune system reacts inside and around the follicle. Skin's own antimicrobial peptides (LL-37, beta-defensins, dermcidin) are raised in acne skin, possibly as a compensating defence of a leaky barrier [verified, same review]. - Newer view: inflammation may start before the visible plug, not only after [reported].

1.2 Hormones, insulin and IGF-1

  • Androgens switch oil glands on. IGF-1 (the growth factor GH raises) and insulin push oil-gland fat production through PI3K/Akt/FoxO1/mTORC1 signalling [verified as the proposed model, doi:10.7759/cureus.116420].
  • Strongest human clue: people born unable to make IGF-1 (Laron syndrome) almost never get acne. Of 13 untreated patients only 1 had slight acne. When women were treated with IGF-1 at 70 to 200 µg/kg/day, 3 of 6 developed acne with signs of excess androgen, and it cleared in all 3 when the dose was cut to 50 µg/kg/day or stopped [verified, Ben-Amitai & Laron 2011, doi:10.1111/j.1468-3083.2010.03896.x]. Tiny study, but a clean on/off signal. This matters for GH peptides (section 3.8).

1.3 Diet: what the evidence actually says

  • Glycaemic load (fast carbs):
  • Korea, 32 people, 10 weeks, low-GL diet vs control: fewer inflamed and non-inflamed lesions, smaller oil glands, less IL-8 and SREBP-1 on biopsy [verified, Kwon 2012, doi:10.2340/00015555-1346].
  • Australia, 31 young men, 12 weeks: low-GL diet changed the fat make-up of sebum, and the change tracked lesion counts (r = -0.39) [verified, Smith 2008, doi:10.1016/j.jdermsci.2007.11.005].
  • A 2026 review calls diet a "modifier, not a uniform cause": trials are small and inconsistent; diet advice is reasonable as an add-on for selected patients [verified, doi:10.7759/cureus.116420].
  • Dairy: two 2018 meta-analyses agree on direction.
  • Any dairy OR 1.25, any milk OR 1.28, skim milk OR 1.32, yogurt 1.36, cheese 1.22 (borderline) [verified, Juhl, above].
  • Highest vs lowest: dairy OR 2.61, total milk 1.48, skim 1.82; yogurt and cheese not significant in this one [verified, Aghasi 2018, doi:10.1016/j.clnu.2018.04.015].
  • Skim milk is often as bad or worse than whole milk, suggesting hormones/whey rather than fat [inferred].
  • All observational. No dairy-elimination RCT with lesion counts found this pass [inferred; gap].
  • Chocolate: a small placebo-controlled cocoa trial (n=14) was terminated; no usable result [verified, NCT01193764].

1.4 Stress

  • 22 Stanford students: acne was worse during exams, and the worsening tracked perceived stress (r = 0.61) even after adjusting for sleep and diet [verified, Chiu 2003, doi:10.1001/archderm.139.7.897]. Small, but prospective.

1.6 Fungal acne lookalike

  • 28.8% of acne patients in a Thai clinic had Malassezia folliculitis on methylene-blue smear; itch OR 7.38; scalp/hairline OR 8.89; upper back OR 9.17 [verified, above].
  • 2026 Cutis review: dermatophyte infections (tinea incognito, Majocchi granuloma) also mimic acne, especially after steroid creams, which make them worse [verified, doi:10.12788/cutis.1417].
  • Why it matters for peptide users: oral antibiotics and steroid creams can feed fungal folliculitis; a "purge" that itches may not be acne at all [inferred].

1.7 Marks and scars

  • Up to 85% of people with darker skin (Fitzpatrick IV to VI) get brown marks after inflamed acne; marks can bother people more than active acne [verified, 2026 review, doi:10.1007/s40257-026-01044-z]. Relevant to Bali/SE Asian readers.
  • Risk factors: female, darker skin, severe acne, face, sun, picking [verified, same].
  • Treatments listed: azelaic acid, retinoids, antioxidants, hydroquinone, oral tranexamic acid, peels, lasers, microneedling, and sunscreen as non-negotiable [verified, same].

1.8 Southeast Asia note (v2)

  • Heat and humidity flare acne. In 171 acne patients in tropical Delhi, 40.4% said acne worsened in summer vs 6.4% in winter; monthly temperature and humidity tracked the flares [verified, Narang 2018, doi:10.1111/jocd.12777]. India, not SE Asia, but same climate band [inferred relevance].
  • Fungal folliculitis is common in hot, humid places. The 28.8% figure (1.6) comes from a Bangkok clinic [verified]. A 2026 review names hot, humid weather and occlusion (tight clothes, sweat) as risk factors [verified, doi:10.36849/JDD.9751]. In subtropical Shantou, China, Malassezia was the leading fungus (42%) and pityrosporum folliculitis the single most common diagnosis (17.6% of 2,635 culture-positive patients, 2022 to 2024) [verified, doi:10.62347/ELKP5340]. No Indonesian or Bali prevalence study found [gap].
  • Brown marks hit Asian skin hardest. In 324 acne patients across 7 Asian countries, 58.2% had post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation; in more than half it lasted at least 1 year and in 22.3% five years or more; picking was common and modifiable [verified, Asian Acne Board, doi:10.1111/1346-8138.13263]. In 419 Jakarta women, PIH prevalence was above about 50% at ages 20 to 30 [verified, doi:10.1111/srt.13189].
  • Scarring is frequent. 52.8% of acne cases among 6,225 young Chinese adults in Singapore and Malaysia had scars [verified, doi:10.1159/000547009]; 61.3% of 225 Thai acne patients had scars, with severe acne (OR 8.98), lingering red marks (OR 4.46) and picking as risk factors [verified, doi:10.1111/jocd.16695].
  • Laser in darker Asian skin: in Thai patients (mostly skin type 4 to 5), brown marks after fractional CO2 laser occurred in 42% of those not on isotretinoin vs 24% of those on it [verified, doi:10.1002/lsm.70056]. Small study.
  • Retinoids work in Asian skin but are underused for fear of irritation; Asian skin is described as more irritation-prone and more prone to sequelae [verified as stated, reviews doi:10.1111/1346-8138.14314, doi:10.1111/ijd.17141].
  • Page message for Bali readers: itchy uniform bumps in heat = think fungal first; protect against brown marks from day one (sunscreen, no picking, treat inflammation early) [inferred from verified data].

2. Standard treatments, with effect sizes

2.1 Guideline grades (AAD, Jan 2024)

Strength Treatment Tag
Strong Topical benzoyl peroxide, retinoids, antibiotics, fixed combinations [reported, Dermatology Times]
Strong Oral doxycycline [reported]
Strong Isotretinoin for severe, scarring, distressing, or treatment-resistant acne [reported]
Conditional Clascoterone, salicylic acid, azelaic acid, minocycline, sarecycline, combined pill, spironolactone [reported]
Conditional against Adding broadband light to adapalene 0.3% [reported]
Good practice Limit oral antibiotics; always pair them with benzoyl peroxide; steroid injection into big cysts [reported]

2.2 Topicals

  • Triple gel (clindamycin 1.2% / BPO 3.1% / adapalene 0.15%): phase 3 (two trials, n=183 and 180, ages 9+), success ~50% vs 20 to 25% on vehicle; inflammatory and non-inflammatory lesions down 72.7 to 80.1% vs 47.6 to 59.6% on vehicle [verified v2, corrected from v1, doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2022.08.069]. In 394 children and teens (phase 2 post hoc): success 55.8% vs 5.7%; beat each two-drug combo (30.8 to 33.9%) [verified, doi:10.1111/pde.15283]. Main side effects: application pain, dryness.
  • Clascoterone 1% cream (androgen blocker in the skin): see section 0 numbers; works in both sexes; adults responded more than teens; side effects mild local [verified, doi:10.36849/JDD.9388; review doi:10.3390/ph19081304].
  • Note vehicle (placebo cream) arms improve 35 to 60% on lesion counts by 12 weeks. Acne has a big placebo/natural-fluctuation effect. Any single "it cleared in 8 weeks" story needs this baseline [inferred from verified trial arms].

2.3 Antibiotics and resistance

  • Meta-analysis of 2,046 C. acnes isolates (2005 to 2025): resistance roxithromycin 48%, azithromycin 43%, erythromycin 29%, clindamycin 22%, vs doxycycline 2.4%, minocycline 0.2%; erythromycin/clindamycin resistance rising over time [verified, doi:10.3389/fmicb.2025.1565111].
  • Practical meaning: clindamycin alone on the skin is a resistance factory; benzoyl peroxide alongside prevents it [reported, guideline good practice].

2.4 Hormonal options

  • Spironolactone (SAFA trial, women ≥18, 50 mg/day to week 6 then 100 mg/day): benefit grows with time; quality-of-life gap 1.27 points at week 12 and 3.45 at week 24; more headaches (20% vs 12%); no serious reactions [verified, above]. Alternative to antibiotics.
  • PCOS: spironolactone did not beat metformin for hirsutism or testosterone in a meta-analysis of small open-label RCTs [verified, doi:10.1111/cen.70062]. Cochrane: evidence on metformin vs the pill for PCOS acne is low to very low quality [verified, doi:10.1002/14651858.CD005552.pub3].
  • Combined pill: conditional AAD recommendation [reported]. Effect sizes not re-read this pass [gap].

2.5 Isotretinoin

  • Standard dose 0.5 to 1.0 mg/kg/day to a cumulative 120 to 150 mg/kg [verified as stated in doi:10.1111/dth.15311].
  • Relapse: 22.5% overall in 19,907 patients (MarketScan claims 2017 to 2020, age 12+, at least 4 months of isotretinoin); higher cumulative dose = lower relapse (HR 0.996 per mg/kg); women relapsed more (HR 1.43); daily dose did not matter once the total reached 120 mg/kg [verified v2, doi:10.1001/jamadermatol.2024.5416].
  • Low dose (≤0.5 mg/kg/day): 4 RCTs (n=210), no difference in 24-week results or post-treatment worsening, better tolerated and higher satisfaction (SMD 0.99) [verified, doi:10.3389/fmed.2026.1771320]. An older meta-analysis found conventional dosing gave more lasting remission in adults [verified, Al Muqarrab 2022]. Evidence quality low in both.
  • Mental health: meta-analysis, 25 studies, 1.6 million people: no increased risk of psychiatric disorders overall (RR 1.08, CI 0.99 to 1.19); suicide attempts lower at 2 to 4 years (RR 0.86 at 3 yrs); 1-year depression absolute risk 3.83% [verified, doi:10.1001/jamadermatol.2023.4579]. Monitoring still advised.
  • Pregnancy rules: section 7.

2.6 Newer options (2025 to 2026)

  • Denifanstat (oral fatty-acid-synthase blocker, cuts oil-gland fat production): phase 2 (n=179), total lesions -61.3% (50 mg) vs -34.2% placebo at 12 weeks; IGA ≥2-grade not significant [verified, doi:10.1111/jdv.70119]. Phase 3 in China (n=480) met all endpoints, success "more than double" placebo [reported, press release Oct 2025]. China NDA accepted Dec 2025 [reported, company filing]. Approval status as of Nov 2026 not confirmed [gap].
  • Clascoterone and triple gel are the newest approved topicals in the US [reported].

2.7 Scar treatments

  • Lasers vs needling (27 studies, 1,137 people, 2026): fractional CO2 (and some Er:YAG) lasers often beat plain microneedling for atrophic acne scars; needling has less downtime and fewer pigment problems; radiofrequency microneedling gets close to laser [verified, doi:10.1111/phpp.70121].
  • Network meta-analysis (56 RCTs, 1,488 patients): combinations beat single treatments; CO2 laser + subcision and CO2 laser + PRP were most consistent [verified, doi:10.1007/s00266-026-06165-8].
  • A second 2026 network analysis found no reliable ranking exists; all intervals crossed zero [verified, doi:10.3389/fsurg.2026.1943995]. Honest message: several work, none clearly best.
  • Subcision (cutting the tethering bands under rolling scars): in a 60-patient pilot RCT, subcision + fat-derived stromal cells + PRP gave "marked" improvement in 53.3% vs 0% for subcision alone at 12 months [verified, doi:10.1007/s00266-026-05871-7]. Small, single centre.
  • Darker skin: favour needling/RF needling over aggressive ablative laser due to brown-mark risk [verified direction, phpp.70121].

3.1 GHK-Cu (copper peptide)

  • What it is: a 3-amino-acid copper peptide in plasma, saliva and urine that falls with age; stimulates both building and breakdown of collagen and glycosaminoglycans; attracts immune and blood-vessel cells to wounds [verified as stated in review, Pickart 2015, doi:10.1155/2015/648108]. Conflict: authors work at a company selling GHK skincare [verified affiliation].
  • Human skin data (topical, ageing): cosmetic trials report tighter skin, better elasticity and density, fewer fine lines, less sun damage and hyperpigmentation [reported in Pickart review]. One secondary summary lists a 12-week thigh study where 70% improved collagen production vs 50% on vitamin C and 40% on retinoic acid [reported, uncited secondary site; primary not re-opened, gap].
  • Scars: a 2026 systematic review (64 records) says the preclinical base is deep but no adequately powered human RCT of GHK-Cu with microneedling exists, and it lists atrophic acne scarring as a trial it recommends running [verified, doi:10.26502/aimr.0255].
  • Acne itself: no human acne trial found [verified absence across PubMed and ClinicalTrials.gov searches this pass].
  • Injected vs topical: all human data is topical. Two RealSelf doctors: a facial plastic surgeon says there is no rigorous clinical evidence for injectable GHK-Cu; a dermatologist says injectable efficacy is "less documented" [reported, RealSelf Q&A].
  • Social media check: a 2023 study of YouTube copper-peptide videos found many unsubstantiated dermatology claims [verified title/journal, letter, doi:10.1177/12034754231188560].
  • Breakouts: a US dermatology clinic page lists "breakouts in acne-prone individuals" as a possible side effect and says highly acne-prone skin may not be ideal [reported, Westlake Dermatology]. Copper + strong acids/vitamin C in the same routine is a common irritation complaint [reported].

3.2 KPV

  • KPV is the last 3 amino acids of α-MSH. It keeps almost all of α-MSH's anti-inflammatory action without tanning, acting on NF-κB and cytokines [verified, Brzoska 2010 review, doi:10.1007/978-1-4419-6354-3_8].
  • KPV-loaded hydrogels reduce inflammation and fight MRSA in wound models [verified as stated in 2025 review, doi:10.7150/ijms.118118].
  • Gut: an oral KPV conjugate reached the colon 3.8x better than free KPV and worked at 20x lower dose in mice with colitis [verified, doi:10.1126/sciadv.aea2989]. The "gut-skin" acne link is plausible but no KPV acne study exists [verified absence].
  • The top web result on "KPV for acne" is a seller's blog with no citations and no human data [reported, fetched].

3.3 BPC-157

  • Rat studies: heals incisional and excisional wounds, deep burns, diabetic ulcers and alkali burns; same dose range across routes [verified as stated in review by the originating Zagreb group, doi:10.3389/fphar.2021.627533].
  • No human skin trial, no acne data [verified absence]. Any acne claim is extrapolation [inferred].

3.4 LL-37 (cathelicidin): the double-edged peptide

  • Acne: LL-37 and other antimicrobial peptides are elevated in acne skin. Reviewers think this is a compensating defence for a leaky barrier, and that AMPs link immune and metabolic (insulin/IGF-1/mTOR) drivers [verified, doi:10.3389/fimmu.2024.1502242]. In mice injected with C. acnes, LL-37 rose with inflammation [verified, doi:10.5021/ad.2015.27.3.257].
  • Rosacea: people with rosacea have abnormally high cathelicidin and an enzyme (SCTE/KLK5) chops it into pro-inflammatory fragments; injecting those fragments into mouse skin caused rosacea-like redness and inflammation [verified, Yamasaki 2007, Nature Medicine, doi:10.1038/nm1616]. Rosacea is now framed as a disease of cathelicidin and innate immunity [verified, doi:10.1038/jidsymp.2011.4].
  • Page message: applying or injecting LL-37 to "kill acne bacteria" could inflame skin, and in anyone with rosacea tendency could worsen it [inferred from verified mechanism; no human LL-37 dosing study for acne found].
  • Related: omiganan, a synthetic antimicrobial peptide (from indolicidin), went through acne trials; an older review says trials "proved efficiency" [reported, doi:10.2174/157489106777452638], but the 2015-16 phase 2 (320 women) has no results posted 10 years on [verified].
  • v2 full trail: four acne trials are registered: NCT00211523 (2000, n=75, 6 weeks), NCT00211497 (2003, n=255, 12 weeks), NCT02066545 (2014, n=327, three strengths 1%, 1.75%, 2.5%), NCT02571998 (2015-16, n=320 women) [verified, ClinicalTrials.gov records: 00211523, 00211497, 02066545, 02571998]. None has results posted, and PubMed holds no paper reporting any of them (6 omiganan + acne/rosacea records, all reviews or lab work) [verified absence].
  • The "effective in acne" line in reviews traces back to company statements, not a published trial [inferred from the citation trail].
  • A 2003 trade-press "positive phase IIb acne" item concerned MBI 594AN, a different peptide from the same company, not omiganan (MBI 226) [reported, Manufacturing Chemist]. Easy to confuse; do not cite it for omiganan.
  • In rosacea, the 2007 phase 2 (n=240) missed its primary endpoint (31% vs 14% lesion reduction, not significant) [reported, company release]; one phase 3 rosacea trial has posted results on the registry but the page would not load [gap]. No omiganan product is approved anywhere [inferred from absence].
  • Unposted results after 4 acne trials usually mean a miss or a dropped programme [speculated].

3.5 Thymosin β4 (TB-500 parent molecule)

  • Topical Tβ4 sped repair in phase 2 trials of pressure ulcers, venous stasis ulcers and epidermolysis bullosa wounds; safe and well tolerated [verified as stated, Kleinman & Sosne 2016 review, doi:10.1016/bs.vh.2016.04.005]. Authors are linked to its development [reported].
  • No acne or acne-scar data. TB-500 (a fragment) is not the molecule in those trials [reported].

3.6 Matrixyl and cosmetic peptides

  • Palmitoyl pentapeptide (Matrixyl) and others have wrinkle data in cosmetic studies [reported]. PubMed search for acne trials returned nothing specific [verified absence this pass]. Mainly fine if non-comedogenic base; not an acne treatment [inferred].

3.7 Melanotan (I and II)

  • Afamelanotide (licensed melanotan I analogue) is listed as being explored for several skin conditions including acne vulgaris; listed harms from melanotan use include new and darkening moles, moles turning into melanoma, systemic toxicity, rhabdomyolysis, brain swelling syndrome (PRES), priapism and oral pigmentation; numerous reports to the Dutch pharmacovigilance centre [verified, de Baat 2020 abstract, doi:10.5177/ntvt.2020.04.19115].
  • A US derm clinic lists nausea, flushing, priapism, mole darkening and new pigmented spots that make melanoma harder to spot [reported, Westlake].
  • Acne as a side effect (v2 traced):
  • The Irish forum study (Gilhooley 2021, 205 UK/Irish users, 623 posts) lists side effects as a theme but the abstract names no acne figure; its concerns are moles, infection, contaminated product and sunbeds [verified, doi:10.1159/000514492]. Full text not reachable [gap].
  • A 2024 Cutis letter lists acne among the common melanotan II side effects (with flushing, stomach upset, sun sensitivity), no rate given [reported, Cutis PDF].
  • A 2017 review of unregulated melanotan centres on mole changes and 4 case reports of melanoma from existing moles; acne not mentioned in the abstract [verified, doi:10.1111/ijd.13585].
  • Bremelanotide (licensed relative, 3,500 trial subjects): top side effects nausea 40%, flushing 20%, headache 11%; focal dark patches in over a third after 16 daily doses; acne not in the reported list [verified, doi:10.1089/jwh.2021.0191].
  • The opposite signal: afamelanotide implants in 3 people with acne reduced lesions in all 3 at day 56 [verified, Böhm 2012, doi:10.1111/j.1468-3083.2012.04658.x].
  • Verdict: acne on melanotan II is plausible and commonly claimed, but no primary source gives a rate [reported]. Mechanism (MC5R on oil glands raising sebum) remains [reported].

3.8 GH peptides and IGF-1 (CJC-1295, ipamorelin, sermorelin, tesamorelin, MK-677)

  • Direct human signal: IGF-1 overdose caused acne in 3 of 6 women, which resolved on dose cut [verified, Laron study above].
  • MK-677 raises GH and IGF-1 to young-adult levels in older people [verified v2, Nass 2008].
  • v2 trial adverse-event check:
  • MK-677 2-year RCT (65 healthy adults aged 60 to 81, 25 mg/day as studied): IGF-1 up 1.5x; side effects reported were increased appetite 67% vs 36% placebo, mild swelling 44% vs 27%, muscle pain 33% vs 9%, small rises in fasting glucose and HbA1c; acne or oily skin not reported [verified, full text, doi:10.7326/0003-4819-149-9-200811040-00003].
  • MK-677 in 563 Alzheimer patients for 12 months (25 mg/day): IGF-1 up 60 to 73%, no benefit; abstract lists no acne [verified, doi:10.1212/01.wnl.0000335163.88054.e7]. Hip-fracture RCT (n=123) stopped early for a heart-failure signal [verified, doi:10.1016/j.archger.2010.10.004].
  • Tesamorelin FDA label (543 vs 263 patients, 26 weeks): rash 4% vs 2%, itch 2% vs 1%, injection-site reactions 17% vs 6%; acne not listed [verified, FDA label 2025].
  • CJC-1295, ipamorelin: no trial adverse-event table with skin outcomes found [gap].
  • Caveat: these trials enrolled older or ill adults, whose oil glands are far less active than a 20 to 35 year old gym user's; absence of acne there does not prove absence in young users [inferred].
  • Expect a mechanistic risk for oily skin and breakouts, especially stacked with testosterone [inferred]; the only proven human link is IGF-1 itself at high doses [verified].
  • Community first-hand reports outside vendor pages: none found this pass (all top results were vendor help-desk pages, excluded).

3.9 GLP-1 drugs (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide)

  • Better: in 1,226 GLP-1 programme patients (survey designed with dermatologists), improvement was reported in acne 17/41 (41%), hidradenitis 13/15 (87%), acanthosis nigricans 9/12 (75%), hirsutism 4/14 (29%). Same survey: skin sagging 72% and hair loss 52% when weight loss >20% [verified, doi:10.1007/s13555-026-01882-z]. Self-report, no control group.
  • Better, doctor-graded (v2): prospective single-arm cohort, Peshawar, 110 adults on semaglutide (titrated 0.25 to 1.0 or 2.0 mg/week as studied) for 24 months: acne grade (IGA) fell from 1.92 to 1.21, measured oil output fell 186 to 139 µg/cm²; improvement tracked falls in BMI, HbA1c and insulin [verified, full text, Jabin 2026, doi:10.7759/cureus.101212]. Quality flag: no control group, and the tables grade every participant for both acne and HS, which is unusual for a cohort enrolled with "acne, HS, or oily skin" [inferred]; treat as weak.
  • Better, clinic records (v2): 58 obese women with PCOS on semaglutide for a median 12 months, 11.4% weight loss: 33% of those with acne reported improvement [verified abstract, doi:10.1007/s11845-025-04194-x].
  • Worse: a JAAD research letter (Feb 2025) found non-diabetic obese women on GLP-1s were 53% more likely to get an acne diagnosis at 90 days and 1 year; no increase in men [reported, Medical Republic]. v2: primary confirmed as Cho et al., J Am Acad Dermatol 2025, doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2025.02.009 (semaglutide, liraglutide, tirzepatide vs obese non-users); a critique letter (Thang et al., doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2025.03.102) and the authors' reply (doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2025.04.085) exist [verified titles]. No abstract in PubMed; journal page blocked; database, n and CI still unopened [gap]. "53% more likely" is a relative figure; on a small baseline rate the absolute rise may be small [inferred].
  • Neutral, strongest design (v2): meta-analysis of 34 placebo-controlled RCTs, 84,243 people: GLP-1s raised hair-loss risk (RR 3.40) but no other skin outcome was significantly linked, acne included by implication; skin events were not pre-set endpoints and may be under-reported [reported, repository abstract, Yu et al., JAAD Reviews 2025, link].
  • Acne in the side-effect lists: a 2026 JAAD CME review lists acne among possible GLP-1 skin reactions with itch, drug eruptions and hair loss, without a rate [verified, doi:10.1016/j.jaad.2025.12.116].
  • Neutral: a 2024 review of 45 papers found no conclusive acne signal for any of 6 GLP-1s; authors suggest weight-loss hormonal shifts, not the drug, explain new acne [reported, PubMed 38586157].
  • Mechanism both ways: lower insulin and IGF-1 signalling should calm oil glands; rapid fat loss can shift sex hormones (fat converts androgens to oestrogen; less fat may unmask androgens) [inferred/speculated].
  • Hidradenitis (acne inversa), the nearest relative: observational data and a 2026 systematic review support GLP-1s as add-ons in HS with obesity; a Danish register found fewer surgeries after GLP-1 start but the same decline in matched non-users [verified, doi:10.1111/jdv.70769; doi:10.12788/cutis.1385; doi:10.1177/00368504261464855].
  • PCOS doctors: acne is not the main GLP-1 target; some women see mild improvement as metabolism improves [reported, reproductive endocrinologist in NewBeauty]. A Colorado PCOS trial's early read: weight loss itself, by drug or diet, is what helps [reported, CU Anschutz].

3.10 Larazotide

  • In keratinocyte cultures it reduced histamine-driven barrier leak (PAR2 blocker); proposed for eczema; skin penetration unknown [verified, doi:10.1016/j.xjidi.2025.100369]. Nothing on acne; gut-skin rationale only [inferred].

3.11 Collagen peptides (oral)

  • One 264-person Indian trial (2023) of four collagen types at 2.5, 5, 10 g/day listed acne severity as a secondary outcome in a subset; no results posted [verified, NCT05613660]. No acne evidence either way.

5. Pairings and related conditions

  • PCOS: hormonal-pattern acne plus cycle problems; androgens and insulin both drive it; spironolactone, the pill, metformin and GLP-1s are the usual levers [verified, 2.4, 3.9]. Cross-link to PCOS Research v2.
  • Insulin resistance: acanthosis nigricans and skin tags are skin signs dermatologists use to spot it [verified, doi:10.1177/12034754261467034].
  • Hidradenitis suppurativa ("acne inversa"): different disease, shares follicle-plugging and metabolic drivers; GLP-1 data strongest here [verified, 3.9].
  • Rosacea: overlaps in looks; LL-37 matters (3.4) [verified].
  • Common stacks people describe: topical GHK-Cu + microneedling; GHK-Cu + retinoid (separate times of day); GLP-1 + spironolactone for PCOS [reported from 4.1 and PCOS file]. "Glow" or "Klow" blends (GHK-Cu + BPC-157 + TB-500 ± KPV) appear only on vendor or spam pages; no independent reports [verified by search].
  • Things that worsen acne in peptide users: GH secretagogues/IGF-1 (verified signal), testosterone/anabolic stacks [reported], melanotan [reported], high-dairy protein shakes (whey) [reported; dairy association verified].

6. Options (ordered by evidence)

  1. Mild comedonal/inflammatory: adapalene or another retinoid + benzoyl peroxide; azelaic acid if marks or sensitive skin [verified guideline strength].
  2. Moderate: triple or double combination gel; add doxycycline short-term with benzoyl peroxide; women: spironolactone or pill [verified].
  3. Severe, scarring, or failed 2 rounds: isotretinoin; low-dose is an option with similar 24-week results [verified].
  4. Hormonal/PCOS: spironolactone, pill, clascoterone; address insulin (diet, metformin, GLP-1 if eligible) [verified/reported].
  5. Diet as an add-on: cut milk (especially skim) and high-glycaemic foods for 10 to 12 weeks; track lesion counts [verified small RCTs].
  6. Fungal lookalike: antifungal, not acne cream; get a smear if itchy [verified].
  7. Marks: sunscreen daily; azelaic acid, retinoid; tranexamic acid for stubborn brown marks [verified].
  8. Scars: subcision for rolling; laser or RF microneedling; combinations best [verified].
  9. Peptide angle, honestly: topical GHK-Cu as a texture/redness/scar-support add-on after microneedling has the most real-world support; test on a patch if acne-prone; keep away from same-night retinoid/acid use [reported/inferred]. No peptide should replace a retinoid or benzoyl peroxide for active acne [inferred from absence of data].

7. Fifteen usual questions (evidence answers)

  1. Does milk cause acne? Associated, not proven. Drinking milk daily raises odds about 40%; skim milk at least as strongly as whole [verified meta-analyses].
  2. Does sugar/white carbs cause acne? Two small RCTs found low-GL diets reduced lesions and oil-gland size in 10 to 12 weeks; reviews call diet a modifier [verified].
  3. Is my adult acne hormonal? Jawline/chin flares around periods in women 20s to 40s fit; 1 in 2 women in their 20s and 1 in 4 in their 40s report acne [verified prevalence].
  4. Could it be fungal? If itchy, uniform, on forehead/hairline/chest/back and not responding to acne creams, yes; 1 in 4 acne patients had fungal folliculitis in one study [verified].
  5. Does GHK-Cu clear acne? No trial says so. Reports favour scars, redness and texture; about 1 in 5 report breakouts [verified absence; reported counts].
  6. Is a GHK-Cu "purge" real? Purging is a retinoid concept (faster turnover). GHK-Cu does not clearly speed turnover; new breakouts in new places are more likely irritation or clogging [inferred].
  7. Is injected GHK-Cu better than topical for scars? No human data for injected; all published human data is topical [verified/reported].
  8. Will BPC-157 or TB-500 help acne? No acne data; animal wound-healing only [verified].
  9. What about KPV? Strong anti-inflammatory lab data, colitis in mice, nothing in human skin acne [verified].
  10. Can LL-37 kill acne bacteria for me? It's already raised in acne skin, and in rosacea its fragments cause inflammation; adding more is not supported [verified mechanism; inferred advice].
  11. Will Ozempic/Mounjaro fix my acne? About 4 in 10 GLP-1 users with acne reported improvement, a doctor-graded cohort improved over 2 years, and 1 in 3 PCOS women with acne improved; one claims cohort found more new acne diagnoses in women, and forum reports split evenly. Big trials show no clear acne signal either way [verified/reported].
  12. Do GH peptides cause acne? IGF-1 at high doses caused acne that went away on dose reduction; expect the same direction with GH secretagogues [verified/inferred].
  13. Does melanotan cause acne? Often claimed, but no study gives a rate and 23 user reviews never mention it; its licensed cousin actually reduced acne in a 3-person pilot. The real concern is moles and melanoma [reported/verified].
  14. Does isotretinoin cure acne permanently? In nearly 20,000 US patients, 22.5% needed acne treatment again and 8.2% a second course; women relapse more, and a higher total dose lowers relapse [verified v2].
  15. What works best for acne scars? Combinations: laser + subcision or PRP; needling is gentler for darker skin; no single best treatment proven [verified].

8. Warnings (bottom of page)

  • Isotretinoin and pregnancy: birth defects in 35% or more of exposed pregnancies; miscarriage up to 40% [verified, MotherToBaby]. US iPLEDGE: two forms of contraception from 1 month before, during, and 1 month after; 2 negative tests before starting and monthly tests [verified, same]. Wait 1 month after the last dose before trying to conceive [verified].
  • Spironolactone: not for pregnancy (anti-androgen effects on a male fetus) [reported]; headaches more common [verified].
  • Melanotan: mole changes, melanoma concern, priapism, PRES, rhabdomyolysis; unlicensed tanning products vary in dose and purity [verified/reported].
  • GH peptides/IGF-1: acne signal; also raise blood sugar (MK-677) [verified IGF-1 acne; MK-677 glucose verified in PCOS v2].
  • GLP-1s: skin sagging, hair loss and facial volume loss rise with weight lost (72% / 52% / 50% at >20% loss) [verified]; pregnancy washout per PCOS v2.
  • Unregulated peptides: research-labelled products are not made for human use; purity, dose and sterility are unverified; copper peptides in particular can arrive with free copper or wrong concentration [inferred].
  • Injecting vs topical: injection adds infection, abscess and lump risk, bypasses the skin barrier, and has no human acne or scar data for any peptide; topical has the only human skin data [inferred/verified].
  • Antibiotic resistance: never use clindamycin or erythromycin alone on skin [verified resistance data].
  • Steroid creams on "acne": can turn a fungal infection into a worse one [verified].
