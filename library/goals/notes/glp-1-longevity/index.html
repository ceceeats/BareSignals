GLP1 Claims Longevity v1

Hub research for baresignals.co: "GLP-1 beyond weight loss". Each claim investigated like a reporter would: what people say, what the studies show, and whether weight loss explains it. Compiled 3 Nov 2026. Version 1 (first pass). Companion to "GLP1 Research v1.md" (mechanism, FAQ, side effects); this file does not repeat those.

Evidence tags: [verified] = primary source abstract or registry record read this pass. [reported] = secondary source (news, review, trade press, company release). [inferred] = my reasoning from verified facts. [speculated] = plausible, no direct support. Verdict scale: Proven (randomised trial hard outcome, replicated or one very large trial) / Likely (RCT on a surrogate or symptom, or consistent large observational data plus mechanism) / Plausible (mechanism plus small or observational signals) / Unproven (anecdote, case reports, animal only) / Contradicted (good data says no or says harm). Rules kept: no dosing instructions (any dose named is the dose a trial used, for identification only); no grey-market supplier names; paraphrased; PubMed IDs and DOIs given so every number can be traced. Big caveat for the whole page [inferred]: almost every human trial below enrolled people with obesity and/or type 2 diabetes. "Works beyond weight loss" and "works in lean, healthy people" are two separate questions. The first has some real evidence. The second has almost none.

1. LONGEVITY CARD: "Why is it called a longevity drug?"

1.1 The claim as people say it

  • "Ozempic is the first real longevity drug." "GLP-1s are the new metformin." "It slows ageing, not just weight." "Microdose it for longevity even if you're lean." [reported, widespread in longevity media, Substack and podcasts; a 2026 explainer calls GLP-1s "the closest thing" to a gerotherapeutic and quotes Nature Biotechnology Nov 2025 and Nature Health 2026 framing (Jacob's Research Journal, 5 Apr 2026, https://jacobbrooke95.github.io/jacobs-research-journal/posts/2026-04-05-glp1-longevity.html)]
  • How common: one of the dominant longevity talking points of 2025 and 2026 [reported]. Reinsurers have joined in: Munich Re reported lower all-cause mortality in GLP-1 users; Swiss Re projected up to 6.4% lower US all-cause mortality by 2045 under optimistic uptake [reported, second-hand via the explainer above; original reinsurer reports not opened, see section 6].

1.2 Why the label exists: five separate lines of evidence

The "longevity drug" label is a stack of five different kinds of evidence. They are not equally strong, and the page should show them separately.

(a) People in big trials died less often (human RCT, strongest) - SELECT (semaglutide 2.4 mg weekly vs placebo; 17,604 adults ≥45, BMI ≥27, established heart disease, no diabetes; mean follow-up 39.8 months): heart attack, stroke or CV death 6.5% vs 8.0%, HR 0.80 (0.72–0.90) [verified, Lincoff NEJM 2023, PMID 37952131, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2307563]. - SELECT death analysis: 833 deaths. All-cause death HR 0.81 (0.71–0.93). CV death HR 0.85 (0.71–1.01, not significant on its own). Non-CV death HR 0.77 (0.62–0.95). Infection was the top non-CV cause and was lower on semaglutide (62 vs 87 deaths; HR 0.71). Semaglutide did not prevent catching COVID-19, but fewer died of it (43 vs 65; HR 0.66) [verified, JACC 2024, PMID 39217559, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jacc.2024.08.007]. - What it means [inferred]: roughly 1 in 5 fewer deaths, and a big share came from fewer deaths from infection, mostly during the pandemic. That is interesting (it hints at immune or resilience effects) but it is also a reason for caution: a pandemic-era infection signal may not repeat. - Important limit: all-cause death was not a confirmatory (pre-ranked, multiplicity-controlled) endpoint in SELECT [reported; consistent with how the JACC paper frames it as an analysis; protocol hierarchy not re-checked this pass]. - FLOW (semaglutide 1.0 mg, 3,533 adults with type 2 diabetes and chronic kidney disease, median 3.4 y): death from any cause 20% lower, HR 0.80 (0.67–0.95), as a confirmatory secondary outcome [verified, Perkovic NEJM 2024, PMID 38785209, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403347]. - SURPASS-CVOT (tirzepatide vs dulaglutide, an active GLP-1 drug, not placebo; 13,165 with T2D and heart disease, ~4 y): MACE HR 0.92 (0.83–1.01), non-inferior but not superior. All-cause death HR 0.84 (0.75–0.94) vs dulaglutide [reported, Healio 19 Sep 2025, https://www.healio.com/news/endocrinology/20250919/tirzepatide-similar-to-dulaglutide-for-cv-outcomes-but-tied-to-lower-rates-of-death; registry NCT04255433 verified as completed, n=13,299]. Because the comparator is itself a GLP-1 drug that lowers death in older trials, a 16% further reduction is notable [inferred]. - Earlier diabetes trials (LEADER liraglutide, SUSTAIN-6 semaglutide, REWIND dulaglutide) cut MACE 12–26% [reported, Eur J Clin Pharmacol 2026 review, PMID 42918905, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00228-026-04199-0]; SOUL (oral semaglutide) 14% MACE reduction [reported, same review].

(b) Benefit appears too early to be the weight (human RCT secondary analysis) - In SELECT, the MACE curves separated from about day 20 and became significant by day 86. In the first 6 months, 67 vs 113 MACE events (41% lower). Weight loss at 3 months was under 5%, and patients had not yet reached the full dose (that takes ≥16 weeks) [reported, TCTMD 14 May 2025, Plutzky at European Congress on Obesity, https://www.tctmd.com/news/select-semaglutides-impact-cvd-events-emerges-quickly]. - SELECT adiposity analysis: benefit was the same whatever the starting weight or waist. In the semaglutide arm, how much weight people lost by week 20 did not predict their later MACE risk. Waist shrinkage did, and an estimated 33% of the MACE benefit was mediated by waist reduction. The authors conclude the benefit was "independent of baseline adiposity and weight loss" with mechanisms beyond fat loss [verified, Lancet 2025, PMID 41138739, https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(25)01375-3]. - Plain reading [inferred]: about one third of the heart benefit tracks with belly fat going down; about two thirds does not track with weight at all. That is the strongest human evidence that GLP-1 drugs do something beyond weight. - Caution: mediation analyses within one arm are not randomised comparisons; people who lose more waist may differ in other ways [inferred].

(c) Inflammation falls fast (human RCT biomarker) - SELECT hsCRP: placebo-corrected drop of 37.8% at week 104. In 6,011 people with early bloods, CRP fell 12% at week 4 and 19% at week 8, when weight loss was only 1.4% and 2.8%. In people who lost under 2% of weight, CRP ratio was 0.88 on semaglutide vs 1.02 on placebo. Adjusting for CRP change moved the MACE HR from 0.80 to 0.86, meaning inflammation explains part of the benefit [reported, Consultant360 17 Sep 2026 summarising Plutzky et al., Circulation 154(11), https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.125.074482; primary paper not opened, see section 6]. - STEP-HFpEF: CRP −43.5% vs −7.3% [verified, Kosiborod NEJM 2023, PMID 37622681, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2306963]. SURMOUNT-OSA (tirzepatide) also lowered hsCRP [verified, PMID 38912654]. - Eloralintide (amylin) phase 1/2 lowered hsCRP and pulse rate [reported, Cardiology in Review 2026, PMID 42745233, https://doi.org/10.1097/CRD.0000000000001468].

(d) Ageing biomarkers and epigenetic clocks (small human studies, mixed) - Positive: post-hoc analysis of a 32-week double-blind RCT in HIV-associated lipohypertrophy (semaglutide n=45, placebo n=39). After adjustment for sex, BMI and inflammation markers: PCGrimAge −3.1 years, PhenoAge −4.9 years, DunedinPACE −0.09 (about 9% slower pace of ageing), OMICmAge −2.2 years; 11 organ-system clocks moved in the same direction, inflammation, brain and heart most; intrinsic capacity clock unchanged [verified as preprint, medRxiv Jul 2025, PMID 40791720, https://doi.org/10.1101/2025.07.09.25331038; NOT peer reviewed at time of indexing]. - Neutral/negative: SLIM LIVER single-arm study (41 people with HIV and fatty liver, semaglutide 24 weeks): median DunedinPACE +0.018, PCGrimAge +0.54 years, i.e. no average slowing. People whose DunedinPACE did fall (41.5%) had more liver-fat loss [verified, npj Aging 2026, PMID 42014432, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41514-026-00383-9]. - Context: a 2026 systematic list of 41 human studies on next-generation clocks puts semaglutide among interventions that lowered epigenetic age, alongside exercise, plant-rich diet, caloric restriction, omega-3 and others; rapamycin, NR and senolytics showed no detectable effect in that list [verified, Frontiers in Genetics 2026, PMID 42294499, https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2026.1836446]. - What this means [inferred]: the positive clock data comes from one small, post-hoc, HIV-specific trial, and the second HIV study did not replicate it on average. Clocks are investigational; moving a clock is not the same as living longer. Mixed is the honest word.

(e) Animal lifespan (mouse, newest and loudest) - Late-life semaglutide in female mice: treatment started at 20 months (roughly a 60-year-old human equivalent [speculated conversion]). Improved function, reduced ageing hallmarks, shifted nutrient sensors; continued treatment extended lifespan. Compared head-to-head with matched calorie restriction, semaglutide matched most CR benefits and beat CR on exploration, spatial memory and glucose control [verified, Nature 2026, PMID 42686906, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10940-7]. - Numbers: median lifespan 742 days (saline, n=39) vs 834 days (semaglutide, n=40), +92 days, about +12.4%. Food intake fell about 24%. Females only. Control lifespan was at the low end for this strain, so replication is needed. No calorie-restriction lifespan arm, so "better than CR for lifespan" cannot be claimed [reported, Lifespan.io summary, https://lifespan.io/late-life-glp-1-treatment-increases-lifespan-in-female-mice/; NIH press release 2 Sep 2026 says "nearly 100 days longer" median, https://nih.gov/news-events/news-releases/glp-1-treatment-late-life-extends-lifespan-animal-model]. Nature full text not reachable from this session (section 6). - Body-wide multi-omic "age-counteracting" effects in aged male mice (exenatide-type GLP-1RA from 11 months for 30 weeks, and from 18 months for 13 weeks), seen at a low dose that barely changed food intake or weight, specific to old not young mice, largely dependent on GLP-1 receptors in the hypothalamus, and molecularly similar to mTOR inhibition (the rapamycin pathway) [verified, Cell Metabolism Nov 2025, PMID 41265449, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2025.10.014]. This is the best evidence that the ageing signal is not just eating less. - Negative mirror in mice: repeated on/off liraglutide cycles in aged obese mice caused high leptin, visceral fat regain and lean mass that did not come back, worsening metabolic health vs never treating [verified, Aging Cell 2025, PMID 40960340, https://doi.org/10.1111/acel.70210]. Relevant to people who cycle on and off [inferred].

1.3 Comparison with metformin and rapamycin (the other two "longevity drugs")

- Fair one-liner [inferred]: GLP-1s have far more human outcome data than either rival, but that data is in sick people with excess weight; rapamycin has the best animal lifespan record; metformin has the weakest mouse lifespan record of the three.

1.4 Independent of weight loss?

  • Partly yes [inferred from verified sources]: SELECT benefit began before weight loss, did not track weight change, CRP fell early and in people who barely lost weight, and two thirds of the benefit was not mediated by waist. Mouse multi-omic effects appeared at a dose that barely moved weight.
  • Partly no: a third of SELECT benefit tracked waist; the Nature mice ate 24% less; most other benefits (liver, OSA, knee) scale with weight.
  • Non-obese people: no human mortality or ageing trial in lean healthy adults found. This is the single biggest gap for the "everyone should take it for longevity" framing [verified absence in searches this pass; see section 5].

1.5 Mechanism in plain language

  • The GLP-1 receptor sits on blood vessels, heart, immune cells, kidney and brain, not just the pancreas [reported, standard reviews]. Switching it on calms immune cells (less NF-κB, lower IL-6/TNF), lowers blood pressure a little, improves blood fats and sugar, and shrinks visceral and heart-surrounding fat [reported, PMID 42918905, 42930907]. In the brain, the hypothalamus GLP-1 receptor seems to act like a master switch that mimics parts of calorie restriction and mTOR inhibition across the body (mouse data, PMID 41265449) [verified for mice].
  • Analogy for the page [inferred]: weight loss is turning down the volume on the "too much fuel" alarm; the extra effect is like also turning down the background static (inflammation) that keeps damaging vessels.

1.6 Drug-class differences

  • GLP-1 only (semaglutide): the only class with a placebo-controlled all-cause death signal in people without diabetes (SELECT) [verified].
  • GIP/GLP-1 (tirzepatide): death 16% lower than dulaglutide in T2D (SURPASS-CVOT) [reported]; placebo-controlled morbidity/mortality trial in obesity SURMOUNT-MMO (NCT05556512, n=15,374, primary completion Oct 2027) [verified registry]. Epigenetic clock trial running (NCT07220473, n=90, ages 55–70, low dose 2.5 mg vs no drug, completion Nov 2027) [verified registry].
  • Triple (retatrutide): no outcome data yet; TRIUMPH-OUTCOMES (NCT06383390, n=10,000, ASCVD and/or CKD, primary completion Feb 2029) [verified registry]. Glucagon arm burns liver fat hardest (see liver card), which some reviewers argue matters more for healthspan than total kilos [reported, J Clin Med 2026, PMID 42590128, https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15156026].
  • Amylin (cagrilintide in CagriSema; eloralintide): no outcome data. REDEFINE 3 CagriSema CV trial (NCT05669755, n=7,101, completion Sep 2027) [verified registry]. Eloralintide phase 3 in T2D + high CV risk (NCT07900633, n=3,000, not yet recruiting, completion Nov 2029) [verified registry]. Eloralintide lowers pulse, while GLP-1 drugs raise it, which could matter long term [reported, PMID 42745233].

1.7 Verdict

Likely for "fewer deaths in people with obesity plus heart disease, or diabetes, partly beyond weight". Plausible for "slows human biological ageing". Unproven for "longevity drug for lean healthy people". The mouse data (Likely in female mice) explains why the label exists; the human data supports "lifesaving drug for sick people", which is not the same thing as "anti-ageing drug" [inferred]. Trials that will settle it: SURMOUNT-MMO NCT05556512 (2027); REDEFINE 3 NCT05669755 (2027); TRIUMPH-OUTCOMES NCT06383390 (2029); Moody Longevity Trial NCT07220473 (2027); tirzepatide organ clocks NCT07906041 (2028); tirzepatide epigenetic ageing in HIV NCT07707778 (2027); tirzepatide menopause plus biological ageing NCT07218445 (2027); semaglutide vs tirzepatide vs metformin "genetic ageing" NCT07293325 (2027) [all verified registry entries this pass].

1.8 Negative mirror for longevity

  • SELECT: 16.6% stopped semaglutide for side effects vs 8.2% placebo [verified, PMID 37952131].
  • SURPASS-CVOT missed superiority on MACE [reported].
  • EVOKE and EVOKE+ (oral semaglutide in early Alzheimer's, n=3,808): failed primary endpoints despite biomarker shifts; extension stopped [reported, NeurologyLive 24 Nov 2025, https://www.neurologylive.com/view/glp-1-semaglutide-fails-outperform-placebo-phase-3-evoke-trial-ad]. Brain ageing was a central pillar of the longevity pitch [inferred].
  • Lean mass: ~39% of weight lost in STEP 1 was lean mass [reported, via explainer; already covered in GLP1 Research v1 4.2]. In an older person, sarcopenia risk cuts against "healthspan" [inferred].
  • Pharmacovigilance: semaglutide reports carried higher adjusted odds of hospitalisation (aOR 2.80) and death (aOR 1.21) than tirzepatide reports in FAERS; this is reporting pattern, not risk [verified, Endocrine 2026, PMID 42955519, https://doi.org/10.1007/s12020-026-04840-z].
  • Industry funding: most outcome trials were sponsor-funded [verified from abstracts: SELECT, FLOW, SUMMIT, ESSENCE funded by manufacturers].

2. HEART CARD (beyond SELECT): heart failure and arrhythmia

2.1 Claim

"It fixed my breathlessness / my heart failure"; "My AFib episodes stopped." [reported; HFpEF symptom relief commonly reported in forums; AFib anecdotes less common]

2.2 Studies

  • STEP-HFpEF (semaglutide, n=529, BMI ≥30): symptom score KCCQ-CSS +16.6 vs +8.7 (difference 7.8), 6-minute walk +20.3 m difference, CRP −43.5% vs −7.3%; serious AEs 13.3% vs 26.7% [verified, PMID 37622681].
  • SUMMIT (tirzepatide, n=731, HFpEF, BMI ≥30, median 104 weeks): CV death or worsening HF 9.9% vs 15.3%, HR 0.62 (0.41–0.95); worsening HF events HR 0.54. CV death alone 8 vs 5 (HR 1.58, wide CI, too few events) [verified, Packer NEJM 2024, PMID 39555826, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2410027].
  • SURPASS-CVOT HF subgroup (exploratory): in people with HF, all-cause death or HF events HR 0.83 vs dulaglutide [verified, Eur J Prev Cardiol 2026, PMID 42875024, https://doi.org/10.1093/eurjpc/zwag452].
  • Atrial fibrillation: network meta-analysis of 94 RCTs (n=155,989): subcutaneous semaglutide RR 0.82 (0.68–0.99) vs placebo and RR 0.57 vs tirzepatide; dulaglutide associated with MORE AF [verified, Heart Rhythm 2026, PMID 42933519, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.hrthm.2026.10.052]. An umbrella review of 10 AF meta-analyses rated none above "low" confidence and found heavy overlap (same trials recycled) [verified, Cureus 2026, PMID 42668799, https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.113641]. Post-ablation recurrence data are non-randomised [verified, PMID 42930907].

2.3 Weight-independent?

HFpEF in obese people is largely an obesity disease; most reviewers treat benefit as mixed weight plus direct effects [reported, PMID 42930907]. Weight-independent antiarrhythmic effect is "unresolved" [verified wording, PMID 42930907].

2.4 Mechanism

Less fluid and fat pressing on the heart, lower blood pressure, less fat around the heart (epicardial fat) which feeds inflammation into the atria [reported, PMID 42930907].

2.5 Class differences

Tirzepatide has the only HF event-reduction RCT (SUMMIT). Semaglutide has the best AF signal; tirzepatide looked worse than semaglutide on AF in the network analysis (indirect comparison, low certainty) [verified, PMID 42933519]. GLP-1 drugs raise heart rate a few beats; eloralintide lowered it [reported]. Retatrutide also raises heart rate (flagged as needing evaluation) [verified, PMID 42942810].

2.6 Verdict

HFpEF events: Proven (tirzepatide). HFpEF symptoms: Proven (semaglutide). AF prevention: Plausible. HF with reduced EF: Unproven (trial NCT07738276, n=60, not yet recruiting) [verified registry].

3. KIDNEY CARD

3.1 Claim

"My kidney numbers improved." [reported, common among T2D users]

3.2 Studies

  • FLOW (above): major kidney events HR 0.76 (0.66–0.88); kidney-specific composite HR 0.79; eGFR decline slower by 1.16 mL/min/1.73m² per year; CV death HR 0.71 [verified, PMID 38785209].
  • 2025 meta-analysis of 11 trials, 85,000+: 16% lower kidney failure, 22% slower eGFR decline [reported, second-hand via explainer, original Lancet Diabetes Endocrinol paper not opened].
  • SURPASS-CVOT: tirzepatide better eGFR trajectory than dulaglutide [reported, Healio].

3.3 Weight-independent?

FLOW enrolled diabetics; effect on albuminuria appears partly independent of glucose and weight in mechanistic work [reported, general review literature; not traced to a mediation analysis this pass]. Gap.

3.4 Mechanism

Lower pressure inside kidney filters, less inflammation and scarring, better sugar and blood pressure [reported].

3.5 Class

Semaglutide proven (FLOW). Tirzepatide and retatrutide outcome trials pending (retatrutide renal impairment trial NCT06297603; TRIUMPH-OUTCOMES includes kidney events) [verified registry].

3.6 Verdict

Proven for T2D plus CKD. Unproven for CKD without diabetes (outcome data not found this pass).

4. LIVER CARD (MASH, formerly NASH)

4.1 Claim

"My fatty liver is gone." [reported, very common]

4.2 Studies

  • ESSENCE (semaglutide 2.4 mg, biopsy F2–F3, first 800 of 1,197 at week 72): MASH resolution without worse fibrosis 62.9% vs 34.3%; fibrosis improvement 36.8% vs 22.4%; weight −10.5% vs −2.0% [verified, Sanyal NEJM 2025, PMID 40305708, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2413258].
  • SYNERGY-NASH (tirzepatide phase 2, n=190): MASH resolution 44–62% vs 10%; fibrosis improvement 51–55% vs 30% [verified, Loomba NEJM 2024, PMID 38856224, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2401943].
  • Retatrutide MASLD substudy (n=98): liver fat −81% to −82% at higher doses at 24 weeks; 79–86% reached normal liver fat; changes "significantly related" to weight and abdominal fat loss [verified, Sanyal Nat Med 2024, PMID 38858523, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-024-03018-2].
  • Network meta-analysis (13 RCTs, n=3,871): survodutide and tirzepatide ranked top for MASH resolution [verified, J Transl Med 2026, PMID 42192439, https://doi.org/10.1186/s12967-026-08219-6].

4.3 Weight-independent?

Mostly no. Retatrutide paper explicitly ties liver fat loss to weight and belly fat loss [verified]. The glucagon component adds direct liver fat burning, so for equal weight loss glucagon-containing drugs clear more liver fat [reported, PMID 42590128, 42947668].

4.4 Verdict

Proven (semaglutide, biopsy, phase 3). Likely (tirzepatide phase 2, retatrutide imaging). Long-term outcomes (cirrhosis, death) still pending in ESSENCE part 2 (240 weeks) [verified design, PMID 40305708].

5. SLEEP APNOEA CARD

5.1 Claim

"I stopped snoring / ditched the CPAP." [reported, common]

5.2 Studies

  • SURMOUNT-OSA (tirzepatide, two 52-week RCTs): AHI −25.3 vs −5.3 events/h (no CPAP) and −29.3 vs −5.5 (on CPAP); hsCRP and blood pressure also fell [verified, Malhotra NEJM 2024, PMID 38912654, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2404881].
  • TRIUMPH-1-OSA and TRIUMPH-2-OSA (retatrutide, 80 weeks, n=243 and 448): AHI difference vs placebo −21.9 to −24.4 (no T2D) and −14.4 to −15.1 (T2D) at 9–12 mg; weight −12.5% to −22.2% [verified, Grunstein NEJM 2026, PMID 42904878, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2611477].

5.3 Weight-independent?

Mostly weight-driven (less fat around the airway) [reported, PMID 42942139]. No study found separating it.

5.4 Verdict

Proven (tirzepatide approved indication; retatrutide phase 3). Semaglutide: no dedicated OSA phase 3 found this pass.

6. KNEE OSTEOARTHRITIS AND JOINT PAIN CARD

6.1 Claim

"My knees stopped hurting within weeks"; "GLP-1 is anti-inflammatory for joints." [reported, very common in forums; people often notice joint relief early]

6.2 Studies

  • STEP 9 (semaglutide 2.4 mg, n=407, BMI ≥30, moderate knee OA, 68 weeks; 81.6% women): weight −13.7% vs −3.2%; WOMAC pain −41.7 vs −27.5 points (0–100 scale); SF-36 physical function +12.0 vs +6.5; AE discontinuation 6.7% vs 3.0% [verified, Bliddal NEJM 2024, PMID 39476339, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2403664].
  • TRIUMPH-1 knee OA subgroup (retatrutide, n=574 with knee OA, 80 weeks): WOMAC pain (1–10 scale) difference −1.6 to −1.8 points (hybrid), −1.4 to −1.6 (ITT) [verified, Jastreboff NEJM 2026, PMID 42814954, https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2604169].
  • ESSENCE MASH trial: bodily pain scores did NOT differ despite 8.5-point weight difference [verified, PMID 40305708]. Useful negative mirror.
  • Pain review: GLP-1 drugs "are not established analgesics"; liraglutide produced extra weight loss without better knee pain in one trial; signals are best seen as indirect [verified, J Clin Med 2026, PMID 42590035, https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15155932].

6.3 Weight-independent?

No study separated weight from pain in STEP 9 (searched; no mediation paper found) [verified absence]. Placebo arm improved 27.5 points too (counselling, diet, regression to mean) [inferred]. Early-weeks relief people describe has no trial data behind it [inferred].

6.4 Mechanism

Less load on the joint (each kg off the body takes several kg off the knee per step [reported, biomechanics literature, not re-checked]), plus possibly lower inflammatory signalling and spinal-cord pain pathways in animals [verified as preclinical only, PMID 42590035].

6.5 Verdict

Proven for pain relief in obese knee OA. Unproven for cartilage protection (CARTIZ registry NCT07567378 will image cartilage on tirzepatide; observational, n=30) [verified registry] and for joint pain in non-obese people.

7. PSORIASIS, PSORIATIC ARTHRITIS AND AUTOIMMUNE CARD

7.1 Claim

"My psoriasis cleared on Ozempic"; "GLP-1 calms autoimmunity." [reported; forums and patient sites carry many stories, e.g. MyPsoriasisTeam, r/PsoriaticArthritis "Ozempic success for arthritis" thread Dec 2024; community data only shows that people notice it]

7.2 Studies

  • Small RCT: semaglutide in 31 obese T2D psoriasis patients on metformin, 12 weeks: median PASI 21 → 10 [verified, Biomolecules 2025, PMID 39858442, https://doi.org/10.3390/biom15010046]. Open-label, tiny.
  • National Psoriasis Foundation primer: PASI reductions of roughly 40–80% reported, but studies tiny (7–48 patients), ≤6 months, mostly uncontrolled [verified, JAMA Dermatol 2026, PMID 42054048, https://doi.org/10.1001/jamadermatol.2026.0859].
  • Systematic review of 26 studies: semaglutide and liraglutide most consistent; most participants had obesity or T2D [verified, J Clin Med 2026, PMID 42452586, https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm15135126].
  • TOGETHER-PsO (n=274, ixekizumab plus tirzepatide vs ixekizumab alone, 36 weeks, open-label): PASI 100 plus ≥10% weight loss 27.1% vs 5.8%; PASI 100 alone 40.6% vs 29.0% [reported, Healio 15 May 2026, https://www.healio.com/news/dermatology/20260515/taltz-zepbound-combo-spurs-dramatic-improvements-in-psoriasis-clearance-body-weight; registry NCT06588283 verified completed, n=281]. Editorialists say it is unknown how much came from direct anti-inflammatory effects, weight, or better biologic drug levels at lower body weight [reported].
  • TOGETHER-PsA (n=271): ACR50 33.5% vs 20.4% (P=0.02), with separation as early as week 4 [verified, Merola Arthritis Rheumatol 2026, PMID 41903163, https://doi.org/10.1002/art.70134].

7.3 Weight-independent?

The week-4 ACR50 separation in TOGETHER-PsA, before meaningful weight loss, is the best human hint of a direct effect [inferred from verified]. Mechanism studies show GLP-1 receptors on iNKT cells in psoriatic plaques [reported, PMID 40835981]. Also: biologic drugs work worse in heavier people, so weight loss alone could boost the biologic [reported, PMID 41627722]. Open-label design limits all of this.

7.4 Class

Tirzepatide has the only phase 3 data (as add-on). Semaglutide placebo-controlled trials running: NCT07401992 (oral, triple-blind, n=62, 2027), NCT06937060 (n=14) [verified registry]. No data for retatrutide or amylin drugs.

7.5 Verdict

Likely as an add-on in people with obesity. Unproven as monotherapy or in lean people. Other autoimmune diseases (lupus, RA, MS): not investigated this pass (gap).

8. ECZEMA (ATOPIC DERMATITIS) CARD

  • Claim: "My eczema got better." [reported, occasional, much rarer than psoriasis stories; not quantified]
  • Studies: the only cohort found looked at heart outcomes, not skin: 24,936 AD patients on GLP-1 drugs had lower odds of CV disease (aOR 0.72) and HF over 20 years [verified, Dermatitis 2026, PMID 41649183, https://doi.org/10.1177/17103568251410211]. JAAD 2026 review lists atopic dermatitis among keywords but cites no outcome trial [verified, PMID 41698604].
  • Weight-independent: no data. Mechanism: eczema is mainly a type 2 (allergic) immune disease, unlike psoriasis (Th17), so the psoriasis logic may not transfer [inferred].
  • Verdict: Unproven. No registered skin-outcome trial found.

9. ACNE AND SKIN CARD

  • Claim: "My hormonal acne cleared" (often women with PCOS) vs "Ozempic face, saggy skin" [reported].
  • Studies: no acne RCT found. JAAD 2026 review lists acne among reported cutaneous adverse events [verified, PMID 41698604, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jaad.2025.12.116]. Improvement in PCOS acne is plausible via lower insulin and androgens [inferred; see PCOS Research v1]. Skin laxity, striae and facial volume loss follow fast big weight loss [verified, Clin Dermatol 2026, PMID 42863768, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.clindermatol.2026.09.041]. Twins facial-ageing imaging study registered (NCT07760532) [verified registry].
  • Verdict: Unproven for acne benefit; skin ageing appearance from rapid weight loss is a real reported downside.

10. HAIR LOSS CARD

  • Claim (two directions): "It grew my hair back" (rare) vs "my hair is falling out" (common) [reported].
  • Studies: RCT meta-analysis (31 trials): alopecia 191/11,564 semaglutide vs 36/9,799 comparators, OR 2.92 (1.99–4.27), low certainty [verified, Cureus 2026, PMID 42966951, https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.117270]. Review: 3–7% in trials vs up to 70%+ in some real-world cohorts; mostly telogen effluvium (stress shedding) driven by speed and size of weight loss; tirzepatide associated with more shedding; female sex and >10% weight loss the strongest predictors; usually reversible; paradoxical improvement reports in androgenetic and scarring alopecia [verified, Acta Diabetol 2026, PMID 42873315, https://doi.org/10.1007/s00592-026-02812-3]. FAERS: alopecia reported relatively more with tirzepatide [verified, PMID 42955519].
  • Weight-independent: shedding looks weight-speed-driven [reported].
  • Trial: tirzepatide for central centrifugal cicatricial alopecia (NCT07734870, n=20, Johns Hopkins, 2028) [verified registry].
  • Verdict: Contradicted for "regrows hair" as a general claim; hair loss is a side effect. Unproven for scarring alopecia benefit.

11. WOUND HEALING CARD

11.1 Claim

"My diabetic ulcer finally healed"; "Heals you faster after surgery." [reported]

11.2 Studies

  • Diabetic foot: TriNetX (semaglutide users n=6,329 vs 118,821 non-users): lower 1-year rates of wound complications (0.19% vs 0.38%), non-healing wounds (0.75% vs 1.23%), dehiscence, amputation [verified, Diab Vasc Dis Res 2025, PMID 40080656, https://doi.org/10.1177/14791641251322909]. Matched only on age, sex, race, ethnicity, so confounding is heavy [inferred]. Danish national cohort (n=74,475) compared GLP-1 and SGLT2 vs sulfonylureas for amputation [verified design, Cardiovasc Diabetol 2023, PMID 37386427; result numbers not in abstract read]. 2026 review: favourable associations, "not uniform", mostly observational, no consistent advantage over other drug classes [verified, Clin Dermatol 2026, PMID 42951118].
  • Surgery (mixed): cervical spine (T2D): lower infection OR 0.72, revision OR 0.33 [verified, Spine J 2026, PMID 42906643]. Lumbar fusion meta (7 cohorts, n=28,525): no significant difference in infection, wounds or revision [verified, Int J Spine Surg 2026, PMID 42754392]. Hip replacement vs bariatric surgery: fewer wound complications [verified, HSS J 2026, PMID 42856761].
  • Harm signal: body contouring after massive weight loss (Denmark, n=282): GLP-1 exposure linked to infection OR 3.72 and wound dehiscence needing general anaesthesia OR 16.7 (very wide CI 1.99–139.9) vs lifestyle weight loss [verified, JPRAS 2026, PMID 42874170, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bjps.2026.09.040]. Possible reasons: low protein intake and lean mass loss [reported, PMID 42951118].

11.3 Weight-independent?

Unknown. Animal and cell work shows GLP-1 signalling helps keratinocyte migration, new blood vessels and macrophage switching to "repair mode" [verified as preclinical, J Invest Dermatol 2025, PMID 41081666].

11.4 Verdict

Plausible for diabetic foot. Mixed / possible harm for plastic surgery skin flaps. No RCT on wound healing found.

12. CANCER CARD

12.1 Claim

"It lowers cancer risk" vs "It causes thyroid cancer." [reported, both widespread]

12.2 Studies

  • Meta-analysis of 78 observational studies: lower risk for 10 of 13 obesity-associated cancers (colorectal, endometrial, oesophageal, gallbladder, liver, ovarian, pancreatic, stomach, meningioma, myeloma); vs insulin: colorectal RR 0.54, liver 0.35, pancreas 0.41; thyroid RR 1.09 (0.98–1.21, not significant) [verified, Cancer Control 2026, PMID 42677883, https://doi.org/10.1177/10732748261483767].
  • Breast cancer survivors with BMI ≥35 (TriNetX): 10-year survival 79% vs 71%, HR 0.54; called hypothesis-generating [verified, J Surg Oncol 2026, PMID 42813380]. Effect size this large in an observational study suggests healthy-user bias [inferred].
  • Thyroid: Scandinavian cohort (145,410 GLP-1 users vs 291,667 DPP-4 users, mean 3.9 y): HR 0.93 (0.66–1.31) [reported, CancerNetwork, BMJ 2024]. A French case-control study (Bezin, Diabetes Care 2023) reported raised thyroid cancer risk with 1–3 years of use [reported from memory of literature; NOT re-verified this pass, flagged]. Medullary thyroid cancer warning rests on rodent C-cell tumours; humans have far fewer GLP-1 receptors on C-cells [reported, PMID 42906947].

12.3 Weight-independent?

Unknown. Reviews say benefit "likely" works by interrupting obesity-driven metabolic dysregulation, i.e. largely weight and insulin [verified wording, Obes Rev 2026, PMID 42906947]. No RCT with cancer as a primary endpoint.

12.4 Verdict

Plausible (lower obesity cancers). Thyroid harm: Unproven in humans, still flagged as a label warning.

13. GUT: IBS AND IBD CARD

  • Claim: "My IBS bloating got better" vs "It wrecked my gut." [reported, both]
  • IBS: TriNetX (6,665 per group, 90-day landmark): lower coded chronic diarrhoea (8.9% vs 10.6%), constipation (19.8% vs 22.0%), pain (31.6% vs 35.8%), bloating (8.3% vs 10.9%) [verified, Dig Dis Sci 2026, PMID 42570075, https://doi.org/10.1007/s10620-026-10171-w]. Negative mirror: in 256 IBS patients, symptom-driven discontinuation rose from 28% on the first GLP-1 to 48% on the third; semaglutide stopped within 6 months more than liraglutide (63.4% vs 43%) [verified, Ann Gastroenterol 2025, PMID 40697433]. A GLP-1 analogue specifically for IBS pain (ROSE-010) had a disease-specific signal [verified mention, PMID 42590035].
  • IBD: tirzepatide vs GLP-1RA (3,042 per group): less IV steroid use (aHR 0.81) [verified, Intest Res 2026, PMID 42717572].
  • Verdict: Unproven (IBS, coded symptoms, observational, and GI side effects are the most common reason to quit). Plausible (IBD).

14. GOUT CARD

  • Claim: "My gout flares stopped." [reported, occasional]
  • Studies: ACR 2025 poster (Mass General Brigham, n=22 with gout starting semaglutide or tirzepatide): each kg lost lowered urate 0.13 mg/dL; >10% weight loss gave 2.36 mg/dL bigger fall than <5% [reported, Medical Republic 3 Nov 2025, https://www.medicalrepublic.com.au/weight-loss-drugs-show-strong-potential/121114]. Target-trial emulations use GLP-1 drugs as the comparator to SGLT2 inhibitors, and SGLT2 drugs win on gout [verified, Diabetes Care 2026, PMID 41615420; JAMA Netw Open 2023, PMID 37624597]. Semaglutide skeletal TTE: gout results "inconsistent" [verified, PMID 42010367].
  • Weight-independent: no, the urate drop tracks weight [reported].
  • Verdict: Plausible via weight loss; SGLT2 inhibitors have better gout data.

15. ASTHMA CARD

  • Claim: "I need my inhaler less." [reported, occasional]
  • Studies: UK CPRD (>2 million adults with asthma screened; SCCS n=4,278): GLP-1RA added to metformin: IRR 0.60 (0.49–0.73) for asthma attacks; authors say it appeared to work through mechanisms other than glucose or weight, across asthma types [verified, JAMA Intern Med 2025, PMID 39556360, https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2024.5982]. COPD: GLP-1 users had fewer exacerbations than DPP-4i or sulfonylurea users [verified, AJRCCM 2023, PMID 37647574]. Review: positive signal in obese and non-obese, RCTs needed [verified, Curr Opin Pulm Med 2026, PMID 41664500].
  • Trial: GATA-3 (semaglutide 2.4 mg vs placebo, obesity-related asthma, n=100, Vanderbilt; primary completion Sep 2026, results not yet found) [verified registry NCT05254314].
  • Verdict: Plausible, one of the few claims with an observational "beyond weight" signal. GATA-3 results are the next thing to watch.

16. Negative mirror across all claims

  • Discontinuation for side effects is about double placebo in big trials (SELECT 16.6% vs 8.2%; STEP 9 6.7% vs 3.0%; SUMMIT 6.3% vs 1.4%) [verified].
  • Failed or neutral: EVOKE/EVOKE+ (Alzheimer's) [reported]; SURPASS-CVOT superiority missed [reported]; SLIM LIVER clocks unchanged on average [verified]; ESSENCE pain unchanged [verified]; liraglutide extra weight loss without extra knee benefit [verified via review]; lumbar fusion no benefit [verified].
  • Harm signals: hair loss OR 2.92 [verified]; body contouring wound dehiscence [verified]; on/off cycling worsens body composition in mice [verified]; lean mass loss [reported]; GI events, pancreatitis, gallbladder, gastric-emptying and NAION reporting signals (FAERS; reporting not causation) [verified, PMID 42955519; safety review PMID 42905300 calls most newer concerns "hypothesis-generating"].
  • Bias pattern [inferred]: almost every positive "beyond weight loss" claim outside heart, kidney, liver and OSA rests on TriNetX-style electronic record studies. These share a known weakness: people who get prescribed and keep taking expensive drugs are healthier and better-insured (healthy-user bias).
