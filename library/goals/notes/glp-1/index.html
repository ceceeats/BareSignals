1. Plain-language mechanism, step by step

1.1 The hormone your gut already makes

  • GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide-1) is a hormone released by "L-cells" in the lining of the small intestine and colon when food arrives. It tells the body "a meal is here" [verified, Drucker 2018 Cell Metab review abstract, PMID 29617641, doi:10.1016/j.cmet.2018.03.001].
  • Natural GLP-1 lasts only about 2 minutes in blood because an enzyme called DPP-4 chops it up, and the kidneys clear it [reported, standard physiology; widely cited in reviews; exact figure not re-checked this pass].

1.2 The incretin effect (the "why does eating sugar release more insulin than injecting sugar?" fact)

  • If you swallow glucose, your pancreas releases far more insulin than if the same glucose is dripped into a vein. The difference is caused by gut hormones called incretins: GLP-1 and GIP [reported, classic physiology]. Commonly cited size: incretins account for roughly 50 to 70% of the insulin response after a meal [reported; figure from textbook/review literature, not re-verified this pass].

1.3 At the pancreas

  • GLP-1 makes beta cells release more insulin, but only when blood sugar is raised ("glucose-dependent"). This is why GLP-1 drugs alone rarely cause dangerous lows [verified, Drucker 2018 abstract describes insulin augmentation and glucagon inhibition; hypoglycaemia point reported].
  • It also tells alpha cells to release less glucagon (glucagon is the hormone that pushes sugar out of the liver) [verified, Drucker 2018].

1.4 At the stomach

  • GLP-1 slows how fast the stomach empties into the gut [verified, Drucker 2018]. Food sits longer, you feel full longer, and blood sugar rises more slowly after meals.
  • This same effect explains nausea, the anaesthesia concern, and the pill-contraceptive interaction with tirzepatide (Sections 4.10, 4.13) [inferred from label mechanism statements].
  • The stomach-slowing effect is strongest early and partly fades with continued dosing (tachyphylaxis). Lilly states the oral drug absorption effect of tirzepatide is greatest after the first 5 mg dose and lessens later [verified, Lilly Medical Information page, 2026].

1.5 At the brain (the main reason for weight loss)

  • GLP-1 receptors sit in two key brain areas [reported, Drucker 2018 and broader review literature]:
  • Hindbrain (brainstem): the area postrema and nucleus of the solitary tract. These areas sit near "leaky" parts of the blood-brain barrier, so the drug can reach them. They signal fullness and, when over-stimulated, nausea.
  • Hypothalamus: the appetite control centre. GLP-1 signalling turns up "I'm full" neurons (POMC) and turns down "I'm hungry" neurons (NPY/AgRP).
  • Reward circuits (mesolimbic dopamine areas): GLP-1 signalling appears to dampen the "want more" pull of food, alcohol and possibly nicotine. This is the basis of the "food noise" and alcohol-craving reports [reported; human trial evidence is early, see 4.8].
  • Net effect: people eat less, mostly by feeling full sooner and thinking about food less. Weight loss comes mainly from eating less, not from burning more [reported; consistent across reviews].

1.6 Why one shot lasts a week (the engineering trick)

  • Semaglutide: two tweaks to the natural GLP-1 chain. (1) An unusual amino acid (Aib) at position 8 blocks DPP-4 from cutting it. (2) A fatty acid "tail" attached at position 26 grips albumin, the most common blood protein, so the drug rides around protected and is released slowly. In mini-pigs half-life was 46 h IV [verified, Lau 2015 J Med Chem, PMID 26308095, doi:10.1021/acs.jmedchem.5b00726]. In humans the half-life is about 1 week (~165 h) [reported, Wegovy label figure; label not re-opened this pass]. [v2 fix] Label: "approximately 1 week" [verified, Wegovy PI via Drugs.com; EU tablet SmPC via fachinfo.de]; phase 1b measured 145 to 165 h at 2.4 mg [verified, Enebo Lancet 2021, PMID 33894838, doi:10.1016/S0140-6736(21)00845-X]. Oral semaglutide absolute bioavailability is only ~1 to 2% [verified, EU SmPC].
  • Liraglutide: same albumin idea but a weaker grip, so half-life ~13 h and it must be injected daily [reported]. [v2 fix] Label: "approximately 13 hours" [verified, Saxenda PI via Drugs.com].
  • Tirzepatide: built on the GIP backbone with a C20 fatty diacid tail; half-life ~5 days [reported, label figure not re-opened]. [v2 fix] Label: "approximately 5-6 days in patients with overweight or obesity" [verified, Zepbound PI via Drugs.com]; Lilly says it is cleared ~30 days after the last dose [verified, Lilly Medical Information].
  • Orforglipron (v2 add): label half-life 29 to 49 h, peak at 4 to 8 h, no meaningful food effect [verified, Foundayo PI, uspl.lilly.com].
  • Cagrilintide (v2 add): half-life 159 to 195 h in phase 1b [verified, PMID 33894838].
  • Retatrutide: lipidated 39-amino-acid peptide, once weekly [verified, Mateescu 2026 Pharmaceutics review, PMID 42942810].
  • Orforglipron: not a peptide at all. A small chemical molecule that fits the GLP-1 receptor, survives the stomach, and is taken daily with no food or water rules [verified, ATTAIN-1 NEJM 2025 abstract; Lilly Medical Info dosing page]. It is a "biased partial agonist", which a 2026 review flags as the reason its heart-outcome question is still open [verified, Frontiers Pharmacol 2026, PMID 42729683].
  • Analogy for the page: natural GLP-1 is a paper note that dissolves in 2 minutes. The drugs are the same note laminated (DPP-4 resistance) and clipped to a bus that circles the body all week (albumin binding) [inferred, explanatory device].

1.7 GLP-1 vs GIP vs glucagon vs amylin (mono, dual, triple)

Hormone Made by What it does (simple) Drugs that hit it
GLP-1 gut L-cells more insulin when sugar is high, less glucagon, slower stomach, fullness in brain all of the drugs on this page
GIP gut K-cells (upper gut) the other incretin; boosts insulin; acts on fat tissue and the brain; may reduce nausea from GLP-1 [reported, mechanism still debated] tirzepatide, retatrutide
Glucagon pancreas alpha cells raises blood sugar, but also increases energy use and burns liver fat [reported, Sergi 2026 survodutide review PMID 42822760 describes energy expenditure and hepatic effects] retatrutide, survodutide
Amylin pancreas beta cells (released with insulin) fullness signal via the hindbrain, slows stomach, lowers glucagon cagrilintide (in CagriSema)
  • Mono-agonist = one key, one lock (semaglutide, liraglutide, orforglipron).
  • Dual = one molecule, two locks (tirzepatide GIP+GLP-1; survodutide glucagon+GLP-1). CagriSema is two separate molecules in one pen, not one dual molecule [verified, REDEFINE 3 design abstract, PMID 42838477].
  • Triple = one molecule, three locks (retatrutide GIP+GLP-1+glucagon) [verified, TRIUMPH-1 NEJM 2026].
  • Why add glucagon if it raises sugar? GLP-1 and GIP pull sugar down hard enough to cancel that, while glucagon adds energy burn and liver-fat clearance [reported, reviews; inferred simplification].
  • Unexpected sign worth explaining: retatrutide raises resting heart rate in a dose-dependent way that peaked at 24 weeks and declined after, in phase 2 [verified, NEJM 2023 phase 2 abstract].

2. Animation storyboard (8 scenes)

# What is shown The one idea it teaches
1 A bite of food travels into the small intestine; cells in the gut wall light up and release glowing GLP-1 dots into the blood. Your gut already makes this hormone every time you eat.
2 A scissors-shaped enzyme (DPP-4) snips the GLP-1 dots apart within seconds; a clock reads "~2 minutes". Natural GLP-1 is powerful but gone almost instantly.
3 Dots reach the pancreas: insulin "doors" open only when sugar cubes are crowding the blood; glucagon taps close. Insulin rises only when sugar is high, which is why lows are rare.
4 The stomach drawn as a funnel; the outflow valve narrows and food drips out slowly; a "full" gauge stays high. Slower stomach = fuller for longer (and the source of nausea).
5 Dots cross into the brain: the hindbrain flashes "full", the hypothalamus dims a "hungry" neuron and brightens a "satisfied" one, a reward centre's "want more" signal quiets. Most weight loss comes from the brain turning down appetite and food noise.
6 The drug molecule appears with a shield (blocks the scissors) and a fatty tail that clips onto a large albumin "bus" that loops the body; calendar flips Mon to Mon. Engineering makes one dose last a week.
7 Three locks on a cell labelled GLP-1, GIP, glucagon; keys for semaglutide (1 lock), tirzepatide (2), retatrutide (3); an amylin key appears beside semaglutide for CagriSema. Newer drugs open more locks, which adds more weight loss.
8 A weight line falls over 72 weeks, then the injection icon disappears and the line climbs back two-thirds of the way within a year. It works while you take it; stopping usually means regain.
9 (optional) A staircase of doses with a person stepping up one step per month, nausea cloud shrinking at each landing. Doses go up slowly so the gut can adapt.

4. The usual questions

4.1 Does the weight come back when you stop?

  • STEP 1 extension: 327 people stopped semaglutide and lifestyle support at week 68. One year later they had regained about two-thirds of what they lost (net loss shrank from 17.3% to 5.6%). Blood pressure, sugar and cholesterol gains also drifted back [verified, Wilding DOM 2022, PMID 35441470, doi:10.1111/dom.14725].
  • SURMOUNT-4: 670 people who lost 20.9% in 36 weeks on tirzepatide were randomised. Switched to placebo: regained 14.0% over 52 weeks. Stayed on drug: lost a further 5.5%. At week 88, 89.5% of continuers kept ≥80% of their loss vs 16.6% of stoppers [verified, Aronne JAMA 2024, PMID 38078870, doi:10.1001/jama.2023.24945].
  • Plain answer: these drugs treat appetite while you take them; they do not reset it. Obesity behaves like blood pressure: stop the medicine, the number drifts back [inferred].
  • Caveat: stoppers in STEP 1 also lost the lifestyle programme at the same time, so the drug's sole effect is not isolated [verified design detail, inferred implication].

4.2 How much of the weight lost is muscle?

  • SURMOUNT-1 DXA substudy (160 people): about 75% of weight lost was fat and 25% was lean mass, the same split as in the placebo group who lost less [verified, Look DOM 2025, PMID 39996356, doi:10.1111/dom.16275].
  • Annals of Internal Medicine systematic review (35 RCTs, Apr 2026): median share of weight loss from muscle-related measures was 28.3% (range 15.9 to 39.9%); about two-thirds of incretin studies exceeded the expected ~25% benchmark, but so did nearly half of diet-only studies. No study measured real physical function [verified, Batsis 2026, PMID 41996180, doi:10.7326/ANNALS-25-00478].
  • "Lean mass" on DXA includes water, organs and connective tissue, not only skeletal muscle [reported, standard DXA caveat].
  • Plain answer: roughly 1 kg in every 4 lost is not fat, similar to dieting. Whether that matters for strength, especially in older people, is unknown [inferred].

4.3 "Ozempic face"

  • Not a defined medical diagnosis. It describes facial volume loss and skin laxity after rapid weight loss. A 2026 review calls it a "proposed phenotype" and says no controlled studies exist yet [verified, Cho 2026 Clin Cosmet Investig Dermatol, PMID 42893183]. Listed among dermatologic effects in a 2026 dermatology review [verified, Narla 2026 Clin Dermatol, PMID 42562129].
  • Most likely cause: facial fat loss in proportion to total fat loss, more visible in older skin [inferred]. No evidence the drug targets facial fat directly [inferred, absence of evidence].

4.4 Side-effect rates

Stomach and gut (most common, mostly during dose increases): - Retatrutide TRIUMPH-1 (4 / 9 / 12 mg vs pbo): nausea 28.6 / 38.4 / 42.4% vs 14.8%; diarrhoea 25.2 / 34.1 / 32.0% vs 13.5%; constipation 23.8 / 25.9 / 26.1% vs 10.9%; vomiting 10.6 / 22.8 / 25.3% vs 4.8% [reported, Lilly press release 21 May 2026]. - CagriSema REDEFINE 1: any GI event 79.6% vs 39.9% [verified]. - Survodutide: any GI event 81 to 90% vs 48% [verified]. - STEP 1 and SURMOUNT-1: nausea and diarrhoea most common, mild to moderate, mostly during escalation [verified]. (Exact STEP 1 per-symptom percentages not captured this pass; gap.)

Rare but serious (real-world, not trials): - Sodhi JAMA 2023 claims study, people using GLP-1s for weight loss vs bupropion-naltrexone: pancreatitis HR 9.09 (wide CI 1.25 to 66), bowel obstruction HR 4.22, gastroparesis HR 3.67, biliary disease HR 1.50 (not significant). Absolute pancreatitis rate 4.6 per 1,000 person-years on semaglutide vs 1.0 on comparator [verified citation PMID 37796527, doi:10.1001/jama.2023.19574; numbers [reported] via TCTMD 2023 because abstract unavailable]. [v2 fix] Full text now read: HRs are for semaglutide and liraglutide pooled vs bupropion-naltrexone; cohort was small (613 semaglutide, 4,144 liraglutide, 654 comparator); semaglutide pancreatitis rate 4.6 per 1,000 person-years rests on 2 events [verified, JAMA 2023 Table 1 and 2]. - Gallbladder: meta-analysis of 76 RCTs (103,371 people) found 37% higher relative risk of gallbladder/biliary disease; 2.29x in weight-loss trials; higher with higher doses and longer use [verified, He JAMA Intern Med 2022, PMID 35344001, doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2022.0338]. Rapid weight loss itself causes gallstones [reported].

Eye (NAION, a stroke of the optic nerve): - EMA PRAC (June 2025): NAION is a "very rare" side effect of semaglutide; about a twofold risk in diabetes studies, roughly 1 extra case per 10,000 person-years; stop semaglutide if it occurs [verified, EMA news page, 6 Jun 2025]. - Harvard claims study (482,912 matched pairs, T2D): GLP-1 vs SGLT2 users HR 1.85, absolute increase 0.29 per 1,000 person-years [verified, Tesfaye DOM 2025, PMID 41104517]. - 2026 meta-analyses: HR ~1.93 overall, significant in diabetes, not significant in obesity-only groups (HR 1.68, CI 0.72 to 3.91) [verified, Khani J Clin Pharmacol 2026, PMID 42612051]; another reports HR 2.37 to 3.36 in T2D [verified, Lampsas Graefes 2026, PMID 42201355]. High heterogeneity (I² up to 97%).

Thyroid C-cell warning (context): - Labels carry a boxed warning because these drugs caused thyroid C-cell tumours in rodents; whether this happens in humans is unknown. Contraindicated with personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer (MTC) or MEN2 [verified, Zepbound label via PrescriberPoint 2026; Foundayo boxed warning reported, BioSpace 2026]. - Scandinavian cohort (145,410 GLP-1 users, mean 3.9 years): no increase in thyroid cancer, HR 0.93 (0.66 to 1.31); medullary HR 1.19 with very wide CI [verified, Pasternak BMJ 2024, PMID 38683947, doi:10.1136/bmj-2023-078225]. - Why rodents differ: rodent C-cells carry far more GLP-1 receptors than human C-cells [reported, widely cited in reviews; not re-verified this pass]. [v2 fix] Primary source: GLP-1 receptors found on rodent C-cells; humans and monkeys had low expression, the drugs did not trigger calcitonin release in primates, and 20 months of liraglutide at >60x human exposure caused no C-cell hyperplasia in monkeys. Authors (Novo-affiliated) note long-term human effects remain unknown [verified, Bjerre Knudsen Endocrinology 2010, PMID 20203154, doi:10.1210/en.2009-1272]. - Label rates (v2 add): acute pancreatitis 0.2 per 100 patient-years on Wegovy vs <0.1 placebo; gallstones 1.6% vs 0.7% [verified, Wegovy PI]. Suicidality: - FDA (13 Jan 2026) found no increased risk of suicidal thoughts or behaviour, based on a 91-trial meta-analysis (107,910 patients) and a Sentinel study of ~2.24 million users, and asked makers to remove the warning from Wegovy, Zepbound and Saxenda labels [reported, HCPLive 13 Jan 2026 summarising FDA release; FDA page itself not opened].

Hair loss: - 2026 meta-analysis (17 studies, 1.09 million exposures): ~40% higher odds of non-scarring hair loss (OR 1.40), mostly telogen effluvium (stress shedding from rapid weight loss) and androgenetic patterns; alopecia areata not increased [verified, Viquez Burboa Skin Appendage Disord 2026, PMID 42621629]. - Trials report 3 to 7%; real-world cohorts much higher. Women and >10% weight loss are the strongest predictors. Usually temporary [verified, Leite Júnior Acta Diabetol 2026, PMID 42873315]. Hair loss is listed among common reactions on the Wegovy HD label [reported, HCPLive 2026].

New "skin sensation" signal (dysaesthesia): - Tingling, burning or sensitive skin rises at high doses: retatrutide 12.5% (12 mg) vs 0.9% [reported, Lilly 2026]; semaglutide 7.2 mg 18.9% vs 4.9% at 2.4 mg [verified, STEP UP T2D]. Mechanism unknown [reported, Mateescu 2026 review flags it as needing evaluation].

4.5 Aspiration under anaesthesia

  • Concern: a slowed stomach may still hold food despite fasting, risking inhaling stomach contents under sedation.
  • Multi-society guidance (ASA, AGA, ASMBS, ISPCOP, SAGES, 29 Oct 2024): most people can continue GLP-1s before elective surgery; higher-risk times are during dose escalation or with active GI symptoms; a 24-hour liquid diet and stomach ultrasound are options [verified, ASA news release 2024].
  • Evidence: aspiration about 0.01 to 0.05% for colonoscopy and under 0.3% in upper endoscopy, with retained food more common [verified, Beig 2026 review, PMID 42494946]. A Henry Ford cohort found retained food in 8.4% and fasting from solids >15 h cut odds by two-thirds [verified, Mendez-Pino Can J Anaesth 2026, PMID 42618722].

4.6 Gastroparesis

  • Sodhi 2023 claims signal HR 3.67 (above) [reported]. Litigation (4.17) required plaintiffs to show proper gastric-emptying tests as of Aug 2025, which underlines that many claims lacked objective diagnosis [reported, RxIndex tracker Oct 2026]. Whether drug-induced slowing is permanent is not established [inferred, no source found].

4.7 Pancreatitis

  • Labels warn; trial rates low. Real-world HR elevated in one small claims study (above). Retatrutide trials excluded anyone with past pancreatitis [verified, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05929066 eligibility]. Pooled phase 3 pancreatitis incidence figures not captured this pass (gap).

4.8 Alcohol and cravings

  • RCT: 48 adults with alcohol use disorder, low-dose semaglutide (0.25 to 1.0 mg) for 9 weeks reduced alcohol drunk in a lab test, drinks per drinking day and craving, but not number of drinking days; smokers cut cigarettes too [verified, Hendershot JAMA Psychiatry 2025, PMID 39937469, doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2024.4789].
  • Status: promising, small, short. Not approved for addiction [inferred].

4.9 Fertility and "Ozempic babies"

  • Most likely explanation: weight loss restores ovulation, especially in PCOS, in women who had irregular cycles [reported, Monash Lens 2024].
  • Pill interaction: tirzepatide's label says to switch from oral birth control to a non-oral method, or add a barrier method, for 4 weeks after starting and 4 weeks after each dose increase, because slowed stomach emptying can reduce pill absorption; effect largest after the first 5 mg dose [verified, Lilly Medical Information 2026 citing Zepbound PI]. Semaglutide labels carry no equivalent instruction [reported; not re-verified]. [v2 fix] Confirmed: UK FSRH guidance summarises semaglutide studies showing no meaningful change in pill hormone levels (injection: 39 women; oral: 25 women) [reported, FSRH/CoSRH interim guidance PDF]. Foundayo label now carries a 30-day switch-or-barrier rule after starting and after each step, like tirzepatide [verified, Foundayo PI §7.3]. Wegovy label: stop at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy [verified, Wegovy PI via Drugs.com].
  • Pregnancy safety: 2026 meta-analyses find no clear rise in major birth defects with early-pregnancy exposure (RR 1.02; OR 0.99), but certainty is "very low" and authors say this is not proof of safety [verified, Liu Endocr Connect 2026, PMID 42447044; Mavigök Front Pharmacol 2026, PMID 42964193].
  • Guidance commonly cited: stop semaglutide at least 2 months before trying to conceive (washout because of the long half-life) [reported, Monash Lens 2024 citing guidelines].

4.11 Compounded vs branded

  • Shortages ended: tirzepatide removed from FDA shortage list 2 Oct 2024; semaglutide 21 Feb 2025. Grace periods for mass compounding ended Mar to May 2025; courts sided with FDA; Fifth Circuit affirmed 27 Aug 2026 [reported, RxIndex trackers Oct 2026; FDA pages not opened].
  • FDA sent warning letters to 30 telehealth companies (3 Mar 2026) and proposed (30 Apr 2026) keeping semaglutide, tirzepatide and liraglutide off the 503B bulks list; final decision not found [reported].
  • FDA had 605 adverse-event reports for compounded semaglutide and 545 for compounded tirzepatide by 31 Jul 2025, many involving dosing errors from multi-dose vials [reported].
  • Compounded products are not FDA-reviewed for identity, purity or dose accuracy [reported].

4.12 Microdosing

  • No published trials of sub-label "microdoses" for weight, longevity or inflammation were found on PubMed (search returned zero) [verified, PubMed search 3 Nov 2026]. Lilly says it has no data on microdosing; Drucker says low-dose anti-inflammatory benefit is "uncertain" [reported, ConsumerAffairs 16 Jan 2026].
  • Note: the alcohol RCT used low label starting doses (0.25 to 0.5 mg) and found effects on craving, which is the closest real data [verified, Hendershot 2025; inferred relevance].
  • Status: anecdote only [inferred].

4.14 Oral versions

  • Wegovy pill (oral semaglutide 25 mg): a peptide that needs an absorption enhancer (SNAC), strict morning fasting rules; ~13.6 to 16.6% loss in OASIS 4; exposure similar to 2.4 mg injection in 82% of users [verified, Overgaard DOM 2026, PMID 42023428; weight figures reported].
  • Foundayo (orforglipron): small molecule, no food rules, cheaper to make; ~11% loss at top trial dose in ATTAIN-1; AE dropout up to 10.3%; Japanese trial only ~8% [verified, ATTAIN-1 and ATTAIN-J, PMID 42962687].
  • A Novo-funded indirect comparison estimated oral semaglutide 25 mg gives ~3 points more weight loss with fewer dropouts than orforglipron 36 mg [verified, Michalak DOM 2026, PMID 42225305; conflict of interest: funded by the competitor].

4.15 Who should not use them

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2 (boxed warning, contraindication) [verified, Zepbound label; retatrutide trials excluded the same, NCT05929066].
  • Serious hypersensitivity to the drug [verified, Zepbound label].
  • Pregnancy: not recommended; stop beforehand (see 4.9) [reported].
  • Caution groups in trials and guidance: past pancreatitis (excluded from retatrutide trials) [verified], severe GI disease/gastroparesis [reported], diabetic eye disease with rapid sugar drops [reported, not checked], severe liver impairment for Foundayo [verified, Lilly dosing page].

6. Negative mirror: what fails

6.1 Non-responders

  • In STEP 1, 13.6% on semaglutide did not reach even 5% loss (86.4% did) [inferred from verified 86.4%]. SURMOUNT-1: 9 to 15% missed 5% depending on dose [inferred from verified 85 to 91%]. ATTAIN-1 Japan: only 46 to 61% reached 5% [verified, ATTAIN-J]. Survodutide: only ~72% reached 5% vs 46% on placebo [verified].
  • Saxenda's label builds non-response in: stop if <4% at 16 weeks [reported].
  • Real-world: switchers from liraglutide had a weaker early response to semaglutide [verified, Milani Front Endocrinol 2026, PMID 42582375].
  • With T2D, every drug underperforms (see 3.1) [inferred].

6.2 Real-world discontinuation

  • Prime Therapeutics (5,780 commercially insured adults with obesity, started 2021 to Q1 2022): only 8% (1 in 12) still on a GLP-1 at three years; 14% for high-potency obesity products; one-year persistence rose from 33% (2021 starters) to 63% (Q1 2024 starters) [reported, press release 25 Jun 2025].
  • Johns Hopkins EHR (9,189 adults with obesity, no diabetes): 36.7% had a prescription lasting under a month and 82.7% of them got no further anti-obesity drug; only 18.6% had ≥336 days coverage at 12 months [verified, Ali Diabetes Obes Cardiometab CARE 2026, PMID 42920957].
  • Claims commentary: about one-third of obesity patients without diabetes still on therapy at 1 year; ~70% of T2D patients stop by 24 months [verified as stated in commentary, Sisodia AJMC 2026, PMID 42663544; underlying studies not traced].
  • Combine with 4.1: most people stop, and stoppers regain about two-thirds within a year [inferred]. This is the single biggest gap between trials and real life [inferred].

6.3 Trial-side failures and weak spots

  • CagriSema missed non-inferiority vs tirzepatide in REDEFINE 4 [reported].
  • SELECT: twice as many people stopped semaglutide for side effects (16.6% vs 8.2%) [verified].
  • Retatrutide 9 mg in T2D: 12% stopped for AEs or death vs 5% placebo; hypotension up to 6% [verified, TRIUMPH-2]. Five obesity phase 3s are positive but only one full peer-reviewed report existed by Sept 2026 per one review (now superseded by Sept to Oct 2026 NEJM/Lancet papers) [verified, Mateescu 2026].
  • Cross-trial comparison problem: estimands, durations and populations differ; head-to-head data exist only for tirzepatide vs semaglutide and CagriSema vs tirzepatide [inferred].
  • Nearly all pivotal trials are company-funded (Novo, Lilly, Boehringer) [verified from abstracts].

6.4 Lawsuits

No bellwether trial date or global settlement identified. Rule 702 expert hearing set for 14 to 18 Sep 2026; outcome not found [reported, RxIndex tracker updated 20 Oct 2026].

  • Court order (Aug 2025): gastroparesis plaintiffs must show an objective gastric-emptying test [reported].
  • Case counts are filings, not proven injuries [reported].

12. The 50 usual questions

Each answer: plain evidence, 2 to 4 sentences, source. Doses appear only as published label or trial facts.

Basics

1. How do GLP-1 drugs work? They copy a gut hormone released after meals, which tells the pancreas to release insulin when sugar is high, slows the stomach, and signals fullness in the brain. Most weight loss comes from eating less, not burning more. [verified, Drucker 2018, PMID 29617641; see Section 1]

2. What's the difference between Ozempic, Wegovy and Rybelsus? All three are semaglutide. Ozempic is the diabetes injection, Wegovy is the weight-loss brand (injection up to 2.4 mg weekly, HD 7.2 mg, or a daily pill up to 25 mg), and Rybelsus is the diabetes pill. Same molecule, different approved doses and uses. [verified, Wegovy PI; Section 0]

3. Which one is strongest? On average in big trials: retatrutide (not approved) ~24 to 25%, tirzepatide ~20 to 21%, CagriSema ~20%, semaglutide 7.2 mg ~19%, semaglutide 2.4 mg or 25 mg pill ~14 to 15%, orforglipron ~11%, liraglutide ~8%. Only two head-to-heads in obesity exist: tirzepatide beat semaglutide (-20.2% vs -13.7%) and CagriSema failed to match tirzepatide (20.2% vs 23.6%). Individual response varies more than these averages. [verified, PMIDs 42814954, 35658024, 40961952, 40353578; Novo release 23 Feb 2026]

4. How much weight will I lose? On the top approved doses, the average person without diabetes lost 15 to 21% over about 16 to 18 months, but 9 to 14% lost less than 5%. People with type 2 diabetes lose less on every drug. [verified, STEP 1, SURMOUNT-1; inferred from Section 3.1]

5. How fast does it start working? Appetite often drops within the first week, but doses rise slowly, so most weight loss happens months 3 to 12 and plateaus around 60 to 72 weeks in trials. Heart-event curves in SELECT separated from about day 20, before much weight was lost. [reported, TCTMD 14 May 2025; inferred from trial curves]

6. Is GLP-1 "better" than a dual or triple drug? Adding GIP or glucagon adds weight loss on average, but also new effects: retatrutide raises resting heart rate early and causes more skin tingling; glucagon parts add liver-fat burning. Only semaglutide (SELECT) and tirzepatide (SUMMIT, SURPASS-CVOT) have outcome trials; retatrutide's are running. [verified, Section 3.2; inferred]

How long, stopping, tapering

7. How long do I need to take it? Trials treat it like blood-pressure medicine: benefits last while you take it. No trial has found a duration after which you can stop without regain. [verified, PMID 42941232]

8. What happens when I stop? In STEP 1, people regained two-thirds of the lost weight within a year after stopping both drug and lifestyle support. In SURMOUNT-4, switching to placebo meant regaining 14% of body weight in a year, while staying on lost 5.5% more. [verified, PMIDs 35441470, 38078870]

9. Can I taper down slowly instead? There is no controlled trial showing that tapering prevents regain. An October 2026 systematic review found only exercise had randomised support for keeping weight off after stopping. [verified, Marchetti 2026, PMID 42941232]

10. Can I take a lower "maintenance" dose? Labels allow some maintenance choice (Wegovy 1.7 or 2.4 mg; Zepbound 5, 10 or 15 mg), but no published trial tested stepping down after reaching goal. Lower doses lose less weight in every trial. [verified, labels; reported absence]

Muscle and body composition

11. How much of the weight lost is muscle? About 25 to 28% of the weight lost is "lean mass," the same share as dieting, and lean mass on scans includes water and organs. In an MRI substudy of semaglutide 7.2 mg, thigh muscle shrank slightly but chair-stand performance held. [verified, PMIDs 39996356, 41996180, 42764399]

12. How do I protect muscle? Strength training and adequate protein are the standard advice; the only randomised evidence after stopping shows exercise helped keep 5.1 kg more off. Drugs designed to protect muscle (such as bimagrumab) are still in trials. [verified, PMID 42941232; reported, PMID 41948476]

13. Am I less strong afterwards? Unknown. A 35-trial review found no study measured real physical function. [verified, Batsis 2026, PMID 41996180]

Side effects

14. What are the most common side effects? Nausea, diarrhoea, vomiting and constipation. On Wegovy 2.4 mg, 44% had nausea vs 16% on placebo; on Zepbound 25 to 29% vs 8%. [verified, labels; table 10.1]

15. When do side effects happen and do they pass? They cluster in the first weeks and after each dose increase, then usually fade. Trials describe them as mostly mild to moderate and transient. [verified, STEP 1 and SURMOUNT-1 abstracts]

16. Why does the dose go up slowly? Because stomach side effects are dose-related and concentrate during escalation; starting lower reduced them in the retatrutide phase 2. Labels build in 4-week (injection) or 30-day (pill) steps. [verified, PMID 37366315; labels]

17. How many people stop because of side effects? In label trials, about 5 to 10% on the drug vs 3 to 4% on placebo. In the 3-year SELECT trial, 16.6% stopped for side effects vs 8.2%. [verified, labels; PMID 37952131]

18. Why do I burp (and why sulfur)? Burping is a listed side effect (7% on Wegovy vs under 1%), from food sitting longer in a slowed stomach. The rotten-egg smell has never been measured in a study. [verified, Wegovy PI; reported absence]

19. Will my hair fall out? Labels list hair loss at 3 to 5% vs 1 to 2% on placebo; it is mostly stress shedding after fast weight loss, more common in women, and usually temporary. [verified, labels; PMID 42621629]

20. What is "Ozempic face"? Facial volume loss in proportion to fat lost (about 7% of midface volume per 10 kg in one scan study), more visible in older skin. It is not a diagnosis and no study shows the drug targets the face. [verified, PMIDs 40407186, 42893183]

21. Can it cause pancreatitis? It is a label warning, but rates in trials are low: Wegovy 0.2 vs <0.1 cases per 100 patient-years; Zepbound 0.14 vs 0.15. One claims study found a raised risk with very wide uncertainty and only 2 semaglutide cases. [verified, labels; JAMA 2023 full text]

22. Gallbladder problems? Yes, modestly: gallstones 1.6% vs 0.7% on Wegovy, and a 76-trial meta-analysis found 37% higher relative risk, more with higher doses and longer use. Fast weight loss itself causes gallstones. [verified, Wegovy PI; PMID 35344001]

23. Thyroid cancer? Labels carry a boxed warning from rodent tumours, and people with personal or family medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2 must not use them. Human C-cells have few GLP-1 receptors, and a 145,410-person Scandinavian study found no rise. [verified, PMIDs 20203154, 38683947]

24. Can it affect my eyes? Europe's regulator lists a rare optic-nerve stroke (NAION) for semaglutide, roughly 1 extra case per 10,000 person-years in diabetes studies. Sudden vision loss means stop and seek care. [verified, EMA PRAC 6 Jun 2025]

25. Does it cause depression or suicidal thoughts? The FDA reviewed 91 trials (107,910 people) and 2.24 million users and found no increased risk, and asked for the warning to be removed from Wegovy, Zepbound and Saxenda. A minority report flat mood at high doses, which is a separate, less-studied issue. [verified, FDA DSC 13 Jan 2026, updated 3 Apr 2026]

26. Does it raise heart rate? Slightly: 1 to 4 bpm on Wegovy, 1 to 3 on Zepbound, 4 to 5 on Foundayo. Retatrutide's rise was dose-dependent and peaked around week 24. [verified, labels; PMID 37366315]

27. Is gastroparesis permanent? Not established. A small claims study linked GLP-1 use to gastroparesis diagnoses (HR 3.67), and courts now require an objective stomach-emptying test for lawsuits. [verified, JAMA 2023; reported, MDL order Aug 2025]

28. Is it safe before surgery or a scope? Most people can continue, but food can remain in the stomach despite fasting, especially during dose increases or with GI symptoms. Tell the anaesthetist; options include a 24-hour liquid diet. [verified, multi-society guidance 29 Oct 2024; PMID 42618722]

Alcohol, cravings, food noise

29. Can I drink alcohol on it? No label bans alcohol, but slower stomach emptying changes how fast alcohol hits, and a small study saw a delayed rise in breath alcohol. People also report feeling it more or less. [reported, Hub v1 fact 2, PMID 41093891]

30. Does it reduce drinking? In a 108-person trial in people with alcohol use disorder and obesity, heavy-drinking days fell 41 points on semaglutide vs 26 on placebo, on top of therapy. It is not approved for alcohol. [verified, Klausen Lancet 2026, PMID 42070571]

31. Does "food noise" really go quiet? Users and small studies report less food preoccupation, sometimes before much weight loss, and it tends to return after stopping. [reported, Hub v1 fact 4, PMIDs 42801078, 42641845]

Pregnancy, fertility, contraception

32. Is it safe in pregnancy? Not recommended; labels say stop when pregnancy is recognised. Meta-analyses of early accidental exposure found no clear rise in birth defects, but certainty is very low. [verified, labels; PMID 42447044]

33. How long before trying to conceive should I stop? The Wegovy label says at least 2 months before a planned pregnancy because of the ~1-week half-life. Zepbound and Foundayo labels give no fixed washout. [verified, Wegovy, Zepbound, Foundayo PIs]

34. Does it affect the pill? Tirzepatide and orforglipron: yes, labels say switch to a non-oral method or add a barrier for 4 weeks (Zepbound) or 30 days (Foundayo) after starting and after each dose increase. Semaglutide: studies showed no meaningful effect, but vomiting or diarrhoea can still make a pill fail. [verified, labels; reported, FSRH guidance]

35. Why "Ozempic babies"? Weight loss can restore ovulation (especially in PCOS), tirzepatide can lower pill absorption, and many users are not on contraception. [verified, Zepbound PI; reported, Hub v1 fact 20]

36. Breastfeeding? Not recommended. Wegovy tablets contain an absorption enhancer found in milk; Foundayo has no human data; one small study found no detectable injected semaglutide in milk. [verified, Wegovy and Foundayo PIs; reported, PMID 39275201]

Access, cost, forms

37. What does it cost? US self-pay prices fell sharply: Lilly's Foundayo starts at $149/month and $25 with commercial insurance. Under the White House pricing deal (TrumpRx), Wegovy and Zepbound were announced at about $350 and $346/month on average, and a Sept 2026 snapshot listed lower starter prices. Prices change often; check the maker's site. [verified, Lilly release 1 Apr 2026; reported, MobiHealthNews (undated) and v1 §4.13]

38. Are there generics? In the US only liraglutide (Saxenda generic, Aug 2025). Semaglutide generics launched in India from March 2026 at roughly a quarter to half of the branded price, and Canada approved its first in April 2026. [reported, Section 10]

39. Compounded vs branded: what's the difference? Compounded versions are not FDA-reviewed for identity, purity or dose, and the FDA logged hundreds of adverse-event reports, many from dosing errors with vials. Shortages ended in 2024 to 2025, and the FDA has proposed keeping these drugs off the bulk-compounding list (final decision not found). [reported, Section 4.11; RxIndex tracker Oct 2026]

40. Pill or injection? The Wegovy pill (25 mg) gave ~14% loss but must be taken on an empty stomach with ≤4 oz water and a 30-minute wait; only 1 to 2% is absorbed. Foundayo has no food rules but gave ~11% at the top trial dose. Injections remain the strongest. [verified, PMID 40934115; Wegovy and Foundayo PIs; EU SmPC]

41. What is "microdosing"? Using doses below the label for wellness or "just food noise." No published trial has tested it, and the makers say they have no data. [verified, PubMed search 3 Nov 2026; reported, ConsumerAffairs Jan 2026]

Who it's for

42. Can lean or normal-weight people use it? Approval is for BMI ≥30, or ≥27 with a weight-related condition. No trial has tested benefit or safety in lean people for weight or longevity; a placebo trial in healthy lean men found no effect on testosterone or desire. [verified, labels; reported, Hub v1 fact 23]

43. Can teenagers use it? Wegovy and Saxenda are approved from age 12 (Saxenda: >60 kg). In STEP TEENS, BMI fell 16.1% vs +0.6% on placebo, with gallstones in 4% vs none. Tirzepatide's teen trial has not reported. [verified, PMID 36322838; Saxenda PI; NCT06075667]

44. Do people with type 2 diabetes lose less? Yes, on every drug: retatrutide 18.8% vs 25.0%, CagriSema 13.7% vs 20.4%, survodutide 9.8% vs 13.0%. [verified, Section 3.1]

45. What if it doesn't work for me? About 1 in 7 on semaglutide and 1 in 10 on tirzepatide lost under 5%. Saxenda's label says stop if under 4% at 16 weeks. Switching to a stronger drug is common practice, but switch trials are few. [verified, PMIDs 33567185, 35658024; Saxenda PI]

46. Does it interact with my other medicines? Slowed stomach emptying can change how oral drugs are absorbed; Wegovy tablets raised levothyroxine levels 33%. With insulin or sulfonylureas the risk of low blood sugar rises. [verified, Wegovy PI; label class warnings]

Beyond weight and the pipeline

47. Does it protect the heart even without much weight loss? SELECT cut heart attack, stroke or heart death by 20%, and the benefit did not depend on starting weight or week-20 weight loss; about a third tracked waist shrinkage. [verified, PMIDs 37952131, 41138739]

48. Does it slow ageing? In old female mice, semaglutide extended median lifespan from 742 to 834 days. No human trial has tested ageing or lifespan. [verified, Nature 2026, PMID 42686906]

49. What is retatrutide and when will it be available? A weekly triple-hormone drug (GIP, GLP-1, glucagon) that gave 25.0% loss at the top dose in TRIUMPH-1 and more than doubled Ozempic's weight loss in diabetes. Lilly plans to file in early 2027, so approval is unlikely before late 2027 at the earliest. [verified, PMID 42814954; Lilly 28 Oct 2026; reported filing timing; inferred approval timing]

50. What are amylin drugs (CagriSema, eloralintide)? Amylin is a fullness hormone made alongside insulin. CagriSema (amylin + semaglutide) gave 20.4% loss and awaits an FDA decision expected Q4 2026; eloralintide (amylin alone) gave up to ~20% in phase 2 with fatigue and nausea as main side effects and is in phase 3. [verified, PMIDs 40544433, 41207310; Novo release 21 Sep 2026]
