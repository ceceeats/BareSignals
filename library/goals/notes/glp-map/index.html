1. The four hormones

All four are gut or pancreas hormones that the body releases around meals or fasting. Each has its own receptor. A drug "copies" a hormone when it switches on that receptor, usually for days instead of the natural hormone's minutes.

GLP-1 (glucagon-like peptide 1). Released by L cells in the small and large intestine after eating, and by a small group of brainstem neurons. Main jobs: tells the pancreas to release insulin only when glucose is high; slows stomach emptying; lowers the drive to eat through hypothalamus and brainstem; turns down the reward (dopamine) response to food and drugs. Natural GLP-1 lasts about 2 minutes in blood; semaglutide lasts about a week. [verified, textbook physiology; drug half-life on label]

GIP (glucose-dependent insulinotropic polypeptide). Released by K cells in the upper small intestine after eating. Main jobs: also boosts insulin when glucose is high; acts on fat tissue (helps fat cells store fat safely instead of letting it spill into liver and muscle); acts on the brain. Animal work shows GIP receptor activation calms nausea in the brainstem, one proposed reason tirzepatide's nausea is not worse than semaglutide's despite more weight loss. [reported; Borner et al. GIPR agonism inhibits PYY-induced nausea-like behaviour, Diabetes 2022, mouse and shrew data] Odd fact: blocking GIP (Amgen's MariTide) also causes weight loss, so the field still argues about which direction matters. [reported]

Glucagon. Made by alpha cells in the pancreas, mainly when fasting. It is the "release stored fuel" hormone: tells the liver to put out glucose, burn fat inside the liver, and take up amino acids. Added to a drug, it is thought to raise energy use and clear liver fat. Downsides on its own: it raises blood sugar (which the GLP-1 and GIP parts offset) and can raise heart rate. [verified physiology; effect sizes inferred]

Amylin. Made by the same beta cells as insulin and released with it after meals. Acts mainly on the area postrema and nearby brainstem (nucleus of the solitary tract, parabrachial nucleus), a spot outside the blood-brain barrier. Main jobs: makes meals end sooner (fullness at a smaller portion), slows stomach emptying, stops glucagon rising after meals. [verified physiology; brain sites confirmed in Carvas et al. eBioMedicine 2025, mouse] Amylin receptors are the calcitonin receptor paired with a helper protein (RAMP1, 2 or 3). Cagrilintide needs the RAMP1 and RAMP3 versions to lower weight in mice. [verified, Carvas 2025]

2. Fullness vs appetite suppression (the four feelings)

People use "appetite suppressant" for four different things. Mapping them is the cleanest way to explain why the drugs feel different. [inferred from the physiology above] - Hunger drive (wanting to start eating, thinking about food): hypothalamus. Mainly GLP-1. - Meal ends sooner (full after a few bites): brainstem. Mainly amylin, also GLP-1. - Staying full for hours: slower stomach. GLP-1 and amylin. - Food noise and cravings, including alcohol: dopamine reward circuit. Mainly GLP-1; amylin also in rodents. Old-style appetite suppressants (phentermine, tesofensine) act differently again: they raise brain stimulants (noradrenaline, dopamine), so hunger drops but a "wired" feeling, heart rate and sleep loss can come with it. [verified class pharmacology]

Tirzepatide is tuned more toward GIP than GLP-1. [reported] Retatrutide leans GIP, with weaker GLP-1 and glucagon activity than the natural hormones (Coskun 2022 cell data). [reported]

Brenipatide is the same receptor pair as tirzepatide but Lilly built it for the brain and inflammation, not weight: phase 1 in 200+ people, half-life about 9 to 12.5 days, GI events 25% on drug and placebo alike, dysesthesia (skin tingling) 15.2% vs 0%. Phase 3: RENEW-ALC-1 and 2 (NCT07219966, NCT07219953), 56 weeks, 2,200 people, alcohol use disorder; RENEW-MDD-1 (NCT07412756) depression. 13 phase 2 and 3 studies including tobacco, opioids, bipolar, schizophrenia, asthma, IBS, COPD. [reported, GEN news on Lilly data; trial IDs verified on ClinicalTrials.gov search]

4. Cagrilintide vs eloralintide

  • Cagrilintide (Novo): non-selective, switches on amylin receptors and the plain calcitonin receptor (sometimes called a dual amylin calcitonin receptor agonist). [reported]
  • Eloralintide (Lilly, formerly LY3841136): built as a selective amylin receptor agonist, mostly leaving the calcitonin receptor alone. [verified, Lilly wording]
  • Claim heard: the calcitonin receptor drives more nausea, so eloralintide may be gentler. [speculated; no head-to-head trial exists]
  • Eloralintide alone, phase 2 (Billings et al. Lancet, online 6 Nov 2025, doi 10.1016/S0140-6736(25)02155-5): 263 adults without diabetes, 48 weeks. Weight change 1 mg -9%, 3 mg -12%, 6 mg -18%, 9 mg -20%, 6 then 9 mg -20%, 3/6/9 mg -16%, placebo -0.4%. Most common: nausea and fatigue, mostly mild; slower step-up gave fewer side effects. [reported, Drug Topics summary of the Lancet paper]
  • Cagrilintide alone in REDEFINE 1: -11.5% at 68 weeks. [reported]
  • Not a fair comparison: different trials, lengths and people. Eloralintide alone reached roughly what semaglutide alone does; cagrilintide alone reached less. [inferred]
  • Community claim (from the editor): eloralintide works better in community dosing. Not yet checked against reports. [gap]

5. Pairings

Why pair at all: GLP-1 drugs and amylin drugs mostly act on different brain cells (hypothalamus and reward vs brainstem), so their effects add. [inferred, supported by combination trial results]

Semaglutide + cagrilintide (CagriSema). REDEFINE 1, 3,417 adults without diabetes, 68 weeks: CagriSema -20.4% (treatment policy; -22.7% if taken as directed), semaglutide alone -14.9%, cagrilintide alone -11.5%, placebo about -3%. [reported, LifeScience Daily summary of Garvey NEJM 2025] Lean tissue was about 33% of weight lost on CagriSema (DXA, 252 people, congress data). [reported] Filed with FDA 18 Dec 2025, decision expected Q4 2026; not approved as of this run. [reported] Novo's Q3 report of 4 Nov 2026 not yet checked for CagriSema news. [gap]

Tirzepatide + eloralintide (EloraTZP). Lilly phase 2b (NCT06603571), 367 adults with obesity and type 2 diabetes, 48 weeks, EASD 2026: up to -23.3% on eloralintide 9 mg + tirzepatide 15 mg vs -14.8% on tirzepatide 15 mg alone; A1C up to -2.9 vs -2.4 points. GI side effects more common on the combination. Stopped for side effects: combination 10.8% to 27.0%, eloralintide alone 0 to 10.8%, tirzepatide 2.9%, placebo 16.7%. Phase 3 starts Q4 2026. [verified, Lilly release 24 Oct 2026] Note: people with diabetes lose less weight on every drug, so -23% here would likely be higher in people without diabetes. [inferred]

Tirzepatide + cagrilintide. No trial; each company pairs its own drugs. Community pairing. [reported, thin]

Retatrutide + cagrilintide. No human trial. Sold as a factory blend (in the Library as Reta + Cagri). Community idea: retatrutide covers hunger drive, insulin and liver; cagrilintide adds the brainstem "meal ends" signal. The 2026 rat pair study claimed by some sites was not found (Library v48 flag). [reported, thin; gap]

Retatrutide + eloralintide. No human trial. Newer community pairing, same logic as Reta + Cagri with the hoped-for gentler amylin drug. [speculated]

Retatrutide + tesamorelin. No trial of the pair. Tesamorelin is a GHRH analogue (pituitary releases more growth hormone, IGF-1 rises). In HIV patients with belly fat, 12 months cut visceral fat about 18%; at 6 months -10.9% vs -0.6% placebo; gains reversed quickly after stopping; no change in glucose in that trial. [reported, NATAP summary of Falutz et al. 2010] Community idea: retatrutide drives weight down while tesamorelin targets deep belly fat and may help keep lean tissue. Whether it actually protects muscle on a GLP drug is untested. [speculated] Growth hormone tends to raise blood glucose and cause water retention or joint aches; retatrutide lowers glucose. [inferred]

6. Muscle and protein

Share of weight lost that was lean tissue (DXA; lean is not the same as muscle): - Semaglutide 2.4 mg STEP 1: about 39 to 40% - Tirzepatide SURMOUNT-1: about 25% - Orforglipron ATTAIN-1: about 27% - Retatrutide phase 2 type 2 diabetes substudy: about 35% (fat mass -15% to -26% at 4 to 12 mg, 36 weeks) - CagriSema REDEFINE 1: about 33% [reported, The RX Index table citing each primary paper] Normal diet-only weight loss is also roughly 20 to 30% lean. [reported, general literature]

Nutrition advisory (ASN, ACLM, OMA, TOS; Mozaffarian et al. 2025, Obesity doi 10.1002/oby.24336): protein 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg per day during weight loss, about 80 to 120 g a day; strength training at least 3 times a week plus 150 minutes of moderate cardio; nutrients run short below about 1,200 kcal (women) or 1,800 kcal (men); watch iron, calcium, magnesium, zinc, vitamins A, D, E, K, B1, B12, C; warning signs are unusual fatigue, hair shedding, dry itchy skin, weakness, slow healing, easy bruising. [verified, advisory text]

7. Alcohol and reward

GLP-1 receptors sit on the dopamine reward circuit (ventral tegmental area, nucleus accumbens). In animals, GLP-1 drugs blunt the dopamine surge alcohol causes. Human trials (already on the GLP-1s Case): Lancet 2026, 108 people, heavy drinking days fell 41 percentage points on semaglutide vs 26 on placebo; Hendershot 2025 JAMA Psychiatry, 48 people, fewer drinks per drinking day and less craving. [verified on the Case page] Amylin: salmon calcitonin (an amylin and calcitonin agonist) cut alcohol-induced dopamine release in mice and cut intake about 39% over 24 h in high-drinking rats. [verified, Kalafateli et al. Addiction Biology 2018] Brenipatide is the first incretin drug in phase 3 for alcohol. [reported]

8. Retatrutide headline numbers

TRIUMPH-1 (NCT05929066), topline 21 May 2026, 80 weeks: 4 mg -19.0%, 9 mg -25.9%, 12 mg -28.3%; 45.3% on 12 mg lost 30% or more. Stopped for side effects 4.1%, 6.9%, 11.3% vs 4.9% placebo. Dysesthesia in a subset, mostly mild. [reported, Pharmacy Times summary of Lilly release] Community reports note higher resting heart rate and feeling warmer. [reported, aggregator, 100 reports, uncounted by symptom]

9. Baseline

Placebo arms in these trials lose 0.4% (eloralintide) to about 3% (REDEFINE 1) with diet and activity advice. Every drug number should be read against that. [verified from the trials above]

10. Second pass findings (5 Nov 2026)

Receptor detail. Retatrutide EC50 in human receptor cells: GIPR 0.064 nM, GLP-1R 0.78 nM, GCGR 5.8 nM, so it is roughly 12x stronger at GIP than GLP-1 and 90x stronger at GIP than glucagon (Coskun et al. Cell Metab 2022, via Cayman datasheet). [reported] Eloralintide EC50: AMY1 23.9 pM, calcitonin receptor 291 pM, AMY3 254 pM (Briere et al. Mol Metab 2025, via Cayman). [reported] So eloralintide is not amylin-only; it is AMY1-preferring, about 12x less active at the plain calcitonin receptor. [inferred from the EC50s]

Head to head trials. SURMOUNT-5: tirzepatide 20.2% vs semaglutide 13.7% at 72 weeks. [reported] REDEFINE 4 (NCT06131437, 809 adults, open label, 84 weeks, reported 23 Feb 2026): CagriSema 23.0% vs tirzepatide 15 mg 25.5%; CagriSema failed non-inferiority. [reported, Clinical Trials Arena] Meaning: an amylin pair on semaglutide did not beat tirzepatide alone. [inferred]

TRIUMPH-1 detail. 2,339 adults randomised 1:1:1:1. Dysesthesia 5.1% (4 mg), 12.3% (9 mg), 12.5% (12 mg) vs 0.9% placebo. Nausea 28.8 / 38.4 / 42.4% vs 14.8%; vomiting 10.6 / 22.8 / 25.3% vs 4.8%. [reported, HCPLive summary of the ADA 2026 abstract] Placebo weight change not in the summaries checked. [gap]

Heart rate. In the retatrutide phase 2 obesity trial, heart rate rose with dose, peaked at 24 weeks, then declined; clinical meaning unclear. [reported, TCTMD on Jastreboff NEJM 2023] No bpm figure in the summary. [gap]

CagriSema safety. GI adverse events 79.6% vs 39.9% placebo in REDEFINE 1; Novo describes low single-digit stopping for side effects. [reported, HCPLive] FDA: not approved per Drugs.com; Novo Q3 call (4 Nov 2026) could not be read. [gap]

Regain after stopping. STEP 1 extension: people regained 11.6 of the 17.3 percentage points lost within a year of stopping semaglutide (about two thirds); net loss at week 120 was 5.6%. [verified, Wilding et al. Diabetes Obes Metab 2022]

Community, eloralintide. Peptide Field Book (aggregator, not a vendor) cites about 8 Reddit posts; most r/Eloralintide posts come from trial participants. Reported: starts around 0.5 to 1 mg weekly, few reach 6 to 9 mg; strong appetite effect; nausea in waves about three days after a shot; tiredness; added to tirzepatide or retatrutide mainly to break a plateau; called "cagri on steroids". [reported, thin] Kratos blog (a vendor, excluded as evidence) lists fatigue as the most persistent complaint and some non-responders; noted only as a direction. Not enough separate people yet to call any effect CONSISTENT (5+). [gap]

11. Forum pass, first-hand counts (5 Nov 2026)

Source: glp1forum.com, four threads, posts Jun 2025 to Nov 2026, read in the editor's built-in browser. Own-use reports only; one secondhand report (a spouse) noted as a warning, not counted. Search on the forum needs a login, so other threads were not found this pass. [reported throughout]

Reta + Cagri (11 people). - Helped appetite or food noise: 7 (CONSISTENT). Typical add: cagrilintide 0.125 to 1.7 mg weekly on top of retatrutide 1 to 12 mg. Lines: quick fullness and small portions; "wow what a difference" at reta 7 + cagri 0.5, 108 lb lost overall; food noise down like tirzepatide at reta 3 + cagri 0.5; restarted loss and reached goal (33% total) after adding cagri to max-dose reta, no side effects; "adds a great deal of self restraint". - No change: 2 (both low cagri doses, 0.25 to 0.5 mg; one had just come off high-dose tirzepatide, one was a retatrutide non-responder). - Side effects: nausea made 1 stop after 6 months; 1 reports some nausea at 0.25 mg; several call cagri "very strong" and stopped after one or two shots. - Pattern: many say retatrutide's own appetite effect only arrives around 6 to 8 mg, and advise letting it build for weeks before stacking. Cagri is used mainly to break stalls.

Tirz + Cagri (8 people). - Helped: 5 (CONSISTENT). 100 lb in a year with cagri added for the last 8 months ("starving all the time before, now I sometimes forget to eat"); broke a stall at tirz 15 + cagri 2.4; 0.5 mg used as needed gives a day of zero hunger; "works too good", hard to eat for 3 days, no nausea; "works great" at 1.2 mg. - Faded: 3. Worked at first, then needed ever higher doses and stopped working within weeks (one went to 2.4 mg and will not reorder). - Side effects: tiredness in weeks 1 to 2 (2 people, eased); vomiting when tirz 15 and cagri 2.4 were taken the same day (1). Warning (secondhand): a 3.75 mg first dose of cagri caused hours of vomiting; replies called it far too high a start.

Eloralintide, mostly added to tirz or reta (11 people). - Helped: 8 (CONSISTENT). Broke long stalls on tirzepatide (2 people, one stalled about a year, one 5 to 6 months then lost about 12 kg); very strong appetite effect at 4 to 5 mg with retatrutide; 1 mg bridges the last days before the weekly tirz shot; works alone at 1 mg (1 person, early); a trial participant on 9 mg went 240 to 180 lb in 48 weeks; at 0.5 mg the urge to drink faded more than on retatrutide (1). - No change: 1 (a whole vial, no real effect on hunger). - Mixed: 1 (strong tiredness and constipation from day 2, suppression only from weeks 4 to 5). - Slow to build: 4 people say it felt weak for the first 2 to 5 weeks, then strong. Posters tie this to its long half-life (about 14 days, so near steady state only after 4 to 8 weeks). [inferred by posters] - Side effects: constipation (3), tiredness (2), thirst (1). - Fading: 1 long-term user felt it weaken after about 13 weeks while starting heavy resistance training. - Elora vs cagri, direct own-use comparisons: 1 (cagri "didn't really work", eloralintide broke the stall). A second person who had cagri fade also found elora working. Two more have just switched with no result yet. So "works better than cagri" has a first signal but under the 5-person bar. [gap]

Reta + Tesa (5 people, often with ipamorelin). - Scale stalled or went up: 5 (CONSISTENT). One saw no extra result and dropped tesamorelin for stinging and the nightly shot; one stalled 7 weeks on reta 12 with tesa and ipa; one reports weight going up after adding tesa; two say the scale stopped but they look leaner or lost some belly fat. - Posters blame ipamorelin, which raises hunger, and water retention from growth hormone; one broke the stall by raising retatrutide and another by dropping tesa and ipa. - Meaning: on the scale, Reta + Tesa looks worse, not better. Any benefit would show as body shape (belly fat) rather than weight, and nobody posted scans. [inferred]

Mixing chemistry (repeated across threads). Several people raise cagrilintide's pH: it is made to sit around pH 4; some adjust BAC water with acetic acid; others use BAC alone without problems. Posters also warn against mixing GLP drugs with anything in one vial or syringe because they can gel. [reported] Ties to the site's Mixing water page.
