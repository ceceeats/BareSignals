Baseline first (what happens untreated or on placebo)

  • Period pain affects more than half of women who menstruate; 10 to 12% have pain severe enough to lose 1 to 3 days a month. [reported, Dawood GLOWM chapter]
  • Placebo: in 35 NSAID trials, 18% of women on placebo got moderate or excellent relief vs 45 to 53% on NSAIDs (ibuprofen, naproxen, mefenamic acid). [verified, Cochrane CD001751, 2015]. So any compound needs to beat roughly 1 in 5 improving on nothing, and ideally get near ibuprofen.
  • Pain varies cycle to cycle with stress, sleep, travel and hormones, so one bad or good month proves nothing. No solid number found for that variation. [inferred]
  • Ovulation link is contested. The textbook view is that primary period pain only happens in ovulatory cycles, because the progesterone drop triggers the prostaglandin burst. [reported, GLOWM]. But a 2018 study of 168 monitored cycles found pain peaked at the same level with or without ovulation (4.77 vs 4.36 out of 10). A UBC year-long study of 53 women found the same. [reported, J Pediatr Adolesc Gynecol 2018; CeMCOR UBC]. So "weight loss restarts ovulation, so periods hurt more" is [speculated] at best.

What people report, by compound

GLP-1s (semaglutide, tirzepatide, retatrutide). The only class with real community volume.

Source: G68 Reddit (ChatGPT) tab, 22 first-person GLP-1 reports, checked against the own-use rule. Tally: - Better 8 (#1, 3, 5, 6, 14, 17, 21, 23) · Partial 6 · Mixed 1 · No change 5 · Worse 2. - 8 separate people reporting improvement meets the CONSISTENT bar (5+). [reported] - The pattern that matters: what improves is mostly daily pelvic pain, bloating, heaviness and fatigue. Period-day pain itself improves least and sometimes gets worse: - #16: daily pain better, period pain worse. - #10: less bloating, but "worst menstrual pain since surgery". - #4: daily pain, migraine and PMDD better, but day 1 to 2 period pain unchanged. - #9: new ovulation pain about 4 months in, then constant pain. - Retatrutide: 1 report (#23), 1 mg/week, daily pelvic and back pain 5/10 to 0/10, still holding months later. Single case. [reported] - Studies: no completed GLP-1 trial in endometriosis as of May 2026. [reported, secondary search; confirm on ClinicalTrials.gov next pass]. One case report: semaglutide brought severe period pain down to 3/10 in one woman; it was given because one earlier patient had improved. [verified, Cureus 2025]. Rodent liraglutide work reported to shrink implants. [reported, via a seller page; primary not traced] - Cycle effects: 27% of 1,700 app users noticed cycle changes on GLP-1s. [reported, Natural Cycles survey]. FDA reports flag heavy bleeding, clots and bleeding between periods for semaglutide, with a narrower set for tirzepatide. [reported, Obstet Gynecol FAERS analysis 2026, via news] - GLP-1 lens: daily pain relief is Unknown whether drug or weight. Period changes are likely Both. Grade: Plausible for daily endometriosis pain; Unproven for period-day pain.

GLP-1s and ordinary period pain, outside endometriosis (new in v2)

First-person, own-use, non-Reddit (Mumsnet weight-loss and women's health boards), 7 Nov 2026.

Better: 5 people (meets the CONSISTENT bar). [reported] 1. Endometriosis thread: first pain-free period ever (periods since age 10), with no pre-period pain, cramps or fatigue. 2. Endometriosis thread: first month on Mounjaro, the first endometriosis-pain-free month since age 17. She credits the drug, not the weight loss. 3. Endometriosis thread, 5 weeks: very little endometriosis pain; a 23-day cycle stretched to 29. 4. Hormone thread: no periods for years on the mini pill; on Mounjaro they came back regular and "much less painful". 5. Off-and-on evidence (Mounjaro = tirzepatide, a GLP-1/GIP drug; no peptides involved): 6 months of manageable periods on Mounjaro; 2 months after stopping, terrible periods again. This is the only report found where pain returned after stopping a compound. [reported]

Worse: 5 people. [reported] 1. 3 regular cycles since starting, but "doubled over", taking painkillers every 4 hours. 2. Regular before, now pain "off the scale" and heavier. 3. Perimenopause: can now feel ovulation; periods more painful and longer. 4. Two first-person Reddit lines quoted in a news piece (Tyla, Nov 2025): "longer, heavier, more painful", and "week 4… worst cramping I have ever experienced (I usually have little to none)".

Also counted, separately: - A blog collected 36 semaglutide users' accounts: 25% reported painful periods, mostly those whose periods also got longer. [reported, self-selected] - A "real users" article from a pharmacy site quotes Reddit handles that look invented; excluded.

Reading it: in endometriosis, the split leans better. In ordinary period pain, it's about even, and worse often comes in the first 1 to 3 cycles, or with heavier and longer bleeding. [inferred]. A second likely-fabricated figure: one GLP-1 seller claims tirzepatide's SURMOUNT-1 trial showed "23.1% menstrual disorder vs 8.7% placebo". The published trial does not report menstrual disorder at that level; treat as unverified. [speculated, pending check]

Mechanism candidates (none proven): - Lower inflammation (CRP falls about 35 to 40% on GLP-1s). [reported] - Changed estrogen as fat is lost. [reported] - Gut side effects that feel like cramps: the Ozempic label lists abdominal pain in about 6 to 7% of users. [reported] - No study found on GLP-1 drugs acting directly on uterine muscle contraction. [verified negative search]

BPC-157, TB-500 / Tβ4, KPV, GHK-Cu (the Glow/Klow parts)

  • v3 open sweep, 7 Nov 2026, 8 more searches: every hit for "period pain/cramps + peptides" was a seller, clinic or blog article. Not one woman's first-hand account. [verified negative search]
  • Klow blend makeup as commonly sold (80 mg vial): 50 mg GHK-Cu + 10 mg each of KPV, BPC-157 and TB-500. So GHK-Cu is about 62% of each shot, and KPV only about 12%. [reported, dosing reference site]. The blend itself has never been studied in humans. [reported]
  • v2 dedicated search (KPV + cramps, BPC/Wolverine/Glow stack + period): still zero first-person reports. Only vendor product pages (a non-injectable KPV range, clinic pages) list "cramps" as a use. [verified negative search]
  • First-person reports found: zero. None outside Reddit, and none in the G68 tab either, even though its header asks for them.
  • What exists is vendor content. One US compounding seller's article hub cites "clinical trials of 186 women, 58% less pain" for BPC-157, a TB-500 "45% vs placebo" figure, and an "International Journal of Peptide Medicine". None could be traced on PubMed. Treat as likely fabricated. [speculated, pending check]
  • A peptide vendor's own page admits no randomised trial has tested BPC-157, KPV or Tβ4 for period pain. [reported]
  • PubMed search (peptide × dysmenorrhea/uterus/menstrual): 11 hits, none on pain. [verified]
  • Tβ4 animal signals cut both ways:
  • Helps repair scarred uterine lining in mice (hydrogel, Nat Commun 2026). [verified]
  • Raised in mouse uteri with adenomyosis, itself a cause of painful periods (In Vivo 2003). [verified]
  • Plus the blood-vessel growth warning already on the Endometriosis case page.
  • Mechanism ranking only: KPV (NF-κB anti-inflammatory) > BPC-157 (nitric oxide and prostaglandin interaction in animals) > TB-500 > GHK-Cu. Grade all four: Unproven.

LDN (low-dose naltrexone)

  • Reddit: 2 no change for endometriosis (both helped another condition), 1 better but mixed with metformin and diet. [reported]
  • The only placebo trial (Penn State, 4.5 mg + norethindrone) was terminated at 9 participants. No usable result. [verified, NCT03970330]

Supplements with human trials on period pain (stronger than any peptide)

  • Ginger: several meta-analyses find less pain severity vs placebo, but no change in how long pain lasts. In one 100-woman trial, ginger 250 mg 4×/day was not significantly different from ibuprofen 400 mg. Trials carry high bias risk. [verified, Cureus 2021 meta; J Integr Complement Med 2024]. Grade: Likely.
  • Vitamin D: pooled pain drop SMD −1.02 at 2 months. [verified, J Caring Sci 2020 meta]. Grade: Plausible, few small trials.
  • Vitamin E: contradictory. The 2020 meta found a small benefit (SMD −0.47); Cochrane 2016 found none (0.00 points, 2 trials). [verified]. Grade: Unproven.
  • Vitamin B1: beat fish oil by 1.6 points in one 120-woman trial. [verified, Cochrane 2016]. Zinc performed the same as ginger in one trial. All low quality.
  • Melatonin (endometriosis): contradictory. 2013 Brazil trial, 10 mg for 8 weeks: pain down, and the risk of needing a painkiller cut by 80% (40 women). 2023 Sweden trial, 20 mg for 2 cycles: no difference (2.9 vs 3.3, p = 0.45; 40 women). [verified]. Grade: Unproven.
  • PEA + polydatin (endometriosis): a 2017 meta-analysis found relevant improvement in chronic pelvic pain and period pain. A 260-woman placebo trial is running in Australia. [reported/verified registry]. Grade: Plausible.
  • NAC (endometriosis): single-group studies only, no placebo arm. Less pain and smaller cysts reported. [reported]. Grade: Plausible.

Weak spots to keep in view

  • Peptide data is empty. Building a peptide period-pain story would rest on vendor claims.
  • The 8 GLP-1 "better" reports are all from Reddit, an endometriosis-only crowd, on mixed hormone treatments. They can't isolate the drug.
