Glossary · 30 terms
Amino acid
One of the 21 building blocks. Same backbone, different side chain.
Peptide
A short chain of amino acids. Usually under about 50.
Protein
A long chain that folds into a working 3D shape.
Residue
One amino acid once it sits inside a chain.
Peptide bond
The link between two amino acids. A water molecule leaves each time one forms.
Backbone
The repeating N, C, C spine every amino acid shares. Navy in the drawings.
Side chain (R)
The part that makes each amino acid different. Orange in the drawings.
N end, C end
The two ends of a chain. Chains are built and read from N to C.
DNA
The gene. Four letters: A, T, C, G.
mRNA
The working copy of a gene. T becomes U.
Codon
Three mRNA letters that call for one amino acid.
Stop codon
UAA, UAG or UGA. Tells the ribosome the chain is done.
Ribosome
The cell’s machine that reads mRNA and links amino acids.
tRNA
The carrier that brings each amino acid to the ribosome.
Essential
Your body can’t make it. It has to come from food.
Conditionally essential
Your body makes it, but not always enough under stress or illness.
BCAA
Branched-chain amino acids: leucine, isoleucine, valine.
g/mol
Grams per mole. The standard unit for molecular weight.
Monoisotopic mass
Weight counting only the most common atom of each element. Labs often report this one.
D amino acid
The mirror image of the natural form. Resists breakdown, so peptides last longer.
Aib
A lab-made amino acid that blocks enzymes from cutting the chain.
Acetylated start
A small cap on the N end. Protects the chain from breakdown.
Amide end
A cap on the C end. Same job: more stable, longer lasting.
Cyclic
A chain closed into a ring. Stiffer and harder to break down.
Bioregulator
Very short peptides, 2 to 4 amino acids, studied mainly in Russia for tissue-specific effects.
Sequence
The order of amino acids in a chain, written N to C in one-letter codes.
Folding
How a chain twists into its working shape: helices, sheets, then 3D.
Lipidation
A fatty acid tail added so the peptide rides on albumin and lasts days.
Pyroglutamate
A starting E that curls into a ring. A natural cap on some peptides.
Recoded codon
UGA usually means stop. With a special signal it calls for selenocysteine.
